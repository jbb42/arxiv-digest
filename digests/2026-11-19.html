Inverse Reconstruction of Two-Field Potentials for a DESI-Motivated Phantom-Divide Transition

RelevantTonghua Liu, Xiaolei Li, Marek Biesiada, Tian-Nuo Li, Jun-Qian Jiang, Arman Shafieloo, Xin Zhang
arXiv:2611.23906 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: The paper addresses dynamical dark energy and phantom-divide crossing prompted by DESI BAO data, which is directly relevant to background and linear growth modeling in beyond-LCDM cosmologies.

Recent cosmological data from DESI DR2, CMB, and supernovae suggest evolving dark energy that crosses the phantom divide ($w = -1$), a trajectory that a single canonical scalar field cannot achieve without mathematical or physical pathologies. The authors present an inverse reconstruction methodology for a two-field 'quintom' system (combining one canonical and one negative-kinetic phantom scalar field) targeting a Metastable Emergent Dark Energy (MEDE) expansion history. By embedding their reconstructed local potential into the CLASS Boltzmann solver, they demonstrate that both the background expansion and linear perturbations remain completely regular and finite through the phantom crossing. Crucially, they show that while the expansion history uniquely fixes the rate of potential-energy release at the transition, local cubic deformations of the potential exist that preserve both background evolution and linear growth predictions.

Main conclusions
  • Joint MCMC constraints using CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, and DES-Dovekie SNe Ia yield a transition redshift $z_t = 0.399^{+0.079}_{-0.071}$ and dark energy variation amplitude $ rac{1}{2}\Delta = 0.246^{+0.075}_{-0.071}$.
  • At the phantom-divide crossing $z_t$, the dark energy density peaks and the dimensionless potential-energy release rate is strictly determined by $\mathcal{R}_t = \Delta/2$, independent of how kinetic energy is partitioned between the two fields.
  • Linear field perturbations and cosmological growth predictions ($f\sigma_{s8}$) integrated via CLASS remain regular through $w=-1$ and show variations well within DESI DR1 measurement uncertainties across different kinetic split choices.
  • Higher-order potential deformations (e.g. cubic terms in the direction normal to the trajectory) leave background and linear perturbation dynamics exactly unchanged, isolating features fixed by observations from model-dependent assumptions.

Abstract

A phantom-divide transition favored by recent cosmological observations poses an inverse problem: which field potentials realize the inferred expansion history, and what features are uniquely fixed by it? We reconstruct local potentials for a canonical-phantom scalar pair with standard matter and radiation, targeting Metastable Emergent Dark Energy histories constrained by CMB, DESI BAO, and Type Ia supernova data. The coupled-field Friedmann evolution reproduces the target background to numerical accuracy across the transition, with regular perturbations through the crossing. At the phantom divide, the transition slope sets a dimensionless potential-energy release rate insensitive to the partition of kinetic energy. We identify local potential deformations that preserve both background expansion and linear dynamics. This construction gives a classical field realization of the inferred transition and disentangles observationally fixed energy balances from interaction terms requiring additional theoretical input.

Interpretable attention-based neural posterior estimation for fast radio burst cosmology

RelevantTian-Yang Sun, Ji-Guo Zhang, Wan-Peng Sun, Jing-Fei Zhang, Xin Zhang
arXiv:2611.24542 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.HE
Why: The paper demonstrates a state-of-the-art neural posterior estimation (NPE) method with attention-based pooling, which is highly relevant to the student's work on emulators and simulation-based inference for beyond-ΛCDM models.

Fast Radio Bursts (FRBs) serve as powerful probes of the intergalactic medium and late-time cosmic expansion through their dispersion measure (DM) dependence on redshift. As FRB catalogs grow, traditional MCMC-based Bayesian inference becomes computationally prohibitive due to the need for repeated likelihood evaluations. The authors introduce an attention-based neural posterior estimator (AttNPE) that uses an attention encoder to compress catalog data into a low-dimensional representation, which then conditions a masked autoregressive flow to generate posterior samples. This approach allows for rapid, amortized inference, achieving a speedup of $O(10^6)$ compared to MCMC while maintaining high accuracy in recovering parameter correlations.

Main conclusions
  • AttNPE generates $5\times 10^4$ posterior samples in $0.036$ s, compared to $72.8$ hours for MCMC, providing a massive reduction in computational overhead.
  • The model successfully recovers the seven-dimensional posterior for both simulated and observed catalogs, with Jensen-Shannon divergence values below $1\times 10^{-2}$ relative to MCMC.
  • Attention-weight analysis reveals that FRBs in the intermediate-redshift range ($0.3 \leq z < 0.7$) currently contribute most significantly to the cosmological constraints, balancing source abundance with redshift-dependent sensitivity.
  • The estimator is well-calibrated, as demonstrated by marginal probability-probability (PP) curves and joint TARP coverage tests lying close to the diagonal.

Abstract

Fast radio bursts (FRBs) probe the integrated free-electron density along extragalactic sightlines and can be used to study late-time cosmic evolution. Conventional Bayesian analyses require repeated evaluations of computationally expensive likelihoods, making inference increasingly challenging as FRB catalogs grow. In this work, we develop an interpretable attention-based neural posterior estimator (AttNPE) for rapid posterior inference of the physical parameters from FRB catalogs with measured redshifts. The attention encoder compresses the catalog into a low-dimensional representation, and a conditional masked autoregressive flow maps samples from a base distribution to the seven-dimensional posterior. AttNPE yields posterior distributions in close agreement with Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), recovering the parameter correlations in both simulated and observed FRB catalogs. For the observed catalog, AttNPE generates $5\times10^4$ posterior samples in $0.036$~s after training, compared with $72.8$~h for the reference MCMC run, giving an elapsed-time ratio of $O(10^6)$. The attention-weight analysis shows that intermediate-redshift FRBs ($0.3\leq z<0.7$) account for the largest share of the total attention averaged over simulated catalogs following the observed redshift distribution, consistent with the combined influence of source abundance and redshift-dependent sensitivity. These results demonstrate the potential of interpretable neural posterior estimation for efficient and repeated inference in FRB cosmology.

Why: While primarily an instrumentation and software paper, Redrock is the standard pipeline tool for the Euclid-adjacent DESI survey, and its methodology for template-based fitting and error propagation is highly relevant to the non-linear modelling and survey-level data analysis tasks in the student's profile.

Redrock is the primary spectroscopic classification and redshift fitting software for the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI). It employs a Principal Component Analysis (PCA) template-fitting approach that operates directly on flux-calibrated spectra, utilizing the full wavelength-dependent resolution matrix to account for non-Gaussian instrumental effects. By fitting templates across the entire observed range rather than relying on individual emission line identification, Redrock effectively captures the information contained in both the continuum and spectral features. This paper details the algorithmic framework, the construction of the galaxy, quasar, and stellar templates, and demonstrates the code's high performance and robustness across DESI's diverse target populations.

Main conclusions
  • Redrock achieves high redshift recovery rates: ~98% for Bright Galaxy Survey (BGS) and Luminous Red Galaxies (LRG), 95.9% for Emission Line Galaxies (ELG), and 92.6% for quasars (QSO).
  • The software delivers unbiased stellar radial velocities with a precision of approximately 5 km/s.
  • The method uses a $\chi^2$ minimization scan over redshift, refined by a parabolic fit to the local minimum, which provides both the best-fit redshift and a statistical error estimate.
  • The use of per-spectrum resolution matrices and flux-calibrated inputs allows the code to maintain an uncorrelated error model, which is critical for the precision requirements of large-scale structure surveys.

Redrock is a software package for spectroscopic classification and redshift fitting, designed to use the full information from spectroperfectionism outputs: flux-calibrated spectra, uncorrelated errors, and a per-spectrum resolution matrix encoding the non-Gaussian wavelength-dependent spectral line-spread function. Redrock was originally developed for the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI), but the Extended Baryon Oscillation Spectroscopic Survey (eBOSS) also used Redrock as their primary galaxy redshift fitter prior to DESI. This paper presents the algorithmic details of Redrock and its performance on DESI data. This paper introduces 'automodel', a portable, agent-based workflow designed to automate the discovery and refinement of scientific models. Rather than relying on domain-specific software, it provides a structured 'recipe'—consisting of setup, baseline, iterative search, and finalization phases—that allows general-purpose coding agents (like Claude or Codex) to propose, implement, and evaluate mathematical models or equations. The authors demonstrate the utility of this approach across five diverse scientific domains, including an astrophysics case study where they successfully identify a new, more efficient analytical fitting function for the galaxy stellar mass function (SMF) that outperforms standard literature models like the Schechter or Bernardi functions.

Main conclusions

  • The discovered A5 model uses fewer operators (19) than the Bernardi function (23) while providing a better fit to the data, demonstrating the ability of agentic workflows to find parsimonious, physically admissible models.
  • The approach proves that general-purpose coding agents can effectively navigate complex model spaces by iteratively proposing structural changes, fitting parameters, and performing rigorous cross-validation without needing custom-built symbolic regression engines.

Radio emission from a local Little Red Dot: a challenge to the ionized cocoon scenario

Relevantcross-listGiovanni Mazzolari, Hannah Übler, Grace E.V. Blomfield, Marcella Brusa, Jianhang Chen, Ric Davies, Francesco D'Eugenio, Frank Eisenhauer, Reinhard Genzel, Roberto Gilli, Yannick Gilmer, Shweta Jain, Xihan Ji, Ignas Juodzbalis, Dharam V. arXiv:2611.24387 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.GA, astro-ph.CO
Why: The paper uses multi-wavelength data to test physical models of AGN obscuration and accretion, which is highly relevant to the student's work on beyond-ΛCDM simulations and the interpretation of galaxy bias and AGN properties in Euclid-like surveys.

Little Red Dots (LRDs) are compact, broad-line AGN discovered by JWST that exhibit peculiar V-shaped spectral energy distributions and X-ray weakness. This paper investigates the local LRD J1047 by combining radio observations with optical H$\alpha$ spectroscopy to test three competing scenarios: heavy neutral gas obscuration, super-Eddington accretion, and ionized gas 'cocoons' that produce electron scattering. The authors find that the radio emission is consistent with a standard radio-quiet AGN obscured by dense neutral gas, while the electron-scattering scenario is strongly disfavored because the required ionized gas column would cause free-free absorption that should have completely suppressed the observed radio signal. The results suggest that the exponential broad-line profiles often attributed to electron scattering in LRDs likely trace a stratified broad-line region instead, which has significant implications for black hole mass estimates in high-redshift LRDs.

Main conclusions
  • The observed radio luminosity of J1047 is consistent with a standard radio-quiet AGN obscured by dense neutral gas, while pure super-Eddington accretion underpredicts the radio flux by a factor of ~30.
  • The statistically preferred exponential fit to the H$\alpha$ profile implies an electron column density of $N_e = 2.4 \times 10^{24} \text{ cm}^{-2}$, which would cause free-free absorption ($ au_{\text{ff}} \sim 10^6$) incompatible with the radio detection.
  • The radio emission must originate from an AGN, as host galaxy star formation rates are insufficient to explain the observed radio luminosity.
  • The findings suggest that non-Gaussian broad-line profiles in LRDs are likely due to BLR stratification rather than electron scattering, implying that current virial black hole mass estimates may not require the large downward corrections previously proposed.

Abstract

Several scenarios have been proposed to explain the properties of Little Red Dots (LRDs), invoking either heavy obscuration by dense neutral gas, super-Eddington accretion, or an ionized gas cocoon that produces electron scattering. Radio observations can distinguish between these scenarios. Radio emission is largely unaffected by neutral gas and dust obscuration, whereas free-free absorption can suppress it in the presence of large free electron columns. Here, we investigate the origin of radio emission from the local LRD J1047. We jointly analyze its radio data and the broad H$\alpha$ emission from optical spectroscopy to test the predictions of different physical scenarios. We find that the observed radio luminosity must have an AGN origin, and it is consistent with that of a standard radio-quiet AGN obscured by dense neutral gas. Pure super-Eddington accretion underpredicts the radio luminosity by a factor of $\sim 30$, although a combination of super-Eddington accretion and obscuration remains viable. Interestingly, the statistically preferred model for the H$\alpha$ profile is an exponential fit, which is often associated with electron scattering in LRDs. Less likely, the radio emission may escape through a partially covering ionized medium or originate outside the putative scattering cocoon.

Seeing through the fog: astrophysical priors and the evidence for new physics in pulsar timing arrays

RelatedTorsten Bringmann, Chris Gordon, Carlo Tasillo
arXiv:2611.23371 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.GA, hep-ph

Pulsar timing arrays (PTAs) recently detected a stochastic gravitational wave background at nanohertz frequencies, which could originate from inspiralling supermassive black hole binaries (SMBHBs) or early-universe cosmological sources like dark-sector phase transitions. Standard searches for exotic physics approximate the SMBHB signal with a simple power law, neglecting the realistic, skewed spectrum and environmental hardening effects predicted by population synthesis codes like holodeck. To overcome the computational bottleneck of running full population synthesis, the authors introduce binaryfog, a polynomial surrogate that evaluates SMBHB spectral distributions in 0.2 ms ($10^6\times$ faster than direct simulation). Using binaryfog to profile over six astrophysical parameters, they demonstrate that replacing a simple power law with realistic SMBHB populations drops the statistical preference for a dark-sector phase transition from $2.8\sigma$ to $1.5\sigma$ ($1.7\sigma$ under updated galaxy priors), showing that claims of new physics in PTAs depend strongly on astrophysical modeling assumptions.

Main conclusions
  • Introduced binaryfog, a polynomial surrogate mapping 6 SMBHB population parameters to nHz gravitational wave spectral distributions in $0.2\text{ ms}$, achieving a $10^6\times$ speedup over full holodeck population synthesis.
  • Replacing a simple $(A,\gamma)$ power-law model for SMBHBs with realistic population synthesis lowers the statistical preference for a dark sector phase transition from $2.8\sigma$ to $1.5\sigma$ (with similar drops for cosmic strings, domain walls, and inflationary GWs).
  • Adopting updated astrophysical priors based on post-2014 galaxy survey data yields a mild $1.7\sigma$ preference for a phase transition, which further decreases to $1.0\sigma$ if pulsar red-noise priors shift the free-spectrum amplitude down.
  • Explaining the signal solely via SMBHBs requires efficient binary hardening that leads to mergers within $\sim 2\text{ Gyr}$ of galaxy mergers; if binary lifetimes exceed $\sim 6.5\text{ Gyr}$, the preference for new physics rises above $3\sigma$.

Fuzzy Dark Matter as a diffractive barrier: Wave-optics Signatures of Ultralight Fields on Lensed Gravitational Waves

RelatedManish Tamta, Han Gil Choi, Diego Blas, Miguel Zumalacárregui
arXiv:2611.23580 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-ph

Ultralight dark matter (ULDM), or fuzzy dark matter, consists of ultra-light bosons ($m_\phi \sim 10^{-21}\text{ eV}$) whose wave nature creates transient interfere patterns ("granules") of order-unity density contrast in dark matter halos. When gravitational waves (GWs) propagate through a lensed ULDM halo, they enter a wave-optics diffractive regime when the GW Fresnel scale ($r_F \sim \sqrt{\lambda D_{\text{eff}}}$) becomes comparable to the granule coherence scale (\ell_c = \hbar / m_\phi \sigma_v), causing frequency-dependent amplitude and phase modulations. The authors calculate the full non-perturbative wave-optics amplification factor for GWs strongly lensed by a singular isothermal sphere host containing ULDM granules, constructing surface density maps via a Karhunen-Lo\`eve expansion of complex Gaussian random fields. They quantify the detectability of these diffractive imprints on massive black hole binary mergers observed by LISA, demonstrating how macro-image magnification and image parity enhance the signals. This work establishes GW diffractive lensing as a direct wave-optics probe to distinguish ULDM from standard cold dark matter and constrain $m_\phi$.

Main conclusions
  • ULDM granules act as a diffractive screen, imprinting stochastic $\mathcal{O}(1)\%$ frequency-dependent amplitude and phase fluctuations on strongly lensed GW waveforms when $r_F \sim \ell_c$.
  • Diffractive modulations are strongly amplified to $\gtrsim 10\%$ for GW macro-images near critical curves and for negative-parity (Type II / saddle-point) images because saddles probe deeper into the halo center where ULDM granule density fluctuations are stronger.
  • For massive black hole binaries observed by LISA, these diffractive signatures are distinguishable from unlensed signals for ULDM boson masses in the range $m_\phi \sim 10^{-22} - 5 \times 10^{-21}\text{ eV}$.
  • Line-of-sight projection reduces ULDM density fluctuations while preserving transverse correlation scales; the projected surface density distribution exhibits notable non-Gaussian tails when the effective halo depth is comparable to the coherence length.

Abstract

Gravitational waves (GWs) propagate through the universe from sources at high redshift, experiencing the gravitational fields of the intervening matter distribution. RelatedAlorika Kar, Souvik Jana, Enrico Barausse, Parameswaran Ajith

This paper investigates how future multiband gravitational-wave (GW) observations—combining space-based (LISA), decihertz (LGWA), and ground-based (XG) detectors—can reconstruct the mass-redshift distribution of supermassive black hole (SMBH) mergers. RelatedCampbell Benedix, Eve Currens, John T. Giblin Jr, Chloe Richards

This paper investigates cosmological first-order phase transitions (FOPTs) using fully nonlinear general relativity, moving beyond the traditional FLRW background with linear perturbations. The authors employ the BSSN formalism to simulate a scalar field and a relativistic radiation fluid, focusing on GUT-scale transitions where bubble nucleation occurs near the horizon scale. A key contribution is a new numerical method to construct constraint-satisfying initial data for these bubbles, which are then evolved to compute the resulting stochastic gravitational wave background. The results demonstrate that nonlinear gravity effects are consistent with previous linear-order studies, while also revealing the late-time formation of oscillons that alter the field's effective equation of state.

Main conclusions
  • The present-day peak amplitude of the stochastic gravitational wave background is found to be $\Omega_{\rm GW,0}h^2 \sim 5 \times 10^{-11}$, consistent with previous linear-order calculations.

  • At late times, the scalar field fragments into long-lived oscillons, shifting the effective equation of state toward that of matter.
  • Numerical stability in these simulations requires the implementation of Kreiss–Oliger dissipation to manage high-frequency grid noise.

Abstract

Cosmological first-order phase transitions can contribute to the stochastic gravitational wave background. For decades, these first-order phase transitions have been studied using a Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker background where the gravitational waves are treated as linear perturbations. Stratonovich approximation for halo bias: An efficient framework within excursion set theory

RelatedMilad Noorikuhani, Shant Baghram
arXiv:2611.24632 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

Excursion set theory models the formation and clustering of dark matter halos by treating smoothed linear density fluctuations as stochastic trajectories crossing a collapse barrier. For realistic real-space smoothing filters (such as Top-Hat or Gaussian filters), the steps in variance space are non-Markovian (correlated), making exact analytical calculations of first-crossing distributions and peak-background split halo biases notoriously difficult. The authors develop an efficient semi-analytical framework based on the Hertz and Stratonovich approximations, deriving explicit expressions for up-crossing rate functions under generic moving barriers (e.g., Sheth-Mo-Tormen) and filter choices. By comparing these approximations to numerical trajectory ensembles generated via Cholesky matrix decomposition, they show that the Stratonovich approximation accurately captures non-Markovian memory effects across all mass scales. This framework provides a fast, differentiable alternative to Monte Carlo trajectory simulations for predicting halo mass functions and bias parameters.

Main conclusions
  • Formulates compact, explicit expressions for the first ($n_1$) and second ($n_2$) up-crossing rate functions for arbitrary smoothing filters and moving collapse barriers.
  • Demonstrates that the Stratonovich approximation achieves excellent agreement with exact numerical trajectory calculations for both halo multiplicity functions and peak-background split halo bias parameters.
  • Shows that the Stratonovich approximation maintains high precision even at large variance $S$ (the low-mass halo regime), where non-Markovian correlation effects are strongest.
  • Provides a fast and computationally lightweight framework suitable for building differentiable emulators of halo abundance and bias parameters.
