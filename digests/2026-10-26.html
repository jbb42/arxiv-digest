Modelling kNN-CDFs of biased tracers in redshift space using hybrid Lagrangian bias expansion

Highly relevantEishica Chand, Arka Banerjee
arXiv:2610.32837 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: This paper directly uses the hybrid Lagrangian bias expansion (including the Pellejero Ibañez et al. redshift-space distortion formalism with central displacements and Finger-of-God kernels) to model non-linear galaxy clustering statistics and lays the groundwork for building emulators of these observables.

Two-point statistics like the galaxy power spectrum $P(k)$ miss a wealth of non-Gaussian information on small scales, which can instead be captured cheaply using $k$-nearest-neighbour cumulative distribution functions ($k$NN-CDFs)—the probability distribution of distances from random volume-filling query points to the $k$-th nearest galaxy, which is sensitive to all $N$-point correlation functions. To use $k$NN-CDFs for cosmological inference in real spectroscopic surveys, one needs an accurate theoretical model that connects galaxies to dark matter in redshift space. In this paper, the authors build a redshift-space modelling pipeline for $k$NN-CDFs using the hybrid Lagrangian bias expansion—advecting initial Lagrangian bias operators ($1, \delta_L, \delta_L^2, s_L^2, \nabla^2\delta_L$, plus the cubic operator $\delta_L^3$) with exact $N$-body displacements and halo velocities from the Quijote simulations, supplemented by an exponential Finger-of-God (FoG) velocity dispersion kernel. Validating their pipeline on Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD) mocks of luminous red galaxies (LRGs) at $z=0.5$, they show that a single set of bias and RSD parameters consistently describes both the two-point power spectrum and higher-order $k$NN-CDFs, and that combining the two observables dramatically breaks degeneracies between galaxy bias and cosmological parameters.

Main conclusions
  • The hybrid Lagrangian bias + RSD model accurately predicts redshift-space $k$NN-CDFs (and joint galaxy–matter $k$NN-CDFs) down to $r = 10\,\mathrm{Mpc}/h$ for $1$NN, with the minimum reliable scale shifting to larger $r$ for higher $k$ because higher-order neighbours probe denser, more non-linear regions.
  • Bias and RSD parameters fit purely to the $k$NN-CDFs simultaneously provide an accurate prediction of the galaxy power spectrum $P(k)$ in both real and redshift space (whereas best-fit parameters from $P(k)$ alone fail to reproduce the $k$NN-CDFs due to parameter degeneracies).
  • In Fisher forecasts for redshift-space galaxy auto-clustering, combining $P(k)$ and $k$NN-CDFs breaks bias degeneracies—especially for the cubic bias $b_3$—and tightens constraints on $\sigma_8$ and $\Omega_m$ by factors of $3.5$ and $1.7$ compared to $P(k)$ alone.
  • Adding galaxy–matter cross-clustering via joint $k$NN-CDFs alongside auto-clustering yields even tighter combined constraints, improving $\sigma_8$ by a factor of $4.2$ and $\Omega_m$ by $1.7$ (with the largest bias improvement on $b_2$) relative to $P(k)$.

Abstract

Higher-order statistics are becoming increasingly useful in clustering studies of the large-scale structure (LSS) of the universe. The kNN-CDFs are one such higher-order statistic, sensitive to all N-point correlations and easy to compute on data. Utilising their statistical gains for cosmological inference requires modelling them accurately for the tracer of interest. We use the hybrid Lagrangian bias expansion to enable this modelling in redshift space, building and validating the pipeline on Quijote simulations. We show that the kNN-CDFs of a luminous red galaxy (LRG)-like sample can be modelled down to scales as small as r = 10 Mpc/h, with the minimum reliable scale increasing with nearest-neighbour order. Moreover, the model parameters inferred from the kNN-CDFs also describe the galaxy power spectrum P(k), indicating model consistency across the two statistics and the feasibility of combining them. Fisher forecasts for galaxy auto clustering show that combining P(k) and kNN-CDFs improves constraints on all parameters relative to either statistic alone. Relative to P(k), the constraints on $b_3$ improve the most, while those on $\sigma_8$ and $\Omega_m$ improve by factors of 3.5 and 1.7, respectively. Including galaxy-matter cross clustering alongside galaxy auto clustering further improves the Fisher constraints from both P(k) and kNN-CDFs, with their combination yielding the tightest constraints overall. Relative to P(k), the largest improvement from this combination is found for $b_2$, while the constraints on $\sigma_8$ and $\Omega_m$ improve by factors of 4.2 and 1.7, respectively. These results demonstrate the applicability of the hybrid Lagrangian bias framework to model the kNN-CDFs of biased tracers in redshift space, and the potential of combining higher-order and two-point statistics to extract cosmological information.

Prospects of Detecting Cosmological Domain Walls in the Cosmic Microwave Background

RelatedBerkin Gürbüz, Robert Brandenberger, Yitian Sun
arXiv:2610.31761 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-ph, hep-th

Domain walls are sheet-like topological defects that form in the early universe when a discrete symmetry is spontaneously broken, separating patches of space that settled into different vacuum states. While high-energy walls would quickly dominate the cosmic energy budget (the Zel'dovich overclosure bound), walls formed at lower symmetry-breaking energy scales ($\eta \lesssim 100\,\mathrm{MeV}$) remain cosmologically viable and exert a repulsive gravitational force perpendicular to their surface. In this paper, the authors analytically compute how a planar domain wall repels surrounding matter after matter-radiation equality—carving out an evacuated void bordered by a compressed, overdense shell—and derive the resulting gravitational lensing deflection of Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) photons. Because this lensing stamps localized, line-like, non-Gaussian distortions onto the CMB temperature map that standard two-point power spectra miss, they forecast detectability for a South Pole Telescope-like (SPT) survey using directional wavelets (curvelets) and neural simulation-based inference (SBI), showing that neural likelihood-ratio estimators can push sensitivity right down to the instrumental noise floor.

Main conclusions
  • A planar domain wall's repulsive gravity creates a caustic-free, evacuated void of half-width $\psi(z)$ bounded by an overdense matter shell inside the causal horizon; this imprints an antisymmetric, two-peaked photon deflection field onto the CMB that vanishes completely when the wall is viewed face-on ($\theta = \pi/2$).
  • For an idealized SPT-like survey, directional curvelet and wavelet kurtosis statistics can detect domain walls with symmetry-breaking scales in the band $\eta \sim 10\text{–}100\,\mathrm{MeV}$ across the accessible redshift range.
  • Simulation-based inference via a Neural Ratio Estimator (NRE) exploits the full 2D map morphology to improve sensitivity by a factor of $1.3\text{–}1.9$ in $\eta$ at $z \gtrsim 20$ (corresponding to a factor of $\sim 2\text{–}7$ in wall tension $\sigma \propto \eta^3$), reaching the instrumental per-pixel noise floor.
  • A Neural Posterior Estimator (NPE) trained on single CMB patches accurately recovers the wall's inclination angle $\theta$ independently, while revealing a geometric degeneracy between the scattering redshift $z$ and energy scale $\eta$.

Abstract

Topological defects are a generic prediction of field theories with spontaneously broken symmetries, and domain walls, the two-dimensional defects left by a broken discrete symmetry, are among the simplest. We study the imprint of dark, purely gravitationally interacting domain walls on the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB), in the regime where a low symmetry-breaking scale $\eta$ allows the walls to evade the Zel'dovich overclosure bound while remaining cosmologically relevant. The relativistic repulsion of matter by a planar wall carves out an evacuated void bounded by an overdense shell, and we compute the resulting photon deflection field analytically for walls both aligned with and arbitrarily inclined to the line of sight. The deflection stamps coherent, strongly non-Gaussian distortions onto the temperature sky, with a characteristic two-peak structure set by the relativistic displacement scale and an inclination dependence that vanishes for face-on walls. Because these signatures are localised and anisotropic, they are poorly captured by two-point statistics; we therefore analyse them with a curvelet/wavelet decomposition matched to the wall's geometry, and with simulation-based inference, training a Neural Posterior Estimator (NPE) to recover the scattering redshift $z$, energy scale $\eta$, and inclination $\theta$, and a Neural Ratio Estimator (NRE) to evaluate the likelihood-ratio test statistic for limit-setting. Casting these methods as sensitivity forecasts for a South Pole Telescope-like (SPT) survey, we find that the curvelet and wavelet statistical thresholds populate a common $\eta \sim 10-100$ MeV band over the accessible redshift range. The neural-ratio sensitivity curve, which exploits the full map morphology, reaches a factor 1.3-1.9 lower in $\eta$ at $z \gtrsim 20$, or $\sim 2-7$ in the wall tension, landing essentially on the per-resolution-element noise floor.

AMICO galaxy clusters in KiDS-1000: Cosmological constraints from the two-point correlation function

RelatedMassimiliano Romanello, Giorgio Francesco Lesci, Federico Marulli, Lauro Moscardini, Carlo Giocoli, Matteo Maturi, Mario Radovich, Sandro Bardelli, Gianluca Castignani, Hendrik Hildebrandt, Lorenzo Ingoglia, Shahab Joudaki, Emanuella Puddu, Mauro Sereno
arXiv:2610.31767 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.GA

Galaxy clusters trace the highest density peaks of the cosmic web, making their spatial clustering a powerful probe of large-scale structure growth that is less affected by non-linearities and small-scale virial motions ('Fingers of God') than ordinary galaxies. A key advantage of clusters over standard galaxy clustering is that their mass can be estimated via observable proxies—such as 'intrinsic richness' ($\lambda^*$), roughly the membership-weighted count of bright cluster galaxies—which allows one to compute the cluster bias theoretically from the halo mass function and break the classic degeneracy between bias and the fluctuation amplitude $\sigma_8$. In this paper, the authors measure the 3D two-point correlation function multipoles (monopole, quadrupole, and hexadecapole) of 9,049 optically selected clusters across $0.1 \le z < 0.8$ from the Kilo Degree Survey (KiDS-1000), identified using the AMICO optimal-filtering algorithm (one of the two cluster finders adopted by Euclid). By modelling linear redshift-space distortions, Alcock–Paczynski geometric distortions, and line-of-sight damping from photometric redshift errors alongside a weak-lensing-calibrated mass–richness relation, they obtain independent late-time $\Lambda$CDM constraints on $\Omega_{\mathrm{m}}$ and $S_8$, testing consistency with cluster number counts and cosmic shear ahead of Euclid and LSST.

Main conclusions
  • The analysis constrains the total matter density to $\Omega_{\mathrm{m}} = 0.24 \pm 0.02$ and the matter fluctuation amplitude to $\sigma_8 = 0.86^{+0.13}_{-0.10}$, improving the figure of merit by a factor of $1.8$ over previous KiDS-DR3 cluster clustering studies.
  • The low inferred matter density ($\Omega_{\mathrm{m}} = 0.24$) agrees with the cluster abundance results from the same KiDS-1000 catalogue, though it stands in a mild $\sim 2\sigma$ tension with Planck 2018 CMB measurements ($\Omega_{\mathrm{m}} \approx 0.315$).
  • The derived structure growth parameter $S_8 \equiv \sigma_8 \sqrt{\Omega_{\mathrm{m}}/0.3} = 0.77^{+0.10}_{-0.08}$ is consistent within $1\sigma$ with both cosmic shear analyses (KiDS-1000, KiDS-Legacy) and Planck CMB constraints, due to the relatively large uncertainty on $\sigma_8$.
  • Photometric redshift uncertainties ($\sigma_z = 0.014(1+z)$) and the algorithm's redshift discretisation ($\Delta z = 0.01$) dominate the small-scale damping of the clustering monopole and artificially boost the quadrupole and hexadecapole, whereas virial Finger-of-God velocity dispersions are subdominant for clusters.

Abstract

In this paper we study the clustering properties of an optically identified photometric galaxy cluster catalogue from KiDS-1000. The sample was identified by the AMICO algorithm and consists of 9049 clusters with $S/N\geq3.5$, in the redshift range $0.1\leq z<0.8$, spread over an effective area of 838.78 deg$^2$. Clusters are selected according to their observed intrinsic richness, with thresholds of $\lambda^*{obs}\geq 20,25,30$ within $0.10\leq z<0.30$, $0.30\leq z<0.45$ and $0.45\leq z<0.80$, respectively. We measure the multipoles of the two-point correlation function over different ranges of scales from 5 $h^{-1}$ Mpc to 150 $h^{-1}$ Mpc. We model the clustering signal in the context of the $\Lambda$CDM cosmology, accounting for the main observational effects, such RSDs and photo-$z$ uncertainties. The effective bias of the cluster sample is estimated via a mass-richness scaling relation. For the MCMC analysis, we adopt Gaussian priors on the parameters of the mass-richness scaling relation, and broad, uniform priors to derive cosmological constraints on the cold matter density parameter, the power spectrum amplitude and the structure growth parameter, $S_8\equiv\sigma_8 \sqrt{\Omega{m}/0.3}$. Specifically, we find $\Omega_{m}=0.24^{+0.02}{-0.02}$ and $\sigma_8=0.86^{+0.13}{-0.10}$ representing an improvement in precision over both the 2D and 3D AMICO clustering analyses performed on the previous KiDS data release. Moreover, we obtain $S_8=0.77^{+0.10}_{-0.08}$ in $1\sigma$ agreement with the AMICO KiDS-1000 number counts constraints estimated from the same catalogue, as well as with some results from external early- and late-Universe data sets. Our work strengthens the potential of cluster clustering as a complementary and competitive probe, to be included and further developed with the final KiDS-Legacy data release, and in other future Stage-IV photometric redshift surveys.

Cluster connectivity in DES Y3 data: impact on galaxy properties

RelatedC. Gouin, C. Laigle, J. Prat, L. O.V. Edwards, P. Ganeshaiah Veena, D. Galárraga-Espinosa, G. Gopidas
arXiv:2610.31800 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

In the cosmic web, galaxy clusters sit at the nodes where large-scale matter filaments intersect, and a cluster's "connectivity"—the number of filaments feeding into it—characterizes its large-scale accretion environment and merger history. Because photometric surveys have large line-of-sight distance uncertainties that blur 3D structure, the authors reconstruct the cosmic web in 2D tomographic slices of comoving thickness $\sim 222\,\text{Mpc}$ across $\sim 4130\,\text{deg}^2$ of Dark Energy Survey Year 3 (DES Y3) data up to $z \sim 0.9$. Using the topological filament-finding algorithm DisPerSE across 50 Monte Carlo realizations of the galaxy photometric redshifts, they measure the connectivity within $2 R_{500c}$ for X-ray (eRASS1, RASS) and Sunyaev–Zel'dovich (ACT, SPT) selected clusters and cross-correlate it with the colours and neural-network-classified morphologies of member galaxies. This demonstrates that wide-field broadband imaging surveys like DES and Euclid can reliably map the cosmic web and probe "pre-processing"—the quenching of star formation and morphological transformation of galaxies inside filaments before they even fall into a cluster.

Main conclusions
  • Cluster connectivity correlates positively with cluster mass ($M_{500c}$) across all redshift bins up to $z < 0.9$, showing good consistency with recent Euclid Q1 measurements and no significant redshift evolution.
  • At fixed cluster mass, more connected clusters host a significantly higher fraction of evolved galaxies, showing partial Pearson correlations of $\rho \approx 0.17\text{–}0.23$ ($p \le 4.6 \times 10^{-6}$) for the red galaxy fraction at $0.2 < z < 0.6$ and $\rho \approx 0.21\text{–}0.22$ ($p \le 2.1 \times 10^{-9}$) for the elliptical galaxy fraction at $0.2 < z < 0.4$.
  • Galaxies located within $0.1\,\text{Mpc}$ of cosmic filament spines around clusters (out to $4 R_{500c}$) exhibit a higher fraction of red and elliptical members than the isotropic average at the same cluster-centric radius; the excess is stronger for colour than morphology, indicating that filament-driven pre-processing quenches star formation faster than it transforms spiral galaxies into ellipticals.

Abstract

Connectivity, namely the number of cosmic filaments reaching out of groups or clusters, has recently emerged as an informative way to quantify environment. With the advent of wide-area photometric surveys, it becomes possible to measure connectivity on a statistically significant sample of clusters. In this work, we extract the cosmic web from the DES Y3 photometric data and measure the connectivity around clusters from several public catalogs up to $z < 1$. Cosmic filaments are extracted in 2D with DisPerSE (the parametrization of which is calibrated on simulations) in tomographic slices of fixed comoving thickness of 220 Mpc, with a constant sampling across the redshift range ($\sim 0.1$ galaxies Mpc$^{-2}$). We first recover the well-established trend that cluster connectivity increases with cluster mass at all redshifts. We then investigate the properties of cluster member galaxies and find that, at fixed mass, clusters with a higher fraction of red (elliptical) galaxies are more strongly connected to the cosmic web. Furthermore, by examining the radial profiles of the red and elliptical galaxy fractions, we obtain hints that galaxy populations may undergo pre-processing within filamentary structures before being accreted into clusters. Overall, we demonstrate that despite photometric redshift uncertainties and projection effects, our method allows for the quantification of the cosmic web environment around clusters in broad-band photometric surveys. This work is consistent with results based on the Euclid Quick Release 1 (Q1). We plan to publicly release the cluster connectivity catalog as well as the cosmic filament catalog.

Semi-Analytic Predictions for Strong-Lensing Perturbers in Cold and Self-Interacting Dark Matter

RelatedJonathan Tekverk, Duncan K. Adams, Simon Birrer, Rouven Essig, Daniel Gilman
arXiv:2610.31881 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, hep-ph

Strong gravitational lensing—where a foreground galaxy bends light from a background source into extended arcs or multiple images—allows cosmologists to detect otherwise invisible, starless dark matter "subhalos" via localized distortions in the lensed images ("gravitational imaging"). Several recently detected lensing perturbers appear surprisingly compact and steep compared to standard cold dark matter (CDM) expectations, motivating explanations using self-interacting dark matter (SIDM), in which particle collisions transport heat inside halos and drive a "gravothermal" evolution from an initial flat core to a hyper-dense "core-collapsed" state. Because high-resolution $N$-body simulations of core collapse are computationally prohibitive across many host halos and parameter choices, the authors combine the semi-analytic subhalo merger-tree code SatGen with a calibrated parametric model of SIDM gravothermal and tidal evolution to rapidly generate thousands of subhalo populations. They compute the 2D projected enclosed masses and density slopes of these synthetic subhalos and compare them directly against three well-known strong-lensing perturbers (in PJ011646, SDSS J0946+1006, and JVAS B1938+666). This modular, fast framework enables statistical scans over SIDM cross-section parameters to test whether a single dark matter model can naturally explain the diverse internal structures of observed lensing perturbers.

Main conclusions
  • Under a fiducial velocity-dependent SIDM model ($\sigma_0/m_\chi = 147.1\text{ cm}^2/\text{g}$, $w = 120\text{ km/s}$), SIDM subhalos match all three observed strong-lensing perturbers significantly more often than CDM counterparts across $10^{13}\,M_\odot$ host halo realizations.
  • For the massive, dense perturber in SDSS J0946+1006 (evaluated at $R_{\text{pivot}} \simeq 0.63\text{ kpc}$), zero matching subhalos are found across 900 CDM realizations, whereas SIDM produces $\sim 3$ core-collapsed candidates per host halo.
  • For the low-mass "detection $\mathcal{V}$" perturber in JVAS B1938+666 ($R_{\text{pivot}} \simeq 90\text{ pc}$), SIDM yields $\sim 220$ core-collapsed and $\sim 20$ cored subhalos per host within the $2\sigma$ observational region compared to $\sim 0.2$ in CDM, with the rare CDM candidates requiring extreme early infall ($z_{\text{infall}} \sim 13$, in the earliest $0.1\%$ of accretion histories).
  • For the intermediate-slope perturber in PJ011646 ($R_{\text{pivot}} \simeq 1.92\text{ kpc}$), core-collapsed halos are too steep, but cored SIDM subhalos still match the perturber $\sim 300$ times more frequently than CDM ($\sim 0.6$ vs. $\sim 0.002$ per host).
  • Simultaneously explaining all three perturbers within a single velocity-dependent SIDM model requires a cross-section normalization $\sigma_0/m_\chi \gtrsim 10^2\text{ cm}^2/\text{g}$ and a turnover velocity $w \gtrsim 10^2\text{ km/s}$ so that the massive ($\sim 5.6 \times 10^{10}\,M_\odot$) progenitors of the SDSS J0946+1006 candidates can undergo core collapse.

Abstract

Strong gravitational lensing is sensitive to the abundance and internal structure of dark matter halos, providing a probe of gravothermal evolution in self-interacting dark matter (SIDM). We combine semi-analytic cold dark matter (CDM) subhalo histories with a model for gravothermal evolution in SIDM to generate CDM and SIDM subhalo populations across SIDM parameter space. We predict their projected enclosed masses and deflection-based projected density slopes and compare them with three perturbers inferred through gravitational imaging in PJ011646, SDSS J0946+1006, and JVAS B1938+666. For SIDM model parameters $\sigma_0/m_\chi=147.1$ cm$^2$/g and $w=120$ km/s, SIDM subhalos reproduce all three perturbers more often than CDM subhalos. For PJ011646, we find ~0.6 cored SIDM subhalos per host matching the perturber, compared with ~0.002 in CDM, while core-collapsed subhalos are too steep. For SDSS J0946+1006, we find ~3 core-collapsed and ~0.004 cored SIDM subhalos per host, compared with none in 900 CDM realizations, although baryons, neglected here, may matter for such massive subhalos. Our results show how semi-analytic models can quantify how often subhalos reproduce individual perturbers in different dark matter models, and our modular framework can be readily updated as models of tidal stripping and late-stage gravothermal evolution in SIDM improve. The Impact of Inclination, Redshift, and Mass Ratio Selection on Cosmological Constraints from Bright Sirens Beyond Conventional Fisher Approximations

RelatedGustavo B. F. Lima, Rafael F. Aranha, Rodrigo S. Gonçalves
arXiv:2610.32008 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc

"Bright sirens" are gravitational-wave (GW) mergers—typically binary neutron stars—accompanied by an electromagnetic counterpart (like a short gamma-ray burst) that provides the host galaxy's redshift, allowing us to pair redshift with the GW-inferred luminosity distance to measure cosmic expansion. A major hurdle in standard siren cosmology is that the GW strain amplitude depends on both distance and the binary's orbital inclination angle ($\iota$), creating a strong distance–inclination degeneracy where standard Gaussian Fisher matrix approximations often fail. In this paper, the authors use the GWDALI framework (which incorporates higher-order derivative expansions beyond the Fisher approximation to capture non-Gaussian posteriors) alongside IMRPhenomD phenomenological waveforms to simulate non-spinning binary neutron star populations for third-generation detectors like the Einstein Telescope and Cosmic Explorer. They systematically vary astrophysical selection assumptions—specifically the binary mass ratio ($q$), the maximum inclination angle ($\iota_{\max}$) at which an electromagnetic jet remains detectable, and the redshift depth ($z_{\max}$)—to quantify how population modelling choices propagate into flat-$\Lambda$CDM constraints on $H_0$ and $\Omega_m$.

Main conclusions
  • Statistical uncertainty on $H_0$ reaches $\lesssim 1.3\%$ with $N \ge 400$ bright siren events and drops below $0.9\%$ for $N \ge 800$ events, confirming that $H_0$ precision is driven primarily by total sample size and local distance calibration.
  • Breaking parameter degeneracies to tightly constrain the matter density $\Omega_m$ strictly requires a deep-redshift catalog ($z_{\max} \ge 1.0$) to probe the cosmic expansion history beyond the local linear Hubble flow.
  • Relaxing the maximum observable jet inclination cutoff from a conservative $20^{\circ}$ to $40^{\circ}$ (motivated by off-axis structured gamma-ray burst models) substantially mitigates the correlation between luminosity distance and orbital inclination.
  • Variations in the binary mass ratio ($q$) distribution introduce subtle shifts in the simulated signal-to-noise ratio distribution that alter the likelihood geometry, meaning waveform and population assumptions will become dominant systematics for sub-percent third-generation GW cosmology.

Introducing the GION Lightcone Simulation Suite for Multi-Wavelength Large-Scale Structure Cosmology

RelatedKen Osato, Satoshi Tanaka, Tomoaki Ishiyama, Hironao Miyatake, Takahiro Nishimichi, Masato Shirasaki
arXiv:2610.32388 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

Modern "Stage-IV" galaxy surveys (like Euclid, DESI, and LSST) and cosmic microwave background (CMB) experiments observe the same underlying cosmic web at different wavelengths, including secondary CMB distortions like the thermal and kinematic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (tSZ and kSZ) effects—where CMB photons scatter off hot, high-pressure cluster gas or bulk-moving ionized gas, respectively. Cross-correlating these probes with galaxy clustering and weak gravitational lensing breaks parameter degeneracies, but testing joint analysis pipelines requires simulations with both huge volumes and fine enough mass resolution to capture the relatively small halos ($M \sim 10^{11} \, h^{-1}\mathrm{M}_\odot$) that host high-redshift emission-line galaxies (ELGs). The authors present the GION suite: 10 independent, full-sky $\Lambda$CDM $N$-body simulations evolved with the massively parallel TreePM code GINKAKU on the Fugaku supercomputer ($6144^3$ particles in a $1.5 \, h^{-1}\mathrm{Gpc}$ box, initialized at $z=24$ with 3LPT via monofonIC). Rather than stacking discrete redshift snapshots, GION outputs particles continuously on-the-fly as they cross the backward lightcone out to $z=5$, immediately processing them into MPI-Rockstar halo catalogs and high-resolution HEALPix density and momentum shells before deleting the raw particle data to save storage. From these shared lightcones, they generate and validate mutually consistent mock galaxy catalogs, weak lensing convergence maps, and baryon-pasted tSZ/kSZ maps that are publicly released as a testbed for multi-wavelength survey pipelines.

Main conclusions
  • The suite provides 10 statistically independent full-sky lightcone realizations out to $z=5$ (each ~10.1 TB of public data), resolving halos down to $6.38 \times 10^{10} \, h^{-1}\mathrm{M}_\odot$ (50 particles) with $25 \, h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}$ radial shells on $N_{\mathrm{side}}=8192$ HEALPix grids ($0.429\text{ arcmin}$ pixel size).
  • Fixed-redshift matter power spectra agree with the DarkEmulator2 neural-network emulator within a few percent for $k \gtrsim 0.1 \, h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ across $0 \le z \le 4$, and halo mass functions match theoretical expectations within $5\%$ for $10^{11} \lesssim M_{\mathrm{200b}}/h^{-1}\mathrm{M}_\odot \lesssim 10^{13}$.
  • Weak lensing convergence ($\kappa$) angular power spectra match theoretical predictions within a few percent across $30 \lesssim \ell \lesssim 5000$, while mock galaxy clustering, tSZ, and kSZ auto- and cross-spectra agree with halo-model/perturbative predictions within $\sim 10\%$ on large/intermediate scales before showing expected non-linear excesses of $15\text{–}50\%$ at $\ell \gtrsim 10^3$.
  • Because the simulations are dark-matter-only in a fixed Planck 2018 $\Lambda$CDM cosmology, baryonic effects and gas pressure profiles are pasted in post-processing (neglecting baryonic backreaction), and weak lensing is computed under the Born approximation (ignoring lens-lens coupling and post-Born ray-tracing effects).

Abstract

We present the GION (Ginkaku Lightcone Multi-wavelength Simulation Project) simulation suite: 10 full-sky high-resolution cosmological lightcone simulations and their associated products for multi-wavelength large-scale structure cosmology. We use the TreePM $N$-body code GINKAKU, which is highly optimized in the massively parallel environment, and integrate the on-the-fly lightcone construction technique. Each lightcone simulation is run with $6144^3$ particles in a volume of $(1.5 \, h^{-1} \, \mathrm{Gpc})^3$, which corresponds to a particle mass of $1.28 \times 10^9 \, h^{-1} \, \mathrm{M}_\odot$, and extends to $z = 5$. The fine mass resolution and the depth of the lightcone simulations allow us to resolve dark matter halos down to $10^{11} \, h^{-1} \, \mathrm{M}_\odot$ in the high-redshift Universe, which is crucial for applications to the Stage-IV cosmological surveys. The fundamental statistics of the simulations, including the matter power spectrum, halo power spectrum, and halo mass function, are validated against theoretical predictions. We also generate various large-scale structure observables from the lightcone outputs: the dark matter halo catalogs, mock galaxy samples, weak lensing convergence maps, and thermal and kinematic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (tSZ and kSZ) maps. The angular power spectra of these observables, including the cross-correlation between weak lensing and the tSZ effect, are in good agreement with theoretical predictions, demonstrating that the suite provides mutually consistent multi-wavelength observables on the same lightcone. The GION simulation suite will be publicly available and can be used for various cosmological analyses to address the observational systematic effects and validate theoretical models.

Luminosity-weighted galaxy and gravitational wave cross-correlations

The Galactic Center Gamma-Ray Excess and the Look-Elsewhere Effect

cross-listIsabel S. Sands, Philip F. Hopkins
arXiv:2610.32845 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.HE, astro-ph.CO, hep-ph

For nearly two decades, an unexplained excess of GeV $\gamma$-rays in the inner $\sim 10^\circ$ of the Milky Way observed by the Fermi-LAT space telescope—the Galactic Center Excess (GCE)—has been debated as either a smoking gun for annihilating dark matter or the glow of an unresolved population of millisecond pulsars. However, identifying this excess relies on subtracting a baseline model of diffuse galactic $\gamma$-ray emission that traditionally assumes cosmic rays (CRs) propagate in a smooth, axisymmetric steady state. In this paper, the authors test that steady-state assumption using two FIRE-2 magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) galaxy formation simulations of Milky Way-mass galaxies coupled to time-dependent, spectrally resolved cosmic-ray transport sourced purely by supernovae. By comparing the simulated $\gamma$-ray emission at individual snapshots over a $\sim 440\text{ Myr}$ window against a quiet, time-averaged baseline template, they find that bursty star formation and dynamic interstellar medium conditions drive large transient fluctuations in the $\gamma$-ray spectrum and morphology. Because some snapshots naturally mimic the $1$–$10\text{ GeV}$ spectrum and roughly spherical shape of the observed GCE without any dark matter or pulsars, the authors argue that the Fermi excess could simply be a "look-elsewhere" coincidence—catching the Milky Way's center during a routine, transient fluctuation in stellar cosmic-ray emission.

Main conclusions
  • Time-dependent cosmic-ray transport in Milky Way-like simulations produces transient $\gamma$-ray flux residuals between $0.1$ and $100\text{ GeV}$ that are frequently larger than the observed Fermi-LAT excess, placing the real Galactic Center Excess comfortably within the 25th–75th percentile range of simulated fluctuations.
  • Because the cosmic-ray diffusion timescale in the central kiloparsec ($\tau_{\text{diff}} \sim 1\text{ Myr}$) is comparable to the timescale of interstellar medium disruption and starburst variability, cosmic rays cannot reach the smooth, steady-state equilibrium assumed by standard analysis tools like GALPROP.
  • Certain simulation snapshots naturally exhibit spherical, $1$–$10\text{ GeV}$ residual excesses within the central $10^\circ$ driven purely by clustered supernovae, closely mimicking the spectrum and spatial morphology of the observed Galactic Center Excess without any dark matter or millisecond pulsar contribution.
  • Central $\gamma$-ray excesses caused by recent starbursts are typically flanked by flux deficits in the galactic disk where supernova feedback has evacuated the surrounding gas, providing a potential observational signature of non-equilibrium cosmic-ray transport.

Abstract

The origin of the excess of GeV gamma-rays in the Milky Way's galactic center observed by Fermi-LAT has been an open question for nearly two decades. The two leading interpretations of this signal are that it is produced either by annihilating dark matter (DM), or arises from unresolved point sources, such as a novel population of millisecond pulsars. In both scenarios, uncertainties and limitations in the modeling of galactic diffuse gamma-ray emission, such as the steady-state approximation of cosmic ray (CR) propagation and assumption of an axisymmetric distribution of CRs, complicate the interpretation of the galactic center excess (GCE). Simulations with full magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) and CR transport can provide a new avenue for understanding the astrophysical phenomena that drive galactic diffuse gamma-ray emission. We utilize MHD simulations of Milky Way analogs with time-dependent, spectrally-resolved CR transport to reconstruct diffuse gamma-ray emission. Comparing instantaneous emission to a time-averaged baseline, we find residuals up to several times larger than the Fermi GCE, with a wide range of spectral shapes and spatial morphologies. At some simulation snapshots, the spectrum and morphology of the residuals resemble the Fermi GCE. Our results suggest that the Fermi GCE may be a manifestation of the look-elsewhere effect- a transient fluctuation in diffuse emission from stellar sources, observed at an inopportune moment- and motivate the development of new Fermi-LAT analysis templates that account for time-dependent CR transport.
