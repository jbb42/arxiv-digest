Two-loop matter power spectrum from FFTLog and kite integrals

Highly relevantPaola Cappellazzi, Johannes M. Henn, Marko Simonović
arXiv:2610.41914 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, hep-th
Why: The paper provides a state-of-the-art, fast, and accurate method for two-loop EFTofLSS calculations, which is directly applicable to the student's work on non-linear theory predictions and emulators for Euclid.

This paper presents a computationally efficient method for calculating the two-loop matter power spectrum within the Effective Field Theory of Large-Scale Structure (EFTofLSS). The authors use FFTLog to decompose the linear power spectrum into complex power laws, which allows the two-loop loop integrals to be reduced to a set of cosmology-independent 'kite' Feynman integrals. To handle the numerical challenges of highly oscillatory integrands with large imaginary powers, they employ a Mellin–Barnes representation evaluated over a uniform lattice. This approach enables the evaluation of the two-loop power spectrum for any new cosmology in milliseconds, with high precision, providing a robust tool for future high-precision galaxy survey analyses.

Main conclusions
  • The method achieves $10^{-4}$ relative precision compared to direct numerical integration up to $k=0.2$ $h$Mpc$^{-1}$.
  • Precomputed matrices allow for the evaluation of the two-loop power spectrum for a new cosmology in just a few milliseconds.
  • The approach is compatible with existing FFTLog-based pipelines and can be extended to biased tracers and redshift-space distortions.
  • The authors provide publicly available code for the kite integrals and the precomputed matrices.

Abstract

We present a fast and accurate method for computing the two-loop matter power spectrum in the Effective Field Theory of Large-Scale Structure (EFTofLSS). Using FFTLog, we decompose the linear power spectrum into complex power laws, reducing the two-loop corrections to cosmology-independent massless Feynman integrals of kite topology. A central numerical challenge is the evaluation of these integrals for complex powers with large imaginary parts, where the highly oscillatory integrands make accurate numerical evaluation particularly challenging. We overcome this difficulty using a Mellin–Barnes representation summed over a uniform lattice, achieving approximately ten digit precision even for large imaginary powers, with an error estimate for each integral. The resulting precomputed matrices allow the two-loop power spectrum to be evaluated for a new cosmology in a few milliseconds. Comparing with direct numerical integration, we find agreement at the $10^{-4}$ level relative to the power spectrum up to $k=0.2$ $h$Mpc$^{-1}$ after accounting for EFTofLSS counterterm contributions and FFTLog decomposition errors. The same framework extends to biased tracers and redshift-space distortions, providing the building blocks for general two-loop calculations in EFTofLSS. We make the code for evaluating the kite integrals publicly available, together with precomputed matrices and an implementation for the two-loop matter power spectrum as a concrete application. Our results can be directly incorporated into existing FFTLog-based codes for large-scale structure, extending their current one-loop capabilities to two loops within the same computational framework.

Model-independent and reconstruction-free evidence for cosmic acceleration from DESI baryon acoustic oscillations

RelevantLaura Herold, Graeme E. Addison, Adam G. Riess
arXiv:2610.40785 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: The paper provides a model-independent test of cosmic acceleration using DESI DR2 BAO data, directly relevant to your background interest in DESI BAO DR1/DR2 expansion history constraints and non-standard dark energy interpretations.

Most evidence for cosmic acceleration relies on assumed parameterizations of the expansion rate $H(z)/H_0$ (such as $\Lambda$CDM or $w_0 w_a$CDM) or on smoothing methods like Gaussian Process Regression, which can introduce kernel-dependent artifacts. To bypass these assumptions, the authors propose three model-independent, reconstruction-free statistical tests ($S_F$, $S_V$, and $S_{VH}$) built purely from geometric ratios of Alcock-Paczyński parameters and BAO distances ($D_V$ and $D_H$). Applying these tests directly to DESI DR2 BAO measurements across $0 \le z \le 0.934$ without assuming Friedmann equations or dark energy properties, they evaluate the null hypothesis of a non-accelerating universe. This provides a robust, purely geometric confirmation of cosmic acceleration from BAO alone that is entirely independent of gravity theories, dark energy models, or data smoothing techniques.

Main conclusions
  • Developed three model-independent, reconstruction-free statistics based on $F_{\rm AP}$, $D_V$, and $D_V/D_H$ to test for cosmic acceleration using uncalibrated BAO data.
  • Assuming spatial flatness, DESI DR2 BAO data yield $2.4\sigma$ to $4.1\sigma$ evidence for cosmic acceleration within $0 \le z \le 0.934$ after accounting for the look-elsewhere effect, with the hybrid statistic $S_{VH}$ providing the highest significance ($4.1\sigma$ point-wise max, $3.2\sigma$ redshift-averaged).
  • Allowing for non-zero spatial curvature ($|\Omega_k| \le 1$), at least two of the statistics retain $2.4\sigma$ to $3.5\sigma$ evidence for acceleration.
  • The analysis confirms cosmic acceleration geometrically from BAO measurements alone without relying on redshift-reconstruction algorithms (e.g., Gaussian processes) that can introduce method-dependent biases.

Abstract

While there is ample evidence that the universe's expansion is accelerating, most of this evidence relies on assuming a shape of the relative expansion rate, $H(z)/H_0$, informed by a cosmological model or a kinematical approach. Model-independent analyses are common for supernova datasets but rarely applied to other data. In this work, we use uncalibrated baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) from DESI DR2 for a model-independent test of acceleration, which makes no assumptions about the theory of gravity or the energy content of the universe. Accounting for the look-elsewhere effect, among all statistics we find $2.4\sigma$ - $4.1\sigma$ evidence for cosmic acceleration within $0 \leq z \leq 0.934$ when assuming flatness; allowing for spatial curvature, $|\Omega_k| \leq 1$, at least two of the three statistics retain $2.4\sigma$ - $3.5\sigma$.

FOMO-Z is a new Python framework designed for galaxy classification and probabilistic inference using photometric data. By leveraging a reference sample with known properties, it provides a transparent, physically interpretable alternative to black-box machine learning models for tasks like photometric redshift estimation and background galaxy selection. The code implements both binary classification (calibrating color cuts to maximize completeness at a fixed purity) and probabilistic methods (estimating individual membership probabilities and reconstructing property distributions). This tool is particularly relevant for Stage IV surveys like Euclid, where efficient, interpretable methods are needed to handle large datasets and optimize the trade-off between sample purity and completeness in cosmological analyses.

Main conclusions
  • FOMO-Z provides a flexible, open-source framework for binary selection and probabilistic inference that avoids assumptions about the functional form of color distributions.
  • The LinearClassification module is specifically optimized for weak lensing background selection, allowing for the calibration of color cuts that maximize completeness at a user-defined purity threshold.
  • The code achieves performance comparable to more complex machine-learning approaches (e.g., self-organizing maps) while maintaining high computational efficiency and transparency.
  • The framework supports the use of reference redshift probability density functions (PDZs) and sample weights, making it robust for realistic survey conditions.

Abstract

We present FOMO-Z (Flexible Optimizer of Multi-dimensional Operators and photo-$z$s), a set of statistical methods implemented in Python for galaxy classification and probabilistic inference from photometry. The methods use a reference sample with known galaxy properties. They rely on explicit, physically interpretable criteria and make no assumptions about the functional form of the color distributions. Binary selections are calibrated using either linear color conditions, particularly suited to background galaxy selection, or flexible color–color grids, seeking to maximize completeness given a threshold on purity. For probabilistic inference, the code identifies reference objects with compatible colors within photometric uncertainties to estimate each test galaxy's probability of satisfying user-defined criteria, as well as probability density functions of continuous properties such as redshift. The code also reconstructs property distributions of selected galaxy populations using photometrically similar galaxies in the reference sample. This framework supports prior weights and reference redshift probability distributions. A built-in feature-selection method identifying informative colors is also introduced. We demonstrate applications to background galaxy selection, galaxy population classification, tomographic redshift calibration, and photometric redshift estimation. The code achieves results consistent with those obtained through machine-learning approaches, such as self-organizing maps, while remaining computationally efficient, making it suitable for large photometric surveys and future Stage IV cosmological analyses. RelevantWilliam Giarè, Kushal Lodha, Robert R. Caldwell, Eleonora Di Valentino, Eric V. Linder
arXiv:2610.41077 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: Directly addresses physical dark energy models and CLASS linear perturbation implementations proposed to explain the DESI BAO evidence for evolving dark energy and phantom divide crossing.

Recent cosmological data from DESI DR2 BAO, supernovae, and CMB hint at dark energy crossing the 'phantom divide' ($w=-1$), evolving from phantom behavior ($w<-1$) at intermediate redshifts to quintessence-like behavior ($w>-1$) today. The authors propose a fundamental physical mechanism for this transition by adding a decay channel to the Vacuum Metamorphosis (VM) model. In this setup, non-perturbative quantum loop corrections freeze the Ricci scalar $R$ at a critical value, inducing a phantom phase, while subsequent exponential decay of the dark energy sector into light particles uplifts $w(z)$ across $w=-1$. Implementing background and linear perturbations in a modified version of CLASS, the authors test the model against DESI DR2, DES-Dovekie SNe Ia, and Planck/ACT/SPT CMB data. The model provides a self-consistent physical explanation for phantom divide crossing without requiring direct dark energy couplings to dark matter.

Main conclusions
  • Vacuum Metamorphosis with decay fits current data significantly better than $\Lambda$CDM, with $\Delta\chi^2 = -8.54$ for BAO+CMB+SN and $\Delta\chi^2 = -12.13$ for BAO+CMB.
  • The model performs comparably to the phenomenological $w_0w_a$CDM parametrization ($\Delta\chi^2 = +5.27$ for BAO+CMB+SN and $-0.25$ for BAO+CMB) while using the same number of extra parameters.
  • The preferred phase transition redshift occurs at $z_* \sim 1.1 - 1.6$ with a natural dimensionless decay rate $\gamma_* = \Gamma/H(z_*) \approx 1$.
  • The scalar field decay naturally drives $w(z)$ across $w=-1$ near $z \approx 0.5$, yielding a present-day dark energy equation of state $w_0 \approx -0.8$.

Abstract

We show that the vacuum metamorphosis model with decay fits current baryon acoustic oscillation and supernova distance data and cosmic microwave background measurements significantly better than $\Lambda$CDM and nearly as well as $w_0w_a$CDM with the same number of parameters. Vacuum metamorphosis with decay provides a physically motivated model of dark energy and an explanation of current cosmological data. In this scenario, non-perturbative quantum effects of a massive scalar field freeze the Ricci scalar curvature when it reaches a critical value proportional to the mass squared, inducing a phase transition in the scalar field, giving a phantom dark energy equation of state $w<-1$. After the phase transition, vacuum energy decays into light particles, providing a natural mechanism for the dark-energy equation of state to cross the phantom divide from $w<-1$ to $w>-1$, in agreement with current cosmological indications, without requiring an explicit coupling to matter.

RelevantYannick Kegel, Ziyang Zheng, Luca Amendola

Why: The paper uses relativistic corrections and multi-tracer methods to test modified gravity/coupled dark energy, which is highly relevant to the student's work on beyond-ΛCDM simulations and testing gravity with Euclid.

This paper investigates a 'doubly coupled dark energy' (DCDE) model, where a scalar field couples both to dark matter (violating the weak equivalence principle) and to gravity (non-minimal coupling). The authors derive the background and linear perturbation equations for this model, showing that the effective gravitational potential is modified by a Yukawa-like correction. They construct a model-independent parameter $E_P$ that quantifies equivalence principle violations on cosmological scales, which can be measured using multi-tracer galaxy clustering and relativistic corrections. Using Fisher forecasts, they evaluate the potential for DESI, Euclid, and SKA2 to constrain these coupling strengths, finding that SKA2 offers the most promising sensitivity.

Main conclusions
  • The DCDE model introduces a Yukawa correction to the gravitational potential, with dark matter feeling a 'charge' of $Q+Q_{ m dm}$ and baryons feeling a 'charge' of $Q$.
  • The parameter $E_P$ provides a robust, model-independent null test for equivalence principle violations in large-scale structure.
  • Forecasts indicate that SKA2 could constrain the dark matter coupling $Q_{ m dm}$ in the coupled dark energy (CDE) scenario with a $1 ext{-} ext{sigma}$ uncertainty of $22\%$.
  • Constraints on the couplings are currently limited by degeneracies and the sensitivity of $E_P$, suggesting that higher galaxy number densities or larger bias differences between tracers are required for tighter bounds.

Abstract

Recent work has shown that a simple form of coupled dark energy with a constant coupling produces a good fit to the DESI data. In this scenario, dark matter, but not baryons, couples to a scalar field, thereby violating the weak equivalence principle (EP). We first generalize the coupled dark energy model by adding a nonminimal coupling between the scalar field and gravity, and dub the resulting model "doubly coupled dark energy". Model-Independent Assessments of Tidal Alignment and Density-Weighting Dependence in Galaxy Intrinsic Alignments

RelevantPaul Rogozenski, Sukhdeep Singh, Jonathan Blazek, Rachel Mandelbaum, Hung-Jin Huang, Ananth Tenneti
arXiv:2610.42151 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: This paper provides a robust, model-independent validation of the tidal alignment assumptions used in LSS modeling, which is highly relevant for the Euclid working group's efforts to improve non-linear theory predictions and systematic error mitigation.

Intrinsic alignments (IA) of galaxies—the coherent orientation of galaxies with the surrounding large-scale structure—represent a major systematic error in weak lensing surveys like Euclid. Standard models like NLA and TATT rely on perturbative assumptions about how galaxy shapes respond to the local tidal field, but these models often struggle to capture non-linear effects and the impact of density-weighting (the fact that shapes are only measured at galaxy locations). The authors use IllustrisTNG hydrodynamical simulations and SDSS-III BOSS data to perform a model-agnostic test of these paradigms. By directly correlating galaxy shapes and positions with the underlying tidal field, they demonstrate that central galaxies align linearly with the non-linear tidal field well into the 1-halo regime, and that density-weighting primarily acts to increase the alignment amplitude at large scales.

Main conclusions
  • The linear alignment paradigm holds remarkably well for central galaxies in IllustrisTNG at scales $r_p \geq 1 \, h^{-1} \text{Mpc}$.
  • Density-weighting increases the alignment amplitude at large scales, consistent with its role in TATT-style models.
  • Alignment strength is strongly dependent on galaxy properties: it increases with stellar mass and bulge-dominance, while disc galaxies show near-zero alignment.
  • Satellite galaxies exhibit more complex, non-linear alignment behavior compared to centrals, likely due to their response to subhalo-scale tidal fields.

Abstract

Intrinsic alignments (IA) of galaxies are an important source of systematic uncertainty in weak gravitational lensing measurements and also serve as a test for models of galaxy formation and evolution. These galaxy alignments are usually modeled assuming that the IA are sourced by the tidal fields around the galaxies. However, galaxy properties, like their proximity to other galaxies and their mass, provide alignment contributions beyond the tidal field. In this work, we perform novel model-agnostic tests to determine the impact of density-weighting on the IA of galaxies using data from the IllustrisTNG cosmological hydrodynamical simulation and SDSS-III (BOSS). We show that the primary effect of density-weighting is an increase of the alignment amplitude at large scales, whereas the small-scale impact is sample-dependent. We also assess the tidal alignment paradigm for IllustrisTNG galaxies using two-point correlation functions of galaxy shapes, tidal fields, and the sampled background tidal field of the simulation box. We find, given the precision of our measurements, that central galaxies align linearly to the underlying (non-linear) tidal field well into the 1-halo regime.

This paper investigates whether the internal density profiles of dark matter halos, as inferred from strong gravitational lensing of 29 quadruply imaged quasars, provide evidence for self-interacting dark matter (SIDM). The authors map these lensing-inferred density signatures to a particle physics model involving Yukawa scattering, finding that the data favor cross-sections that induce core collapse in low-mass halos. This work demonstrates how small-scale structure observations can constrain dark sector parameters, such as mediator mass and coupling strength, while remaining consistent with existing cluster and satellite galaxy constraints.

  • The analysis disfavors standard CDM with Bayes factors ranging from 8:1 to 24:1, depending on the assumed subhalo properties and tidal evolution models.

RelatedDaniela Breitman
arXiv:2610.40770 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.GA

As observational precision improves, parameter inference using approximate forward models or emulators faces the risk of model misspecification—where the true data-generating process falls outside the assumed model family, leading to tightly constrained yet systematically biased parameter estimates. To address this without requiring an explicit alternative model, the author adopts 'suspiciousness' (a Bayesian internal consistency metric quantifying tension between dataset subsets) as a model criticism tool. Using the fast 21cmEMU neural-network emulator suite, the diagnostic is applied to real HERA 21-cm upper limits, HST and JWST UV luminosity functions, and mock SKA 21-cm power spectrum detections. The diagnostic successfully uncovers hidden model misspecifications—such as a $3.4\sigma$ internal tension when fitting mock SKA data with an incomplete galaxy model—even when the misspecified model passes visual and standard $\chi^2$ goodness-of-fit checks.

Main conclusions
  • A misspecified 21-cm forward model can produce biased astrophysical parameter inferences while yielding visually accurate power spectrum fits and passing standard $\chi^2$ tests.
  • The suspiciousness metric, which compares conditional evidence ratios against information gain, detects model misspecification at $\sim 3.4\sigma$ in mock SKA 21-cm observations without requiring an explicit alternative candidate model.
  • Applying the diagnostic to JWST and HST UV luminosity functions reveals a $5.0\sigma$ internal tension between $z\le 10$ and $z>10$ data within the 21cmEMUv3 framework, highlighting model limitations at high redshifts.
  • Current HERA 21-cm power spectrum upper limits are insufficient to constrain parameter space tightly enough to reveal internal model misspecification.

Abstract

As observations of the cosmic dawn (CD) and epoch of reionisation (EoR) rapidly improve in precision, the approximate forward models used to interpret them might struggle to keep pace. Bayesian inference with a misspecified model, one that cannot represent the processes generating the observations, can yield tightly constrained, yet misleading and biased astrophysics, with different parts of the observations favouring incompatible parameter values. The Effect of Large Apertures on Atmospheric Noise Spectra for Millimeter-wave Telescopes

Thomas W. Morris, Neelima Sehgal
arXiv:2610.41252 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.IM

Ground-based millimeter-wave telescopes mapping the cosmic microwave background face significant contamination from atmospheric water vapor turbulence. Standard forecasts typically extrapolate atmospheric noise to high multipoles (small angular scales) using simple power laws, which can overly penalize high-resolution observations. The authors develop a semianalytic model incorporating near-field beam optics, recognizing that for large apertures ($D \gtrsim 8\text{ m}$) the Rayleigh range extends well above the atmospheric scale height, causing the telescope to average over near-field turbulent eddies. They derive closed-form expressions for the map-level atmospheric power spectrum as a function of primary aperture size, field of view, atmospheric scale height, and turbulence correlation length. Their findings indicate that atmospheric noise at high multipoles is suppressed far below conventional power-law expectations, meaning high-resolution ground-based CMB observations are less atmosphere-limited at small scales than previously assumed.

Main conclusions
  • Derived a semianalytic 3D model combining near-field beam optics with Kolomogorov atmospheric turbulence theory to predict map-level millimeter-wave noise spectra.
  • Large primary apertures ($D \gtrsim 8\text{ m}$) suppress small-scale atmospheric noise by orders of magnitude compared to standard power-law extrapolations because the beam footprint averages over turbulent fluctuations near the aperture.
  • The multipole scale of the atmospheric noise knee is determined by optical design (aperture and field of view) and local atmospheric conditions, rather than survey duration or sky coverage area.
  • High-resolution, ultra-sensitive ground-based CMB experiments (such as CMB-HD) are unlikely to be fundamentally limited by atmospheric noise at high multipoles.

Inferring dark matter halo mass profiles in intermediate-mass galaxy groups ($10^{11.3} - 10^{14} M_\odot/h$) is challenging due to low tracer counts per system, complicating observational tests of the standard NFW profile against alternative dark matter or modified gravity models. To overcome this limitation, the authors introduce a concentration-based rescaling and stacking pipeline that combines line-of-sight tracer kinematics across thousands of simulated groups in the IllustrisTNG-300 hydrodynamical simulation. Using higher-order Jeans modeling with the gravsphere code, they evaluate how tracer selection cuts—such as subhalo infall time, group relaxation state, or satellite quenching—impact the recovered dynamical mass profiles.
