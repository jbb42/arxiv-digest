Connecting field-level and n-point function measurements of galaxy bias

Highly relevantHsiang-Ming Huang, Henrique Rubira, Fabian Schmidt
arXiv:2611.14456 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: The paper directly addresses the field-level bias modeling and LPT-based forward modeling central to the student's work on Euclid simulations and emulator development.

This paper establishes a rigorous connection between two common ways of measuring galaxy bias: field-level inference (which depends on a numerical lattice cutoff) and $n$-point function analyses (which use renormalization conditions at low $k$). The authors employ the Renormalization Group (RG) framework to derive equations that describe how bias parameters evolve as the cutoff scale $\Lambda$ changes. By using a Lagrangian perturbation theory (LPT) forward model and testing against both mock catalogs and $N$-body halo catalogs, they demonstrate that the RG flow accurately predicts the running of bias parameters. This work provides a systematic method to compare bias measurements across different analysis schemes and ensures consistent bias priors for large-scale structure surveys.

Main conclusions
  • The RG flow equations successfully describe the running of first-order ($b_\delta$) and second-order ($b_{\delta^2}, b_{\mathcal{G}_2}$) bias parameters as a function of the cutoff scale $\Lambda$.
  • In the limit $\Lambda \to 0$, the field-level bias measurements converge to the standard tree-level $n$-point function bias definitions.
  • The authors identify that a 4LPT matter forward model is required to capture the bias running accurately up to $\mathcal{O}((\Delta\sigma^2)^2)$ for linear bias and $\mathcal{O}(\Delta\sigma^2)$ for quadratic bias.
  • The framework allows for the estimation of systematic errors in bias running, which is crucial for interpreting field-level measurements in realistic halo catalogs.

Abstract

Precise measurements of galaxy bias parameters are essential for extracting cosmological information from galaxy surveys. Field-level analyses infer bias coefficients at a finite cutoff $\Lambda$, typically related to the lattice resolution, while $n$-point function analyses typically adopt renormalization conditions defined in the low-$k$ limit. In this work, we use the renormalization group (RG) to relate field-level measurements at different cutoffs and connect them to $n$-point function measurements. We generate mock galaxy fields with a Lagrangian perturbation theory forward model and a galaxy bias expansion, and then measure the bias parameters across a range of cutoffs. We show that the RG predictions describe the measured running and, in the limit $\Lambda\to 0$, agree with the (tree-level) $n$-point function measurements of first-order $b_\delta$ and second-order $b_{\delta^2}, b_{{G}_2}$. We also measure the running on halo catalogs based on full $N$-body simulations and show that the RG prediction describes the measurements well within the predicted systematic errors. Our results establish the RG flow as a natural formulation for relating galaxy bias measurements at different cutoffs and connecting the schemes used in field-level and $n$-point function analyses.

OASIS: A Fast Dynamical Halo Finding Algorithm

RelevantBayli Hayes, Edgar M. Salazar, Eduardo Rozo, Benedikt Diemer, Calvin Osinga, John Hugon, Vadim Bernshteyn, Ze'ev Vladimir
arXiv:2611.13542 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: The paper provides a fast, single-snapshot alternative to particle-tracking for defining halos, which is highly relevant for the student's work on fast N-body methods and halo-based statistics in Euclid.

This paper introduces OASIS (Orbiting-mass ASsIgnment Scheme), a fast, single-snapshot algorithm designed to identify 'dynamical halos'—defined as the collection of particles orbiting their self-generated potential rather than those defined by traditional spherical overdensity criteria. By exploiting the physical intuition that orbiting particles are virialized and thus possess lower kinetic energy than infalling particles at the same radius, the authors establish a robust, cosmology-dependent energy cut to classify particles. This method provides a computationally efficient alternative to multi-snapshot particle-tracking algorithms like SPARTA, achieving $\simeq 9\%$ scatter in mass estimates. The authors demonstrate that OASIS enables large-scale statistical studies of dynamical halos, revealing that their mass function is nearly universal at $z=0$ but deviates from simple Press-Schechter predictions at high masses.

Main conclusions
  • OASIS provides a fast, single-snapshot method to distinguish orbiting from infalling particles with $\simeq 9\%$ scatter relative to full particle-tracking codes.
  • The kinetic energy cuts are largely universal, showing only a logarithmic dependence on $\Omega_m(z)$ and no significant dependence on halo mass when scaled by $R_{200m}$ and $v_{200m}$.
  • The $z=0$ dynamical halo mass function is approximately universal with respect to cosmology to within $\simeq 5\%$ accuracy.
  • A modified Press-Schechter model with a collapse threshold $\delta_{sc} = 1.54$ describes the mass function well at intermediate masses but fails at the high-mass end.

Abstract

In recent work (García et al. 2023), we advocated for defining a dynamical halo as the collection of particles that orbit their self-generated potential. Here we introduce the Orbiting-mass ASsIgnment Scheme (OASIS), a fast energy-based algorithm for constructing dynamical halo catalogs. The unified bound dark energy–bound dark matter (BDE-BDM) model attempts to explain both components of the dark sector from a single supersymmetric $SU(3)$ dark gauge group, eliminating free parameters in the dark energy sector. Below a condensation scale $\Lambda_c$, the lightest dark meson acts as a scalar field driving late-time thawing dark energy, while heavier bound states form dark matter created with a characteristic velocity $v_c$ that causes free-streaming power suppression. In this work, the authors confront BDE-BDM with cosmological data including DESI DR2 BAO, Planck/ACT/SPT-3G CMB data, CMB lensing, and the Unite supernova compilation. They find that BDE-BDM naturally reproduces DESI distance measures without entering a phantom regime ($w < -1$), while simultaneously providing a mild suppression of small-scale structure growth.

To address the Hubble tension, various early-time modifications to $\Lambda$CDM have been proposed, such as self-interacting neutrinos (SI$\nu$) and hot new early dark energy with dark radiation matter decoupling (DRMD). However, combining these multi-component extensions results in high-dimensional parameter spaces with flat directions and bimodal posteriors that are computationally prohibitive to sample with standard Boltzmann solvers. The authors use a neural network active-learning likelihood emulator, CLiENT, to sample and compute profile likelihoods for a 12-parameter SI$\nu$+DRMD cosmology using datasets including Planck, ACT, SPT, DESI BAO, Pantheon+, and SH0ES calibrations. They find that combining both mechanisms yields a synergistic fit to small-scale CMB and $H_0$ data far superior to either model on its own, illustrating the power of emulator-driven inference for complex non-standard cosmologies.

Main conclusions
  • The combined SI$\nu$+DRMD model improves the fit to high-$\ell$ CMB data ($H_0\text{WC}$ + SH0ES) over $\Lambda$CDM by $\Delta\chi^2 = -27.6$, outperforming DRMD alone by $\Delta\text{AIC} = -15.7$.
  • In the $H_0\text{WC}$ + SH0ES dataset combination, the posterior yields an inferred Hubble parameter of $H_0 = 70.94 \pm 0.60 \text{ km s}^{-1}\text{ Mpc}^{-1}$, significantly reducing the Hubble tension.
  • Small-scale CMB measurements from ACT DR6 and SPT-3G severely suppress the strongly-interacting regime of neutrino self-interactions, leaving only an intermediate/weak coupling mode.
  • Significant prior volume effects exist in the 12D parameter space: profile likelihoods reveal strong preferences for non-zero interacting dark matter fractions that appear only as upper bounds in 1D marginalized posteriors.

A large number of extensions of the $\Lambda$CDM model have been proposed as solutions to the Hubble tension, but they are almost always tested separately. Stable cosmology under flexible covariances: Rescaling and cross-survey correlations in DESI BAO and supernova data

RelevantEleni-Anna Kolonia, Haoye Li, Hanyu Zhang, Niayesh Afshordi

Why: This paper examines the statistical robustness of the DESI DR2 BAO + SNe + CMB preference for evolving dark energy ($w_0 w_a$CDM), directly addressing key topics under medium relevance in your research profile.

Recent combined analyses of cosmic microwave background (CMB), baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO), and Type Ia supernova (SN) data disfavor standard $\Lambda$CDM in favor of dynamical dark energy ($w_0 w_a$CDM) at the $3\text{–}4\sigma$ level. To test whether this result is driven by under-estimated uncertainties or ignored cross-survey correlations, the authors introduce a flexible likelihood framework that rescales published covariance matrices by free factors $s_X \ge 1$ and includes pairwise correlation parameters $r$ between SN compilations (Pantheon+, Union3, DES-Dovekie) projected onto a common redshift basis. Analyzing DESI DR2 BAO, Planck 2018 CMB, and joint SN data, they find that while datasets permit standard deviations up to $\sim 1.7$ times larger at 95% credibility, freeing the covariance parameters yields almost no best-fit improvement ($\Delta\chi^2 < 1$). Crucially, the preference for evolving dark energy remains solid, shifting only slightly from $\Delta\chi^2 = 12.8$ ($3.15\sigma$) to $\Delta\chi^2 = 12.3$ ($3.07\sigma$), confirming its robustness against covariance misestimation.

Main conclusions
  • Allowing free covariance scaling factors ($s_X \ge 1$) permits standard deviation increases up to a factor of $\sim 1.7$ at 95% credibility, but improves the overall best fit by $\Delta\chi^2 < 1$.
  • The preference for dynamical dark energy over $\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}$ remains robust under flexible covariances, with $w_0 w_a\mathrm{CDM}$ retaining a $\Delta\chi^2 = 12.3$ fit improvement over $\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}$ (nominally $3.07\sigma$), compared to $\Delta\chi^2 = 12.8$ ($3.15\sigma$) with baseline covariances.
  • A clear positive effective correlation is found between Pantheon+ and Union3 ($r \simeq 0.4\text{–}0.5$), reflecting shared supernova samples, while the Union3 and DES-Dovekie correlation is consistent with zero.
  • DESI DR2 BAO, Union3, and DES-Dovekie are consistent with their published covariances ($s_X \approx 1.0$), while Pantheon+ shows a mild preference for added covariance ($s_{\mathrm{PP}} = 1.46$ mode).

Why: This paper provides a methodology for handling systematic effects in photometric galaxy surveys, which is highly relevant to the Euclid survey's goal of accurate non-linear theory predictions and galaxy clustering measurements.

DustER is a new method for reconstructing Galactic dust reddening maps using the colors of red-sequence galaxies as 'standard crayons'. By leveraging the tight, predictable color-magnitude relation of red galaxies, the authors infer dust column density from the coherent spatial color residuals observed in photometric surveys. Because photometric redshifts and red-sequence calibrations are themselves affected by dust, the method employs a Wiener filtering and statistical debiasing procedure to isolate the extinction field. While the resulting dust maps exhibit some contamination from large-scale structure, the authors demonstrate that using these maps to deredden galaxy samples does not introduce measurable bias into the angular two-point clustering statistics, making it a robust tool for photometric survey analysis.

Main conclusions
  • The DustER algorithm achieves a mean bias of 0.5 mmag and an rms scatter of 6.3 mmag in reconstructed dust maps compared to input simulations.
  • The reconstructed dust maps show residual large-scale structure contamination, which the authors suggest requires future joint forward-modeling of dust and galaxy density fields.
  • Despite the map-level contamination, dereddening red-sequence galaxies with DustER maps yields angular power spectra consistent with those derived using the true dust field within cosmic variance.

Abstract

We present DustER, a new method for reconstructing dust reddening maps from photometric galaxy survey data. DustER uses red galaxies as "standard crayons". That is, it relies on the mean color of red-sequence galaxies being independent of their position in the sky. Given the galaxy's redshift, the difference between a galaxy's observed color and its expected color measures the reddening by dust along the galaxy's line of sight. When using photometric galaxy survey data, the method must rely on photometric redshifts, which, combined with selection effects, bias the recovered dust maps. DustER uses a novel approach to characterize and correct for these biases by leveraging the known statistical properties of the distribution of dust around the Milky Way. Testing on simulations, we find that the recovered dust maps are contaminated by large-scale structure. However, when we use the DustER map to deredden and select red-sequence galaxies, we find the resulting two-point function is not contaminated by dust.

Less is More: Unbiased Galaxy Clustering from Fewer Merger Trees

RelatedOscar J. Hickman, Sownak Bose, Kai Wang, Carlton M. Baugh, Baojiu Li
arXiv:2611.13083 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

Constraining galaxy formation models using the galaxy two-point correlation function (2PCF) is typically hindered by the high computational cost of running semi-analytic models (SAMs) across millions of dark matter halo merger trees for every parameter evaluation. Subsampling a fraction $m/k$ of merger trees naively biases small-scale clustering because one-halo galaxy pairs are kept or dropped together, over-representing one-halo relative to two-halo pairs by a factor of $k/m$. The authors introduce the Sparse and Unbiased Clustering Estimator (SaUCE), which derives exact, parameter-free Horvitz-Thompson weights for within-partition and cross-partition pairs to eliminate this clustering bias entirely. Tested on the P-Millennium simulation with the GALFORM SAM, the approach recovers the full-box real- and redshift-space clustering down to small scales using as little as 7% of the merger trees, achieving a more than tenfold computational speedup suitable for MCMC calibration and simulation-based inference.

Main conclusions
  • SaUCE introduces exact analytic weights ($w_{\rm auto} = m/k$ and $w_{\rm cross} = m(k-1)/[k(m-1)]$) that eliminate the selection bias incurred when measuring two-point statistics from sparsely sampled halo merger trees.
  • For a galaxy number density of $\bar{n} = 10^{-2}\,h^3\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$ at $z=0$, processing only 7.3% of the P-Millennium trees recovers the full-box $\xi(r)$ within 0.05 dex across $0.03\text{–}30\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}$, yielding a 14-fold computational saving.
  • The required tree fraction increases at higher redshift (reaching ~29% at $z\approx 1.5$ for $\bar{n}=10^{-3}\,h^3\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$) and lower number densities, and applies directly to redshift-space multipoles and projected clustering.
  • Scatter across separation bins is strongly correlated, meaning that likelihood analyses and simulation-based inference incorporating sparse catalogues must account for the full bin-to-bin covariance.

Abstract

Galaxy clustering could strongly constrain galaxy formation models but is rarely used, because the two-point correlation function (2PCF) requires a complete galaxy catalogue at every point in parameter space. Semi-analytic models (SAMs) process each halo merger tree independently, so running a SAM on $m$ of $k$ disjoint partitions of the trees, each spanning the full box, cuts the cost by $k/m$. Quantum gravity arguments such as the Trans-Planckian Censorship Conjecture (TCC) and horizon scrambling suggest that an exact de Sitter vacuum cannot endure indefinitely, decaying over a characteristic timescale $t_{\rm scr} \sim H^{-1} \ln(M_{\rm Pl}/H)$. The authors explore whether this horizon-scale physics imprints on cosmic expansion by introducing an extra term $-\Gamma H^2 / \ln(M_{\rm Pl}^2/H^2)$ directly into the background Raychaudhuri equation. In the slowly varying logarithm limit, they find closed-form background solutions where the correction acts as dynamical dark energy with a vacuum mode ($w = -1 + 2\sigma/3$) alongside tracking terms sourced by cosmic fluid history. They then evaluate its observational viability using proxy mappings to BBN, CMB, and DESI DR2 data, and investigate field-theoretic completions via the effective field theory of dark energy.

Main conclusions
  • The vacuum solution is given analytically by a Lambert-$W$ function, replacing constant de Sitter expansion with a slowly evolving Hubble rate that yields an effective equation of state $w_{\rm vac} = -1 + 2\sigma/3$ (where $\sigma \equiv \Gamma / \ln(M_{\rm Pl}^2/H^2)$).
  • Tracking terms in matter and radiation eras effectively rescale the expansion-rate gravitational coupling, leading to proxy constraints of $\Gamma = -3.7 \pm 6.1$ from BBN and $\Gamma = -2.8 \pm 0.9$ (implying $\sigma \approx -0.01$ today) from CMB+BAO+SNe fits.
  • The negative-$\Gamma$ branch naturally yields a mildly phantom-like dark energy evolution at low redshifts, qualitatively matching trends observed in DESI DR2 CPL fits for $z \gtrsim 1$.
  • Minimal covariant clock-field completions reproduce the background evolution but exhibit gradient instabilities ($c_s^2 < 0$), though these can be regularized on sub-horizon scales by adding Hořava–Lifshitz / cuscuton-type operators.

Abstract

Active learning is becoming standard practice in undergraduate STEM instruction in North America and is slowly being adopted in graduate courses. Graduate summer schools, one- to two-week programs in which students from many institutions gather for intensive, specialized instruction in their field, are common in physics; they are taught by researchers chosen for their expertise, and their pedagogy remains almost entirely traditional lecturing. We report a study at a one-week graduate school in neutrino and astroparticle physics attended by 38 students from 8 countries. Six lecturers with limited or no prior experience of teaching with active learning introduced Peer Instruction-style active learning into their lectures after approximately four hours of preparation led by a physics-education specialist. Each of the three main lecture streams was taught traditionally on one day and in the active format on the other. Students were surveyed after each of the first two days and at the end of the week; all six instructors were surveyed as well. In every active lecture, spanning three instructors and three topics, 82-100% of respondents reported that the interactive segments helped them stay engaged and check their understanding, and in every stream the difficulty was rated as better matched on the active day. At the end of the week, 33 of 36 wanted such teaching in future graduate courses, and not one respondent disagreed. All six instructors judged the approach productive and intend to use it again; only two found implementation straightforward. With brief expert-led preparation, lecturers new to active learning implemented it successfully for a heterogeneous audience, and both students and instructors want more. The school was also the first time any of the six instructors implemented structured active learning: a summer school can develop its lecturers as well as its students.

Self-interacting dark matter and neutrino masses from MeV-scale lepton-number breaking

This paper proposes a unified framework where a single scalar field, breaking lepton number at the MeV scale, simultaneously generates light neutrino masses via the inverse seesaw mechanism and acts as a light mediator for self-interacting dark matter (SIDM). The dark matter is modeled as a pseudo-Dirac fermion, where the mass splitting and self-interaction couplings are inherently linked to the lepton-number-breaking scale. The authors demonstrate that the mediator decays into neutrinos, effectively bypassing the tension between Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN) and direct detection constraints that typically plague light-mediator models. By solving the Schrödinger equation for the self-interaction cross-section and fixing the coupling via relic abundance, the model successfully reproduces the velocity-dependent scattering required to explain galactic structure, while remaining consistent with current cosmological and astrophysical bounds.

Main conclusions
  • The model predicts a narrow parameter band for SIDM, with a best fit at $m_{\chi} \simeq 7.6$ GeV and a mediator mass $m_s \simeq 15$ MeV.
  • The lepton-number-breaking scale is constrained to a window of a few hundred MeV, with a strong preference for normal neutrino mass ordering.
  • The model is highly predictive and testable by future experiments, including $N_{\text{eff}}$ measurements, low-threshold direct detection, and neutrino oscillation experiments like JUNO and DUNE.
  • The cluster-scale data place an upper bound on the dark matter mass of $m_{\chi} \lesssim 70$ GeV at 95% confidence level.

Abstract

We show that a scalar whose vacuum expectation value (vev) breaks lepton number at the MeV scale, generating the light-neutrino masses through the inverse seesaw, can also serve as the light mediator of self-interacting dark matter. Dark matter is a pseudo-Dirac fermion whose mass splitting arises from the same vev and is predicted to be proportional to the self-interaction coupling times the vev. A strong central interaction between the dark states requires this splitting to be sizable. The CP-even mediator decays into neutrinos at a rate fixed by the neutrino masses, independent of its mixing with the Higgs boson. Fixing the coupling by the relic abundance and evaluating exact partial-wave cross sections, the model reproduces the velocity dependence of the self-interaction cross section inferred from dwarf galaxies to clusters along a narrow band, $m_s\simeq1.9~{\rm MeV}\times(m_\chi/{\rm GeV})$, extending up to $m_\chi\simeq12$~GeV with a best fit at $m_\chi\simeq7.6$~GeV and $m_s\simeq15$~MeV, and on a sequence of resonant islands of comparable fit quality at higher masses. The cluster data bound the dark matter mass from above, so that the allowed masses extend from about $3$ to $70$~GeV ($95\%$ confidence level), with correlated mediator masses of $6$-$28$~MeV. Combining constraints from the effective number of neutrino species, SN 1987A and direct detection, we find the lepton-number-breaking scale confined to a window of a few hundred MeV and a strong preference for normal neutrino mass ordering. The allowed parameter space is testable by future measurements of $N_{\rm eff}$, low-threshold direct detection and the neutrino mass ordering.

Ultra-Heavy Dark Matter Through Exponential Production Mechanism

Relatedcross-listDisha Bhatia, Ranjeet Kumar, Rahul Srivastava
arXiv:2611.13842 · PDF · HTML · hep-ph, astro-ph.CO

This paper investigates the 'exponential production' mechanism for dark matter (DM), a non-thermal process where DM abundance grows exponentially through scatterings of the form $\chi + \xi \to \chi + \chi$ with a thermal bath particle $\xi$. Unlike standard freeze-in or freeze-out, this mechanism is highly sensitive to the initial DM abundance and the coupling strength, allowing for a distinct parameter space for DM production. The authors use a simplified scalar model with $Z_3$ symmetry to show that this mechanism can support ultra-heavy DM candidates, and they derive an upper mass bound of approximately $10^{15}$ GeV based on tree-level perturbativity. Finally, they propose a UV-complete Dirac Type-I seesaw model where the $Z_3$ symmetry emerges from a broken $U(1)_{B-L}$ symmetry, linking DM production to neutrino mass generation.

Main conclusions
  • Exponential production allows for ultra-heavy dark matter with masses up to $\mathcal{O}(10^{15})$ GeV, significantly higher than the $\sim 100$ TeV limit typically found in freeze-out scenarios.
  • The final relic abundance is exponentially sensitive to the coupling $\lambda_{\text{exp}}$, requiring a small initial DM population to kickstart the growth.
  • The required coupling to achieve the observed relic density increases with both the DM mass and a decrease in the initial comoving DM abundance.
  • The mechanism is successfully integrated into a UV-complete framework where $Z_3$ symmetry arises from $U(1)_{B-L}$ breaking, providing a unified origin for DM and Dirac neutrino masses.

Abstract

Exponential production of dark matter has been introduced recently as one of the alternatives to the widely studied thermal production of dark matter scenarios. This non-thermal production mechanism leads to the exponential growth in the abundance of the dark matter ($\chi$) through its scatterings with the bath particle ($\xi$), viz. $\chi + \xi \to \chi + \chi$. In our framework, we consider a scalar dark matter $\chi$ charged under $Z_3$ symmetry and bath particle $\xi$ to be standard model singlet and determine the maximum mass of the dark matter using the criteria of perturbativity. We find that dark matter has an upper mass around the ballpark of $\mathcal{O}(10^{15})$ GeV for the cases when the bath particle mass is similar to the dark matter mass. The computations are largely performed with minimal model dependence and will hold in any scenario where the renormalizable couplings driving the exponential growth effectively reduce to $\chi^3\xi$ interactions. Thus, in exponential production of dark matter, we have a possibility of an ultra-heavy dark matter with large couplings, making the scenario phenomenologically interesting. Later, we extend the simplified dark matter model to a UV-complete Dirac Type-I seesaw model where the $Z_3$ symmetry is obtained as a residual unbroken subgroup of a global $U(1)_{B-L}$ symmetry, providing a natural connection with the neutrino sector.

This paper investigates the feasibility of using gravitational lensing to provide early warnings for binary neutron star (BNS) and neutron star-black hole (NSBH) mergers, enabling electromagnetic (EM) follow-up with the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's LSST. By simulating lensed populations detectable in the O5 gravitational wave (GW) observing run, the authors generate kilonova light curves using benchmark forward models (MOSFiT and POSSIS) to assess their detectability in optical bands. They find that lensing magnification significantly boosts the chances of joint GW-EM detection, particularly for BNS systems, and introduce a 'detectability index' (DI) based on GW parameters to predict the likelihood of an observable optical counterpart. This work provides a framework for optimizing multi-messenger follow-up strategies for lensed transients in the upcoming era of high-cadence surveys.

Main conclusions
  • Approximately 60% of detectable lensed BNS kilonovae and 10% of NSBH kilonovae are expected to be observable within a single LSST exposure during O5.
  • Joint GW+EM detections are dominated by quadruple-image lensing systems, which provide the high magnification necessary to bring these transients above the LSST detection threshold.
  • NSBH joint detections are significantly rarer than BNS detections, as they require specific conditions—high mass ratios and rapid BH spins—to avoid direct plunge and ensure sufficient ejecta mass.
  • The proposed detectability index (DI) provides a robust, data-driven method to screen GW triggers for high-probability EM counterparts, facilitating efficient target-of-opportunity observations.

Abstract

This capability could, in turn, enable electromagnetic (EM) observatories to capture transient emissions surrounding the time of the merger. In this work, we demonstrate the viability of jointly detecting gravitationally lensed BNS and NSBH mergers via GWs during the O5 observing run, alongside their associated kilonova counterparts using the Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST). We simulate a population of lensed BNS and NSBH mergers detectable during O5 and utilize current benchmark forward models to generate the corresponding lensed kilonova light curves. We then assess the detectability of these light curves across LSST bands under various exposure limits. We find that among the detectable lensed mergers in O5, approximately 60% of the BNS kilonova and 10% of the NSBH kilonova will be observable within a single LSST exposure, with mostly quads dominating the detectable population. Furthermore, we define a detectability index ($\rm DI$), which determines the EM-detectability in optical of lensed GW-detectable BNS and NSBH mergers. We demonstrate the robustness and generalizability of the $\rm DI$ on our lensed BNS and NSBH population. Our work demonstrates that lensing-driven early warnings will be highly applicable in the near future, enabling the LSST to effectively follow up on BNS and NSBH triggers within GW localization areas during the O5 observing run.
