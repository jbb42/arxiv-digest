Using the 0i Einstein equation to break dark-sector degeneracies

RelevantCamille Bonvin
arXiv:2611.22498 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
Why: The paper presents a relativistic, model-independent diagnostic framework using gravitational redshift and Einstein constraint equations to break dark-sector degeneracies in LSS surveys, highly relevant for interpreting beyond-$\Lambda$CDM signals in Euclid.

Cosmological tensions in growth rate and expansion history could signify modified gravity, dynamical or clustering dark energy, or non-standard dark matter properties, but standard large-scale structure probes fail to uniquely identify the underlying physical mechanism. Standard analyses rely on measuring galaxy velocities ($V_g$) from redshift-space distortions and the Weyl potential ($ rac{\Phi+\Psi}{2}$) from gravitational lensing, both of which are degenerate with the unobservable matter density perturbation ($\delta$) and galaxy bias. In this paper, the author demonstrates these degeneracies explicitly across five representative beyond-$\Lambda$CDM models (generalized Brans-Dicke gravity, coupled quintessence, viscous dark matter, evolving dark energy, and clustering dark energy). To break these dark-sector degeneracies in a model-independent way, the author constructs a set of three tests ($\mathcal{N}^{\rm slip}$, $\mathcal{N}^{0i}$, and $\mathcal{N}^{\rm Euler}$) built strictly from directly observable quantities: $V_g$, the Weyl potential, and the time distortion potential $\Psi$, which is accessible through galaxy gravitational redshift measurements. Crucially, the paper demonstrates that the $0i$ Einstein momentum-constraint equation—which is typically omitted in standard tests due to Bianchi identity redundancy when assuming a specific model—provides the key information required to unambiguously differentiate between modified gravity, dark energy, and dark matter signatures.

Main conclusions
  • Standard observations combining galaxy peculiar velocities $V_g$ and lensing potential $\Psi_W$ are fundamentally degenerate, meaning any observed departure from $\Lambda$CDM can be mimicked by modified gravity, dark energy, or extended dark matter models.
  • Gravitational redshift measurements grant direct observational access to the metric temporal potential $\Psi$, enabling model-independent tests of the gravitational slip equation ($\mathcal{N}^{\rm slip}$) and the Euler velocity evolution equation ($\mathcal{N}^{\rm Euler}$).
  • Testing the $0i$ Einstein equation via $\mathcal{N}^{0i}$ provides essential complementary information that breaks the remaining degeneracies between modified gravity, dark energy, and dark matter.
  • The triplet of observables ($\mathcal{N}^{\rm slip}, \mathcal{N}^{0i}, \mathcal{N}^{\rm Euler}$) yields distinct matrix signatures: dark energy leaves all tests zero; modified gravity induces non-zero $\mathcal{N}^{\rm slip}$ and $\mathcal{N}^{0i}$; coupled dark matter induces non-zero $\mathcal{N}^{0i}$ and $\mathcal{N}^{\rm Euler}$; and viscous dark matter affects only $\mathcal{N}^{\rm Euler}$.

Abstract

Over the past decade, cosmological observations have started to challenge the $\Lambda$CDM model. If these tensions persist, they immediately raise one fundamental question: what new physics could be responsible for them? More specifically, do these deviations arise from a modification of gravity, from an evolving or clustering dark energy component, or from dark matter properties beyond the cold dark matter paradigm? In this paper we show that, with current methods, any observed deviation can always be attributed to more than one sector, preventing us from determining if the tension is due to gravity, dark energy or dark matter. The main reason is that current tests rely on unobservable quantities. These quantities are then either related to observables by assuming the validity of specific equations, or compared with theoretical predictions computed within a given class of theories. Both approaches inevitably break the model independence of the tests, leading to degeneracies between gravity, dark energy, and dark matter. To overcome this limitation, we construct a set of three tests that rely only on observable quantities and that, when combined, can distinguish between the three sectors. We show that the $0i$ Einstein equation – which is never tested in standard analyses – adds crucial information to that provided by the gravitational slip equation and the Euler equation.

A Universal Ratio Linking Cosmic Expansion and the Growth of Structure

RelevantAnjan A Sen, Purba Mukherjee
arXiv:2611.23200 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
Why: The paper introduces a model-independent theoretical diagnostic linking linear growth ($f\sigma_8$) turnover and cosmic acceleration to test modified gravity (DGP, Horndeski) and dynamical dark energy models.

In standard cosmology, late-time cosmic acceleration begins at redshift $z_t$ (where the deceleration parameter $q(z_t)=0$), while the linear growth observable $f\sigma_8(z)$ reaches a turnover peak at redshift $z_{\rm pk}$ due to the competition between gravitational attraction and Hubble friction. The authors derive an exact relation for flat $\Lambda$CDM, showing that the ratio $R \equiv (1+z_t)/(1+z_{\rm pk}) = 1.1596$ is strictly universal and independent of $\Omega_{m0}$, $H_0$, the sound horizon $r_d$, and $\sigma_{8,0}$. They generalize this framework to show that $R$ remains invariant across any dark energy or modified gravity (MG) theory possessing a single characteristic energy scale under a background/growth scaling symmetry. Evaluating this ratio across various MG and dynamical dark energy scenarios reveals distinctive shifts (e.g., $R < 1$ for DGP gravity), and Gaussian Process reconstructions from DESI DR2 BAO, supernova, and RSD data yield $R \sim 1.07 - 1.22$ with $10-15\%$ precision, demonstrating $R$ as a clean, calibration-independent consistency test for Euclid-era growth data.

Main conclusions
  • In flat $\Lambda$CDM, the ratio $R \equiv (1+z_t)/(1+z_{\rm pk}) = 1.1596$ is exact and completely independent of $\Omega_{m0}$, $H_0$, $r_d$, and $\sigma_{8,0}$.
  • The invariance of $R$ extends to a broad universality class of flat dark energy and modified gravity models whose dynamics scale uniformly under $\rho_m \to c\rho_m$ and $\rho_* \to c\rho_*$.
  • Alternative models alter $R$ predictably: DGP gravity yields $R = 0.9685$ ($16.5\%$ below $\Lambda$CDM, where structure growth peaks before acceleration begins), while shift-symmetric Horndeski gravity yields $1.15 \lesssim R \lesssim 1.25$.
  • Observational reconstruction using DESI DR2 BAO, SNe Ia, and RSD growth data gives $R = 1.212^{+0.159}_{-0.182}$ (or $1.074^{+0.092}_{-0.106}$ when including the Gold RSD compilation), fully consistent with $\Lambda$CDM within $1\sigma$.

Beyond the point lens: radial structure and the horizon in wave-optics gravitational lensing

Relevantcross-listAlice Garoffolo, Gianmassimo Tasinato
arXiv:2611.22618 · PDF · HTML · gr-qc, astro-ph.CO, hep-th
Why: The paper's focus on systematic corrections to lensing and the use of effective field theory/matching techniques for compact objects is highly relevant to the student's work on beyond-ΛCDM simulations and relativistic inhomogeneous cosmology.

This paper develops a systematic analytic framework to calculate gravitational-wave lensing corrections beyond the standard point-mass (Newtonian) approximation. By using a distorted-wave Born expansion, the authors treat the Newtonian potential non-perturbatively while systematically incorporating higher-order radial corrections (e.g., relativistic exterior geometry) and inner boundary conditions (e.g., the Schwarzschild horizon). This approach resolves long-standing issues with the standard Born series, such as infrared divergences, and allows for the inclusion of absorptive effects at the horizon that cannot be captured by thin-lens diffraction integrals. The results provide a rigorous way to model how the internal structure of a lens, such as a black hole, modifies the lensed waveform.

Main conclusions
  • The authors demonstrate that the standard Born expansion around a free wave fails due to long-range phase accumulation, necessitating a resummation of the Newtonian potential.
  • The Schwarzschild horizon acts as an absorptive contact term in the amplification factor, reproducing the expected horizon-area cross section at low frequencies.
  • For an aligned source, finite-distance optical corrections and horizon-related terms appear at the same order, with the optical term cancelling 15/16 of the horizon contribution in the forward amplitude.
  • The framework successfully separates exterior radial profile effects from local, inner-boundary-dependent terms, providing a robust method for beyond-point-mass lensing predictions.

Abstract

Gravitational-wave lensing in the wave-optics regime is sensitive to the structure of the lens beyond its total mass: to its exterior radial profile and, as we show, to the boundary condition at its centre. We develop an analytic framework to compute these effects for a scalar wave, as corrections to the wave lensed by a Newtonian point mass. The corrections are organized in inverse powers of the distance from the lens and computed with dressed radial kernels, which include the Newtonian interaction to all orders and resum the long-range phase that invalidates the Born expansion around the free wave. Retaining wavefront curvature, we find that the scattered wave is not the whole response: an inverse-square deformation changes the direct wave by an equal amount, even when the scattered wave is faint. Steeper terms of the exterior potential lead to short-distance divergences, which signal that the exterior geometry alone does not determine the lensed wave: the missing information is a set of local terms fixed by the inner boundary condition. For a Schwarzschild black hole we determine them by matching to the ingoing solution at the horizon. The horizon enters the amplification factor as an absorptive contact term, which reproduces the horizon-area cross section; it is absent for a non-absorbing lens of the same mass, and cannot be described by the thin-lens diffraction integral. It completes the correction quadratic in the mass, where it appears at the same order as finite-distance optical corrections, and largely cancels against them for an aligned source. Together, these results connect Newtonian dressing, diffraction and inner boundary conditions, in a systematic description of corrections to point-mass gravitational-wave lensing.

StochAXtic: a JAX-Accelerated, End-to-End Differentiable Framework for Gravitational-Wave Backgrounds from Compact Binary Populations

Relevantcross-listHaowen Zhong, Ethan Vislosky, Emanuele Berti
arXiv:2611.22999 · PDF · HTML · gr-qc, astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.HE, astro-ph.IM
Why: While the paper focuses on gravitational waves, the methodology of using JAX-accelerated emulators and differentiable, precomputed lookup tables for fast likelihood evaluation is highly relevant to the student's work on building fast emulators for beyond-ΛCDM cosmology.

StochAXtic is a new JAX-based computational framework designed to efficiently calculate the stochastic gravitational-wave background (SGWB) from compact binary coalescences. By exploiting the scale invariance of gravitational-wave waveforms—where the signal depends on total mass only through the dimensionless product $Mf$—the authors replace expensive, repeated Monte Carlo sampling with a precomputed, compressed lookup table. This approach, combined with Fast Fourier Transforms and truncated singular value decomposition, allows for the evaluation of the energy density spectrum $\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(f)$ in milliseconds. The framework is fully differentiable, enabling its direct use in gradient-based Bayesian inference and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo pipelines for next-generation gravitational-wave detectors.

Main conclusions
  • The framework achieves a relative numerical error below $10^{-3}$ at frequencies up to 1 kHz, significantly outperforming the statistical requirements for current and future detector networks.
  • The use of truncated singular value decomposition reduces the dimensionality of the precomputed intensity table, allowing for rapid evaluation of complex population models.
  • The end-to-end differentiability in JAX facilitates seamless integration into gradient-based parameter estimation, which is essential for jointly inferring population hyperparameters and cosmological parameters.
  • The method is validated against existing Monte Carlo-based tools like popstock, showing agreement within statistical uncertainties while offering orders-of-magnitude speed improvements.

Abstract

We present StochAXtic, a package for computing the dimensionless energy density spectrum $\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(f)$ of the stochastic gravitational-wave background generated by compact binary coalescences. Rather than sampling binary populations via Monte Carlo, StochAXtic exploits the scale invariance of the waveforms: at fixed mass ratio, spins and tidal deformabilities, the waveform depends on the total mass only through the dimensionless combination $Mf$. This allows all computationally expensive waveform evaluations using LALSuite to be performed once and stored in a population-independent lookup table. Once the table has been computed and cached, StochAXtic evaluates $\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(f)$ in a few milliseconds, achieving a relative numerical error below $10^{-3}$ at frequencies up to $1$ kHz. Efficient and Optimal Analysis of Galaxy Surveys on Large Scales

RelatedJulius Adolff, Uroš Seljak
arXiv:2611.21718 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

On the largest cosmological scales, galaxy clustering is close to Gaussian and contains vital signals such as scale-dependent halo bias from primordial non-Gaussianity ($f_{\rm NL}$). However, survey geometry and selection effects couple Fourier modes into a dense covariance matrix, making standard pseudo-$P(k)$ estimators suboptimal and exact Gaussian field-level matrix inversions prohibitively slow. The authors develop an optimal, computationally efficient field-level quadratic compression scheme based on the Marginal Unbiased Score Expansion (MUSE) framework restricted to linear scales ($k \le 0.08\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$). By framing the window function, integral constraints, and angular imaging systematics as linear projection operators and solving the linear systems via conjugate gradient methods, they extract maximal information without needing direct matrix inversion or high-dimensional Markov Chain Monte Carlo sampling.

Main conclusions
  • Field-level quadratic score compression retains the full Fisher information of the Gaussian field on large scales while bypassing the inversion of dense, window-coupled covariance matrices.
  • Forecasted for DESI DR2 LRG and QSO samples, the optimal field-level analysis yields an uncertainty $\sigma(f_{\rm NL}) = 4.1$, an approximate $14\%$ improvement over standard power spectrum methods ($\sigma(f_{\rm NL}) = 4.8$).
  • Survey window effects, radial integral constraints, and unmodelled angular imaging systematics are cleanly handled via linear mode deprojection operators without throwing away informative radial modes.
  • The analysis is computationally lightweight, requiring only $\sim 4$ GPU hours on four A100 GPUs to evaluate mock realizations and calibrate the parameter-dependent likelihood.

Large-scale peculiar velocities directly trace the growth of cosmic structure, and the kinetic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (kSZ) effect—a Doppler shift of CMB photons scattering off moving free electrons—allows the reconstruction of 3D radial velocity fields when cross-correlated with galaxy surveys. In this work, the authors reconstruct large-scale velocities using South Pole Telescope (SPT-3G D1) temperature maps at 95, 150, and 220 GHz cross-correlated with photometric luminous red galaxies from the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys. They measure the galaxy-velocity cross-correlation dipole and monopole, the velocity auto-power spectrum, and use multi-frequency modeling to separate thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (tSZ) and cosmic infrared background (CIB) foreground contamination. The phase gradient spectrum: probing the squeezed bispectrum of the 21-cm signal during reionization

Standard analyses of the 21-cm signal from the epoch of reionization (EoR) rely on the power spectrum, which discards Fourier phase correlations that encode the rich, non-Gaussian spatial morphology of growing ionized hydrogen regions. To capture this information efficiently, the authors introduce the phase gradient spectrum, a Fourier-space summary statistic that measures the circular coherence of phase differences between short-wavelength modes separated by a long-wavelength mode. They demonstrate analytically that this statistic directly probes the angle-averaged normalized squeezed bispectrum without requiring the computationally expensive triangle configurations of full higher-order statistics. When applied to 21-cm EoR simulations, the phase gradient spectrum displays a characteristic non-monotonic trajectory, reaching a minimum midway through reionization that marks a sign change in how small-scale power responds to large-scale modes.
