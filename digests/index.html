Mercury (Hg): a relativistic $N$-body simulation for Horndeski gravity

Highly relevantAhmad Nouri-Zonoz, Farbod Hassani, Julian Adamek, Emilio Bellini, Martin Kunz
arXiv:2611.03637 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, physics.comp-ph
Why: This paper presents a relativistic N-body simulation code for modified gravity (Horndeski/Brans–Dicke/Galileon) and includes a researcher from your own institute (Farbod Hassani at ITA Oslo), hitting both your core topics of beyond-ΛCDM N-body simulations and relativistic cosmological modelling.

Horndeski gravity is the most general scalar-tensor framework for dark energy and modified gravity that avoids unstable higher-derivative ghosts, and on linear scales its behaviour is neatly summarised by the effective Planck mass $M_*^2$ and four time-dependent Effective Field Theory (EFT) functions: kineticity ($\alpha_{\rm K}$), kinetic braiding ($\alpha_{\rm B}$), Planck-mass run rate ($\alpha_{\rm M}$), and tensor speed excess ($\alpha_{\rm T}$). To push tests of these theories into the nonlinear regime of structure formation while keeping full relativistic effects, the authors introduce Mercury, an extension of the relativistic particle-mesh $N$-body code gevolution that interfaces directly with the Einstein–Boltzmann solver hi_class. Mercury evolves matter and weak-field metric perturbations (scalar, vector, and tensor modes in the Poisson gauge) nonlinearly alongside the Horndeski scalar field, which is governed by its linear $\alpha$-basis perturbation equations but dynamically sourced by the nonlinear matter and metric fields. This provides a unified, GPU-accelerated relativistic simulation pipeline for both phenomenological $\alpha$-parametrisations and covariant theories like Brans–Dicke and Cubic Galileon, serving as a crucial stepping stone toward full nonlinear scalar screening in relativistic $N$-body simulations.

Main conclusions
  • Mercury successfully bridges the linear Horndeski solver hi_class with gevolution (including GPU acceleration via gevolution 2.0), supporting both phenomenological EFT $\alpha$-function parametrisations and full covariant models (validated on Brans–Dicke, Cubic Galileon, and nKGB).
  • A non-zero Planck-mass run rate ($\alpha_{\rm M} \neq 0$, meaning an evolving effective gravitational strength $M_*^2$) directly generates gravitational slip ($\Phi \neq \Psi$) via scalar anisotropic stress, alters the friction/damping terms for vector and tensor metric perturbations, and changes both the normalisation and scale dependence of the dark-energy density power spectrum.
  • Because the scalar field itself is evolved using linear perturbation equations (with nonlinearities arising only from its coupling to the nonlinear matter and metric fields), this release captures backreaction onto the metric potentials without yet modelling nonlinear scalar self-interactions or Vainshtein/chameleon screening.
  • Resolution convergence tests up to $3072^3$ particles/grid cells in a $4000\,h^{-1}\text{Mpc}$ box show that individual matter and dark-energy power spectra converge to within $2\%$ up to $k \simeq 0.29\,h\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$ and $0.36\,h\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$ respectively, while power spectrum ratios between different models cancel grid effects and remain accurate to within $\sim 1\text{–}2\%$ to much smaller scales.

Abstract

We present Mercury, a relativistic $N$-body code for cosmological simulations in the $\alpha$-basis formulation of Horndeski gravity. Mercury extends a particle-mesh scheme by incorporating the dynamics of the additional scalar degree of freedom and the corresponding modified Einstein equations, expressed in terms of the time-dependent functions $\alpha_{\rm K}, \alpha_{\rm B}, \alpha_{\rm M}, \alpha_{\rm T}$ and the effective Planck mass $M_*^2$. This generalises our previous implementation of Horndeski subclass models, namely $k$-essence and kinetic gravity braiding (KGB), to the general linear Horndeski $\alpha$-basis. The code evolves the additional scalar degree of freedom and its effective stress–energy tensor alongside the nonlinear matter and metric fields, so that nonlinear structure formation sources the scalar-field evolution and feeds back on the gravitational potentials. Through its interface with hi_class, Mercury supports both phenomenological $\alpha$-function parametrisations and covariant Horndeski theories; we demonstrate the latter using nKGB, the Cubic Galileon, and Brans–Dicke models. We validate the implementation against hi_class in the linear regime and recover the expected nonlinear $k$-essence and KGB limits. For the luminal Horndeski models considered here, we show how a running effective Planck mass generates gravitational slip through the scalar anisotropic stress and modifies the friction terms governing the vector and tensor metric perturbations. The evolution of $M_*^2$ also modifies the normalisation and scale dependence of the effective dark-energy density contrast, leaving a corresponding imprint on its power spectrum. The present framework provides an intermediate step towards nonlinear Horndeski simulations, in which nonlinear scalar self-interactions and screening effects can be incorporated consistently within the same relativistic framework.

Cuscuton Fifth Force: Implications of Non-Dynamical Scalars for Structure Growth

RelevantAdam Smith, Ilaria Andrei, Maria Mylova, Niayesh Afshordi
arXiv:2611.01228 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-th
Why: Directly investigates scale-independent modified gravity ($\mu(a)/G_{\rm eff}$ and modified Hubble friction) and linear structure growth in a beyond-$\Lambda$CDM scalar-tensor framework, alongside discussion of breakdown in inhomogeneous/nonlinear regimes.

Scalar-tensor modified gravity models usually introduce an extra propagating scalar wave that mediates a fifth force, which requires screening mechanisms to pass tight Solar System bounds. The "cuscuton" is a special class of scalar field with a degenerate kinetic term whose equation of motion acts purely as an algebraic constraint rather than a dynamical wave equation—meaning it modifies gravity while propagating only the two standard tensor modes of General Relativity. In this paper, the authors formulate an effective cuscuton theory where matter is minimally coupled in the Jordan frame (and thus conformally coupled in the Einstein frame) and implement it into a modified version of CLASS and HyRec to study structure growth and the CMB in power-law "tracking" and "sign-switching" dark energy regimes. They demonstrate that even without a propagating scalar mode, the field's instantaneous algebraic response to matter density perturbations still mediates a cosmological fifth force, though its effects on the matter power spectrum and CMB are tightly locked to—and overshadowed by—the accompanying background expansion and time-varying electron mass.

Main conclusions
  • Even without a propagating scalar degree of freedom, the algebraic constraint governing the cuscuton mediates a quasi-static fifth force and modifies Hubble friction in the matter growth equation, with its strength and sign fixed directly by the background expansion rather than an independent coupling parameter.
  • Across both tracking and sign-switching power-law benchmarks, the direct fifth-force correction to the effective gravitational coupling $G_{\rm eff}/G_N$ on sub-horizon scales is at most $\sim 1\%$ (peaking around $10^{-2}$ for tracking parameter $\lambda = -10^2$ and sign-switching parameter $\zeta = 10^{-2}$).
  • In the sign-switching regime—where the effective cuscuton energy density transitions from negative during matter domination to positive at late times—the fifth-force correction is negative for $a \lesssim 0.8$, acting as a repulsive fifth force that weakens gravitational attraction over most of cosmic history.
  • Cosmological observables are dominated by the modified background expansion and evolving electron mass during recombination rather than the $1\%$ fifth force itself, shifting CMB acoustic peaks by a few percent and suppressing the linear matter power spectrum by $\sim 25\%$ at $k = 1\,h\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$.
  • These cosmological $G_{\rm eff}$ predictions cannot be naively extrapolated to Solar System tests because strong local spatial gradients $|\nabla\phi|$ around localized sources can violate the timelike-gradient condition $X = \dot{\phi}^2 - |\nabla\phi|^2 > 0$, causing the effective cuscuton description to break down in the nonlinear regime.

Abstract

The cuscuton is a scalar field that obeys a constraint rather than an evolution equation, so it modifies gravity without adding a propagating scalar degree of freedom. We study the cosmology of effective cuscuton theory when matter is minimally coupled in the Jordan frame, and therefore conformally coupled to the cuscuton in the Einstein-frame. We derive the background and linear perturbation equations and show that the algebraic cuscuton response mediates a fifth force even though the scalar itself does not propagate. The same conformal factor also changes Einstein-frame particle masses and the friction of matter perturbations, tying the strength and sign of the force to the background evolution rather than to an independent coupling. We study a power-law family of models which contain both tracking solutions and sign-switching solutions; in the latter the effective cuscuton density runs from negative during matter domination to a positive, cosmological-constant-like value today. Using a modified version of CLASS, including the induced particle-mass dependence of recombination, we illustrate the corresponding changes to CMB temperature and linear matter power spectra for benchmark models, without fitting to data. In the strongest benchmarks the fifth force changes the effective gravitational coupling on sub-horizon scales by about one percent, smaller than the accompanying changes to the matter power spectrum and the acoustic scale. For the sign-switching solutions this change is negative over most of cosmic history, so a non-propagating field can weaken rather than strengthen gravitational attraction.

Bayesian and frequentist constraints on dark energy parameterizations using non-CMB data

RelevantShubham Barua, Ansh Chandrakar, Shantanu Desai
arXiv:2611.01505 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: Directly addresses dynamical dark energy ($w_0 w_a$ and quintessence parameterizations) and cosmological constraints from DESI DR2 BAO and supernovae, which are medium-relevance topics in your research profile.

Recent results combining DESI Baryon Acoustic Oscillations (BAO) with the CMB have hinted that dark energy might be evolving over time and even crossing the "phantom divide" ($w(z) < -1$, where dark energy density grows with expansion, violating standard energy conditions). However, because CMB and BAO data already have a mild tension in their preferred matter density $\Omega_{m0}$ within $\Lambda$CDM, it is unclear how much of this dynamical dark energy signal is an artifact of forcing those datasets together. To isolate late-time physics, the authors drop the CMB entirely and test four dynamical dark energy parameterizations—including standard $w_0 w_a$ (CPL), emergent dark energy (GEDE), and two "thawing quintessence" models where a scalar field sits frozen at $w = -1$ early on before rolling at late times—using only DESI DR2 BAO, Type Ia supernovae (DES-Dovekie), Gamma-Ray Bursts, and Redshift-Space Distortions ($f\sigma_8$). By comparing both Bayesian MCMC posteriors and frequentist profile likelihoods (which avoid prior-volume effects), they show that whether dynamical dark energy or phantom crossing is actually preferred depends heavily on the chosen parameterization and statistical penalty metric.

Main conclusions
  • In the standard CPL ($w_0 w_a$) parameterization using DESI DR2 BAO + SNe + GRBs + RSD, $w_0 = -0.83^{+0.07}_{-0.08}$ ($\sim 2\sigma$ away from $-1$) and $w_a = -0.47^{+0.47}_{-0.58}$ (consistent with $0$ within $1\sigma$), exhibiting a phantom crossing around $z \sim 0.5$ at $2\text{–}3\sigma$ significance.
  • The two quintessence-inspired thawing parameterizations (TQD and AT) improve the best-fit $\chi^2$ over $\Lambda$CDM without ever crossing into the phantom regime ($w < -1$), showing that non-phantom scalar field dynamics can explain the late-time expansion data well.
  • Model preference depends heavily on the statistical metric: while AIC and DIC favor CPL, TQD, and AT when supernovae are included, the Bayes factor penalizes the larger prior volume of CPL (weakly preferring $\Lambda$CDM over CPL) and only moderately favors the TQD model.
  • Matter density ($\Omega_{m0} \approx 0.306$) and structure growth ($\sigma_8 \approx 0.82\text{–}0.83$) remain robust across all dark energy parameterizations and agree well between Bayesian and frequentist methods; high-redshift Gamma-Ray Bursts add negligible constraining power compared to SNe.

Recovering Missing Degrees of Freedom in Simulation-Trained Field-Level Generative Models

RelevantMasato Shirasaki, Shiro Ikeda
arXiv:2611.02627 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, cs.LG
Why: Directly addresses machine-learning surrogates/generative models trained on finite simulation suites, field-level inference for weak lensing, and physics-informed corrections motivated by super-survey modes (separate-universe-like effects).

In field-level cosmological inference, deep generative models are trained on expensive simulations to map initial Gaussian random fields directly to complex, non-linear observables like weak-lensing convergence ($\kappa$) maps. Because training suites contain a finite number of simulations, these models often suffer from "variance shrinkage"—accurately learning the ensemble-average statistics while underestimating the realization-to-realization scatter caused by cosmic variance and super-survey modes. The authors address this without running additional simulations by modifying the velocity field of a Continuous Normalizing Flow (CNF, a model that evolves Gaussian noise into non-Gaussian maps via an ordinary differential equation) trained on 10,000 gravity-only $\kappa\text{TNG}$ maps. Guided by how super-survey density modes affect local growth and mean density, they add a 20-parameter physics-informed latent correction to the flow dynamics and fit it to match higher-order moments of the field distribution. When performing field-level Bayesian reconstruction on noisy weak-lensing maps, these learned latent variables act as effective, scale-dependent nuisance parameters that restore missing sample variance and absorb out-of-distribution systematic errors like unmodeled baryonic feedback and photometric redshift shifts.

Main conclusions
  • A baseline CNF trained on 10,000 gravity-only $\kappa\text{TNG}$ weak-lensing maps accurately matches mean power spectra and peak counts within $0.5\sigma$, but suffers from severe variance shrinkage across realizations.
  • Introducing a 20-parameter physics-informed correction to the flow velocity field recovers a substantial fraction of the missing realization-level variance without requiring new training simulations, with a single dominant latent mode ($\theta_{\text{add}}$) acting as a scale-dependent growth-amplitude modulation.
  • During MALA field-level inference on out-of-distribution (OoD) mock data, the posterior of $\theta_{\text{add}}$ systematically shifts negative for hydrodynamical (IllustrisTNG) maps (reflecting baryonic power suppression) and positive for source-redshift shifts of $\Delta z_s \approx 0.05\text{–}0.07$ (reflecting enhanced lensing efficiency).
  • Marginalizing over these latent degrees of freedom consistently reduces the OoD power-spectrum discrepancy metric $\mathcal{D}^2$ across 30 paired realizations ($500 \le \ell \le 10^4$) and improves non-Gaussian peak-count reconstructions compared to the latent-free baseline.

Abstract

Simulation-trained generative models are emerging as a powerful framework for field-level inference in cosmology, particularly in strongly nonlinear regimes where analytical approaches become intractable. However, the finite size of training datasets often leads to variance shrinkage, indicating that part of the realization-level variability of the underlying physical process is not captured by the learned generative dynamics. In this Letter, we interpret variance shrinkage as a diagnostic of missing effective degrees of freedom and introduce physics-informed latent modifications to the generative dynamics of a continuous normalizing flow. We show that these latent corrections recover a substantial fraction of the missing variability without requiring additional training simulations. Using weak-lensing field-level inference as a representative cosmological application, we further demonstrate through posterior analysis that the learned latent directions are actively exploited during inference and behave as effective nuisance parameters that absorb part of the mismatch between the training and target distributions. Baryonic feedback and intrinsic alignments in profile: a frequentist view of DES Y3 cosmic shear

RelevantAnoma Ganguly, Elisabeth Krause, Tanvi Karwal, Vivian Miranda
arXiv:2611.03537 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: Directly addresses the $S_8$ tension, weak lensing non-linear modelling (baryonic feedback and intrinsic alignments), and emulator-accelerated cosmological inference, which are core medium-relevance topics for your Euclid work.

Cosmic shear (the coherent distortion of background galaxy shapes by foreground weak gravitational lensing) primarily constrains the matter clustering parameter $S_8$ and matter density $\Omega_{\mathrm{m}}$, leaving many astrophysical nuisance parameters poorly constrained. In standard Bayesian analyses, integrating (marginalizing) over these weakly constrained parameters—such as baryonic feedback (gas heating by active galactic nuclei, parameterized by $T_{\mathrm{AGN}}$, which suppresses small-scale structure) and intrinsic alignments (IA, the physical alignment of galaxy shapes with local tidal fields, modeled via the simpler NLA or extended TATT frameworks)—can induce "prior-volume effects" that shift or artificially tighten posterior constraints. To isolate purely data-driven constraints from prior effects, the authors construct a fast frequentist profile likelihood pipeline (which maximizes rather than integrates the likelihood at each parameter value) using a neural-network power spectrum emulator (CosmoPower for HMCode2020) and the CMA-ES optimizer, applying it to the KiDS-excised DES Y3 cosmic shear dataset. This matters because it reveals how much of the apparent $S_8$ constraint and its tension with the CMB in cosmic-shear-only analyses is genuinely driven by lensing data versus prior boundaries on feedback and IA parameters.

Main conclusions
  • For DES Y3 cosmic shear alone, the $S_8$ profile likelihood is noticeably broader than the Bayesian marginal posterior because unconstrained cosmological and nuisance parameters are driven to the edges of their allowed ranges, where negligible prior volume artificially shrinks the Bayesian posterior.
  • Cosmic-shear-only constraints on the upper $1\sigma$ limit of $S_8$ are limited by a strong degeneracy with the baryonic feedback parameter $\log_{10}(T_{\mathrm{AGN}}/\mathrm{K})$, where stronger small-scale power suppression compensates for higher clustering amplitudes.
  • Adding CMB information (specifically on $\omega_{\mathrm{b}}$, $n_{\mathrm{s}}$, and $h$) breaks the shape degeneracies of the matter power spectrum, bringing the profile likelihood and Bayesian posterior into close agreement and yielding $S_8$ consistent with Planck within $1.0\sigma$ (profile) and $1.3\sigma$ (posterior), while preferring moderate baryonic feedback matching hydrodynamical simulations.
  • Replacing the 2-parameter NLA intrinsic alignment model with the 5-parameter TATT model shifts and broadens the $S_8$ profile due to newly opened degenerate directions, though incorporating DES Y3 shear-ratio data successfully breaks these degeneracies and reconciles the frequentist and Bayesian constraints.

As CMB experiments like the Simons Observatory (SO) map the sky with higher precision, gravitational lensing of CMB photons by intervening matter becomes sensitive to small-scale non-linear clustering and baryonic feedback (such as active galactic nuclei, or AGN, heating and expelling gas from halos). If unmodelled, these poorly understood small-scale physics bias cosmological parameter inference, especially because line-of-sight lensing projections mix high-$k$ modes into larger angular scales. Main conclusions
  • Despite parameterizing the redshift evolution of the EFTofLSS counterterms up to $z=50$ with 32 spline knots per coefficient (yielding up to 160 parameters at two-loop), PCA compression shows that varying just a single principal component is enough to keep 1D cosmological parameter biases below $\sim 0.2\sigma$ across all tested baryonic feedback models, with this mode acting primarily as an effective proxy for the AGN heating temperature $T_{\rm AGN}$.

Beyond Point Photo-$z$ Estimates: Normalizing Flows for Tomographic $N(z)$ Reconstruction with DESI DR1

RelatedZhuo Chen, Antoine Rocher, Kwan Chuen Chan, Jean-Paul Kneib
arXiv:2611.00121 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

In wide-field imaging surveys like Euclid and LSST, cosmological analyses of weak lensing and galaxy clustering rely on grouping hundreds of millions of galaxies into broad redshift slices ("tomographic bins") using broadband colours (photometric redshifts, or photo-$z$'s). Because colour-redshift degeneracies often make an individual galaxy's redshift probability density function (PDF) asymmetric or multi-peaked, simply assigning a single best-guess redshift per galaxy biases the overall redshift distribution $N(z)$ of each bin. The authors apply conditional Normalizing Flows (NFs)—generative neural networks that smoothly deform a simple Gaussian into complex, multi-modal probability distributions via invertible transformations—to estimate full photo-$z$ PDFs for over 3 million galaxies and quasars cross-matched between DESI DR1 spectroscopy and Legacy Surveys DR10 photometry. By stacking these continuous NF posteriors and applying a post-hoc statistical recalibration, they reconstruct tomographic $N(z)$ profiles across independent sky patches significantly more accurately than standard tree-based machine learning baselines (Random Forests and Quantile Random Forests).

Main conclusions
  • For single-object point estimates at low redshift ($0 < z_{\rm spec} < 1.6$), the Normalizing Flow achieves the lowest normalized scatter ($\sigma_{\rm NMAD} = 0.0223 \pm 0.0002$) and catastrophic outlier rate ($3.79\%$), while at high redshift ($z_{\rm spec} > 1.6$) it beats both tree-based baselines across all metrics ($\sigma_{\rm NMAD} = 0.1015$, outlier fraction $25.74\%$).
  • When stacking individual PDFs to reconstruct tomographic redshift bins, the NF reproduces the true spectroscopic distribution shapes, widths, and extended tails better than QRF, especially in the sparse highest-redshift bin ($z_{\rm median} > 2.5$) where it slashes the absolute mean-redshift bias from $0.318$ (QRF) down to $0.060 \pm 0.031$ and raises distribution overlap from $0.787$ to $0.905$.
  • Applying a post-hoc empirical cumulative distribution function (CDF) calibration from a validation sample brings the NF mean-redshift bias across four intermediate bins ($0.2 < z_{\rm median} < 1.0$) down to $0.0016\text{–}0.0019$, meeting the stringent Stage IV Euclid accuracy requirement of $|\Delta z| \lesssim 0.002(1+z)$ on this spectroscopic target sample.
  • The authors caution that while NFs excel at capturing complex redshift uncertainties, run-to-run variance across random seeds and selection biases when transferring models trained on bright spectroscopic targets to deeper, broader photometric survey populations remain key hurdles.
