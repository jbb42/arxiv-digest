Cubic Galileon Gravity on Nonlinear Scales with Hi-COLA for LSST Year 1

arXiv:2611.18211 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: This paper directly aligns with your PhD focus on COLA-based fast simulations, beyond-$\Lambda$CDM power spectrum emulators, and modified gravity predictions for Stage IV surveys.

Many existing modified gravity emulators enforce a standard $\Lambda$CDM expansion background by construction, missing modified background histories present in wider classes of Horndeski scalar-tensor theories. In this work, the authors build a 5-parameter Gaussian Process emulator for the 3D non-linear matter power spectrum boost in Cubic Galileon gravity (restricted to its background tracker solution) using the fast simulation code Hi-COLA. This model self-consistently accounts for modifications to background expansion, scale-independent linear growth, and Vainshtein screening on non-linear scales via a single gravity parameter $f_{\phi}$. The emulator achieves high precision relative to COLA simulations and moderate agreement with full N-body simulations, demonstrating that COLA-based emulators with conservative scale cuts can successfully support Stage IV $3\times 2$pt parameter inference.

Main conclusions
  • Developed a 5D ($\Omega_{m0}, n_s, A_s, h, f_{\phi}$) Gaussian Process emulator for the Cubic Galileon matter power spectrum boost $P_{\rm CuGal}/P_{\Lambda\text{CDM}}$ using Hi-COLA simulations.
  • Achieved $\lesssim 2\%$ emulation error relative to Hi-COLA for $k \le 1.2\,h\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$ near fiducial parameters, with errors remaining $\lesssim 5\%$ near parameter space boundaries.
  • Found that the primary source of small-scale modeling error stems from the COLA particle-mesh approximation rather than Gaussian Process interpolation, showing a $\sim 5\%$ discrepancy at $k \approx 1.2\,h\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$ relative to high-resolution EFT-RAMSES N-body runs.
  • Verified via simulated LSST Y1 $3\times 2$pt forecasts that applying conservative scale cuts ($k_{\rm max} = 0.2\,h\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$) yields unbiased parameter estimation even when validated against full N-body benchmark data.

Abstract

Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) is in a prime position to test Modified Gravity (MG) with Weak Lensing (WL) and Large Scale Structure (LSS). However, many nonlinear MG emulators in the literature enforce a $\Lambda$CDM background by design, missing where data suggest the most interesting deviations lie. Simple multi-interacting fluids in SymBoltz

Why: The paper directly addresses the development of fast, accurate, and flexible tools for beyond-ΛCDM cosmologies, which is central to the student's work on emulators and modified gravity simulations.

This paper presents a new framework within the Einstein–Boltzmann solver SymBoltz for implementing arbitrary interactions between baryons, dark matter, and dark energy. Unlike traditional Boltzmann codes like CLASS or CAMB, which often require complex, model-specific approximation schemes (e.g., tight-coupling or radiation streaming) to handle the stiffness of interacting equations, SymBoltz uses a symbolic-numeric interface to generate an analytical Jacobian and an efficient implicit ODE solver. The authors demonstrate that once a model is defined covariantly via a transfer four-vector, it can be implemented in SymBoltz with minimal code changes, enabling the study of complex, multi-species interacting cosmologies that are otherwise difficult to prototype.

Main conclusions
  • SymBoltz version 2 introduces a flexible background integration framework that supports mixed forward and backward integration, allowing for stable solutions of interacting models without manual shooting methods.
  • The framework successfully reproduces results from modified versions of CLASS for single-interaction models, with power spectrum agreement typically within 0.1%–1%.
  • The code allows for the implementation of simultaneous interactions between all three species (baryons, dark matter, dark energy) in approximately 300 lines of code, significantly reducing the boilerplate required compared to traditional Einstein-Boltzmann solvers.

Abstract

We implement a simple and general way of specifying arbitrary interactions between baryons, dark matter and dark energy in the Einstein–Boltzmann code SymBoltz. Due to the symbolic-numeric interface and approximation-free structure of SymBoltz, the model definition is remarkably compact and convenient to modify by specifying only the covariant coupling. The full equations are stiff, but stable and fast to integrate with an efficient implicit solver, particularly with the sparse and analytical ODE Jacobian generated from the symbolic equations. We first validate it using single-interaction models, comparing their background evolution and matter and CMB power spectra with those from modified versions of CLASS, finding $0.1\%$–$1\%$ agreement at most scales. Finally, we show how our framework makes it trivial to implement models with any interactions of any complexity among all three species simultaneously, which requires a more involved implementation in other Einstein–Boltzmann codes like CLASS or CAMB. Our full model is specified in only around 300 lines of code and is easy to modify for any other interaction by just specifying it in three lines.

WebON is a new computational framework designed to generate large-scale mock catalogues for spectroscopic surveys like DESI by mapping the evolving cosmic web directly onto the observer's past lightcone. Unlike traditional methods that stitch together discrete simulation snapshots or replicate periodic boxes, WebON uses a continuous-redshift correction applied to a limited set of approximate gravity (ALPT/eALPT) displacement fields to compute particle positions in a single forward pass. This approach provides a phase-coherent matter backbone that, when combined with the HOLI (Higher Order LIghtcone) strategy, allows for the efficient generation of tracer populations using hierarchical cosmic-web conditioning and subgrid bias models. The method is highly relevant for producing the large ensembles of mocks required for covariance estimation and systematic testing in modern LSS surveys.

Main conclusions
  • WebON enables the construction of full-sky matter lightcones without periodic-box replication or snapshot stitching, achieving percent-level consistency in angular power spectra.

  • The HOLI strategy leverages a spectral hierarchy of cosmic-web operators to recover tracer clustering beyond the resolution limits of the underlying approximate matter solver.
  • The modular design allows the same matter lightcone to be reused for different tracer calibrations, significantly reducing the computational cost of generating large mock ensembles.

Abstract

Wide-area spectroscopic surveys such as DESI require large ensembles of mock catalogues that follow gravitational evolution and tracer populations across the observer's past lightcone. We present WebON (Web ON the lightcone), a fast method for mapping the evolving dark-matter cosmic web onto the lightcone without periodic-box replication or the stitching of separately evolved snapshot shells. Displacements computed at a limited set of redshifts are combined with a particle-wise Zel'dovich estimate of the emission redshift and a continuous-redshift correction, allowing lightcone positions to be generated in a single forward pass. The method supports regularised ALPT and eALPT gravity solvers, with an optional short-range ridging correction. We illustrate full-sky matter and cosmic-web evolution and find percent-level agreement between lightcone and fixed-redshift angular power spectra over the tested redshift and multipole ranges, providing an internal consistency check of the lightcone construction. The matter bispectrum is a powerful probe of non-Gaussianity and gravity, but its theoretical prediction is computationally expensive, often necessitating the use of emulators. This paper introduces bihalofit-JAX, a fully differentiable, GPU-accelerated implementation of the BiHalofit fitting formula for the nonlinear matter bispectrum and its projected statistics (e.g., weak lensing, skew spectra, and aperture statistics). By utilizing fixed-grid quadrature and Newton-based root-finding optimized for JAX, the authors achieve speed-ups of up to four orders of magnitude compared to existing implementations. This tool enables gradient-based inference and eliminates the need for emulators in many bispectrum-based cosmological analyses.

Main conclusions
  • The code computes 10,000 bispectrum triangles in ~1–2 ms, which is 1–3 orders of magnitude faster than previous implementations.
  • The pipeline is fully differentiable, allowing for gradient-based sampling (e.g., NUTS) in high-dimensional cosmological inference.
  • It supports multiple linear power spectrum backends (baccoemu, syren-new, and Eisenstein & Hu) and is natively vectorized for batch processing of cosmologies and redshifts.
  • The implementation achieves <0.02% agreement with the original BiHalofit code, while providing significant performance gains for projected statistics like third-order aperture statistics.

Abstract

Theoretical predictions for the nonlinear matter bispectrum and related projected statistics have been historically very expensive to evaluate, leading many analyses to rely on emulators to speed up slow numerical codes. We show in this paper that this need not be the case: improved numerical techniques can be leveraged to directly evaluate predictions for the nonlinear matter bispectrum and projected statistics at speeds preferable for inference. The pipeline is fully differentiable, and we demonstrate its performance in a simple inference problem, comparing the No-U-Turn Sampler (NUTS) to gradient-free inference with the affine-invariant ensemble sampler (emcee).

They demonstrate that while standard bremsstrahlung does not lift this suppression due to the conformal invariance of the dark QED stress-energy tensor, the trace anomaly of dark QED provides an alternative production channel via a Dalitz-like process. This mechanism allows for significantly lighter dark fermions, potentially down to $m_\chi \sim 10^7$ GeV, depending on the reheating temperature and the dark gauge coupling.

Main conclusions
  • The minimal gravitational production of dark fermions is helicity-suppressed, requiring $m_\chi \gtrsim 10^{10}$ GeV to match the observed relic abundance.
  • The trace anomaly of dark QED enables a new production channel for dark fermions, effectively bypassing the helicity suppression.

  • Sequential production of dark fermions via dark photon annihilation is found to be sub-leading compared to the anomaly-induced Dalitz process.

Abstract

We revisit the production of fermionic dark matter $\chi$ through the graviton portal, sourced by inflaton annihilation at the end of inflation. Because the production rate is helicity-suppressed, $\propto m_\chi^2$, the dark fermion must be very heavy to account for the observed relic abundance, $m_\chi \gtrsim 10^{10}$ GeV, with the exact bound set by the reheating temperature $T_{\rm RH}$. Here we examine how interactions within the dark sector could modify this result. As a toy model, we consider dark QED, in which $\chi$ couples to a light dark photon. We first show that, as a consequence of classical conformal invariance, bremsstrahlung of a dark photon does not lift the helicity suppression for light fermions. We then show that the trace anomaly of dark QED, which affects the coupling of the graviton to dark photons, can produce light dark fermions via a Dalitz process. This allows to extend the fermionic dark mass range possibly down to $m_\chi \sim 10^7$~GeV, depending on $T_{\rm RH}$ and the dark QED coupling. We also determine under which conditions the hidden sector reaches thermal equilibrium and derive a lower bound on the temperature ratio at reheating $(T'/T)_{\rm RH}$ as function of the dark QED coupling and the reheating temperature.
