Anisotropic Fluid Inhomogeneous Cosmology

Highly relevantcross-listMariano Cadoni, Andrea Cozzumbo, Riccardo Murgia, Matteo Pili, Mirko Pitzalis, Andrea P. Sanna, Matteo Schulz, Ilaria Usai
arXiv:2611.31461 · PDF · HTML · gr-qc, astro-ph.CO
Why: This paper directly develops a new exact GR inhomogeneous cosmology framework (explicitly contrasted with LTB and Buchert backreaction) to explain late-time acceleration without a cosmological constant, matching your core research focus on exact inhomogeneous models and relativistic cosmology.

Standard inhomogeneous General Relativity models—such as Lemaître–Tolman–Bondi (LTB) void models—typically assume pressureless, isotropic dust, which restricts their generality and struggles to explain cosmic acceleration without violating the Copernican principle. In this paper, the authors introduce Anisotropic Fluid Inhomogeneous Cosmology (AFIC), an exact, spherically symmetric GR framework sourced by a fluid with unequal radial and tangential pressures ($p_{\parallel} \neq p_{\perp}$). The entire nonlinear evolution is governed by a single free function—the Misner–Sharp mass $M(r,a)$, which measures the quasi-local mass enclosed within a given radius—and naturally recovers standard spatially flat FLRW cosmology both at early times and on large scales ($r \to \infty$) at late times. If the inhomogeneous part of $M(r,a)$ retains an extensive volume-scaling term ($\propto R^3$) on large scales, an effective dark energy component emerges at late times without entering the local gravitational potential, offering a potential geometric explanation for cosmic acceleration and DESI's hints of dynamical dark energy.

Main conclusions
  • AFIC provides exact, non-perturbative GR solutions parametrized by a single input function $M(r,a)$, contrasting with LTB models (which need three free functions) and avoiding the fitting problem of averaging/backreaction approaches.
  • An effective dark energy term (either a constant $\Lambda$-like term or dynamical $w_{\rm DE}(a)$) emerges in the large-scale FLRW limit from late-time matter inhomogeneities, while cancelling out of the leading-order local metric functions $\alpha$ and $\beta$.
  • In an analytically solvable realization of the model, the effective cosmological constant is determined by the ratio of a constant inhomogeneity mass mode $M_0$ to the large-scale mass quadrupole $Q_{ij}$.
  • Using the linear matter power spectrum on scales $\mathcal{R} \sim 10^2\text{–}10^3\,\text{Mpc}$ to estimate $Q_{ij}$, the authors obtain a present-day effective dark energy density within $1$ to $5$ orders of magnitude of the observed $\Lambda$ value.

Abstract

We propose a new framework for inhomogeneous cosmology based on General Relativity sourced by an anisotropic fluid. The model is parametrized by a single input function, the radial Misner–Sharp mass distribution at different redshifts, and admits exact solutions to Einstein's equations. Going beyond the standard assumption of small metric perturbations, it provides a fully relativistic framework to describe the non-linear regime of cosmic structure formation. The Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) cosmology is recovered both at early times and – on large scales only – at late times. Our Anisotropic Fluid Inhomogeneous Cosmology (AFIC) model provides a natural mechanism through which matter inhomogeneities give rise to late-time acceleration. The extensive term driving this effect does not enter the exact metric solution and therefore does not contribute to the local gravitational potential. Rather, it emerges as a genuinely large-scale effect induced by the inhomogeneous distribution of matter at late times. This mechanism may provide a physical interpretation of the results from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI). Finally, we present a simple analytical realization of the model and use it to provide an estimate of the present-day effective dark energy density.

Abstract

Dust is an important systematic in precision Type Ia supernova (SN Ia) cosmology, and while dust in the Milky Way and SN host galaxies has been extensively studied, the impact of potential intergalactic dust is less well constrained. Using estimates from B. Ménard et al. (2010), we find that current intergalactic dust bounds could shift SN distance moduli by up to 0.02 mag at $z \gtrsim 1$ from simulations. However, we show dust models can be constrained independently by SN samples themselves via a specific predicted redshift-dependent evolution observed in SN colors. While current data from the Dark Energy Survey (DES) cannot distinguish between various amounts of intergalactic dust in our universe, we demonstrate that the Roman Space Telescope (Roman) will place a significant constraint on intergalactic dust models ($>$ 3$\sigma$). In contrast, the Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST), even with its unprecedented statistics, will only reach $\lesssim$ 2$\sigma$ significance on any given intergalactic dust prediction relative to no dust. We propagate multiple parameterizations of intergalactic dust through to cosmology. Why: While focused on primordial non-Gaussianity rather than modified gravity, the paper presents a method for deriving LSS bias parameter priors using empirical subhalo models (SHAMe) and separate-universe simulations.

Large-scale structure constraints on local primordial non-Gaussianity ($f_{\mathrm{NL}}^{\mathrm{loc}}$) rely heavily on knowing the scale-dependent galaxy bias parameter $b_\phi$, which describes how galaxy formation responds to a long-wavelength gravitational potential fluctuation. Standard analyses often assume an analytical universality relation or fix $b_\phi$ using hydrodynamical simulations, which introduces theoretical bias and neglects parameter uncertainties. The authors introduce a data-driven method to construct informative priors on $b_\phi$ by fitting an empirical galaxy formation model—extended Subhalo Abundance Matching (SHAMe)—to small-scale galaxy clustering and running separate-universe dark-matter-only simulations. Validating their pipeline against full IllustrisTNG hydrodynamical separate-universe simulations across $z=0, 1, 2$, they show that propagating small-scale clustering constraints through dark-matter-only response simulations recovers the true hydrodynamical $b_\phi$ significantly better than traditional models.

Main conclusions
  • Empirical SHAMe modeling coupled with dark-matter-only separate-universe simulations predicts the true hydrodynamical $b_\phi$ with a median error of 10% across redshifts $z \in [0, 2]$.
  • SHAMe outperforms standard benchmarking models: a fixed Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD) model exhibits a 38% median error (because it ignores subhalo population responses to the long-wavelength potential), while the standard universality relation $b_\phi = 2\delta_c(b_1-p)$ exhibits a 23% median error.
  • The residual 10% error in SHAMe stems from central galaxy stellar mass assignment near selection thresholds, showing that galaxy-formation details (such as halo formation history/assembly bias) carry a small fraction of the $\phi$-response not captured by $V_{\mathrm{peak}}$ alone.
  • The methodology provides a general framework for constructing data-informed priors on large-scale perturbation theory/EFT bias parameters using small-scale non-linear measurements.

Recent DESI baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) measurements combined with supernova and cosmic microwave background (CMB) data hint at dynamical dark energy whose equation of state crosses the phantom divide $w = -1$. Abstract
