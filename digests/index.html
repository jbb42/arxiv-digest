Macrocanonical Generator Networks: Data-Efficient Neural Surrogates for Amortized Physics Simulation

Highly relevantcross-listNiall Jeffrey, Benjamin D. Wandelt
arXiv:2611.05157 · PDF · HTML · cs.LG, astro-ph.CO, stat.ML
Why: The paper directly addresses the development of fast, data-efficient neural surrogates for cosmological simulations, which is central to the student's work on emulators and beyond-ΛCDM modelling.

This paper introduces 'macrocanonical generator networks,' a neural framework for creating fast, data-efficient surrogate simulators for stationary physical processes like cosmological density fields or turbulent flows. Unlike traditional generative models that require large datasets, this approach uses the statistical stationarity of physical fields to learn from as few as a single simulation realization. By training a generator to match multiscale statistics (such as wavelet phase harmonics) in expectation rather than per-sample, the method amortizes the cost of iterative simulation into a single forward pass. The authors prove that their training dynamics, specifically using residual architectures initialized near the identity, preserve local entropy and symmetry, effectively acting as a fast, diversity-preserving surrogate for expensive N-body simulations.

Main conclusions
  • Macrocanonical generators can be trained on as little as a single simulation realization, leveraging spatial stationarity to match target statistics.
  • The method achieves inference speeds more than $10^6$ times faster than reference simulators and $10^3$ times faster than iterative microcanonical synthesis.
  • Residual parameterizations initialized near the identity are critical to preventing mode collapse and maintaining the statistical diversity required for simulation-based inference.
  • Synthetic pretraining using these surrogates significantly improves the performance of neural density estimators in dark energy inference tasks where the real simulation budget is limited.

Abstract

Many scientific simulations are computationally expensive, limiting their use in simulation-based inference, uncertainty quantification, and decision making. We introduce macrocanonical generator networks, a framework for learning fast, data-efficient neural surrogates for amortized simulation of stationary physical processes by matching multiscale statistics of a target process. Inspired by microcanonical maximum-entropy synthesis, which matches feature statistics per sample by inner-loop optimization, our approach instead trains a neural generator to satisfy the same constraints in expectation, preserving physically realistic sample-to-sample fluctuations. We prove that gradient-descent training induces preconditioned gradient-descent dynamics in sample space that inherit the symmetry-preservation properties of the microcanonical gradient descent of Bruna and Mallat, amortizing its per-sample inner-loop optimization into a single training run after which every sample is generated in one forward pass. We further show that residual generator parameterizations initialized near the identity, trained with a small learning rate, preserve entropy locally during early optimization and mitigate mode collapse. Experiments on cosmology and fluid simulations show that macrocanonical generators recover realistic samples from extremely small training sets, in some cases a single training example, while generating new realizations more than $10^6$ times faster per sample than the reference simulator and more than $10^3$ times faster than microcanonical gradient descent. The trained generators can be reused for downstream pretraining, including simulation-based inference, providing a data-efficient route to multi-fidelity scientific machine learning workflows.

The formation of the first cosmic structures in hybrid Milgromian cosmology

RelevantTomas Ondro, Nikolaos Samaras
arXiv:2611.03770 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: The paper uses hydrodynamical simulations to test beyond-$\Lambda$CDM gravity and dark sector models, which is highly relevant to your work on Euclid-era non-linear theory and beyond-$\Lambda$CDM simulation emulators.

This paper investigates the structure formation of 'hybrid' cosmological models that replace Cold Dark Matter (CDM) with a light sterile neutrino (acting as Hot Dark Matter) while replacing Newtonian gravity with Milgromian Dynamics (MOND). The authors perform hydrodynamical simulations using the RAMSES and Phantom of RAMSES codes to compare the matter power spectrum $P(k)$ and structure formation history of these hybrid models against the standard $\Lambda$CDM model. They find that while the sterile neutrino component allows for realistic initial conditions consistent with the CMB, the combination of MONDian gravity and neutrino free-streaming leads to an excess of power at small scales ($k > 10^{-2} \, h \, \text{Mpc}^{-1}$) and a delayed onset of bound structure formation compared to $\Lambda$CDM.

Main conclusions
  • Hybrid $\nu$HDM models produce an excess of power in the matter power spectrum $P(k)$ at late times ($z \lesssim 1$) compared to $\Lambda$CDM across all collapsing scales.
  • The sterile neutrino component acts as Hot Dark Matter, which suppresses early structure formation, creating a tension with recent JWST observations of high-redshift galaxies.
  • The density and velocity fields in hybrid MOND simulations appear more coarse-grained and exhibit higher anisotropy than those in $\Lambda$CDM, reflecting the distinct nature of the modified gravitational law.

Abstract

Tackling cosmology with Milgromian Dynamics (MOND) has been a challenge ever since its theoretical inception. In a hybrid MOND scheme, a hypothesized light sterile neutrino of $\approx$ 11 eV/$c^2$ rest-mass is able to provide realistic initial conditions, fitting the third peak of the Planck CMB power spectrum as a Hot Dark Matter (HDM) component. At the same time, MOND governs the gravitational dynamics of density perturbations, while the background expansion follows the standard FLRW metric. We conduct and analyze hydrodynamical simulations of the fiducial $\Lambda$CDM model ($H_0 = 67.8$ km/s/Mpc, $\Omega_m \approx 0.31$, $\Omega_{\text{CDM}} \approx 0.26$), the progenitor $\nu$HDM model ($H_0 = 67.64$ km/s/Mpc, $\Omega_m = 0.3, \Omega_{\nu}\approx 0.26$), and its optimized version (opt-$\nu$HDM model, $H_0 \approx 55.6$ km/s/Mpc, $\Omega_m \approx 0.49$, $\Omega_{\nu} \approx 0.42$), utilizing the publicly available codes RAMSES and Phantom of RAMSES. We compare for the first time the total matter power spectrum $P(k)$ of the hydrodynamical simulations at various redshifts, examining the systematic differences of the three investigated cosmologies. The redshift evolution of the power spectrum of the $\Lambda$CDM model compared to the $\nu$HDM variants' counterpart offers important insight about the onset of structure formation. The combination of a light sterile neutrino and MOND gravity exceeds the $\Lambda$CDM model's matter $P(k)$ over all collapsing scales at late times ($z\lesssim 1$). The density and velocity fields reveal a distinct difference between the underlying laws of gravity. Regardless of the modeled dependence in the jointly estimated data from Planck, DES Y1, SDSS, and eBOSS, the $\nu$HDM hybrid models seem to produce an excess of power at associated resolution scales due to their cosmological design.

Abstract

Mapping the three-dimensional distribution of dark matter is key to tracing the growth of cosmic structure. Two-dimensional weak lensing mass maps recover its projected distribution, but they blend structures at different distances along the line of sight. We reconstruct the three-dimensional matter distribution in the 0.54 deg$^2$ COSMOS-Web field using deep James Webb Space Telescope imaging, with an effective source density of 121 galaxies arcmin$^{-2}$ We combine a physical weak lensing forward model with a diffusion-based Bayesian generative prior learned from cosmological simulations, yielding a posterior distribution of the matter field across 20 lens planes to $z \simeq 2$, doubling the redshift reach of previous Hubble Space Telescope-based 3D reconstructions and approaching cosmic noon, an epoch of rapid galaxy growth. These results extend three-dimensional weak lensing mapping into the high-redshift, non-Gaussian regime and establish a route towards probabilistic mapping of the evolving cosmic web with current wide-field lensing surveys.

Reconstructing the Large-Scale Velocity Field from DESI DR1 with Bayesian Inference

RelevantRaeed Mundow, Adi Nusser
arXiv:2611.04830 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: The Bayesian reconstruction and Zel'dovich forward modeling techniques are highly relevant to the student's work on fast approximate simulations (like COLA) and non-linear theory predictions for Euclid.

Reconstructing the cosmic velocity field is crucial for testing gravity and understanding the growth of structure, but it is complicated by survey geometry and non-linearities. This paper presents a Bayesian framework to infer the initial density field from the DESI DR1 Bright Galaxy Survey (BGS) using two different forward models: linear perturbation theory and the Zel'dovich approximation (a first-order Lagrangian perturbation theory that accounts for the displacement of matter). The authors produce Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) density and velocity maps, validating them against independent peculiar velocity measurements from the Cosmicflows-4 catalog and testing their impact on Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) reconstruction. This work provides a high-fidelity map of the local universe's dynamics out to $z \sim 0.5$, offering a benchmark for future tests of the $\Lambda$CDM model and modified gravity.

Main conclusions
  • Both linear and Zel'dovich reconstructions successfully recover coherent large-scale velocity patterns, showing strong agreement with independent Cosmicflows-4 radial velocity data.
  • The reconstructed displacement fields successfully sharpen the BAO feature in the DESI BGS sample, with the Zel'dovich model providing a slightly more pronounced peak than linear theory.
  • The reflex-dipole profiles (a measure of bulk flow) are consistent between methods but exhibit a deficit at scales $r < 50 \, h^{-1} \, \mathrm{Mpc}$ relative to fiducial $\Lambda$CDM expectations.
  • Survey boundaries and galaxy bias remain the dominant sources of uncertainty, with the reconstruction becoming less reliable within $50 \, h^{-1} \, \mathrm{Mpc}$ of the footprint edges.

Abstract

Survey boundaries and nonlinear dynamics complicate reconstruction of large-scale matter-density and peculiar-velocity fields from galaxy redshift surveys. We develop and compare two reconstruction methods within a common Bayesian framework, based on linear perturbation theory and the Zel'dovich approximation, and apply them to the DESI Data Release 1 Bright Galaxy Survey. We infer the initial density field using a Gaussian prior and likelihood, with the Zel'dovich model displacing particles from a uniform Lagrangian grid. Both yield maximum a posteriori (MAP) fields; only the linear posterior is sampled. Our main findings are: (i) Both reconstructions yield similar large-scale density structures and coherent velocity patterns in the survey interior, beyond roughly \(50\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}\) from the footprint boundaries. (ii) Comparison with Cosmicflows-4 groups, predominantly from the SDSS Fundamental Plane sample in the DESI overlap, shows close agreement between mean observed radial velocities and MAP predictions when binned by the latter, despite substantial individual scatter. (iii) Both yield similar reflex-dipole profiles, defined as mean shell velocities relative to those at randomly sampled centres, out to \(\sim225\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}\). The profiles depend weakly on the prior normalization \(\sigma_8\), but lie below the fiducial linear \(\Lambda\)CDM reference at \(r<50\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}\). Sparse sampling limits the largest radii. (iv) In an exploratory BAO test, shifting galaxies and randoms to a target redshift \(z=8\) using reconstructed displacements sharpens the acoustic feature in both cases, with a slightly more pronounced peak for Zel'dovich. Survey geometry and galaxy bias remain the principal limitations.

Linear perturbation theory and structure formation in a Horndeski theory of gravity without dark matter

RelevantLorenzo Gervani, Antonaldo Diaferio, Francesco Pace
arXiv:2611.05112 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
Why: The paper uses Horndeski theory and linear perturbation theory to test a gravity-only model, which is highly relevant to the student's work on beyond-ΛCDM simulations and modified gravity parametrizations.

This paper investigates whether a broad class of Horndeski scalar-tensor theories can explain cosmological observations without invoking dark matter, specifically by requiring the theory to reduce to 'Refracted Gravity' (RG) in the weak-field limit. RG is a non-relativistic phenomenological model that uses a density-dependent gravitational permittivity to boost gravity on galactic scales, effectively mimicking dark matter. The authors derive the necessary conditions for Horndeski theories to match this RG behavior and analyze the resulting background expansion and linear structure growth. They find that while the model can reproduce the observed expansion history, it fails to produce cosmic structure at the correct times, predicting a late onset of growth ($z < 1$) that contradicts the existence of high-redshift galaxies.

Main conclusions
  • The authors define 'generalized Covariant Refracted Gravity' (gCRG), a class of Horndeski theories that recover the RG weak-field limit.
  • The model successfully reproduces the observed Hubble-Lemaître parameter $H(z)$ evolution.
  • Linear perturbation analysis reveals a structural tension: parameters that fit the background expansion suppress the growth of density fluctuations, delaying structure formation to $z < 1$.
  • The delayed structure formation is a generic feature of this class of theories, not an artifact of specific potential choices, due to the suppression of the gravitational source term by the boost factor $G_4(a)$ at high redshift.

Abstract

We study the formation and evolution of the cosmic large-scale structure (LSS) in a scalar-tensor Horndeski theory of gravity without dark matter. We impose that this theory reduces to Refracted Gravity (RG) in the weak-field limit. RG is a non-relativistic theory where the gravitational permittivity $\epsilon(\rho)$, with $\rho$ the baryonic matter density, provides the gravitational boost required to describe both the dynamics of galaxies and galaxy clusters without dark matter. We show that $\epsilon(\rho)$ equals the function $G_4(\varphi)$ of the scalar field $\varphi$ of our Horndeski model. The background evolution of a flat, matter-dominated, homogeneous and isotropic universe shows that $\varphi$ is also responsible for the accelerated expansion of the universe and that the model is capable of reproducing the observed redshift evolution of the Hubble-Lemaître parameter. We finally develop the linear perturbation theory and study the growth factor equation. The model fails to correctly describe the LSS by predicting a late onset of the formation of cosmic structure at redshift $z\lesssim 1$.

Why: The paper uses FLAMINGO simulations to derive field-level bias models for a new tracer, which is highly relevant to your work on emulators and bias expansion for beyond-ΛCDM models.

Fast Radio Bursts (FRBs) provide a unique probe of the intergalactic medium through their dispersion measure, which tracks the distribution of free electrons. This paper demonstrates that the free electron density field is an exceptionally faithful tracer of the total matter distribution, even on non-linear scales, by analyzing the FLAMINGO hydrodynamical simulations. The authors quantify this relationship using field-level bias parameters and show that the electron distribution can be modeled as a simple, near-linear copy of the matter field. By combining this insight with Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) weak lensing, they perform cosmological forecasts for $\sigma_8$ and $\Omega_m$, showing that this combination provides a robust, galaxy-formation-independent probe of large-scale structure.

Main conclusions
  • The free electron distribution tracks the total matter field with a linear bias $b_e \approx 0.96$ at $z=0.5$, with non-linear bias corrections being subdominant.
  • The electron-matter cross-correlation coefficient remains above 99% across various baryonic feedback models, confirming electrons as a high-fidelity tracer of matter.
  • Joint analysis of FRB dispersion and CMB lensing can constrain $\sigma_8$ and $S_8$ to approximately 3% precision, competitive with current galaxy-lensing-based surveys.
  • This approach is largely insensitive to complex baryonic feedback and galaxy formation physics, making it a clean, complementary probe of the growth of structure.

Latin hypercube sampling to reduce the variance in Monte Carlo integration: a practical guide and an application to gravitational-wave astronomy

Relevantcross-listMatteo Boschini, Davide Gerosa
arXiv:2611.04045 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.IM, astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.HE, gr-qc
Why: The paper discusses variance reduction techniques for high-dimensional Monte Carlo integration, which is highly relevant for the student's work on emulators and approximate N-body simulations where efficient sampling of parameter space is critical.

This paper provides a practical introduction to Latin hypercube sampling (LHS) and its extension, orthogonal-array Latin hypercube sampling (OA-LHS), as techniques to reduce the variance of Monte Carlo integration. These methods improve upon standard random sampling by stratifying the parameter space, which asymptotically removes variance contributions from individual coordinates (LHS) or pairwise interactions (OA-LHS). The authors apply these techniques to the selection function integral in gravitational-wave population inference, finding that while the variance reduction is marginal for this specific high-dimensional problem, the implementation cost is negligible, making it a potentially useful tool for other computationally expensive cosmological or astrophysical simulations.

Main conclusions
  • LHS and OA-LHS are mathematically guaranteed to have an asymptotic variance no greater than standard Monte Carlo estimators.
  • OA-LHS (strength 2) effectively reduces variance for integrands dominated by additive terms and pairwise interactions.
  • In the context of gravitational-wave selection functions, the variance reduction is marginal (~10%) because the integrand is dominated by higher-order interactions (third-order and above).
  • Given the negligible computational overhead, these sampling designs are recommended for future injection campaigns where Monte Carlo noise is a bottleneck.

Abstract

In applied statistics, likelihoods involving high-dimensional integrals are often approximated via Monte Carlo summation. Controlling the resulting numerical variance is crucial for reliable inference. We provide a practical introduction to Latin hypercube sampling (LHS) and its extension based on orthogonal arrays (OA-LHS) as strategies to reduce this variance by stratifying the sampling distribution. Gravitational-wave lensing beyond the diffraction integral: proper-time methods and the bound lens

Relevantcross-listGinevra Braga, Raul Jimenez
arXiv:2611.05152 · PDF · HTML · gr-qc, astro-ph.CO, hep-th
Why: The paper's focus on exact wave-optics solutions and the failure of standard approximations in complex, non-static lensing scenarios is highly relevant to the student's work on beyond-ΛCDM simulations and inhomogeneous cosmology.

This paper provides a rigorous, self-contained derivation of gravitational-wave (GW) lensing using the proper-time (worldline) formalism, which avoids the standard eikonal, paraxial, and thin-lens approximations. By treating the wave equation as a first-order Schrödinger-like equation in an auxiliary proper time, the authors solve the point-mass lens exactly and demonstrate that standard diffraction integrals are merely specific stationary-phase limits of this more general framework. They apply this method to the 'bound lens'—a source orbiting a massive black hole—showing that standard approximations fail significantly near conjunction and on the far side of the orbit, where kinematic effects and backscattered echoes become dominant.

Main conclusions
  • The proper-time representation provides an exact solution for point-mass lensing, showing that the standard diffraction integral is a valid approximation only under specific geometric and frequency conditions.
  • For a bound lens, standard approximations (like the paraxial or static-lens models) fail to capture 46–100% of the lensing signal beyond the Shapiro delay at $w=0.3$.
  • Geometric optics is shown to be insufficient for $w o 1$ regimes, as it misses frequency-dependent diffraction and phase-modulation effects that are detectable with current and future interferometers.
  • The authors demonstrate that for $w imes y o 0$ (near conjunction), the wave-optics signal is highly sensitive to the exact orbital dynamics, making it a potential probe for compact binaries near supermassive black holes.

Abstract

Gravitational waves (GWs) can have wavelengths comparable to the Schwarzschild radius of their lenses, and detectors record their phase, so lensing requires wave optics. Its standard tool, the diffraction integral, follows from the particle path integral of a scalar wave on a static weak field, under the eikonal (one-way) and paraxial approximations, plus the thin-lens limit. We give a self-contained account of how the proper-time (worldline) representation overcomes these steps. An auxiliary proper time makes the wave equation exactly first order, with frequency as $1/\hbar$, so geometric optics and, for paraxial thin-lens paths, the diffraction integral follow from stationarity of an exact integral. For a point mass, the Duru-Kleinert (DK) gauge and Kustaanheimo-Stiefel map give the exact propagator; for a distant observer the DK time and fibre angle become the radius and azimuth of a lens plane, the result is the point-lens amplification factor with the offset set by the excess path, and geometric optics requires $w\gg1$ and $wy\gg1$. This agreement reflects the Coulomb potential, not the approximations: the exact eikonal condition, $\varepsilon_E\simeq\bar\lambda/d+GM/c^2d+\mathcal O(\sin^2\theta/2)$, fails near the lens and at large angles, and the one-way step is a non-relativistic limit. We then treat the bound lens, a GW source orbiting its lens, which emits a backscattered echo and moves during the lensing delays. Its exact time-domain signal gives each worldline its own retarded time and Doppler factor, so no single amplification factor describes it. In the diffraction regime the Shapiro phase alone misses all lensing beyond it, geometric optics misses 46-100% of it near conjunction at $w=0.3$, the paraxial geometry fails on the far side, and the static amplification factor along the orbit errs by $\approx(1-2)\,wM/R$ near conjunction and by the echo amplitude elsewhere.

Cosmic birefringence, a rotation of the CMB polarization plane, is a key signature of parity-violating physics but is difficult to measure due to its degeneracy with gravitational lensing curl modes and the high sample variance of lensed B-modes. This paper introduces a joint maximum-a-posteriori (MAP) reconstruction method that simultaneously estimates the lensing gradient, lensing curl, and birefringence fields. By iteratively delensing and de-rotating the CMB maps, the authors effectively remove the lensing-induced noise that limits traditional quadratic estimators. This approach significantly improves the sensitivity to birefringence, with predicted constraints on the birefringence amplitude $A_{ m CB}$ improving by up to a factor of 5 for deep surveys like SPT-3G+.

  • The joint MAP reconstruction reduces the error on the birefringence amplitude $A_{ m CB}$ by a factor of up to 5 compared to standard quadratic estimators at $1 \mu\text{K-arcmin}$ noise levels.
  • The improvement is primarily driven by the removal of lensing-induced B-mode sample variance and the suppression of the $N^{(1)}$ bias, which accounts for nearly 50% of the noise in standard estimators at high sensitivity.
  • The method successfully marginalizes the degeneracy between the lensing curl and cosmic birefringence, providing an unbiased inference of the birefringence signal.

Measuring the ultraviolet background at $z\simeq3$ from the quasar proximity effect in DESI DR1

Patrick Shaw, Rupert A. C. Croft
arXiv:2611.04316 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

Abstract

Quasi-stars, massive envelopes surrounding rapidly accreting black hole (BH) seeds, are promising candidates for explaining the rapid formation of supermassive black holes in the early Universe and may account for some of the JWST-discovered Little Red Dots (LRDs). We present a self-consistent framework combining stellar evolution calculations with non-local thermodynamic equilibrium (NLTE) spectral synthesis to test this connection. Our models drop the polytropic assumptions of previous work: the envelope is described entirely by mixing-length theory, with saturated convection and radiative energy setting the luminosity at an inner boundary a few hundred Schwarzschild radii from the BH. This removes the BH-mass limits of earlier models, and we follow the evolution up to BH-to-total mass ratios approaching unity. We then use TLUSTY to compute the first converged NLTE atmospheric models for the surface conditions and primordial hydrogen-helium composition expected in quasi-stars. Our models produce cool photospheres, prominent Balmer breaks, and Balmer and Paschen absorption features. NLTE effects substantially modify line strengths, increasing Balmer absorption depths by $\sim 35-55\%$ relative to LTE models, while having a minor effect on the continuum. Comparison with observed LRD spectral energy distributions shows broad agreement in continuum shape, although our models do not reproduce their commonly observed strong emission lines. Under the quasi-star interpretation, comparisons with individual LRDs suggest characteristic quasi-star masses of approximately $10^5-10^7 M_\odot$. These results establish a physically motivated connection between quasi-star evolution and observable spectra, providing a framework for testing quasi-stars as progenitors of LRDs while identifying limitations of current atmospheric models.
