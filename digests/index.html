The Universality of Halo Growth and Merger Rates in Modified Gravity Theories

Highly relevantHaicong Xu, Yechun Yong, Feihong He, Zhongxu Zhai, Jiaxin Han, Baojiu Li
arXiv:2611.25472 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
Why: The paper directly addresses modified gravity simulations (f(R), nDGP) and the consistency of halo/subhalo definitions, which is central to your work on beyond-ΛCDM emulators and simulation methodology.

This paper investigates whether the hierarchical growth and merger rates of dark matter haloes, which are known to be nearly universal in $\Lambda$CDM, remain universal in modified gravity (MG) scenarios. Using the ELEPHANT suite of $N$-body simulations, the authors compare the Hu–Sawicki $f(R)$ and nDGP models against GR. While halo mass accretion histories show systematic, model-dependent deviations from GR—with growth accelerated by the fifth force—the halo merger rates exhibit a remarkable degree of universality across all tested gravity models. The authors conclude that this universality is a robust feature of gravitational clustering, provided that halo masses are defined consistently with the underlying gravity model.

Main conclusions
  • Halo mass accretion histories show significant deviations from GR in MG models, with growth rates increasing by up to 15% in strong $f(R)$ scenarios.
  • Halo merger rates are largely independent of the gravity model, confirming that the 'unbiased accretion' framework holds even when the gravitational force law is modified.
  • Apparent violations of merger rate universality can arise from inconsistent halo mass definitions, specifically when using Newtonian-based subhalo finders that neglect the fifth force in MG simulations.
  • The double-Schechter function provides a robust fit for the specific merger rate across the gravity models tested.

Abstract

In the standard $\Lambda$CDM framework, the merger rate of dark matter haloes has been found to follow a nearly universal form, with only weak dependence on halo mass and redshift. We examine whether this universality is preserved in modified gravity (MG) models, where the growth of cosmic structure can deviate from that of general relativity (GR). We use the ELEPHANT suite of large $N$-body simulations to study halo merger rates and mass accretion histories in different modified gravity models, including two representative scenarios beyond-GR: the Hu–Sawicki chameleon $f(R)$ model and the normal branch of the Dvali–Gabadadze–Porrati model. For the mass accretion histories, the modified gravitational effect can produce systematic departures from GR, generally corresponding to more rapid halo growth. The deviations are relatively small when the MG effect is weak, while they become more pronounced in models with strong deviation from GR and can exceed $15\%$ in the extreme case. For the halo merger rate, we find that the nearly universal behavior is well preserved across all the gravity models, with only weak dependence on redshift and halo mass, reflecting the unbiased accretion nature of halo growth. However, apparent deviation from this universality can arise if the halo mass is not defined consistently with the modified gravity model.

We show that the traditional approach of parameterizing the background and linear-perturbation evolutions, through the equation-of-state parameter $w(a)$ and a time-dependent sound speed squared $\bar{c}^2_s(a)$, does not fully specify the cosmological behavior of dark energy theories. This paper investigates the use of simulation-based inference (SBI) to reconstruct the parameters of a first-order electroweak phase transition (EWPT) from stochastic gravitational-wave background (SGWB) signals expected at LISA. Because the mapping from fundamental model parameters to the GW spectrum is computationally expensive and plagued by degeneracies, traditional MCMC methods are often inefficient. The authors demonstrate that neural density estimators can learn an amortized posterior, providing near-instantaneous parameter reconstruction after training. They further show that these SBI posteriors can serve as highly efficient proposals for importance sampling, combining the speed of neural networks with the rigorous accuracy of likelihood-based methods.

Main conclusions
  • SBI provides reliable parameter reconstruction for both thermodynamic transition parameters and BSM model parameters (cxSM) at negligible cost per data set after training.
  • SBI posteriors act as excellent proposals for importance sampling with the exact likelihood, achieving sampling efficiencies of 5–90%, compared to 10⁻³–10⁻⁷ for uniform prior sampling.
  • The reconstruction is primarily limited by intrinsic degeneracies in the GW spectral templates rather than the inference methodology itself.
  • Jointly inferring the bubble wall velocity $v_w$ with model parameters is more precise than model-independent reconstruction, as the model constraints break degeneracies.

Abstract

The detection of a stochastic gravitational-wave background (SGWB) from a cosmological first- order phase transition would be unambiguous evidence for physics beyond the Standard Model, with a strong electroweak phase transition (EWPT) at LISA as a prime example. Reconstructing the underlying model from such a signal is challenging, since the map from the model parameters to the spectrum is degenerate and strong transitions occupy only small and sparse regions of parameter space. We show that simulation-based inference (SBI), a likelihood-free approach applicable to generic SGWB sources, provides a reliable parameter reconstruction despite these difficulties. We first apply SBI to the thermodynamic parameters characterizing the transition, namely its strength, inverse duration, temperature and bubble wall velocity. As a concrete application, we then consider a strong EWPT in a singlet extension of the Standard Model and reconstruct the model parameters from the resulting SGWB. We find that the SBI posteriors agree with those of a likelihood-based Markov chain Monte Carlo analysis, at negligible cost per data set once the network has been trained. Using the network posterior as the proposal for importance sampling with the exact likelihood further corrects its remaining deviations, at a sampling efficiency several orders of magnitude higher than sampling from the prior. SBI thus offers a fast and flexible framework for the cosmological science case of LISA

Abstract

We present high-resolution cosmological simulations of individual cosmic sheets ($z=3-6$) using the moving-mesh code AREPO to study cold gas ($T\approx10^4\rm ~K$) in the diffuse cosmic web. We find cosmic sheet gas forms a fragmented morphology, evident as kiloparsec-scale variations in the neutral hydrogen column density ($N_{\rm HI}$). Fragmentation arises from the bending and disruption of the cold-dense sheet core into a multiphase medium. Nonlinear thin shell instability (NTSI) and later shear-induced vorticity drive this bending, rather than previously suggested thermal or Richtmyer-Meshkov instabilities. Higher resolution enhances fragmentation. Across a fourfold range in spatial resolution, $N_{\rm HI}$ covering fractions increase by factors of $\approx 1.5-3.5$ for partial Lyman-limit systems (pLLS) and Lyman-limit systems (LLS). This paper establishes a rigorous renormalization framework for galaxy bias in the presence of primordial non-Gaussianity (PNG) of arbitrary order. By analyzing the multi-soft limits of primordial $n$-point functions, the authors demonstrate that the ultraviolet sensitivity of biased tracers is controlled by the soft configurations of initial conditions, which dictates the necessary counterterms to ensure the bias basis remains closed under renormalization. They provide explicit one- and two-loop calculations for deterministic and stochastic bias operators up to fifth order, deriving the corresponding renormalization group (RG) equations. This work provides a systematic organizing principle for LSS modeling, connecting inflationary physics directly to the running of bias parameters in the non-linear regime.

Main conclusions
  • Renormalization of bias operators is determined by the multi-soft limits of primordial correlators, where $L$-loop contributions probe primordial $n$-point functions with $L+1$ hard legs.
  • Two-loop renormalization of the extended bias basis reduces to two distinct topological structures, $[22]$ and $[3]$, which can be described by two universal functions, simplifying higher-loop computations.
  • The authors derive the RG flow for bias parameters, showing that hierarchical contributions at two loops cancel against weighted one-loop-squared terms, ensuring consistent matching.
  • The framework is general and applicable to various inflationary models, including those with massive scalar exchange (quasi-single-field inflation) or higher-spin PNG.

Primordial non-Gaussianity (PNG) leaves an imprint on large-scale structure through a scale-dependent galaxy bias, and properly incorporating the effects of higher order PNG into the bias expansion requires a consistent renormalization framework that extends beyond the squeezed limit of the bispectrum. UV Bounds on Inflationary Parity Violation

Relatedcross-listAlexander Cassem, Mark P. Hertzberg
arXiv:2611.26594 · PDF · HTML · hep-th, astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-ph

This paper investigates the theoretical feasibility of detecting parity violation in the large-scale structure of the Universe, specifically signatures originating from the early universe. Parity violation, which would manifest as an asymmetry in the four-point correlation function (trispectrum) of density fluctuations, can be modeled during inflation using the dynamical Chern-Simons (dCS) operator, a leading-order correction to General Relativity. The authors evaluate whether a detectable signal can exist within the bounds of consistent Effective Field Theories (EFTs) by examining UV completions such as chiral fermions and string theory compactifications. They find that the requirements for a signal to be large enough for future surveys like Euclid to detect are in strong tension with the mathematical consistency of these UV models and existing constraints on the tensor-scalar-scalar (TSS) bispectrum.

Main conclusions
  • Detecting parity violation in the trispectrum requires a parity-odd amplitude $\Pi_c$ that is significantly larger than what is allowed by standard UV completions.
  • For chiral fermion UV completions, the parity-violating parameter is constrained to $\Pi_c < 5.9 \times 10^{-3}$, which is likely below the threshold for future detection.
  • String-theoretic completions (Heterotic and Type IIB) similarly restrict $\Pi_c$ to very small values to maintain perturbativity and EFT validity.
  • Existing bounds on the tensor-scalar-scalar (TSS) bispectrum further squeeze the parameter space, making a detectable signal highly fine-tuned or physically inconsistent.
  • A detectable signal would require an extreme and currently unmotivated enhancement of the TSS vertex.

Abstract

A question of fundamental interest is whether parity is violated on large scales in the Universe. This possibility has been motivated by recent analyses of the four-point matter correlation function. It has also been suggested that detectable parity violation could be generated during inflation by the dynamical Chern-Simons operator, a leading effective field theory correction to general relativity. In this work, we examine this possibility in realistic UV completions, including fermionic and string-theoretic examples. We show that existing bounds on the tensor-scalar-scalar (TSS) bispectrum strongly constrain the possibility of generating a detectable parity-odd trispectrum. For tensor-to-scalar ratios near current observational limits, detectability is incompatible with, or pushes against, the regime of validity of these UV completions. A detectable signal can remain possible for a sufficiently small tensor-to-scalar ratio, but only at the cost of an extremely large enhancement of the TSS vertex. Thus, while a future search for large scale parity violation is worth pursuing, generating a detectable signal within a consistent effective theory is highly nontrivial.
