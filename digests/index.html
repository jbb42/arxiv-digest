The unified bound dark energy–bound dark matter model confronted with DESI DR2, ACT and SPT-3G, and the Unite supernovae measurements

RelevantJose Agustin Lozano Torres, Axel de la Macorra
arXiv:2611.13567 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: Directly relevant to your secondary interests in dynamical dark energy and joint cosmological likelihoods (DESI DR2, CMB, and SNe Ia), presenting a field-theory alternative to w0waCDM that avoids phantom crossing and addresses the S8 tension via power spectrum suppression.

Recent DESI BAO data combined with CMB and supernovae show hints of dynamical dark energy, but phenomenological fits like w0waCDM require dark energy to cross into the unphysical "phantom" regime (w < -1) in the past without providing an underlying physical mechanism. The authors test an alternative, unified dark sector model where dark energy and dark matter originate from a single supersymmetric SU(3) gauge group that undergoes confinement (condensation) at an energy scale Λc ~ 40 eV, producing a light scalar meson that drives late-time cosmic acceleration (bound dark energy) and heavier bound states that act as dark matter with an initial formation velocity vc (bound dark matter). Confronting this model with DESI DR2 BAO, high-resolution CMB data (Planck, ACT DR6, SPT-3G), CMB lensing, and the Unite supernova compilation, they find it reproduces the DESI expansion history through physical "thawing" (w >= -1) rather than a phantom past, all while having no extra free dark energy parameters once Λc is specified by gauge unification. Furthermore, the free-streaming of the relativistic bound dark matter naturally suppresses small-scale clustering, easing the S8 tension.

Main conclusions
  • Excellent fit to DESI without phantom crossing: The model reduces χ²_BAO for DESI DR2 by 20% compared to ΛCDM (fitting BAO better than w0waCDM) via a purely thawing equation of state (w0 = -0.9267 ± 0.0003, wa = -0.847 ± 0.004) that stays strictly non-phantom throughout cosmic history.
  • Physical condensation scale confirmed: The fitted condensation scale Λc = 43.74 ± 0.11 eV matches the theoretical SUSY gauge coupling unification prediction (34^+16_-11 eV), with 94% of the condensation energy funnelled into dark energy (f_BDE = 0.9398 ± 0.0037) and 6% into dark matter.
  • Bound dark matter velocity detected: Small-scale CMB and lensing data constrain the dark matter emergence velocity to vc = 0.37 ± 0.14 (0.066 < vc < 0.60 at 95% CL), ruling out standard cold dark matter (vc -> 0) within this unified framework at 95% CL and yielding a Bayesian preference of ln B = +3.2 over BDE+CDM.
  • Suppression of small-scale structure (S8): Free-streaming of the bound dark matter imposes a matter power spectrum cutoff at k_{1/2} = 1.49 h/Mpc, lowering the structure amplitude to S8 = 0.807 ± 0.007 (σ8 = 0.795 ± 0.005) and predicting a lower growth rate fσ8 at DESI redshifts compared to ΛCDM.
  • Hubble tension remains unchanged: Because the dark energy density is negligible around recombination and leaves the sound horizon unaffected (rd = 147.50 ± 0.17 Mpc), the inferred Hubble constant from BAO+CMB remains low at H0 = 67.22 ± 0.22 km/s/Mpc (5.5σ below SH0ES).

Abstract

The unified bound dark energy-bound dark matter (BDE-BDM) model derives the whole dark sector from a single supersymmetric SU(3) dark gauge group, whose lightest meson is the dark energy and whose heavier bound states, created with a velocity $v_c$ when the group condenses, are the dark matter. Self-Interacting Neutrinos in NEDE and DRMD Cosmologies: Posteriors, profiles and evidence using CLiENT

RelevantMagnus Uggerhøj, Tobias Johannessen, Jeppe Pagaard Thybo, Steen Hannestad, Thomas Tram
arXiv:2611.13756 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: Relevant to the student's profile under Hubble tension models, cosmological likelihoods combining Planck, DESI BAO, and Pantheon+, and neural network emulation techniques for complex beyond-$\Lambda$CDM parameter spaces.

Early-universe solutions to the Hubble tension—such as self-interacting neutrinos ($\text{SI}\nu$), which delay free-streaming and suppress anisotropic stress, or Dark Radiation Matter Decoupling (DRMD) from hot New Early Dark Energy, which alters early expansion and perturbation growth—are almost always analyzed in isolation because exploring combined high-dimensional parameter spaces is computationally prohibitive. In this work, the authors combine $\text{SI}\nu$ and DRMD into a unified 12-parameter beyond-$\Lambda$CDM framework and confront it with combinations of Planck, ACT, SPT, DESI BAO, Pantheon+, and SH0ES data. To overcome severe degeneracies, bimodality, and flat directions that would require tens of core-years of traditional MCMC, they employ the CLiENT likelihood emulator, an active-learning deep neural network trained directly on $\sim 3\times 10^5$ likelihood calls per dataset. They demonstrate that the two mechanisms act complementarily, finding that the combined model can fit aggressive CMB and distance-ladder data significantly better than either extension alone, while illustrating the power of neural likelihood emulation for complex cosmological models.

Main conclusions
  • Synergistic tension relief: When fitted to small-scale CMB data (ACT and SPT) alongside Planck, DESI BAO, Pantheon+, and SH0ES, the combined $\text{SI}\nu+\text{DRMD}$ model yields $H_0 = 70.94 \pm 0.60\text{ km s}^{-1}\text{ Mpc}^{-1}$ and improves the fit over $\Lambda$CDM by $\Delta\chi^2 = -27.6$ (more than the sum of $-9.9$ from DRMD and $-4.0$ from $\text{SI}\nu$ alone), strongly favouring the combination with $\Delta\text{AIC} = -15.7$ relative to DRMD.
  • Physical mechanism of complementarity: At intermediate coupling strengths, self-interacting neutrinos delay free-streaming and alter acoustic driving, allowing for higher dark radiation density ($\Delta N_\mathrm{eff}$) and a higher $H_0$ without requiring the anomalously high primordial spectral index ($n_s$) that DRMD alone needs to match the CMB damping tail.
  • Dataset sensitivity: For conservative data (Planck + DESI + Pantheon+) or when excluding the SH0ES prior, the additional parameter complexity is not justified by the AIC, showing that the preference for combined models is driven by the local distance ladder tension and small-scale CMB.
  • Strongly interacting neutrino mode suppressed: High-multipole ACT and SPT data suppress the strongly interacting neutrino mode ($\log_{10}(G_\mathrm{eff}\,\text{MeV}^2) \sim -1.7$) that was present in Planck-only analyses, favouring intermediate or moderate interaction strengths.
  • Feasibility via likelihood emulation: Using CLiENT (active-learning neural network likelihood emulation) achieved sub-unit log-likelihood RMS precision ($0.09\text{–}0.37$), making an otherwise intractable 12-cosmological-parameter nested sampling run (~76 core-years of exact Boltzmann evaluations) computationally practical.

Stable cosmology under flexible covariances: Rescaling and cross-survey correlations in DESI BAO and supernova data

RelevantEleni-Anna Kolonia, Haoye Li, Hanyu Zhang, Niayesh Afshordi
arXiv:2611.13829 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: This paper tests the robustness of the joint Planck CMB, DESI BAO, and SNe Ia likelihoods favoring dynamical dark energy ($w_0w_a$CDM), directly matching the profile's medium-relevance focus on cosmological likelihood combinations and evolving dark energy tensions.

Recent joint analyses combining the cosmic microwave background (CMB), baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) from DESI, and Type Ia supernovae (SNe) have generated intense interest by favoring dynamical dark energy—where the equation of state evolves as $w(a) = w_0 + (1-a)w_a$ ($w_0w_a$CDM)—at the $\sim 3\text{–}4\sigma$ level over standard $\Lambda$CDM. However, because independent SN compilations (Pantheon+, Union3, and DES-Dovekie) share calibration pipelines and overlapping low-redshift objects, a crucial question is whether this tension is an artifact of underestimated systematic uncertainties or unaccounted cross-survey correlations. To investigate this, the authors introduce a phenomenological framework that projects the three SN compilations onto a common 22-redshift grid and introduces nuisance parameters to rescale survey covariance matrices ($s_X \ge 1$, inflating errors by $\sqrt{s_X}$) and model pairwise inter-survey correlations ($r$). They find that while the data allow error bars up to $\sim 1.7\times$ larger at 95% credibility and reveal a clear correlation ($r \sim 0.4\text{–}0.5$) between Pantheon+ and Union3, the preference for $w_0w_a$CDM remains essentially unchanged ($\Delta\chi^2 = 12.3$ vs $12.8$, or $\sim 3.1\sigma$). Crucially, the cosmological contours merely broaden along existing degeneracies without shifting central values, demonstrating that the evidence for evolving dark energy is robust against this class of covariance misestimation.

Main conclusions
  • Robust preference for evolving dark energy: Allowing covariance rescaling and cross-survey correlations minimally impacts the fit, with the preference for $w_0w_a$CDM over $\Lambda$CDM across the full Planck-lite + DESI DR2 + SN dataset shifting only from $\Delta\chi^2 = 12.8$ to $12.3$ (nominally $3.15\sigma$ to $3.07\sigma$). This indicates that unmodeled covariance errors do not explain away the dynamical dark energy signal.
  • Consistency with published error bars: DESI DR2 BAO, Union3, and DES-Dovekie remain fully consistent with their reported uncertainties (marginalized modes $s_X \approx 1$), while Pantheon+ exhibits a mild preference for added variance ($s_{\rm PP} = 1.46$). At 95% credibility, the data allow 1D error bars up to $\sim 1.7\times$ larger ($\sqrt{s_X} \lesssim 1.7$), but freeing these parameters improves the best fit by $\Delta\chi^2 < 1$.
  • Detected inter-survey supernova correlations: Harmonizing the SN compilations onto a shared 22-bin redshift grid reveals an effective correlation of $r \simeq 0.4\text{–}0.5$ between Pantheon+ and Union3 (reflecting shared low-$z$ supernovae), whereas Union3 and DES-Dovekie show correlations consistent with zero.
  • Broadened but unshifted parameter posteriors: The additional covariance freedom widens the marginalized contours along existing parameter degeneracy lines (e.g. the $w_0$–$w_a$ degeneracy) without shifting central parameter values, confirming that the inferred late-time expansion history is stable.

Combinations of cosmic microwave background (CMB), baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO), and Type Ia supernova (SN) data currently favor evolving dark energy over $\Lambda$CDM at the $\sim 3$–$4\sigma$ level, making it important to test whether this preference could be driven by imperfectly modeled uncertainties. Connecting field-level and n-point function measurements of galaxy bias

RelevantHsiang-Ming Huang, Henrique Rubira, Fabian Schmidt
arXiv:2611.14456 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: While not directly focused on beyond-ΛCDM simulations or COLA, this paper provides a valuable methodology for your Euclid LSS modeling by rigorously connecting lattice/field-level bias priors to standard renormalized perturbation-theory parameters.

In large-scale structure analyses, galaxy bias describes how galaxies trace the underlying dark matter distribution, but different inference methods employ different definitions of bias. Field-level forward modeling fits galaxy density fields directly on a computational grid, which inherently introduces a finite ultraviolet cutoff scale $\Lambda$, whereas traditional $n$-point correlation function analyses define renormalized bias parameters in the large-scale limit ($\Lambda \to 0$). To bridge this gap, the authors use the Wilsonian renormalization group (RG) to compute how bias parameters "run" (evolve) systematically as the cutoff scale $\Lambda$ changes. Using the forward-modeling code LEFTfield with Lagrangian perturbation theory (up to 4LPT) and full $N$-body halo catalogs, they measure the running of linear ($b_\delta$) and quadratic ($b_{\delta^2}, b_{\mathcal{G}_2}$) bias parameters and show that the RG predictions accurately describe the measurements and converge to standard $n$-point values as $\Lambda \to 0$. This establishes a mathematically consistent bridge allowing field-level measurements and simulation-based priors to be translated directly into survey-scale clustering analyses for experiments like Euclid.

Main conclusions
  • The Wilsonian renormalization group (RG) framework accurately predicts the running of linear ($b_\delta$) and second-order ($b_{\delta^2}, b_{\mathcal{G}_2}$) galaxy bias parameters with the smoothing cutoff $\Lambda$, matching field-level measurements in both perturbation theory mocks and full N-body halo catalogs within theoretical error bars.
  • In the limit $\Lambda \to 0$, the finite-cutoff field-level bias measurements smoothly extrapolate to the standard tree-level renormalized bias coefficients measured from $n$-point functions, resolving the scheme difference between the two approaches.
  • A high-order matter forward model is essential: at least 4th-order Lagrangian perturbation theory (4LPT) is required to capture the running of $b_\delta$ at order $(\Delta\sigma^2)^2$ and quadratic bias operators at $\mathcal{O}(\Delta\sigma^2)$, whereas 2LPT and 3LPT miss crucial non-linear displacement couplings.
  • Higher-mass halos exhibit larger bias values and faster RG running with cutoff scale, making them more sensitive to truncated higher-order operators, whereas lower-mass halos run much more slowly and yield smaller theoretical systematic uncertainties.

Abstract

Precise measurements of galaxy bias parameters are essential for extracting cosmological information from galaxy surveys. Field-level analyses infer bias coefficients at a finite cutoff $\Lambda$, typically related to the lattice resolution, while $n$-point function analyses typically adopt renormalization conditions defined in the low-$k$ limit. In this work, we use the renormalization group (RG) to relate field-level measurements at different cutoffs and connect them to $n$-point function measurements. We generate mock galaxy fields with a Lagrangian perturbation theory forward model and a galaxy bias expansion, and then measure the bias parameters across a range of cutoffs. We show that the RG predictions describe the measured running and, in the limit $\Lambda\to 0$, agree with the (tree-level) $n$-point function measurements of first-order $b_\delta$ and second-order $b_{\delta^2}, b_{{G}_2}$. We also measure the running on halo catalogs based on full $N$-body simulations and show that the RG prediction describes the measurements well within the predicted systematic errors. Our results establish the RG flow as a natural formulation for relating galaxy bias measurements at different cutoffs and connecting the schemes used in field-level and $n$-point function analyses.

Less is More: Unbiased Galaxy Clustering from Fewer Merger Trees

RelatedOscar J. Hickman, Sownak Bose, Kai Wang, Carlton M. Baugh, Baojiu Li
arXiv:2611.13083 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

Calibrating semi-analytic models (SAMs)—which populate dark matter halo merger trees from $N$-body simulations with galaxies using simplified baryonic physics prescriptions—against observed galaxy clustering is normally too computationally expensive for MCMC or simulation-based inference, because evaluating the two-point correlation function ($\xi(r)$) at each parameter point requires processing millions of merger trees across an entire simulation box. While one can cut runtime by running the SAM on only a random subset ($m$ out of $k$ partitions) of whole merger trees scattered across the box, doing so naively biases small-scale clustering upwards by a factor of $k/m$: galaxies within the same halo (one-halo pairs) are always kept or discarded together with probability $m/k$, whereas pairs in different haloes (two-halo pairs) require both host trees to be selected with probability $\sim (m/k)^2$. The authors introduce the Sparse and Unbiased Clustering Estimator (SaUCE), which eliminates this bias exactly without free parameters by separately weighting galaxy pairs within the same tree partition and across different partitions by their exact inverse selection probabilities. Validating SaUCE with the GALFORM SAM on the P-Millennium and Millennium simulations, they show it recovers accurate real- and redshift-space clustering at a fraction of the computational cost, making full two-point clustering viable for SAM calibration and simulation-based inference.

Main conclusions
  • Naively running a SAM on a random fraction $m/k$ of merger trees overestimates small-scale clustering by a factor of $k/m$ (up to $\sim 128\times$ in their tests) because intra-halo galaxy pairs are diluted linearly ($m/k$) while inter-halo pairs are diluted quadratically ($\sim (m/k)^2$).
  • The proposed SaUCE estimator applies exact Horvitz–Thompson inverse-probability weights—$w_{\rm auto} = m/k$ for same-partition pairs and $w_{\rm cross} = m(k-1)/[k(m-1)]$ for cross-partition pairs—with zero free parameters, yielding an unbiased two-point correlation function across real space, redshift-space multipoles, and projected statistics.
  • At $z=0$ and a galaxy number density of $\bar{n} = 10^{-2}\,h^3\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$ in the P-Millennium simulation, running GALFORM on just $7.3\%$ of merger trees recovers the full-box $\xi(r)$ within $0.05\,\mathrm{dex}$ ($12\%$) across $0.03 < r < 30\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ ($14\times$ speedup).
  • The required partition fraction increases at higher redshifts and lower number densities (reaching $29\%$ at $z \approx 1.5$, $\bar{n} = 10^{-3}\,h^3\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$, and $96\%$ at $\bar{n} = 10^{-4}\,h^3\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$), and because subsample errors are strongly correlated across radial bins, parameter inference requires using the full bin-to-bin covariance matrix.

Abstract

Galaxy clustering could strongly constrain galaxy formation models but is rarely used, because the two-point correlation function (2PCF) requires a complete galaxy catalogue at every point in parameter space. Semi-analytic models (SAMs) process each halo merger tree independently, so running a SAM on $m$ of $k$ disjoint partitions of the trees, each spanning the full box, cuts the cost by $k/m$. Because a halo's galaxies are kept or discarded together, one-halo pairs are diluted less than two-halo pairs, and the naive estimate overstates small-scale clustering by a factor $k/m$. The required fraction rises with redshift and as the number density falls, is the same for two model variants, and transfers between boxes through the galaxy and pair counts. The scatter, derived in closed form, is strongly correlated between bins, so a likelihood must use the full covariance. A recipe for choosing the fraction makes the 2PCF affordable for model calibration and simulation-based inference.

Fast Scrambling Cosmology

RelatedAliv Sahoo, Fatima Abbasi, Jake McNaughton, Niayesh Afshordi
arXiv:2611.13100 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-th

In quantum gravity, both the trans-Planckian censorship conjecture (which forbids sub-Planckian quantum fluctuations from stretching across the Hubble horizon) and black hole thermodynamics suggest that a de Sitter horizon cannot be eternal, but should instead scramble information and destabilize over a "fast-scrambling" timescale $t_{\rm scr} \sim H^{-1}\ln(M_{\rm Pl}^2/H^2)$. The authors explore whether this horizon clock leaves an observable imprint on cosmic expansion by adding a phenomenological decay/growth term $-\Gamma H^2/\ln(M_{\rm Pl}^2/H^2) \equiv -\sigma H^2$ to the background Raychaudhuri equation for $\dot{H}$. Solving the modified expansion history analytically in the slowly varying $\sigma$ limit, they show that this term acts as an effective dynamical dark energy with two pieces: a slowly evolving vacuum mode and a "memory" integral over the cosmic fluid history that rescales the effective gravitational constant $G_{\rm cosmo}$ in each era. They also construct a covariant scalar-field "clock" action to reproduce this background and obtain preliminary proxy constraints on $\Gamma$ by mapping the fluid-tracking terms onto existing BBN and CMB+DESI BAO+SNe analyses, motivating future perturbation and structure-formation studies.

Main conclusions
  • In the nearly constant $\sigma \equiv \Gamma/\ln(M_{\rm Pl}^2/H^2) \ll 1$ regime, the modified Raychaudhuri equation decomposes into an effective dark energy vacuum mode with equation of state $w_{\rm vac} = -1 + 2\sigma/3$ and tracking terms that rescale the effective gravitational coupling $G_{\rm eff}/G_{\rm N}$ during radiation and matter domination.
  • Mapping these early-time tracking terms onto existing "cosmic glitch in gravity" fits (combining Planck/ACT/SPT CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, and DES SNe) yields a proxy estimate of $\Gamma \simeq -2.8 \pm 0.9$ ($\sigma \simeq -0.0110 \pm 0.0033$, a $\sim 3\sigma$ preference for negative $\Gamma$), while BBN light-element bounds give $\Gamma = -3.7 \pm 6.1$ (68% CL).
  • For $\Gamma < 0$ ($\sigma < 0$), the effective vacuum energy is mildly phantom-like and its background evolution qualitatively tracks the $w_0w_a$CDM trajectory inferred from DESI DR2 at $z \gtrsim 1$, ultimately driving $H$ toward the Planck scale over $\sim 100\,H_0^{-1} \simeq 1.4\text{ Tyr}$.
  • Because the effective dark energy includes an integral over the source history, any change in the equation of state of cosmic species leaves a permanent residual; for example, massive neutrinos becoming non-relativistic shift the late-time dark energy density by $\mathcal{O}(\sigma m_\nu^4)$.
  • A minimal covariant scalar-clock completion of this background suffers from a sub-horizon gradient instability ($c_s^2 < 0$ at all epochs), though adding a Hořava–Lifshitz/cuscuton-type $\Theta^2$ operator regulates short-wavelength instabilities in the dark-energy era.

Millimeter-Wavelength Constraints on Circular Polarization of the Cosmic Microwave Background at Arcminute Scales with the Simons Array

POLARBEAR Collaboration : Kam Arnold, Darcy R. Barron, Matthew R. Chu, Nicole Farias, Takuro Fujino, Masaya Hasegawa, Haruaki Hirose, Kailey Hwang, Ethan H. Ish, Jennifer Ito, Brian Keating, Adrian T. Lee, Anto I. Lonappan, Alec Miyashita, Lindsay Ng Lowry, Luke M. Porter, Shahed Shayan Arani, Praween Siritanasak
arXiv:2611.13345 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.IM

In standard $\Lambda$CDM cosmology, the cosmic microwave background (CMB) exhibits linear polarization ($E$ and $B$ modes) from Thomson scattering at recombination, but virtually zero circular polarization (Stokes $V$ modes, where the electric field vector rotates in a circle). However, a small circular polarization signal could be generated by secondary effects—such as Faraday conversion as CMB photons pass through magnetized relativistic plasmas in galaxy clusters or Population III supernova remnants—or by beyond-Standard-Model physics like primordial magnetic fields and axion-like pseudoscalar fields. In this paper, the POLARBEAR Collaboration places the first arcminute-scale ($300 \leq \ell \leq 2000$) constraints on CMB circular polarization at $90\text{ GHz}$ using five months of data across $\sim 12\text{ deg}^2$ of sky from the Polarbear-2b receiver on the Simons Array. To measure circular polarization with detectors built for linear polarization, they placed a stationary grooved-plastic quarter-wave plate in front of their continuously rotating cryogenic half-wave plate, converting incoming Stokes $V$ signals into linear Stokes $U$ polarization that is cleanly modulated away from low-frequency atmospheric noise. While the resulting upper limits are still orders of magnitude above theoretical expectations due to elevated instrument noise, this work triples the maximum multipole $\ell$ ever probed in $V$ modes and establishes a practical optical and analytical blueprint for measuring circular polarization with existing CMB telescopes.
