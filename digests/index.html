Prospects of Detecting Cosmological Domain Walls in the Cosmic Microwave Background

RelatedBerkin Gürbüz, Robert Brandenberger, Yitian Sun
arXiv:2610.31761 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-ph, hep-th

Domain walls are two-dimensional topological defects resulting from spontaneously broken discrete symmetries, which can evade the Zel'dovich overclosure bound if their symmetry-breaking scale $\eta$ is sufficiently low ($\,\le 100\text{ MeV}$). The authors analytically calculate the density profile generated by the relativistic gravitational repulsion of planar domain walls—an evacuated void bounded by an overdense matter shell—and compute the resulting non-Gaussian lensing deflection imprinted on CMB photons for arbitrarily inclined walls. Because two-point statistics fail to capture these localized, line-like anisotropic deflection patterns, the authors deploy curvelet/wavelet multiscale transforms alongside simulation-based inference (Neural Posterior Estimator and Neural Ratio Estimator) to forecast detection limits for a South Pole Telescope (SPT-3G)-like survey. They demonstrate that machine learning techniques exploiting the full spatial morphology can reach the noise floor of current experiments, significantly enhancing constraints on early-universe symmetry breaking.

Main conclusions
  • Planar domain walls produce a repulsive matter void flanked by a dense shell, creating coherent, non-Gaussian CMB temperature lensing deflections with a two-peak structure that vanishes for face-on orientations.
  • Simulation-Based Inference via Neural Ratio Estimation (NRE) achieves sensitivities down to $\eta \sim 10-100\text{ MeV}$ for redshifts $z \ge 20$, outperforming curvelet and wavelet kurtosis statistics by a factor of $1.3-1.9$ in $\eta$ (a factor of $2-7$ in wall tension $\sigma$).
  • Single-patch Neural Posterior Estimation (NPE) breaks degeneracies to cleanly isolate the wall inclination angle $\theta$, though a strong parameter degeneracy remains between scattering redshift $z$ and energy scale $\eta$.
  • Under SPT-3G survey noise conditions, NRE sensitivity lands near the per-resolution-element instrumental noise floor, highlighting the advantage of morphology-aware inference over standard two-point or moment-based estimators.

Abstract

Topological defects are a generic prediction of field theories with spontaneously broken symmetries, and domain walls, the two-dimensional defects left by a broken discrete symmetry, are among the simplest. We study the imprint of dark, purely gravitationally interacting domain walls on the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB), in the regime where a low symmetry-breaking scale $\eta$ allows the walls to evade the Zel'dovich overclosure bound while remaining cosmologically relevant. The relativistic repulsion of matter by a planar wall carves out an evacuated void bounded by an overdense shell, and we compute the resulting photon deflection field analytically for walls both aligned with and arbitrarily inclined to the line of sight. The deflection stamps coherent, strongly non-Gaussian distortions onto the temperature sky, with a characteristic two-peak structure set by the relativistic displacement scale and an inclination dependence that vanishes for face-on walls. Because these signatures are localised and anisotropic, they are poorly captured by two-point statistics; we therefore analyse them with a curvelet/wavelet decomposition matched to the wall's geometry, and with simulation-based inference, training a Neural Posterior Estimator (NPE) to recover the scattering redshift $z$, energy scale $\eta$, and inclination $\theta$, and a Neural Ratio Estimator (NRE) to evaluate the likelihood-ratio test statistic for limit-setting. Casting these methods as sensitivity forecasts for a South Pole Telescope-like (SPT) survey, we find that the curvelet and wavelet statistical thresholds populate a common $\eta \sim 10-100$ MeV band over the accessible redshift range. The neural-ratio sensitivity curve, which exploits the full map morphology, reaches a factor 1.3-1.9 lower in $\eta$ at $z \gtrsim 20$, or $\sim 2-7$ in the wall tension, landing essentially on the per-resolution-element noise floor.

AMICO galaxy clusters in KiDS-1000: Cosmological constraints from the two-point correlation function

Massimiliano Romanello, Giorgio Francesco Lesci, Federico Marulli, Lauro Moscardini, Carlo Giocoli, Matteo Maturi, Mario Radovich, Sandro Bardelli, Gianluca Castignani, Hendrik Hildebrandt, Lorenzo Ingoglia, Shahab Joudaki, Emanuella Puddu, Mauro Sereno
arXiv:2610.31767 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.GA

(Gemini summary failed - see abstract.)

Abstract

In this paper we study the clustering properties of an optically identified photometric galaxy cluster catalogue from KiDS-1000. The sample was identified by the AMICO algorithm and consists of 9049 clusters with $S/N\geq3.5$, in the redshift range $0.1\leq z<0.8$, spread over an effective area of 838.78 deg$^2$. Clusters are selected according to their observed intrinsic richness, with thresholds of $\lambda^*{obs}\geq 20,25,30$ within $0.10\leq z<0.30$, $0.30\leq z<0.45$ and $0.45\leq z<0.80$, respectively. We measure the multipoles of the two-point correlation function over different ranges of scales from 5 $h^{-1}$ Mpc to 150 $h^{-1}$ Mpc. We model the clustering signal in the context of the $\Lambda$CDM cosmology, accounting for the main observational effects, such RSDs and photo-$z$ uncertainties. The effective bias of the cluster sample is estimated via a mass-richness scaling relation. For the MCMC analysis, we adopt Gaussian priors on the parameters of the mass-richness scaling relation, and broad, uniform priors to derive cosmological constraints on the cold matter density parameter, the power spectrum amplitude and the structure growth parameter, $S_8\equiv\sigma_8 \sqrt{\Omega{m}/0.3}$. Specifically, we find $\Omega_{m}=0.24^{+0.02}{-0.02}$ and $\sigma_8=0.86^{+0.13}{-0.10}$ representing an improvement in precision over both the 2D and 3D AMICO clustering analyses performed on the previous KiDS data release. Moreover, we obtain $S_8=0.77^{+0.10}_{-0.08}$ in $1\sigma$ agreement with the AMICO KiDS-1000 number counts constraints estimated from the same catalogue, as well as with some results from external early- and late-Universe data sets. Our work strengthens the potential of cluster clustering as a complementary and competitive probe, to be included and further developed with the final KiDS-Legacy data release, and in other future Stage-IV photometric redshift surveys.

Cluster connectivity in DES Y3 data: impact on galaxy properties

C. Gouin, C. Laigle, J. Prat, L. O.V. Edwards, P. Ganeshaiah Veena, D. Galárraga-Espinosa, G. Gopidas
arXiv:2610.31800 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

(Gemini summary failed - see abstract.)

Abstract

Connectivity, namely the number of cosmic filaments reaching out of groups or clusters, has recently emerged as an informative way to quantify environment. With the advent of wide-area photometric surveys, it becomes possible to measure connectivity on a statistically significant sample of clusters. In this work, we extract the cosmic web from the DES Y3 photometric data and measure the connectivity around clusters from several public catalogs up to $z < 1$. Cosmic filaments are extracted in 2D with DisPerSE (the parametrization of which is calibrated on simulations) in tomographic slices of fixed comoving thickness of 220 Mpc, with a constant sampling across the redshift range ($\sim 0.1$ galaxies Mpc$^{-2}$). We first recover the well-established trend that cluster connectivity increases with cluster mass at all redshifts. We then investigate the properties of cluster member galaxies and find that, at fixed mass, clusters with a higher fraction of red (elliptical) galaxies are more strongly connected to the cosmic web. Furthermore, by examining the radial profiles of the red and elliptical galaxy fractions, we obtain hints that galaxy populations may undergo pre-processing within filamentary structures before being accreted into clusters. Overall, we demonstrate that despite photometric redshift uncertainties and projection effects, our method allows for the quantification of the cosmic web environment around clusters in broad-band photometric surveys. This work is consistent with results based on the Euclid Quick Release 1 (Q1). We plan to publicly release the cluster connectivity catalog as well as the cosmic filament catalog.

Semi-Analytic Predictions for Strong-Lensing Perturbers in Cold and Self-Interacting Dark Matter

RelatedJonathan Tekverk, Duncan K. Adams, Simon Birrer, Rouven Essig, Daniel Gilman
arXiv:2610.31881 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, hep-ph

Strong gravitational lensing can reveal dark matter subhalos that lack stars by detecting localized light distortions in background images, offering a direct window into small-scale dark matter physics. In self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) scenarios, particle collisions drive gravothermal evolution, causing subhalo cores to first expand into low-density cores and later undergo core collapse into dense, compact central profiles. To test whether SIDM can explain compact observed perturbers, the authors combine semi-analytic subhalo merger trees from the SatGen code with calibrated prescriptions for SIDM gravothermal evolution across dark matter parameter space. Comparing predicted subhalo populations against three candidate strong-lensing perturbers (PJ011646, SDSS J0946+1006, and JVAS B1938+666), they demonstrate that SIDM subhalos match the inferred projected masses and density slopes substantially more often than standard Cold Dark Matter (CDM). This modular semi-analytic framework enables rapid population-level constraints on velocity-dependent dark matter cross sections without requiring computationally expensive cosmological N-body simulations for every model.

Main conclusions
  • SIDM subhalos reproduce the observed projected masses and deflection-based density slopes of all three analyzed lensing perturbers significantly more frequently than CDM subhalos.
  • For PJ011646, cored SIDM subhalos best match the perturber (~0.6 matching subhalos per host vs ~0.002 in CDM), while core-collapsed subhalos produce density profiles that are too steep.
  • For SDSS J0946+1006 and JVAS B1938+666, core-collapsed SIDM subhalos provide the required highly compact density profiles (~3 and ~220 per host, respectively), whereas CDM realizations produce virtually zero candidates unless extremely early subhalo accretion ($z_{\mathrm{infall}} \sim 13$) is invoked.
  • Simultaneously reproducing the characteristics of all three perturbers requires an SIDM cross-section velocity scale $w \gtrsim 10^2\text{ km/s}$ and normalization $\sigma_0/m_\chi \gtrsim 10^2\text{ cm}^2/\text{g}$.

Abstract

Strong gravitational lensing is sensitive to the abundance and internal structure of dark matter halos, providing a probe of gravothermal evolution in self-interacting dark matter (SIDM). We combine semi-analytic cold dark matter (CDM) subhalo histories with a model for gravothermal evolution in SIDM to generate CDM and SIDM subhalo populations across SIDM parameter space. We predict their projected enclosed masses and deflection-based projected density slopes and compare them with three perturbers inferred through gravitational imaging in PJ011646, SDSS J0946+1006, and JVAS B1938+666. For SIDM model parameters $\sigma_0/m_\chi=147.1$ cm$^2$/g and $w=120$ km/s, SIDM subhalos reproduce all three perturbers more often than CDM subhalos. For PJ011646, we find ~0.6 cored SIDM subhalos per host matching the perturber, compared with ~0.002 in CDM, while core-collapsed subhalos are too steep. For SDSS J0946+1006, we find ~3 core-collapsed and ~0.004 cored SIDM subhalos per host, compared with none in 900 CDM realizations, although baryons, neglected here, may matter for such massive subhalos. For "detection V" in JVAS B1938+666, we find ~220 core-collapsed and ~20 cored SIDM subhalos per host within the $2\sigma$ posterior region, compared with ~0.2 in CDM; the CDM candidates require exceptionally early accretion, at median redshift $z\sim13$. Simultaneously reproducing all three perturbers requires $w\gtrsim10^2$ km/s, so that the massive (~$5.6\times10^{10}M_\odot$) progenitors of SDSS J0946+1006 candidates can collapse, and is achieved for $\sigma_0/m_\chi\gtrsim10^2$ cm$^2$/g in the parameter sets studied. Our results show how semi-analytic models can quantify how often subhalos reproduce individual perturbers in different dark matter models, and our modular framework can be readily updated as models of tidal stripping and late-stage gravothermal evolution in SIDM improve. The Impact of Inclination, Redshift, and Mass Ratio Selection on Cosmological Constraints from Bright Sirens Beyond Conventional Fisher Approximations

RelatedGustavo B. F. Lima, Rafael F. Aranha, Rodrigo S. Gonçalves
arXiv:2610.32008 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc

Bright gravitational-wave sirens with electromagnetic counterparts (such as binary neutron star mergers) provide a direct measurement of luminosity distance, offering an independent probe of $H_0$ and cosmological parameters without relying on the cosmic distance ladder. This paper explores how key astrophysical population choices---specifically binary mass ratio $q$, maximum observable inclination angle $\iota_{\max}$, and maximum redshift $z_{\max}$---impact cosmological parameter forecasts beyond basic Fisher matrix approximations. The authors simulate populations of non-spinning binary neutron stars using the phenomenological IMRPhenomD waveform model and analyze them with the GWDALI Bayesian framework. They demonstrate that while tight $H_0$ constraints rely mainly on event statistics, constraining matter density $\Omega_m$ critically requires high-redshift detections and wider inclination coverage to break degeneracies between luminosity distance and inclination angle.

Main conclusions
  • Statistical precision on $H_0$ reaches $<1.3\%$ with $N \ge 400$ bright siren events and falls below $0.9\%$ for $N \ge 800$ events.
  • A deep-redshift catalog ($z_{\max} \ge 1.0$) is strictly necessary to break cosmological parameter degeneracies and place competitive constraints on $\Omega_m$.
  • Relaxing the maximum observable inclination cutoff from $\iota_{\max} = 20^\circ$ to $40^\circ$ (consistent with off-axis emission from structured short GRB jets) significantly reduces the distance-inclination degeneracy.
  • Variations in the mass ratio $q$ alter the signal-to-noise distribution and posterior volume, showing that astrophysical population assumptions will be key systematic bottlenecks for 3G detectors (Einstein Telescope and Cosmic Explorer).

Abstract

Future generations of gravitational-wave observatories are projected to detect up to thousands bright siren events within a decade of operation. However, the precision of these cosmological forecasts depends heavily on the astrophysical assumptions embedded within the simulation pipelines. In this work, we systematically investigate the impact of variations in the binary mass ratio ($q$), the maximum observable inclination angle ($\iota_{\max}$), and the maximum redshift ($z_{\max}$) on the constraints of the Hubble constant ($H_0$) and the matter density parameter ($\Omega_m$). We perform our analyses based on the GWDALI framework, the GW Doublet Approximation, and the phenomenological waveform approximant IMRPhenomD. By adopting sets of non-spinning binary neutron star populations we explore the impact of astrophysical parameters on the constraints of cosmological parameters. Furthermore, expanding the maximum inclination cutoff from the conservative $20^{\circ}$ up to $40^{\circ}$ substantially mitigates the correlation between distance and inclination.

Introducing the GION Lightcone Simulation Suite for Multi-Wavelength Large-Scale Structure Cosmology

RelatedKen Osato, Satoshi Tanaka, Tomoaki Ishiyama, Hironao Miyatake, Takahiro Nishimichi, Masato Shirasaki
arXiv:2610.32388 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

Stage-IV cosmological surveys like Euclid, DESI, and Rubin require large-volume, high-resolution lightcone simulations that simultaneously capture galaxy clustering, weak lensing, and CMB secondary anisotropies across cosmic time to validate joint-analysis pipelines. To meet this need, the authors present GION, a suite of 10 full-sky N-body lightcone simulations extending to $z=5$, each evolving $6144^3$ particles in a $(1.5\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Gpc})^3$ box using the GPU-optimized TreePM code GINKAKU on the Fugaku supercomputer. By employing an on-the-fly lightcone extraction algorithm, the simulations avoid snapshot-stacking artifacts and achieve a fine particle mass resolution of $1.28 \times 10^9\,h^{-1}\mathrm{M}_\odot$. The continuous particle outputs are post-processed into halo catalogs, density/momentum shells, weak lensing convergence maps, and thermal/kinematic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich maps, establishing a self-consistent multi-wavelength synthetic dataset for Stage-IV LSS observations.

Main conclusions
  • The GION suite provides 10 full-sky $\Lambda$CDM realizations reaching $z=5$ with particle mass $1.28 \times 10^9\,h^{-1}\mathrm{M}_\odot$, resolving dark matter halos down to $M_{\mathrm{200b}} \sim 10^{11}\,h^{-1}\mathrm{M}_\odot$.
  • Validation of snapshot statistics shows matter power spectra agreeing with DarkEmulator2 within a few percent for $k \ge 0.1\,h\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ and halo mass functions matching theoretical predictions within 5% over $10^{11} \le M_{\mathrm{200b}}/(h^{-1}\mathrm{M}_\odot) \le 10^{13}$ for $0 \le z \le 4$.
  • Angular auto- and cross-power spectra for simulated observables (weak lensing convergence, DESI-like LRG/ELG mock catalogs, tSZ Compton-$y$, and kSZ Doppler-$b$) demonstrate good consistency with theoretical halo-model predictions.
  • All post-processed data products (Level 1 halo catalogs and HEALPix $N_{\mathrm{side}}=8192$ mass/momentum shells, plus Level 2 observable maps totaling ~10.1 TB per realization) are released publicly.

Abstract

We present the GION (Ginkaku Lightcone Multi-wavelength Simulation Project) simulation suite: 10 full-sky high-resolution cosmological lightcone simulations and their associated products for multi-wavelength large-scale structure cosmology. We use the TreePM $N$-body code GINKAKU, which is highly optimized in the massively parallel environment, and integrate the on-the-fly lightcone construction technique. Each lightcone simulation is run with $6144^3$ particles in a volume of $(1.5 \, h^{-1} \, \mathrm{Gpc})^3$, which corresponds to a particle mass of $1.28 \times 10^9 \, h^{-1} \, \mathrm{M}_\odot$, and extends to $z = 5$. The fine mass resolution and the depth of the lightcone simulations allow us to resolve dark matter halos down to $10^{11} \, h^{-1} \, \mathrm{M}_\odot$ in the high-redshift Universe, which is crucial for applications to the Stage-IV cosmological surveys. The fundamental statistics of the simulations, including the matter power spectrum, halo power spectrum, and halo mass function, are validated against theoretical predictions. We also generate various large-scale structure observables from the lightcone outputs: the dark matter halo catalogs, mock galaxy samples, weak lensing convergence maps, and thermal and kinematic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (tSZ and kSZ) maps. The angular power spectra of these observables, including the cross-correlation between weak lensing and the tSZ effect, are in good agreement with theoretical predictions, demonstrating that the suite provides mutually consistent multi-wavelength observables on the same lightcone. The GION simulation suite will be publicly available and can be used for various cosmological analyses to address the observational systematic effects and validate theoretical models.

Luminosity-weighted galaxy and gravitational wave cross-correlations

Charles Dalang, Isabela Santiago de Matos, João Ferri, Raul Abramo, Tessa Baker
arXiv:2610.32436 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

(Gemini summary failed - see abstract.)

Abstract

Cross-correlations of galaxies and gravitational waves (GW) can be used to extract cosmological parameters and the gravitational wave bias. In this work, we study how to include galaxy luminosities in these spatial cross-correlations, inspired by the standard approach of dark sirens line-of-sight redshift priors. We present a formalism to include luminosity information in galaxy-GW simulations and study the effects of assigning a hosting probability that depends on luminosity through a simple power law $p_{\rm{host}} \propto L^\beta$ with $\beta \in \{0,1,2\}$ corresponding to uniformly, linear and quadratically weighted in luminosity. This effectively changes the GW linear bias with respect to dark matter and, in principle, allows to gain signal-to-noise and a sharper measurement of cosmological parameters. For a realistic luminosity dependence of the galaxy bias taken from DESI data, the gain is marginal, making the method robust to different luminosity weighting prescriptions. We show that the cross-correlation inference is able to recover the injected GW bias within $10\%$ if there is a mismatch between the injected linear bias of host galaxies and observed galaxies, provided that one sticks to relatively large scales. We also show that the Hubble constant and matter energy density can be recovered to $0.5\%$ and $5\%$ respectively with a third generation network of GW detectors, regardless of the modeling of the GW bias.

Joint Angular-Power Distributions of Interfering Nanohertz Gravitational Waves

Yan-Chen Bi, Yu-Mei Wu, Qing-Guo Huang
arXiv:2610.32809 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc

(Gemini summary failed - see abstract.)

Abstract

Finite populations of supermassive black-hole binaries produce fluctuations in both gravitational-wave power and pulsar timing correlations. We characterize the realization-dependent, orientation-averaged Earth-term angular correlation through its frequency-resolved Legendre coefficients. A hybrid model of bright sources and a Gaussian faint background extends the conditional Gamma distribution in the many-weak-source limit to non-central $\chi^2$ statistics, yielding correlated multipoles after population marginalization. Our analytical variance decomposition and SMBHB population simulations reveal a population-dependent transition from interference dominated variance at low frequencies to population-power-dominated variance at high frequencies. In the many-weak-source regime, a multivariate lognormal approximation captures the main features of the joint distribution using only the first two log-cumulants. These results provide statistical benchmarks for fluctuations in both the overall amplitude and angular structure of the realized correlation.

Modelling kNN-CDFs of biased tracers in redshift space using hybrid Lagrangian bias expansion

Eishica Chand, Arka Banerjee
arXiv:2610.32837 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

(Gemini summary failed - see abstract.)

Abstract

Higher-order statistics are becoming increasingly useful in clustering studies of the large-scale structure (LSS) of the universe. The kNN-CDFs are one such higher-order statistic, sensitive to all N-point correlations and easy to compute on data. Utilising their statistical gains for cosmological inference requires modelling them accurately for the tracer of interest. We use the hybrid Lagrangian bias expansion to enable this modelling in redshift space, building and validating the pipeline on Quijote simulations. We show that the kNN-CDFs of a luminous red galaxy (LRG)-like sample can be modelled down to scales as small as r = 10 Mpc/h, with the minimum reliable scale increasing with nearest-neighbour order. Moreover, the model parameters inferred from the kNN-CDFs also describe the galaxy power spectrum P(k), indicating model consistency across the two statistics and the feasibility of combining them. Fisher forecasts for galaxy auto clustering show that combining P(k) and kNN-CDFs improves constraints on all parameters relative to either statistic alone. Relative to P(k), the constraints on $b_3$ improve the most, while those on $\sigma_8$ and $\Omega_m$ improve by factors of 3.5 and 1.7, respectively. Including galaxy-matter cross clustering alongside galaxy auto clustering further improves the Fisher constraints from both P(k) and kNN-CDFs, with their combination yielding the tightest constraints overall. Relative to P(k), the largest improvement from this combination is found for $b_2$, while the constraints on $\sigma_8$ and $\Omega_m$ improve by factors of 4.2 and 1.7, respectively. These results demonstrate the applicability of the hybrid Lagrangian bias framework to model the kNN-CDFs of biased tracers in redshift space, and the potential of combining higher-order and two-point statistics to extract cosmological information.

Non-Markovian Cosmology with Causal Memory Kernels

cross-listAritra Sanyal, Prabir Rudra
arXiv:2610.31714 · PDF · HTML · gr-qc, astro-ph.CO

(Gemini summary failed - see abstract.)

Abstract

We investigate a non-Markovian extension of Friedmann cosmology in which the present expansion rate retains information about the past cosmic evolution through a causal memory kernel, with \LCDM{} recovered in the limit of vanishing kernel amplitude, $A\to0$. We show that five kernels (Linear, Power Law, Exponential, Gaussian and Oscillatory) arise as cosine transforms of the spectral densities of an unresolved hidden sector, and that the Exponential and Linear kernels also follow independently from Israel-Stewart causal viscosity and from Deser-Woodard non-local gravity, respectively. We constrain the kernels with 30 Cosmic Chronometer (CC) measurements and with $H(z)$ derived from the DESI DR1 and DR2 baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) data, using multi-start $\chi^{2}$ minimisation followed by Markov chain Monte Carlo sampling, and compare them with \LCDM{} through the Akaike and Bayesian information criteria. The Linear amplitude is consistent with zero ($A=0.002\pm0.030$ for CC+DR2). The Power-law, Exponential and Gaussian kernels are observationally degenerate: the data select sharply peaked kernels that constrain only an integrated amplitude $\Aeff\simeq0.01$--$0.03$, equivalent to a uniform $0.5$--$1.5\%$ rescaling of $H(z)$, and they are disfavoured relative to \LCDM{} ($\Delta{\rm AIC}=+2.5$ to $+3.6$). Only the Oscillatory kernel improves the fit appreciably ($\dchi=4.5$--$8.1$), but it requires a negative damping rate, which is incompatible with the causal derivations, and it is not favoured once CC data are included ($\Delta{\rm BIC}=+4.4$ to $+6.2$)

Physics-Informed Neural Networks vs U-Net: Application to thermal dust emission recovery in the microwave

cross-listNicolas Morant Juan, José M. Casas, Laura Bonavera
arXiv:2610.31813 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.IM, astro-ph.CO

(Gemini summary failed - see abstract.)

Abstract

Galactic thermal dust emission is the dominant foreground contaminant of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) at frequencies above 100 GHz, and its accurate separation is essential for current and future CMB experiments. Deep learning methods, in particular convolutional architectures such as the U-Net, have demonstrated strong performance for CMB component separation, but typically operate as purely data-driven models with no explicit enforcement of the physical laws governing the emission they reconstruct. In this work, we present a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) that embeds the Modified Black Body (MBB) spectral model as a hard, differentiable architectural constraint, and we compare it against a standard U-Net baseline trained under an identical pipeline. Both architectures are trained on realistic simulated Planck HFI patches at four frequencies (143, 217, 353 and 545 GHz) and evaluated on a shared validation set as well as on three independent Galactic dust realizations from the PySM phenomenological dust models named D4, D7 and D10. We find that both networks achieve comparable reconstruction quality across all evaluated datasets, with a Structural Similarity Index Measure consistently above 0.96 and a fitted angular power-spectrum spectral index that agrees with the input to within 1% in every region. Embedding the physical constraint in the PINN does not measurably degrade reconstruction accuracy relative to the unconstrained U-Net, while additionally guaranteeing that every predicted output corresponds to a thermodynamically admissible physical configuration. These results establish the physics-informed formulation as a viable methodological alternative for dust component separation, providing physical interpretability without a trade-off in reconstruction fidelity.

Finite-distance effects and scalar suppression in massive gravity

cross-listChris Choi, Tina Kahniashvili, Barnabás Póczos
arXiv:2610.31912 · PDF · HTML · gr-qc, astro-ph.CO

(Gemini summary failed - see abstract.)

Abstract

Pulsar timing arrays (PTAs) probe the stochastic gravitational wave (GW) background through the angular cross-correlations of timing residuals. For a GW background in general relativity (GR), the expected overlap reduction function (ORF) is given by the Hellings--Downs (HD) correlation. We investigate how a nonzero graviton mass modifies this prediction through a massive dispersion relation and additional vector and scalar polarizations, producing a modified effective ORF. In the massive limit, the finite-distance pulsar terms survive angular averaging, multiplying the correlations of all three polarization sectors by a pulsar-dependent factor that can enhance, suppress, or reverse them. We adopt a tensor Earth--Earth normalization that preserves the continuous finite-distance effect. In the massless limit, we find that the vector ORF vanishes and the scalar ORF approaches a finite breathing mode. We propose the phenomenological prescription $\Omega_S/\Omega_T=A_S(m_g/\omega)^{n_S}$, with scalar amplitude parameter $A_S$ and suppression index $n_S>0$. These results provide a consistent framework for future PTA analyses of massive gravity.

Local Analogs of Little Red Dots: Clues to the X-ray Weakness

cross-listShi-Jiang Chen, Jun-Xian Wang, Zhen-Ya Zheng, Ruqiu Lin, Luis C. Ho, Zhen-Yi Cai, Chang-Hao Chen, Zsofi Igo, Jia-Lai Kang, Ruancun Li, Teng Liu, Wenke Ren, Zhen-Bo Su
arXiv:2610.32430 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.GA, astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.HE

(Gemini summary failed - see abstract.)

Abstract

A major question about JWST's little red dots (LRDs) at $z\sim4-8$ is the origin of their apparent X-ray weakness. Even deep Chandra stacks yield only tentative detections or broad-band limits and cannot distinguish among super-Eddington accretion, intrinsic X-ray weakness, and Compton-thick (CT) obscuration. Local analogs offer a powerful complementary route. At $z\sim0.3$, similarly luminous sources require $\sim10^{3-4}$ less exposure for comparable signal-to-noise, suffer less source confusion, and directly probe the diagnostic rest-frame soft X-rays. Here we present a pilot Einstein Probe/FXT study of six "local little red dots" (LLRDs), V-shaped broad-line green pea galaxies selected as low-redshift counterparts of LRDs. These sources show X-ray diversity: "X-normal" LLRDs resemble type 1 AGNs in X-ray luminosity and optical variability, whereas the "X-weak" stack lies $\sim0.5-0.7$ dex below standard unobscured-quasar scaling relations while remaining compatible with the representative LRD stack and 95% of published individual limits. The X-weak LLRDs also lack detected optical variability and have redder optical continua than X-normal LLRDs, making their V-shaped SEDs closer to those of high-redshift LRDs. The detection of the X-weak stack at $\sim3.3\sigma$ with the soft-X-ray-sensitive EP-FXT provides rest-frame $\sim2-3$ keV constraints difficult to reconcile with a typical unobscured super-Eddington model or a local-AGN-like CT geometry examined here, although modified variants remain possible. The simplest alternative is a mildly X-ray-weak corona ($\Delta\alpha_{\mathrm{ox}}\sim-0.1$ to $-0.2$) with Compton-thin absorption ($N_{\mathrm{H}}\sim10^{22-23}$ cm$^{-2}$), consistent with the independent Balmer decrement/break diagnostics. Together with growing samples of local LRD analogs, our results offer a practical path for testing the physical nature of LRD X-ray weakness.

Stochastic dynamics of cosmological inflation in general relativity

cross-listYoann L. Launay
arXiv:2610.32592 · PDF · HTML · gr-qc, astro-ph.CO, hep-th

(Gemini summary failed - see abstract.)

Abstract

The theory of Inflation, supplemented by fluctuations of the quantum vacuum, is the leading paradigm to explain the correlations of inhomogeneities at very large distances in the Universe. Particle physics methods fail when strong non-linearity and non-perturbativity are involved, leaving a whole landscape of untestable scenarios of inflation. Starobinsky's stochastic inflation can in principle treat inflationary inhomogeneities nonlinearly and classically as the Hubble radius becomes smaller than their wavelengths, but it was never formulated in full general relativity, which motivates this thesis. Using the Keldysh formalism of nonequilibrium quantum field theory, we first develop a validity criterion for the use of classical computations, and thus simulations, to probe inflationary interactions, finding analytically that the bispectrum can be extracted from slightly sub-Hubble scales in quasi-de Sitter spacetimes. We then provide the first analytical solutions to the constraints for inhomogeneous initial conditions of numerical relativity simulations of inflation, and use them to explore its nonperturbative regime. Our main contribution is to obtain stochastic equations for the full set of inflaton equations in general relativity: this description incorporates the full nonlinear and backreacting evolution, is small-gauge-invariant, and is free of historical assumptions such as the gradient-free separate universe approximation. We implement these equations in the BSSN formulation of numerical relativity, demonstrating their correctness in slow-roll and ultra slow-roll scenarios via the satisfaction of the constraints, with a first look at eternal inflation. Finally, we extend our coarse-graining methods beyond General Relativity and single-field inflation, offering a phenomenological framework with the ambition of serving as a numerical particle collider for the early universe.

Abstract

Coupling gauge fields to the inflaton can amplify their quantum fluctuations during inflation, with potential applications to a range of cosmological phenomena. A central challenge is to define finite and physically meaningful observables, such as the gauge-field energy density. In this work, we study an Abelian gauge field kinetically coupled to the inflaton through $f^2(\phi)F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$ in a de Sitter background with $f\propto a^{\gamma_1}$, and compare conventional quantum-field-theoretic regularization with horizon-cutoff prescriptions. Using a hard physical-momentum cutoff and dimensional regularization, we derive the ultraviolet divergences in closed form and fix the remaining one-parameter finite-part ambiguity by requiring the coupling-induced total energy density to be nonnegative. We then examine five horizon-cutoff prescriptions based on characteristic scales of the mode equations. Their predictions depend sensitively on the choice of horizon scale and, for weak gauge-field production, can deviate substantially from the regularized result; for the adopted finite-part prescription, the minimal horizon agrees best with the regularized total energy density. As $|\gamma_1|\to 2$, where infrared modes dominate, the relative deviation of all prescriptions from the regularized result vanishes, independently of the finite-part prescription. Outside this regime, the separate electric and magnetic contributions, which can become negative after regularization, are not reliably reproduced by any of the horizon-cutoff prescriptions.

The Galactic Center Gamma-Ray Excess and the Look-Elsewhere Effect

cross-listIsabel S. Sands, Philip F. Hopkins
arXiv:2610.32845 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.HE, astro-ph.CO, hep-ph

(Gemini summary failed - see abstract.)

Abstract

The origin of the excess of GeV gamma-rays in the Milky Way's galactic center observed by Fermi-LAT has been an open question for nearly two decades. The two leading interpretations of this signal are that it is produced either by annihilating dark matter (DM), or arises from unresolved point sources, such as a novel population of millisecond pulsars. In both scenarios, uncertainties and limitations in the modeling of galactic diffuse gamma-ray emission, such as the steady-state approximation of cosmic ray (CR) propagation and assumption of an axisymmetric distribution of CRs, complicate the interpretation of the galactic center excess (GCE). Simulations with full magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) and CR transport can provide a new avenue for understanding the astrophysical phenomena that drive galactic diffuse gamma-ray emission. We utilize MHD simulations of Milky Way analogs with time-dependent, spectrally-resolved CR transport to reconstruct diffuse gamma-ray emission. Comparing instantaneous emission to a time-averaged baseline, we find residuals up to several times larger than the Fermi GCE, with a wide range of spectral shapes and spatial morphologies. At some simulation snapshots, the spectrum and morphology of the residuals resemble the Fermi GCE. Our results suggest that the Fermi GCE may be a manifestation of the look-elsewhere effect- a transient fluctuation in diffuse emission from stellar sources, observed at an inopportune moment- and motivate the development of new Fermi-LAT analysis templates that account for time-dependent CR transport.
