Modelling kNN-CDFs of biased tracers in redshift space using hybrid Lagrangian bias expansion

RelevantEishica Chand, Arka Banerjee
arXiv:2610.32837 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: It applies the hybrid Lagrangian bias expansion (advected basis fields from simulations, with RSD) to a higher-order statistic and plans an emulator, which is closely related to the student's hybrid EFT work, though it focuses on kNN-CDFs and ΛCDM rather than beyond-ΛCDM models.

The paper extends the hybrid Lagrangian bias expansion to model kNN-CDFs (nearest-neighbour distance distributions, sensitive to all N-point correlations) of biased tracers in redshift space. Lagrangian bias operators (1, δ, δ², s², ∇²δ, plus δ³) are advected with Quijote N-body displacements, and RSDs are added through central distortion (halo velocities) plus an exponential Finger-of-God kernel with a satellite fraction. The model is validated on an HOD LRG-like sample at z=0.5 from Quijote. The authors then run Fisher forecasts for bias, FoG and cosmological parameters, combining kNN-CDFs with P(k) for galaxy auto and galaxy-matter cross clustering.

Main conclusions
  • Redshift-space galaxy kNN-CDFs are modelled down to r≈10 Mpc/h for 1NN; the minimum reliable scale grows with neighbour order k.
  • Parameters fitted to kNN-CDFs also describe real- and redshift-space P(k), but P(k)-fitted parameters do not reproduce the kNN-CDFs.
  • Fisher forecast, auto clustering: P(k)+kNN-CDFs improves σ8 by a factor 3.5 and Ωm by 1.7 relative to P(k) alone, with the largest gain on b3.
  • Adding galaxy-matter cross clustering: σ8 improves by 4.2 and Ωm by 1.7 relative to P(k), with the largest gain on b2.
  • An emulator for kNN-CDFs and simulation-based inference are planned, and the unphysical δ_T<-1 regions are noted as a limitation of the hybrid framework.

Abstract

Higher-order statistics are becoming increasingly useful in clustering studies of the large-scale structure (LSS) of the universe. The kNN-CDFs are one such higher-order statistic, sensitive to all N-point correlations and easy to compute on data. Utilising their statistical gains for cosmological inference requires modelling them accurately for the tracer of interest. We use the hybrid Lagrangian bias expansion to enable this modelling in redshift space, building and validating the pipeline on Quijote simulations. We show that the kNN-CDFs of a luminous red galaxy (LRG)-like sample can be modelled down to scales as small as r = 10 Mpc/h, with the minimum reliable scale increasing with nearest-neighbour order. Moreover, the model parameters inferred from the kNN-CDFs also describe the galaxy power spectrum P(k), indicating model consistency across the two statistics and the feasibility of combining them. Fisher forecasts for galaxy auto clustering show that combining P(k) and kNN-CDFs improves constraints on all parameters relative to either statistic alone. Relative to P(k), the constraints on $b_3$ improve the most, while those on $\sigma_8$ and $\Omega_m$ improve by factors of 3.5 and 1.7, respectively. Including galaxy-matter cross clustering alongside galaxy auto clustering further improves the Fisher constraints from both P(k) and kNN-CDFs, with their combination yielding the tightest constraints overall. Relative to P(k), the largest improvement from this combination is found for $b_2$, while the constraints on $\sigma_8$ and $\Omega_m$ improve by factors of 4.2 and 1.7, respectively. These results demonstrate the applicability of the hybrid Lagrangian bias framework to model the kNN-CDFs of biased tracers in redshift space, and the potential of combining higher-order and two-point statistics to extract cosmological information.

Prospects of Detecting Cosmological Domain Walls in the Cosmic Microwave Background

Berkin Gürbüz, Robert Brandenberger, Yitian Sun
arXiv:2610.31761 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-ph, hep-th

The paper asks whether dark, gravitationally interacting planar domain walls (low symmetry-breaking scale $\eta$, evading the Zel'dovich bound) can be detected through CMB lensing. The authors compute the relativistic matter displacement around a wall (an evacuated void bounded by an overdense shell, with no shell crossing), then analytically/numerically derive the photon deflection for walls of finite extent at arbitrary inclination, using the Born approximation. They apply the deflection to Gaussian CMB maps and analyse them with curvelet/wavelet statistics and simulation-based inference (NPE for $z,\eta,\theta$; NRE for a likelihood-ratio test). They forecast idealized sensitivity for an SPT-3G-like survey, with no foregrounds or LCDM lensing.

Main conclusions
  • Wall repulsion gives a void plus overdense shell; the deflection is line-like, non-Gaussian, with a two-peak structure set by the displacement scale $\psi(z)$, and vanishes for face-on walls by mass conservation.
  • Curvelet and wavelet statistics reach a common $\eta\sim10$-$100$ MeV detectability band over the accessible redshift range.
  • The neural ratio estimator is a factor 1.3-1.9 deeper in $\eta$ at $z\gtrsim20$ (~2-7 in tension), close to the noise floor.
  • NPE shows a $z$-$\eta$ degeneracy in a single patch while recovering $\theta$ independently; the forecasts are idealized (single aligned wall, Gaussian sky, no foregrounds).
domain wallsCMB lensingsimulation-based inferencetopological defectscurvelets
Abstract

Topological defects are a generic prediction of field theories with spontaneously broken symmetries, and domain walls, the two-dimensional defects left by a broken discrete symmetry, are among the simplest. We study the imprint of dark, purely gravitationally interacting domain walls on the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB), in the regime where a low symmetry-breaking scale $\eta$ allows the walls to evade the Zel'dovich overclosure bound while remaining cosmologically relevant. The relativistic repulsion of matter by a planar wall carves out an evacuated void bounded by an overdense shell, and we compute the resulting photon deflection field analytically for walls both aligned with and arbitrarily inclined to the line of sight. The deflection stamps coherent, strongly non-Gaussian distortions onto the temperature sky, with a characteristic two-peak structure set by the relativistic displacement scale and an inclination dependence that vanishes for face-on walls. Because these signatures are localised and anisotropic, they are poorly captured by two-point statistics; we therefore analyse them with a curvelet/wavelet decomposition matched to the wall's geometry, and with simulation-based inference, training a Neural Posterior Estimator (NPE) to recover the scattering redshift $z$, energy scale $\eta$, and inclination $\theta$, and a Neural Ratio Estimator (NRE) to evaluate the likelihood-ratio test statistic for limit-setting. Casting these methods as sensitivity forecasts for a South Pole Telescope-like (SPT) survey, we find that the curvelet and wavelet statistical thresholds populate a common $\eta \sim 10-100$ MeV band over the accessible redshift range. The neural-ratio sensitivity curve, which exploits the full map morphology, reaches a factor 1.3-1.9 lower in $\eta$ at $z \gtrsim 20$, or $\sim 2-7$ in the wall tension, landing essentially on the per-resolution-element noise floor.

AMICO galaxy clusters in KiDS-1000: Cosmological constraints from the two-point correlation function

RelatedMassimiliano Romanello, Giorgio Francesco Lesci, Federico Marulli, Lauro Moscardini, Carlo Giocoli, Matteo Maturi, Mario Radovich, Sandro Bardelli, Gianluca Castignani, Hendrik Hildebrandt, Lorenzo Ingoglia, Shahab Joudaki, Emanuella Puddu, Mauro Sereno
arXiv:2610.31767 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.GA

The paper measures the two-point correlation function multipoles (monopole, quadrupole, hexadecapole) of 9049 AMICO-selected optical galaxy clusters in KiDS-1000 (0.1≤z<0.8, 838.78 deg²) in three redshift bins, over 5–150 h^-1 Mpc. The model uses a linear-theory (Eisenstein-Hu) Kaiser plus dispersion-model power spectrum. It includes geometric distortions, photo-z damping (σ_z=0.014(1+z)) and the AMICO redshift discretisation. The effective bias comes from a mass-richness scaling relation (Tinker bias) with Gaussian priors from the earlier weak-lensing and number-count calibration. Covariances come from 500 bootstraps. An MCMC in ΛCDM gives constraints on Ω_m, σ_8 and S_8 that are more precise than the earlier KiDS-DR3 clustering analyses, and consistent with the KiDS-1000 cluster abundance results.

Main conclusions
  • Ω_m=0.24±0.02 and σ_8=0.86 (+0.13/-0.10); S_8=0.77 (+0.10/-0.08), consistent with the AMICO KiDS-1000 number counts and with several external datasets.
  • Figure of merit improves by a factor 1.8 over the KiDS-DR3 AMICO clustering analyses, mainly from a tighter Ω_m; there is a ~2σ tension in Ω_m with Planck18 and with the DR3 w(θ) result.
  • Effective bias rises with redshift: 2.40, 2.89 and 3.68 in the three bins, with 15–17% uncertainties.
  • Photo-z errors dominate the small-scale damping and shift signal into the quadrupole and hexadecapole. AMICO's Δz=0.01 discretisation destabilises the higher multipoles at small scales, while FoG is negligible.
  • The authors suggest extending to KiDS-Legacy, Euclid and LSST, and eventually to alternative cosmologies.

Abstract

In this paper we study the clustering properties of an optically identified photometric galaxy cluster catalogue from KiDS-1000. The sample was identified by the AMICO algorithm and consists of 9049 clusters with $S/N\geq3.5$, in the redshift range $0.1\leq z<0.8$, spread over an effective area of 838.78 deg$^2$. Clusters are selected according to their observed intrinsic richness, with thresholds of $\lambda^*{obs}\geq 20,25,30$ within $0.10\leq z<0.30$, $0.30\leq z<0.45$ and $0.45\leq z<0.80$, respectively. We measure the multipoles of the two-point correlation function over different ranges of scales from 5 $h^{-1}$ Mpc to 150 $h^{-1}$ Mpc. We model the clustering signal in the context of the $\Lambda$CDM cosmology, accounting for the main observational effects, such RSDs and photo-$z$ uncertainties. The effective bias of the cluster sample is estimated via a mass-richness scaling relation. For the MCMC analysis, we adopt Gaussian priors on the parameters of the mass-richness scaling relation, and broad, uniform priors to derive cosmological constraints on the cold matter density parameter, the power spectrum amplitude and the structure growth parameter, $S_8\equiv\sigma_8 \sqrt{\Omega{m}/0.3}$. Specifically, we find $\Omega_{m}=0.24^{+0.02}{-0.02}$ and $\sigma_8=0.86^{+0.13}{-0.10}$ representing an improvement in precision over both the 2D and 3D AMICO clustering analyses performed on the previous KiDS data release. Moreover, we obtain $S_8=0.77^{+0.10}_{-0.08}$ in $1\sigma$ agreement with the AMICO KiDS-1000 number counts constraints estimated from the same catalogue, as well as with some results from external early- and late-Universe data sets. Our work strengthens the potential of cluster clustering as a complementary and competitive probe, to be included and further developed with the final KiDS-Legacy data release, and in other future Stage-IV photometric redshift surveys.

Cluster connectivity in DES Y3 data: impact on galaxy properties

C. Gouin, C. Laigle, J. Prat, L. O.V. Edwards, P. Ganeshaiah Veena, D. Galárraga-Espinosa, G. Gopidas
arXiv:2610.31800 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

The paper measures cluster connectivity (number of cosmic filaments reaching a cluster) in DES Y3 photometric data and asks how it relates to cluster galaxy populations. Filaments are extracted in 2D with DisPerSE in 15 tomographic slices of ~222 cMpc thickness at constant galaxy density. The persistence threshold (1.5σ) is calibrated on simulations, and 50 photo-z realisations propagate redshift uncertainty. Connectivity is measured around X-ray and SZ selected clusters (eRASS1, RASS, ACT, SPT) at z<0.9. At fixed mass, more connected clusters have higher red and elliptical fractions, and galaxies near filaments show an excess of red galaxies, which hints at filament pre-processing. The method is a test bed for Euclid and LSST.

Main conclusions
  • Connectivity increases with cluster mass in all redshift bins, consistent with Euclid Q1. DES values are systematically lower, mainly at z<0.6, and photo-z errors lower connectivity by about 1.
  • At fixed mass, connectivity correlates positively with red fraction, with partial Pearson ρ≈0.17–0.23 (p down to ~1e-12) at z<0.6.
  • Connectivity correlates with elliptical fraction at fixed mass, with ρ≈0.21–0.22 at z<0.4. The signal drops at higher z, but a stricter morphology cut gives ρ=0.14 at 0.4<z<0.6.
  • Galaxies within 0.1 Mpc of filaments around clusters have higher red and elliptical fractions than the isotropic average, with a stronger effect for red. This suggests filament pre-processing affects colour more than morphology.
  • A filament catalogue and a cluster connectivity catalogue are to be released.

Abstract

Connectivity, namely the number of cosmic filaments reaching out of groups or clusters, has recently emerged as an informative way to quantify environment. With the advent of wide-area photometric surveys, it becomes possible to measure connectivity on a statistically significant sample of clusters. In this work, we extract the cosmic web from the DES Y3 photometric data and measure the connectivity around clusters from several public catalogs up to $z < 1$. Cosmic filaments are extracted in 2D with DisPerSE (the parametrization of which is calibrated on simulations) in tomographic slices of fixed comoving thickness of 220 Mpc, with a constant sampling across the redshift range ($\sim 0.1$ galaxies Mpc$^{-2}$). We first recover the well-established trend that cluster connectivity increases with cluster mass at all redshifts. We then investigate the properties of cluster member galaxies and find that, at fixed mass, clusters with a higher fraction of red (elliptical) galaxies are more strongly connected to the cosmic web. Furthermore, by examining the radial profiles of the red and elliptical galaxy fractions, we obtain hints that galaxy populations may undergo pre-processing within filamentary structures before being accreted into clusters. Overall, we demonstrate that despite photometric redshift uncertainties and projection effects, our method allows for the quantification of the cosmic web environment around clusters in broad-band photometric surveys. This work is consistent with results based on the Euclid Quick Release 1 (Q1). We plan to publicly release the cluster connectivity catalog as well as the cosmic filament catalog.

Semi-Analytic Predictions for Strong-Lensing Perturbers in Cold and Self-Interacting Dark Matter

Jonathan Tekverk, Duncan K. Adams, Simon Birrer, Rouven Essig, Daniel Gilman
arXiv:2610.31881 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, hep-ph

The paper asks whether strong-lensing perturbers found by gravitational imaging (PJ011646, SDSS J0946+1006, JVAS B1938+666) are more naturally explained by CDM or self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) subhalos. The authors generate CDM subhalo populations in 10^13 Msun hosts with the semi-analytic code SatGen. They map these to SIDM using the parametric gravothermal model of Yang et al. (2024), which gives cored or core-collapsed profiles. For each subhalo they compute the projected enclosed mass and the deflection-based projected slope, and they count how often these fall in each perturber's posterior region. The framework is fast and modular, so it can scan SIDM parameter space and be updated as tidal stripping and late-stage collapse modelling improve.

Main conclusions
  • For fiducial SIDM (σ0/m=147.1 cm²/g, w=120 km/s), SIDM subhalos match all three perturbers more often than CDM.
  • PJ011646: ~0.6 cored SIDM subhalos per host match, versus ~0.002 in CDM. Collapsed subhalos are too steep.
  • SDSS J0946+1006: ~3 collapsed and ~0.004 cored SIDM subhalos per host, with no CDM matches in 900 realizations. Baryons are neglected and may matter for these massive (~5.6e10 Msun) progenitors.
  • JVAS B1938+666 detection V: ~220 collapsed and ~20 cored SIDM subhalos per host, versus ~0.2 in CDM. The CDM candidates need very early infall (median z~13).
  • Reproducing all three perturbers at once requires w ≳ 100 km/s and σ0/m ≳ 100 cm²/g. Results are fairly robust to the collapsed profile and tidal prescription, but severe tidal stripping suppresses the SIDM counts.

Abstract

Strong gravitational lensing is sensitive to the abundance and internal structure of dark matter halos, providing a probe of gravothermal evolution in self-interacting dark matter (SIDM). We combine semi-analytic cold dark matter (CDM) subhalo histories with a model for gravothermal evolution in SIDM to generate CDM and SIDM subhalo populations across SIDM parameter space. We predict their projected enclosed masses and deflection-based projected density slopes and compare them with three perturbers inferred through gravitational imaging in PJ011646, SDSS J0946+1006, and JVAS B1938+666. For SIDM model parameters $\sigma_0/m_\chi=147.1$ cm$^2$/g and $w=120$ km/s, SIDM subhalos reproduce all three perturbers more often than CDM subhalos. For PJ011646, we find ~0.6 cored SIDM subhalos per host matching the perturber, compared with ~0.002 in CDM, while core-collapsed subhalos are too steep. For SDSS J0946+1006, we find ~3 core-collapsed and ~0.004 cored SIDM subhalos per host, compared with none in 900 CDM realizations, although baryons, neglected here, may matter for such massive subhalos. For "detection V" in JVAS B1938+666, we find ~220 core-collapsed and ~20 cored SIDM subhalos per host within the $2\sigma$ posterior region, compared with ~0.2 in CDM; the CDM candidates require exceptionally early accretion, at median redshift $z\sim13$. Simultaneously reproducing all three perturbers requires $w\gtrsim10^2$ km/s, so that the massive (~$5.6\times10^{10}M_\odot$) progenitors of SDSS J0946+1006 candidates can collapse, and is achieved for $\sigma_0/m_\chi\gtrsim10^2$ cm$^2$/g in the parameter sets studied. Our results show how semi-analytic models can quantify how often subhalos reproduce individual perturbers in different dark matter models, and our modular framework can be readily updated as models of tidal stripping and late-stage gravothermal evolution in SIDM improve. The Impact of Inclination, Redshift, and Mass Ratio Selection on Cosmological Constraints from Bright Sirens Beyond Conventional Fisher Approximations

Gustavo B. F. Lima, Rafael F. Aranha, Rodrigo S. Gonçalves
arXiv:2610.32008 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc

The paper forecasts how astrophysical assumptions in simulated bright-siren (binary neutron star with EM counterpart) catalogues affect constraints on H0 and Omega_m from third-generation gravitational-wave detectors. Using the GWDALI framework (a Fisher plus higher-order "doublet" likelihood approximation) with the IMRPhenomD waveform and non-spinning BNS populations, they vary the mass ratio q, the maximum inclination for a detectable counterpart (20° vs up to 40°), and the maximum redshift z_max. A large part of the text reviews the PhenomD waveform construction. The goal is to show how selection assumptions propagate into cosmological forecasts.

Main conclusions
  • H0 precision is driven mainly by event number: below ~1.3% for N>=400 and below ~0.9% for N>=800.
  • Constraining Omega_m and breaking parameter degeneracies requires a deep-redshift catalogue (z_max >= 1.0).
  • Raising the maximum inclination cutoff from 20° to 40° substantially reduces the distance-inclination correlation.
  • Variations in the mass ratio q cause subtle shifts in the SNR distribution and affect the credible-region geometry.
  • Systematics from waveform and selection modelling are expected to dominate over statistical errors in the 3G era.

Introducing the GION Lightcone Simulation Suite for Multi-Wavelength Large-Scale Structure Cosmology

RelatedKen Osato, Satoshi Tanaka, Tomoaki Ishiyama, Hironao Miyatake, Takahiro Nishimichi, Masato Shirasaki
arXiv:2610.32388 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

The paper presents GION, a suite of 10 full-sky dark-matter-only lightcone simulations run with the TreePM code GINKAKU on Fugaku. Each run uses 6144^3 particles in a (1.5 Gpc/h)^3 box (particle mass 1.28e9 Msun/h) out to z=5, with on-the-fly lightcone output, MPI-Rockstar halo finding and HEALPix (Nside=8192) density and momentum shells. From these the authors build halo catalogs, DESI-like mock LRG/ELG samples, Born-approximation convergence maps, and tSZ and kSZ maps. They validate the matter power spectrum, halo mass function, halo bias, and the angular spectra of all observables, including the kappa-y cross-correlation, against emulator, halo-model and perturbative predictions. The data will be released publicly. The suite is LCDM only (Planck 2018) and is intended as a testbed for Stage-IV and CMB joint analyses.

Main conclusions
  • Matter power spectrum agrees with DarkEmulator2 to within a few percent for k>~0.1 h/Mpc at 0<=z<=4; the halo mass function is reproduced to within 5% for 1e11-1e13 Msun/h.
  • Lensing convergence spectra match theory to within a few percent over 30<l<5000; tSZ agrees to ~10% over 100<l<2000; kSZ agrees to ~10% only around l~100-300 and exceeds the perturbative prediction by up to ~45% at l~5000.
  • kappa-y cross-spectra agree with the halo model to ~10% for z_s=0.5 and 1, with ~15% excess for z_s=2.
  • Mock galaxy spectra agree with the halo model to ~10% at l<~100 but exceed it by up to ~50% at l~1e3 because of non-linear bias.
  • Cost is ~7.8e4 s per realization on 8192 Fugaku nodes; data volume is 10.1 TB per realization. Limitations: single cosmology, dark matter only, Born approximation, box replication.

Abstract

We present the GION (Ginkaku Lightcone Multi-wavelength Simulation Project) simulation suite: 10 full-sky high-resolution cosmological lightcone simulations and their associated products for multi-wavelength large-scale structure cosmology. Luminosity-weighted galaxy and gravitational wave cross-correlations

Charles Dalang, Isabela Santiago de Matos, João Ferri, Raul Abramo, Tessa Baker
arXiv:2610.32436 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

The paper asks how weighting galaxies by luminosity affects galaxy–gravitational-wave (GW) cross-correlation cosmology (the "Peak Sirens" method). The authors split DESI DR1 Bright Galaxies into 5 equi-populated luminosity bins, measure a bias for each, and build GLASS lightcone simulations with per-bin bias. Binary black hole hosts are sampled with $p_{\rm host}\propto L^\beta$, $\beta=0,1,2$. Mock ET+2CE GW catalogues (~70,000 sources) are cross-correlated with the weighted galaxy maps, using a rescaling technique to evaluate other cosmologies and a Gaussian likelihood. With realistic DESI biases the effective bias barely changes, so constraints on $H_0$, $\Omega_m$ and the GW bias are insensitive to $\beta$. The method also recovers the GW bias when the true hosts have a different bias than the observed galaxies, but only at large scales.

Main conclusions
  • With DESI-like luminosity-dependent biases, the gain from luminosity weighting ($\beta=1,2$ vs 0) is marginal, so the method is robust to the host-probability prescription. A toy model with the brightest bin at 4x the bias does tighten the contours.
  • The SNR scales with the product of the galaxy bias squared and the GW bias, $b_g^2 b_{gw}$, so the uncertainties shrink as the injected $b_{gw}$ increases.
  • At $\ell_{\rm max}=110$ the injected GW bias is recovered to ~10% even with a host/observed bias mismatch, with $\sigma_{H_0}/H_0\approx2\%$ and $\sigma_{\Omega_m}/\Omega_m\approx26\%$.
  • At $\ell_{\rm max}=190$ the GW bias becomes biased (probably nonlinearity and scale dependence), but $H_0$ and $\Omega_m$ are still recovered within $1\sigma$, at 0.5% and 5% for ET+2CE.

Abstract

Cross-correlations of galaxies and gravitational waves (GW) can be used to extract cosmological parameters and the gravitational wave bias. In this work, we study how to include galaxy luminosities in these spatial cross-correlations, inspired by the standard approach of dark sirens line-of-sight redshift priors. We present a formalism to include luminosity information in galaxy-GW simulations and study the effects of assigning a hosting probability that depends on luminosity through a simple power law $p_{\rm{host}} \propto L^\beta$ with $\beta \in \{0,1,2\}$ corresponding to uniformly, linear and quadratically weighted in luminosity. Joint Angular-Power Distributions of Interfering Nanohertz Gravitational Waves

Yan-Chen Bi, Yu-Mei Wu, Qing-Guo Huang
arXiv:2610.32809 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc

The paper asks how a finite population of supermassive black-hole binaries makes the realized pulsar-timing angular correlation fluctuate around the Hellings–Downs curve. The authors decompose the orientation-averaged Earth-term correlation into Legendre coefficients $S_\ell$, each split into a total-power term and a wave-interference term. They model the many-weak-source limit with a conditional Gamma law, then add one bright source plus a Gaussian faint background, which gives a non-central $\chi^2$ law. They marginalize over the Poisson source population to obtain joint multipole distributions and covariances, and they derive a multivariate lognormal approximation. They test all of this against Monte Carlo SMBHB populations.

Main conclusions
  • Power-normalized multipoles follow $Q_\ell\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(2\ell+1,2\ell+1)$ for many weak sources. With one bright source of power fraction $f_B$ they follow a scaled non-central $\chi^2$ law, with $\mathrm{Var}[Q_\ell|f_B]=(1-f_B^2)/n_\ell$.
  • The covariance of $S_\ell$ splits into an interference term, which is diagonal in $\ell$, and a total-power variance term that correlates different multipoles.
  • Variance is interference-dominated at low frequencies and population-power-dominated at high frequencies. The crossover depends on the population model.
  • The non-central $\chi^2$ model matches the simulated variances at all frequencies, while the Gamma model fails at high frequencies.
  • A multivariate lognormal built from the first two log-cumulants captures the main features of the joint distribution at low frequencies, though it is less accurate than the full model.

Abstract

Finite populations of supermassive black-hole binaries produce fluctuations in both gravitational-wave power and pulsar timing correlations. We characterize the realization-dependent, orientation-averaged Earth-term angular correlation through its frequency-resolved Legendre coefficients. A hybrid model of bright sources and a Gaussian faint background extends the conditional Gamma distribution in the many-weak-source limit to non-central $\chi^2$ statistics, yielding correlated multipoles after population marginalization. Our analytical variance decomposition and SMBHB population simulations reveal a population-dependent transition from interference dominated variance at low frequencies to population-power-dominated variance at high frequencies. In the many-weak-source regime, a multivariate lognormal approximation captures the main features of the joint distribution using only the first two log-cumulants. These results provide statistical benchmarks for fluctuations in both the overall amplitude and angular structure of the realized correlation.

Non-Markovian Cosmology with Causal Memory Kernels

The paper studies how a nonzero graviton mass changes the pulsar timing array (PTA) overlap reduction function (ORF), the Hellings–Downs correlation in GR.
