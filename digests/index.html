Using fixed $w$ to reproduce evolving $w_0$-$w_a$ dark energy at the field level

Highly relevantYuxi Meng, Daniela Saadeh, Cheng-Zong Ruan, Baojiu Li, Kazuya Koyama
arXiv:2611.29597 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: Directly addresses your core goal of fast, accurate nonlinear predictions and field-level/displacement emulators for beyond-$\Lambda$CDM cosmologies (specifically DESI-motivated $w_0 w_a$ dark energy, with explicit extension to scale-independent modified gravity), using MG-GLAM and Hans Winther's FML library.

Recent DESI measurements hint that dark energy may evolve over time—commonly described by the Chevallier–Polarski–Linder (CPL) equation of state $w(a)=w_0+w_a(1-a)$—which makes training new $N$-body simulation suites and field-level emulators across this larger parameter space computationally expensive. The authors exploit a neat property of the $N$-body equations of motion: when written in superconformal time (${\rm d}\tau \propto {\rm d}t/a^2$), the background cosmology enters solely through $\Omega_{\rm m} a(\tau)$, meaning any two scale-free models with the same expansion history $a(\tau)$ produce identical particle trajectories. By matching a target CPL cosmology to the single constant-$w$ model that best approximates its $a(\tau)$, evaluating outputs at matched linear growth factor $D(z)$, and applying a simple linear-theory velocity rescaling, they reproduce the full nonlinear CPL simulation at all redshifts $0\le z\le 3$ from just one fixed-$w$ run. Validated on 100 pairs of $1024^3$-particle MG-GLAM simulations spanning DESI-favoured cosmologies, the mapping achieves sub-percent accuracy not just in the matter power spectrum, but across field-level phases, displacements, velocities, redshift-space distortions, bispectra, and halo/void abundances—allowing existing constant-$w$ simulations and field-level emulators to be reused for dynamical dark energy without retraining.

Main conclusions
  • DESI-like cosmology accuracy: For a DESI+CMB+DESY5 posterior-mean target ($w_0=-0.752, w_a=-0.86$), a single matched constant-$w$ simulation ($w_{\rm fix}=-0.9748$) reproduces the matter power spectrum to within $0.24\%$ for $k\lesssim 1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$ and $0.8\%$ up to $k\lesssim 3\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$ across $0\le z\le 3$, with density-field stochasticity below $0.02\%$ (meaning phases match almost identically).
  • Broad parameter-space validation: Across 100 simulation pairs with $w_a<0$, the median matter power spectrum error and density stochasticity remain sub-percent up to $k\lesssim 1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$ (and up to the Nyquist frequency for most models), with comparable sub-percent agreement for Lagrangian displacements, velocities, Eulerian momenta, redshift-space multipoles, reduced bispectra, and halo/void abundances.
  • Predictive error diagnostic and limits: Errors in all nonlinear statistics correlate tightly with a single background-only matching error $\epsilon$ (the RMS difference in $\ln a(\tau)$); the method breaks down for models with large positive $w_a$ (tested at $w_a=+0.4$), where early-time dark energy cannot be mimicked by a constant $w$.
  • Efficiency and generality: Unlike earlier redshift-dependent matching schemes that require a separate simulation per output redshift, one constant-$w$ simulation covers all redshifts at once, is independent of the $N$-body code (verified with PKDGRAV3 and MP-GADGET), and can be generalised to any scale-independent beyond-$\Lambda$CDM model (such as $\mu$–$\Sigma$ modified gravity) without retraining existing field-level emulators.

Abstract

Recent results from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) hint at dynamical dark energy, making the accurate modelling of such cosmologies increasingly important for future analyses. In this work, we show that because, when written in superconformal time $\tau$, the $N$-body equations of motion depend on the background cosmology only through the expansion history $a(\tau)$, matching $a(\tau)$ allows us to map one cosmology onto another. As a first, observationally relevant, application of this new method, we match a Chevallier–Polarski–Linder (CPL) cosmology to a constant-$w$ cosmology with the closest $a(\tau)$. The method allows constant-$w$ simulations and field-level emulators to be used for CPL cosmologies, and generalises to any cosmology with no additional scale dependence.

Modelling of post-reconstruction galaxy clustering with a hybrid bias expansion

Highly relevantVicente Izzo Dominguez, Raul E. Angulo, Antonio D. Montero-Dorta, Marcos Pellejero-Ibáñez
arXiv:2611.29911 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: This paper directly extends the exact 5-operator hybrid Lagrangian bias expansion ($1, \delta_{\rm L}, \delta_{\rm L}^2, s^2, \nabla^2\delta_{\rm L}$) and its 15 basis correlations—a core method in your highest-relevance profile—to post-reconstruction galaxy clustering in a form ready for emulation.

Nonlinear gravitational evolution smears out the baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) peak and shifts cosmological information out of two-point statistics into higher-order correlations. Density-field reconstruction partially reverses these large-scale bulk flows to sharpen the BAO feature, and jointly analysing pre- and post-reconstruction clustering can recover much of that lost information—provided we have an accurate model for both nonlinear dynamics and galaxy bias. Here, the authors extend the BACCO hybrid Lagrangian bias expansion (which advects five perturbative Lagrangian basis fields using nonlinear $N$-body displacements) to post-reconstruction clustering in configuration space by applying a single reconstruction displacement directly to each Eulerian basis field. Because the displacement is independent of the bias weights, the reconstructed correlation function remains a bilinear combination of 15 reconstructed basis correlations governed by the exact same Lagrangian bias parameters as before reconstruction. Validated against subhalo abundance-matching (SHAMe) mocks from the fixed-and-paired BACCO simulations and paired with map2map emulated realisations for covariances and Fisher derivatives, this framework separates cosmology from galaxy formation and is naturally suited for building emulators for future full-shape survey analyses.

Main conclusions
  • The post-reconstruction hybrid model accurately reproduces the real- and redshift-space correlation-function multipoles ($\ell = 0, 2, 4$) of SHAMe mocks down to $r > 35\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}$ using a single shared set of Lagrangian bias parameters for both PRE and POST clustering, with the minimum scale mainly limited by omitting small-scale satellite virial velocities (Fingers of God).
  • In a Fisher forecast for a $3\,h^{-3}\mathrm{Gpc}^3$ volume at $z \simeq 0$ ($\bar{n} = 5 \times 10^{-4}\,h^3\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$), POST alone tightens the marginalised $\Omega_{\rm m}$ uncertainty by a factor of $\sim 1.3$ compared to PRE, but slightly weakens the $\sigma_8$ constraint due to a stronger degeneracy between galaxy bias and clustering amplitude.
  • Jointly analysing PRE and POST breaks bias–cosmology degeneracies, reducing both marginalised $\Omega_{\rm m}$ and $\sigma_8$ uncertainties by about one third and boosting the $(\Omega_{\rm m}, \sigma_8)$ figure of merit by a factor of $\sim 2.2$.
  • Both standard reconstruction conventions (RecSym, which preserves large-scale redshift-space anisotropy, and RecIso, which removes it at linear order) yield comparable joint constraints, and preliminary tests indicate that estimating the displacement from a noisy galaxy field rather than the matter field has only a small impact.

Closure of averaged Einstein equations by DESI data

Highly relevantThomas Buchert
arXiv:2611.31041 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: Directly addresses Buchert averaging, kinematical backreaction $\Omega^{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{Q}}$, emerging spatial curvature $\Omega^{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{W}}$, and Räsänen light propagation—core topics in the student's inhomogeneous cosmology profile—while setting a concrete DESI DR2 benchmark for GR simulations.

In general relativity, spatially averaging an inhomogeneous universe does not recover a standard FLRW model; instead, structure formation generates extra "backreaction" terms—kinematical backreaction ($\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}$, from expansion-rate fluctuations minus shear) and an emerging average spatial curvature ($\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{D}}$)—which can be mapped onto an effective scalar field (the "morphon") that mimics dynamical dark energy and even crosses into phantom behaviour ($w < -1$) without any dark energy fluid. Because the exact $\Lambda = 0$ averaged dust equations need one extra closure relation to be solved, Buchert closes them directly using DESI 3-year (DR2) BAO data, testing both the baseline CPL ($w_0 w_a$) parametrization and a parametrization-free reconstruction. He also uses Räsänen's light-propagation framework to check how sensitive the results are to transverse distance calibrations, including a radial-BAO-only reconstruction that avoids focusing-equation biases altogether. The resulting redshift evolution of $\Omega^{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{Q}}$ and $\Omega^{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{W}}$—along with a concrete prediction for the Clarkson–Bassett–Lu curvature-consistency test—serves as a quantitative benchmark that any dark-energy-free general-relativistic structure formation model or simulation must meet to explain the DESI observations.

Main conclusions
  • Closing the $\Lambda=0$ averaged equations with DESI DR2 data requires strong negative average spatial curvature ($\Omega^{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{W}} = +0.895 \pm 0.029$ at $z=0$ in the CPL baseline) and positive kinematical backreaction ($\Omega^{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{Q}} = -0.214 \pm 0.029$ at $z=0$), with the effective morphon field crossing the phantom divide ($w=-1$) at $z_{\times} = 0.405$ without violating energy conditions.
  • Restricting the fit to radial BAO ($D_H/r_d$) alone—which bypasses transverse light-propagation assumptions—keeps $\Omega^{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{Q}}$ and $\Omega^{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{W}}$ stable within $\sim 0.03$ for $z \lesssim 1$, confirming the qualitative benchmark is robust against FLRW distance-calibration biases.
  • Under Räsänen's dust-only light propagation, the focusing mismatch in mean angular diameter distance $\bar{D}_A$ relative to FLRW stays below $0.5\%$ across the DESI BAO range ($z \le 2.33$), but grows to $-2.06\%$ at the CMB last-scattering surface ($z_* = 1089$), far exceeding the $0.03\%$ precision on $\theta_*$.
  • The closure predicts a nonzero Clarkson–Bassett–Lu curvature-consistency diagnostic $\mathcal{O}^{\mathcal{D}}(z)$ (identically $0$ in flat FLRW), reaching $+0.253 \pm 0.057$ ($4.4\sigma$) at $z=0$ for CPL ($2.4\sigma$ nonparametrically), which translates to a $0.2\text{–}0.4\%$ departure of $D_M'/D_H$ from unity at $z \lesssim 0.5$ and $2.3\%$ at the Ly$\alpha$ point ($z=2.33$).

We benchmark the hypothesis that the redshift dependence of postulated dark energy in the standard model, measured by the DESI collaboration, is a natural outcome of dark energy-free models of structure formation in general relativity. A hybrid effective field theory description of diffuse baryons

Highly relevantRyan Raikman, Kiyoshi Masui, James M. Sullivan, Josh Borrow
arXiv:2611.31069 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: This paper is centrally about Hybrid Effective Field Theory (HEFT) using advected Lagrangian bias basis fields from dark-matter-only N-body simulations, which is a core topic in your research profile, applied here to baryonic feedback systematics relevant to weak lensing.

Baryonic feedback—processes like supernovae and active galactic nuclei pushing gas out of halos—is a major theoretical uncertainty on small scales for weak lensing and for direct probes of free electrons such as Fast Radio Burst dispersion measures (FRB DM, the frequency-dependent arrival delay of radio pulses through ionized gas) and the kinetic Sunyaev–Zeldovich (kSZ) effect (CMB temperature shifts from photons scattering off moving electrons). To model the clustering of diffuse ionized gas into the non-linear regime without introducing many nuisance parameters, the authors build a Hybrid Effective Field Theory (HEFT) model that uses the full gravitational displacement field from a dark-matter-only $N$-body simulation and adds just two effective parameters: a Lorentzian feedback suppression scale $c_f^2$ (treating feedback as a small-scale pseudo-pressure) and a single linear Lagrangian bias $b_{1,d}^L$ (justified because $\sim 95\%$ of baryons remain diffuse, suppressing higher-order bias terms from stars and neutral gas). They validate this prescription against nine hydrodynamical feedback variants in the FLAMINGO simulation suite and compare it to a full second-order HEFT expansion, confirming that higher-order Lagrangian bias terms and stochasticity are negligible. By achieving percent-level accuracy down to $k \sim 1$–$1.5\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$ with only two parameters, the model provides a compact way to constrain baryonic feedback with FRB DM and kSZ observations and inform weak-lensing cosmology.

Main conclusions
  • A minimal two-parameter HEFT model—combining a dark-matter-only $N$-body displacement field with a linear Lagrangian bias $b_{1,d}^L$ and a Lorentzian feedback suppression $(1 + k^2 c_f^2)^{-2}$—reproduces the diffuse electron auto-power spectrum $P_{\rm dd}(k,z)$ to $\mathcal{O}(1\%)$ accuracy out to $k = 1\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$ at $z = 0.5$ and $k = 1.5\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$ at $z = 1.0$ across most of the nine FLAMINGO feedback variants.
  • For the most extreme feedback variant ("Jet, fgas $-4\sigma$"), the model's range of validity shrinks to $k_{\max} = 0.75\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$ at $z = 0.5$ and $1\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$ at $z = 1.0$, consistent with the physical expectation that the effective feedback expansion breaks down near the inverse feedback displacement scale $k_{\max} \sim R_{\rm fdb}^{-1}$.
  • Fitting a full second-order HEFT expansion confirms that higher-order Lagrangian bias operators ($b_{2,d}^L$, $b_{\mathcal{G}_2,d}^L$) and baryon–CDM stochasticity contribute negligibly on these scales, validating the near-conservation argument that because only $\epsilon_{n*} \sim 5\%$ of baryons are locked in stars or neutral gas, non-linear bias terms are strongly suppressed.
  • Because feedback effects are amplified by a factor of $\Omega_m/\Omega_b \sim 6$ in baryon-tracer cross-correlations relative to the total matter power spectrum, measuring $c_f^2$ with FRB DM or kSZ data can directly constrain feedback models and tighten baryonic priors for weak-lensing constraints on $S_8$ and neutrino mass.

Abstract

Galaxy feedback mechanisms that displace baryonic gas on large scales are a dominant uncertainty in many cosmological analyses. RelevantPavel Demidov, Carolina Cuesta-Lazaro, Peter L. Taylor
arXiv:2611.29776 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: This paper introduces a practical, field-level shortcut for generating fast, accurate non-linear and emulator-based predictions for dynamical dark energy ($w(z)$) models without running new simulation suites, directly complementing your work on beyond-$\Lambda$CDM emulators and Euclid non-linear modelling.

Recent DESI measurements hint that dark energy's equation of state $w(z)$ may evolve with time, usually parametrized by the simple two-parameter CPL form $w(a) = w_0 + w_a(1-a)$, yet building $N$-body simulation suites and emulators for arbitrary, non-parametric $w(z)$ shapes is computationally prohibitive. This paper extends "spectral equivalence"—a method that matches any dynamical dark energy model to a simpler constant-$w_0$ ($w_0$CDM) simulation by equating the linear clustering amplitude $\sigma_8$ and the comoving distance to the CMB last scattering surface, which effectively matches the recent average expansion history—from the power spectrum all the way to the full 3D matter density field. Using $1024^3$-particle Gadget-III $N$-body simulations at $z=0$ with identical initial seeds, the authors test a DESI-like CPL model and two exotic spline-based $w(z)$ models (including an "adversarial" one designed to break the method) against a single $w_0 = -0.8818$ predictor across the power spectrum, bispectrum, one-point density PDF, wavelet scattering transform (WST, a non-Gaussian summary using multi-scale wavelets), and mode-by-mode field residuals. They show that existing $w_0$CDM simulation suites and emulators can already predict non-linear and non-Gaussian clustering for virtually any smooth $w(z)$ model at sub-percent accuracy, opening the door to field-level constraints on general dark energy with surveys like Euclid and DESI.

Main conclusions
  • Power spectrum & bispectrum accuracy: Across all matched $w(z)$ models (including an adversarial spline), the non-linear matter power spectrum agrees with the single $w_0$CDM predictor within $0.2\%$ up to $k = 0.5\,h\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$ and $0.6\%$ up to $k = 1\,h\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$, while the bispectrum agrees within $0.26\%$ and $1.75\%$ on the same scales—well below typical baryonic and galaxy-bias uncertainties.
  • Field-level & non-Gaussian agreement: The field-level decorrelation $1 - r^2(k)$ between matched target fields and the $w_0$CDM predictor stays below $0.006\%$ ($r > 0.9999$) up to $k = 1\,h\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$, one-point density PDFs match within $0.2\%$ across $97.3\%$ of cells (and $2.3\%$ in the noisy tails), and WST coefficients agree within $0.05\%$ (under $0.25\sigma$ of cosmic variance).
  • Velocities & redshift space: Naive velocity fields fail because the linear prefactor $aHf$ differs between models, but applying a simple linear-theory rescaling to halo bulk velocities restores accurate redshift-space galaxy clustering.
  • Emulator inference: A toy power-spectrum analysis in the $w_0w_a$ parameter space shows that a $w_0$CDM-only emulator paired with spectral equivalence recovers the same cosmological posterior as a full $w_0w_a$ model, eliminating the need for massive new $N$-body suites for each dark energy parametrization.

Abstract

We show that clustering in general dynamical dark energy models $w(z)$ can be predicted with constant dark energy $w_0\text{CDM}$ models at the field level via spectral equivalence. The prescription of this method requires equating comoving distances to the last scattering surface between predictor and target models, which is equivalent to requiring equal recent background expansion history averages. Dark energy models with the same distance thus form approximate equivalence classes, with nearly identical matter fields. Redshift-space 3$\times$2pt statistics of galaxy clustering and intrinsic alignments at one-loop order

RelevantToshiki Kurita, Atsushi Taruya, Teppei Okumura
arXiv:2611.30596 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: This paper develops one-loop EFTofLSS and TNS perturbation theory models for nonlinear redshift-space galaxy clustering and intrinsic alignments, directly matching your medium-relevance interests in LSS perturbation theory and nonlinear power spectrum modelling for Euclid.

Galaxies tend to align with the surrounding cosmic tidal field—a phenomenon called intrinsic alignments (IAs)—which was long treated only as a nuisance systematic in weak lensing, but is now emerging as a valuable cosmological probe alongside galaxy clustering. To combine 3D galaxy positions from spectroscopic surveys (like DESI or Euclid) with projected galaxy shapes (decomposed into $E$- and $B$-modes) from imaging surveys, we need accurate theoretical models in redshift space that go beyond linear theory and handle redshift-space distortions (RSD), including small-scale "Fingers-of-God" velocity dispersion. In this paper, Kurita, Taruya, and Okumura derive the full one-loop redshift-space power spectra for the galaxy density–shape cross correlation and the shape auto correlations using two complementary frameworks: an Effective Field Theory (EFT) model (which expands small-scale velocities perturbatively and absorbs unknown small-scale physics into counterterms and stochastic terms) and a Taruya–Nishimichi–Saito (TNS) model (which keeps small-scale velocity dispersion in an unexpanded damping factor). They show that all required loop integrals reduce to known functions computable with FFTLog, and validate both models against dark matter halo catalogs from $N$-body simulations. This provides a practical theoretical foundation for joint "redshift-space $3\times 2\mathrm{pt}$" full-shape analyses that can break parameter degeneracies and tighten cosmological constraints.

Main conclusions
  • All redshift-space one-loop integrals for the density–shape cross spectrum ($P_{\mathrm{g}E}$) and shape auto spectra ($P_{EE}, P_{BB}$) reduce to loop functions already used for real-space intrinsic alignments and redshift-space galaxy clustering, allowing fast evaluation via FFTLog.
  • For the sky-projected shape field, the one-loop EFT model requires 6 renormalized bias parameters and 2 counterterms, while stochastic terms up to $\mathcal{O}(k^2)$ require 2 parameters for the density–shape cross spectrum and 4 for the shape auto spectra.
  • The TNS-based model extends previous linear-bias cross-spectrum work to include nonlinear density/shape bias and the $E$- and $B$-mode shape auto spectra, keeping small-scale line-of-sight velocity dispersion in a single unexpanded damping parameter.
  • Both the EFT and TNS-based models agree well with halo density and shape power spectra measured from $N$-body simulations on mildly nonlinear scales, laying the groundwork for joint full-shape analyses with surveys like Euclid, DESI, and LSST.

Abstract

Intrinsic alignments of galaxies, long regarded as a systematic in weak lensing, are now recognized as a cosmological probe complementary to galaxy clustering. Exploiting them with spectroscopic surveys requires accurate models in redshift space beyond the linear regime. In this work, we derive the galaxy density–shape cross-power spectrum and shape auto-power spectra at one-loop order in redshift space. Accuracy, Convergence and Consistency of Dark Matter Evolution with the PhotoNs-3 Code

RelevantXiaoyang Zhang, Ming Li, Qiao Wang, Liang Gao
arXiv:2611.30647 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: This is a thorough N-body simulation convergence and cross-code validation study (testing softening, time-stepping, and nonlinear observables against Gadget-3/4), which directly aligns with your interest in simulation methodology and resolution/convergence studies for Stage IV surveys like Euclid.

Stage IV galaxy surveys like Euclid require trillion-particle $N$-body simulations to predict nonlinear dark matter clustering at sub-percent accuracy, which pushes traditional CPU-only codes past their memory and communication limits. PhotoNs-3 is a GPU-accelerated $N$-body code built for extreme-scale runs like the HyperMillennium simulation; it splits gravity into a long-range Particle-Mesh (PM) force and a short-range Fast Multipole Method (FMM) solver, while adding aggressive memory optimizations like coordinate quantization and a revised softening kernel. In this paper, the authors systematically test whether those optimizations introduce numerical biases by running internal convergence suites (varying gravitational softening, adaptive time-stepping, and tree/FMM parameters) and benchmarking four simulation suites—spanning different resolutions and $\pm 10\%$ cosmology shifts—against Gadget-3 and Gadget-4 from identical initial conditions. They find that PhotoNs-3 converges to sub-percent precision and agrees with Gadget-4 at least as closely as Gadget-3 and Gadget-4 agree with each other across matter power spectra, projected density fields, halo profiles, and halo/subhalo mass functions, while running $2\text{–}5\times$ faster. This establishes PhotoNs-3 and the HyperMillennium simulation as numerically reliable benchmarks for precision cosmology.

Main conclusions

Chameleon models are a popular class of screened scalar-field dark energy and modified gravity theories in which the field's effective mass increases with local matter density, hiding any associated "fifth force" in dense environments like the Solar System while leaving it active on cosmological scales. Recent CMB data from ACT favor a slightly less red-tilted primordial power spectrum than Planck alone, putting pressure on many standard single-field inflation models. To address this without modifying General Relativity, the author constructs a fully analytic $k$-essence inflation model—a scalar-field theory with a non-standard kinetic term $K(X) = X^n / X_s^{n-1}$—by imposing a "tracking" relation $\dot{\phi}^2 = \gamma H^{-m}$ between the field's velocity and the Hubble rate. This ansatz allows the Friedmann and Raychaudhuri equations to be solved in closed form, fixing the scalar potential and expressing the spectral index $n_s$, tensor-to-scalar ratio $r$, and running $\alpha_s$ purely in terms of two parameters $(m, n)$ and the number of $e$-folds $N_*$. While the pure scalar-field solution hits a finite-time "pressure singularity" (where the effective pressure diverges even though the scale factor and energy density remain finite) shortly after inflation ends, numerical integrations show that adding classical radiation and dark matter fluids disrupts the tracking condition just in time to avoid the singularity smoothly.

Main conclusions
  • The model yields closed-form expressions for $H(t)$, $\phi(t)$, and the inflationary observables solely in terms of $(m, n)$ and the $e$-folding number $N_*$, with a constant perturbation sound speed $c_A^2 = (2n-1)^{-1}$ that avoids gradient instabilities for $n > 1/2$.

Indirect dark matter searches look for gamma rays produced when weakly interacting massive particles (WIMPs) annihilate in dense, dark-matter-dominated systems like Milky Way satellite dwarf galaxies, which have very little competing astrophysical gamma-ray emission. The expected signal from each dwarf scales with its $J$-factor—the line-of-sight integral of the squared dark matter density—and combining many dwarfs in a joint likelihood strengthens constraints on the annihilation cross section $\langle\sigma v\rangle$. To forecast how upcoming optical surveys will impact these searches, the authors introduce BabyBison, a Monte Carlo pipeline that combines Milky Way-like dark-matter-only zoom-in simulations (SymphonyMilkyWay and MilkyWay-est), an empirical galaxy–halo connection model, and survey selection functions to simulate gamma-ray observations with Fermi-LAT and the proposed Advanced Particle-astrophysics Telescope (APT). They find that although the Rubin Observatory's LSST will discover dozens of new Milky Way satellites, typical realizations yield only modest (<20%) improvements in annihilation constraints because the limits are driven by just a few high-$J$-factor systems. Achieving much stronger gains—potentially probing thermal-relic dark matter up to the TeV scale and testing dark matter interpretations of the Galactic Center gamma-ray excess—will require either discovering rare high-$J$ dwarfs or completing a census of fainter, more compact satellites.

Main conclusions
  • LSST is projected to discover a median of $66^{+20}_{-16}$ (SMW) or $45^{+16}_{-11}$ (MWest) new Milky Way dwarf galaxies, yet in typical realizations this improves stacked gamma-ray upper limits on the dark matter annihilation cross section $\langle\sigma v\rangle$ by less than 20%.

  • A complete "ultimate" census of Milky Way satellites—including faint, compact systems ($M_V > 0$, $r_{1/2} < 10\,\mathrm{pc}$) excluded from the baseline LSST sample—shifts the highest expected $J$-factors from $\log_{10}[J/(\mathrm{GeV^2\,cm^{-5}})] \sim 19.8$ to $\sim 20.5$, allowing Fermi-LAT to test thermal-relic dark matter up to several hundred GeV and the proposed APT to reach the TeV scale.
