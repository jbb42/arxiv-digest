Beyond the Peak: Constraining early dark energy with the damping tail

RelevantHao Wang, Gen Ye, Yun-Song Piao
arXiv:2611.27069 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, hep-th
Why: The paper investigates early dark energy scalar field dynamics and cosmological constraints (Planck, DESI BAO, Pantheon+) targeting the Hubble tension, matching medium relevance topics in Jonas's research profile.

Early Dark Energy (EDE) is a prominent scalar-field proposal to alleviate the Hubble ($H_0$) tension by introducing a transient energy fraction near recombination, which reduces the sound horizon size. Standard EDE analyses parameterize the scalar field dynamics primarily through the peak energy fraction ($f_{\mathrm{EDE}}$) and transition redshift ($z_c$), ignoring post-peak dynamics. In this work, the authors introduce a multiscale potential combining an axion-like regime ($\phi>0$) with an $\alpha$-attractor-like regime ($\phi<0$) to explicitly test how the post-peak scalar field evolution (the "EDE tail") affects observables. Implementing the model in a modified AxiCLASS/CLASS Boltzmann solver, they constrain the scenario using Planck NPIPE high-$\ell$ CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, and PantheonPlusSH0ES data. They demonstrate that the high-$\ell$ CMB damping tail is sensitive to post-peak field dynamics (such as sharp field reflections or transient secondary slow-roll injections), providing enhanced fit improvements and modified inferred cosmological parameters relative to standard axion EDE.

Main conclusions
  • The post-peak dynamics ('EDE tail') of scalar field EDE models alter sub-horizon perturbation evolution and leave distinct observable signatures on the high-$\ell$ CMB damping tail, going beyond the traditional $f_{\mathrm{EDE}}$ and $z_c$ peak parameterization.
  • The proposed Axion-\alpha$-attractor (AtR) EDE model improves the fit to combined Planck NPIPE, DESI DR2 BAO, and PantheonPlusSH0ES data by $\Delta \chi^2 = -3.34$ relative to standard AxionEDE (and $\Delta \chi^2 = -32.67$ relative to $\Lambda$CDM), driven mainly by the high-$\ell$ CMB data.
  • The best-fit AtR model yields an inferred Hubble constant of $H_0 \approx 72.3 \text{ km s}^{-1}\text{Mpc}^{-1}$, showing that modified EDE tail dynamics can achieve higher best-fit $H_0$ values.
  • Different EDE tail dynamics (e.g., steep field reflection vs. secondary slow-roll energy injection) produce distinguishable features in the gravitational (Weyl) potential evolution $\Delta \Phi_W / \Phi_W$ and small-scale CMB $TT$/$EE$ power spectra, which can be probed by future CMB-S4 and Simons Observatory measurements.

Abstract

Early dark energy is commonly characterized by the amplitude and location of its primary energy-injection peak. We show that its post-peak evolution, which we call the EDE tail, provides an additional observable probe of the underlying scalar dynamics and can materially affect its ability to alleviate the Hubble tension. We construct a simple phenomenological framework in which distinct regions of a multiscale scalar potential govern the dynamics at different field scales. As a representative realization, we consider a potential that combines axion-like and $\alpha$-attractor-like regimes, thereby allowing the EDE tail to be reshaped through reflection and asymmetric oscillations. Confronting this framework with cosmic microwave background, baryon acoustic oscillation, and supernova observations, we find an improved fit to the high-$\ell$ CMB data together with a higher inferred bestfit $H_0$. Our results show that the EDE tail retains observable information about the underlying scalar potential that is not captured by the conventional peak parameters alone. This provides a complementary route for constructing and observationally distinguishing EDE scenarios, and potentially for further alleviating the Hubble tension.

Fast and accurate large-volume Lyman-$α$ forest mocks with three-dimensional U-Net trained on IllustrisTNG and FLAMINGO

RelevantBhaskar Arya, Soumak Maitra, Shadab Alam
arXiv:2611.27151 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: This paper is a useful methodological reference for your work on fast approximate simulations and ML emulators, as it demonstrates field-level emulation from 2LPT and $N$-body inputs and validates small-to-large-box volume transfer, though its target observable is the Lyman-$\alpha$ forest rather than Euclid galaxy/lensing observables or beyond-$\Lambda$CDM gravity.

The Lyman-$\alpha$ forest consists of absorption features in distant quasar spectra caused by neutral hydrogen in the intergalactic medium, making it a powerful probe of cosmic structure at $z \gtrsim 2$, though creating Gpc-scale mock catalogues with full hydrodynamical simulations is computationally prohibitive. To overcome this, the authors train an anisotropic three-dimensional U-Net (a convolutional neural network) to map inexpensive gravity-only dark matter density and line-of-sight velocity fields directly to the Lyman-$\alpha$ optical depth and transmitted flux at $z \sim 3$. They test whether a network trained on a small box can generalize to a $\sim 20\times$ larger independent volume in two suites: IllustrisTNG ($110\,\mathrm{cMpc}$ to $300\,\mathrm{cMpc}$ using both 2LPT and $N$-body inputs) and FLAMINGO ($1\,\mathrm{cGpc}$ to $2.8\,\mathrm{cGpc}$ using $N$-body inputs). This field-level emulation approach successfully preserves both small-scale non-Gaussian physics and large-scale modes beyond the training box size, offering a practical way to generate Gpc-scale mocks for spectroscopic surveys like DESI.

Main conclusions
  • Within each simulation suite, the 3D U-Net generalizes to $\sim 20\times$ larger volumes without degrading, recovering the 1D flux power spectrum $P_{\rm 1D}$ to $2.8\%$ in IllustrisTNG and $5.6\%$ in FLAMINGO ($2$–$3\%$ for $k \le 1\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$), compared to $17$–$35\%$ errors from the standard Fluctuating Gunn-Peterson Approximation (FGPA).
  • Non-Gaussian statistics that were never targeted during training are also accurately reproduced: the 1D flux bispectrum is recovered to $4$–$5\%$ and the connected trispectrum (which governs non-Gaussian power-spectrum covariance) to $10$–$16\%$, vastly outperforming FGPA ($60$–$97\%$ discrepancies).
  • In IllustrisTNG, training the emulator on fast 2LPT inputs yields $P_{\rm 1D}$ predictions consistent with $N$-body inputs (a $3.4\%$ mean difference), showing that approximate gravity solvers are sufficient at this resolution.
  • Cross-suite transfer remains challenging: a network trained on downsampled IllustrisTNG fields underestimates FLAMINGO's $P_{\rm 1D}$ by $\sim 10\%$ or more, reflecting genuine physical differences in cosmology, thermal history, and feedback between the suites.

Abstract

The Lyman-$\alpha$ forest probes matter structure at $z\gtrsim2$, constraining baryon acoustic oscillations, neutrino masses, and dark matter properties. Interpreting current and upcoming spectroscopic surveys requires mock catalogues spanning Gpc-scale volumes while capturing small-scale intergalactic medium physics. This combination of high resolution and large volume is prohibitively expensive for hydrodynamical simulations. We present a field-level emulator, based on a three-dimensional U-Net, that maps approximate density and velocity fields directly to Ly$\alpha$ optical depth and transmitted flux. At $z\sim3$, we train in small volumes and test on much larger, independent ones in two settings: IllustrisTNG (training on a $110\,\mathrm{cMpc}$ box and testing on a $300\,\mathrm{cMpc}$ box with 2LPT and $N$-body inputs) and FLAMINGO (training on $1\,\mathrm{cGpc}$ and testing on $2.8\,\mathrm{cGpc}$ with $N$-body inputs). The hydrodynamical statistics of TNG and FLAMINGO differ appreciably over their common $k$-range, by $\sim15\%$ in $P_{\rm 1D}$, $\sim17\%$ in the bispectrum, and $\sim40\%$ in the connected trispectrum, although their normalized power-spectrum correlation structures remain broadly similar. Within each suite, the emulator reproduces $P_{\rm 1D}$ to $2.8\%$ in TNG and $5.6\%$ in FLAMINGO, the bispectrum to $4$–$5\%$, and the trispectrum to $10$–$16\%$, while also recovering the flux PDF and broad correlation structure. Field-level emulation thus provides a practical route to large-volume Ly$\alpha$-forest mocks for current and future surveys.

SciPIC-lite: A flexible Halo Occupation Distribution pipeline aimed at clustering observables

RelatedGabriele Parimbelli, Martin Crocce, Giosuè Gambardella, Alexander Eggemeier, Pablo Fosalba, Elizabeth J. Gonzalez, Sujatha Ramakrishnan, Isaac Tutusaus, Zahra Baghkhani, Jorge Carretero, Francisco Castander, Andrea Pezzotta, Pau Tallada-Crespí
arXiv:2611.26785 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

To connect dark matter $N$-body simulations to galaxy surveys like Euclid—which primarily targets H$\alpha$ emission-line galaxies around $z \sim 1$—cosmologists use Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD) models to statistically populate dark matter haloes with central and satellite galaxies. The authors introduce SciPIC-lite, a new public, simulation-agnostic Python library that builds galaxy mocks on both periodic boxes and light cones, measures clustering statistics (two-point correlation functions and power spectrum multipoles), and fits HOD parameters using differential evolution. Beyond standard mass-only occupation functions, the code natively supports physically motivated extensions such as assembly bias (dependence on halo properties beyond mass), 1-halo galactic conformity (correlations between central and satellite occupation), velocity bias, and customisable satellite density profiles. To validate the pipeline, the authors populate AbacusSummit dark matter haloes across three redshifts ($z = 0.950, 1.250, 1.625$) and fit the small-scale projected and redshift-space two-point correlation functions of H$\alpha$-selected galaxies from Euclid's Flagship 2 simulation, demonstrating that the resulting mocks also accurately reproduce Flagship 2's large-scale clustering.

Main conclusions
  • SciPIC-lite offers a portable, extensible alternative to simulation-specific tools like AbacusHOD, supporting arbitrary halo catalogues, light-cone outputs, user-defined HOD shapes, Jeans-equation satellite velocities, and end-to-end clustering fits.
  • Fitting Flagship 2 H$\alpha$ galaxy two-point correlation functions ($w_{\mathrm{p}}(r_{\mathrm{p}})$ and $\xi_{\ell}(s)$) across $z \in [0.85, 1.80]$ yields reduced $\chi^2$ values that cluster tightly around a common value regardless of the central HOD parametrisation, showing strong degeneracies that two-point clustering alone cannot break.
  • Including HOD extensions (assembly bias, velocity bias, conformity, and halo profile rescaling) provides statistically significant improvements in specific cases, but does not fully resolve the residual offset from $\chi^2_{\mathrm{red}} = 1$, indicating limitations beyond the choice of HOD parametrisation.
  • A suite of 1,500 full-box AbacusSummit galaxy mocks (3 redshifts $\times$ 4 HODs $\times$ 5 model variants $\times$ 25 realisations) built from the small-scale best-fit HODs reproduces the Flagship 2 power spectrum multipoles well into the large-scale regime outside the fitting range.

Abstract

The Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD) framework provides a flexible approach to modelling the galaxy–halo connection in cosmological simulations, encoding the statistical relationship between galaxies and their host dark matter haloes. We present SciPIC-lite, a new public Python library for fast, flexible, and user-friendly HOD modelling, designed to populate dark matter haloes extracted from cosmological simulations with galaxies and to provide a comprehensive suite of tools for their subsequent analysis. The library is built around a modular and extensible codebase that incorporates the full breadth of the HOD framework, from the core occupation functions to a wide range of physically motivated extensions, making it suitable for a broad spectrum of galaxy clustering applications. We validate and characterise the performance of SciPIC-lite by developing a pipeline to fit the two-point correlation functions of H$\alpha$-selected galaxies from the Flagship 2 simulation across multiple redshift bins. To this end, we populate AbacusSummit dark matter haloes with galaxies according to a variety of HOD parametrisations. We assess the robustness of the fits with respect to the choice of HOD parametrisation and the inclusion of physically motivated extensions, including assembly bias, velocity bias, conformity, and halo profile rescaling. The best-fit models are found to be remarkably robust across different HOD parametrisations and fitting configurations, with reduced $\chi^2$ values clustering tightly around a common value regardless of the model adopted. We exploit these results to generate new AbacusSummit-based galaxy mocks that accurately reproduce the clustering properties of the Flagship 2 H$\alpha$ galaxy population.

Kinetic Sunyaev Zel'dovich Velocity Reconstruction Using Vision Transformers

Sophie Coulombe, Matthew C. Johnson
arXiv:2611.26822 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

Kinetic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (kSZ) velocity reconstruction combines cosmic microwave background (CMB) temperature maps with large-scale structure (LSS) density maps to infer the large-scale peculiar velocity field of the Universe. Standard approaches use Quadratic Estimators (QE) that are optimal only for purely Gaussian fields, thereby ignoring rich non-Gaussian information present on non-linear scales. In this work, the authors develop a machine learning pipeline based on a Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture, dividing full-sky HEALPix maps into localized tiles to reconstruct tomographic radial peculiar velocity fields. Using a curriculum training strategy on mock Gaussian data followed by fine-tuning on hydrodynamical maps from the FLAMINGO simulation, they demonstrate that the ViT outperforms standard quadratic estimators on small angular scales by successfully leveraging non-Gaussian mode coupling.

Main conclusions
  • Developed a Vision Transformer (ViT) pipeline to perform tomographic kSZ velocity reconstruction from $N_{\rm side}=2048$ HEALPix maps split into $256\times 256$ pixel tiles.
  • Implemented a novel multi-term loss function (combining pixel MSE, tile-average MSE, and log power spectrum MSE) alongside a curriculum training procedure to isolate the kSZ signal from dominant primary CMB anisotropies.
  • Showed that fine-tuning the ViT on rotated FLAMINGO hydrodynamical simulation maps allows it to extract non-Gaussian correlations, outperforming the standard Quadratic Estimator (QE) on small angular scales.
  • Identified a major limitation in potential network overfitting due to the availability of only a single full-physics FLAMINGO lightcone realization for training and validation.

Abstract

Kinetic Sunyaev Zel'dovich (kSZ) velocity reconstruction uses correlations between maps of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) and a tracer of large scale structure (LSS) to infer the large-scale peculiar velocity field. Standard approaches to kSZ velocity reconstruction use quadratic estimators that are Fisher optimal for purely Gaussian fields. Here, we develop a pipeline for kSZ velocity reconstruction based on Vision Transformers (ViT) and show that this approach can improve the fidelity of velocity reconstruction. This is consistent with the network utilizing non-Gaussian information in the inputs that the quadratic estimators do not, though with the single simulation realization we have access to, we cannot exclude overfitting. Our approach can be applied to full-resolution maps from existing and near-term CMB and LSS datasets.

Information-Criterion-Driven, Non-linear Deprojection of Angular Imaging Systematics for the SPHEREx Galaxy Clustering Sample

RelatedJames R. Cheshire, Sean T. Bruton, Charuhas W. Shiveshwarkar
arXiv:2611.26864 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

Large-scale structure galaxy clustering analyses, particularly searches for local primordial non-Gaussianity ($f_{\rm NL}$) on large angular scales, are severely vulnerable to observational imaging systematics like stellar contamination and dust extinction. The authors present an automated template deprojection methodology designed for the upcoming SPHEREx survey that mitigates these systematics while avoiding spurious cosmological signal loss. Their framework expands spatial templates into non-linear bases (Legendre polynomials or B-splines) and decouples template selection from amplitude fitting using an information-criterion-penalized selection step followed by an unbiased regression. Tested on contaminated lognormal mock catalogues, the approach automatically selects minimal-complexity template sets, capturing non-linear systematic couplings while preserving large-scale clustering signals necessary to reach SPHEREx's target of $\sigma(f_{\rm NL}) < 0.5$.

Main conclusions
  • Decoupling template selection (via calibrated information criteria) from regression amplitude estimation eliminates shrinkage bias and reduces false-positive template selection by 4-10x relative to standard cross-validated ElasticNet.
  • Non-linear basis expansions (Legendre polynomials up to order 6 or B-splines) enable data-driven determination of template-density coupling complexity on a per-template basis.
  • Power suppression from deprojection on contaminated mocks is restricted to $\ell < 32$, reducing large-scale power loss to 8.6-21.1% (for $5\sigma_{\rm clean}$ contaminants) compared to 23.6% when blindly deprojecting an unselected 42-template kit.
  • Using agreement between two distinct selectors (IC-penalized ElasticNet and Iterative Forward Selection) acts as an internal consistency diagnostic to ensure stable template curation.

Abstract

Imaging systematics that inject false, or suppress true, galaxy clustering signals are of primary concern for current- and next-generation galaxy surveys, such as the Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization, and Ices Explorer (SPHEREx) in its search for local primordial non-Gaussianity $f_{\rm NL}$. As statistical errors shrink with growing galaxy samples, systematic rather than statistical uncertainties come to dominate the cosmological error budget. For effects whose spatial patterns are known or well-modelled a priori, a common mitigation approach regresses (deprojects) the density variation associated with a known angular template. In this work we present and demonstrate novel aspects of this procedure as will be applied to the SPHEREx galaxy clustering sample. These include flexible – but, crucially, constrained and interpretable – non-linear basis regression with polynomial/spline bases, and aggressive information criterion-driven automated template selection with an associated unbiased two-stage regression, using agreement between two independent selectors as a consistency diagnostic. The method automatically selects a template set of minimal-but-sufficient complexity while limiting the number of templates actually regressed, minimising cosmological information loss and making modern additive-plus-multiplicative approaches tractable. We demonstrate these features on SPHEREx-like lognormal mock catalogues with injected contamination of various spatial profiles, amplitudes, and levels of complexity. Principled, signal-preserving deprojection of the kind demonstrated here is a crucial step towards achieving SPHEREx's goal of $\sigma(f_{\rm NL}) < 0.5$.

Baryon-Enhanced Strong Lensing Signatures of Self-Interacting Dark Matter Subhalos

RelatedMeiwen Yang, Daneng Yang, Siyuan Hou, Yi-Ying Wang, Yiping Shu, Yue-Lin Sming Tsai
arXiv:2611.27249 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.GA

In self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) models, dark matter particles scatter off each other, which normally creates low-density cores in subhalos and weakens their gravitational lensing signal until a much later phase of runaway core contraction (gravothermal collapse). This paper demonstrates that compact stellar components in galaxy-hosting subhalos deepen the inner gravitational potential, raising the inner dark matter velocity dispersion and weakening or reversing its radial gradient so that core formation is suppressed or bypassed from early times. Using six controlled $N$-body simulations with live stars and dark matter (both isolated and orbiting in a cluster tidal field), the authors recalibrate a parametric SIDM model and apply it along the assembly histories of subhalos in the three most massive clusters of the TNG300-1 cosmological simulation. They show that baryon-enhanced SIDM subhalos produce stronger lensing deflections than their cold dark matter (CDM) counterparts, providing a natural explanation for the observed excess of galaxy-galaxy strong lensing (GGSL) in clusters that lies beyond the resolution of current full-physics SIDM cluster simulations.

Main conclusions
  • Compact stellar potentials capable of suppressing SIDM core formation are common in massive cluster members: in TNG300-1 subhalos above $10^{11}\,M_{\odot}$, $43\%$ have $\Phi_b > 0.25\Phi_h$ and $75\%$ confine the maximum core radius within the stellar scale ($r_{\mathrm{c,max}} < r_H$).
  • Across all six controlled benchmarks after $2\,\mathrm{Gyr}$ of tidal evolution, SIDM subhalos yield larger effective Einstein radii and source-plane tangential caustic areas than matched CDM halos by factors of $1.3$ to $5.0$, even under moderate tidal truncation ($r_t/r_s \approx 2.0\text{–}5.7$).
  • Applying the recalibrated baryonic parametric SIDM model to the three most massive TNG300-1 clusters at $z_l = 0.439$ increases the total GGSL cross section by factors of $1.43\text{–}2.34$ (for $\sigma_0/m = 20\,\mathrm{cm^2/g}$, $w = 500\,\mathrm{km/s}$) and $1.45\text{–}2.65$ (for $\sigma_0/m = 70\,\mathrm{cm^2/g}$, $w = 120\,\mathrm{km/s}$), matching the $\sim 2\text{–}3\times$ excess reported in recent cluster lensing analyses.
  • Beyond cluster-wide GGSL rates, baryon-driven contraction predicts stronger inner deflection angles around luminous satellites (illustrated with the observed perturber G4 in SDSS J120602.09+514229.5), offering a direct observational test via resolved distortions of lensed arcs at known galaxy positions.

Abstract

Self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) is generally expected to suppress subhalo strong lensing through core formation, with subsequent gravothermal collapse potentially enhancing it. We show that compact stars can make a viable population of galaxy-hosting SIDM subhalos more efficient lenses than their cold dark matter (CDM) counterparts. When the baryonic potential weakens or reverses the inner velocity-dispersion gradient, SIDM halos suppress or bypass core formation, allowing inner densities to grow from early times. The evolved SIDM subhalos produce larger deflection angles and tangential caustics than in CDM. Such galaxy-hosting subhalos also dominate the galaxy-galaxy strong-lensing (GGSL) budget in clusters, yet lie far below the resolution of cosmological simulations. We calibrate a baryonic parametric SIDM model to predict their effects in the three most massive TNG300-1 clusters, finding GGSL cross sections 1.4–2.7 times those in CDM, comparable to the excess inferred in recent analyses. Baryon-enhanced SIDM lensing can thus account for the observed GGSL excess and predicts stronger perturbations from luminous satellites, testable through resolved distortions of background arcs and rings.

Features investigated: A detailed study of axion monodromy inflation using CLiENT

RelatedSteen Hannestad, Rasmus Lumbye, Andreas Nygaard, Thomas Tram
arXiv:2611.27265 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, hep-ph

Axion monodromy inflation predicts logarithmic oscillations in the primordial power spectrum, which produce complex, highly multi-modal likelihood surfaces that hamper standard MCMC and nested sampling algorithms. To overcome this computational bottleneck, the authors apply CLiENT, a neural network framework designed to emulate the full cosmological likelihood function directly across all cosmological, oscillation, and nuisance parameters. Using both a conservative dataset (Planck PR4, DESI DR2 BAO, and Pantheon+ SNe) and an aggressive dataset (adding small-scale CMB temperature/polarization from ACT DR6 and SPT-3G), they perform a comprehensive search for oscillatory features up to frequencies $\omega = 200$, allowing for running of the scalar spectral index $\alpha_s$. They find no robust evidence for primordial oscillations: although the conservative dataset shows a peak at $\omega \simeq 51$ (improving $\chi^2$ by $10.1$), this region is disfavoured when high-$\ell$ ACT and SPT data are included, and model selection metrics (AIC, Bayes factors, and look-elsewhere corrections) show the improvement is statistically insignificant.

Main conclusions
  • Current cosmological data shows no statistically significant evidence for logarithmic primordial oscillations from axion monodromy inflation.
  • The conservative dataset (Planck PR4 + DESI DR2 + Pantheon+) exhibits a nominal best fit at frequency $\omega \simeq 51$ with $\Delta\chi^2 = 10.1$, but small-scale ACT and SPT data disfavour this signal (best fit shifts to $\omega \simeq 31$ with $\Delta\chi^2 = 7.4$).
  • Model comparison yields Bayes factors of $\ln B \approx -1.0$ to $-1.2$, weakly favoring the standard featureless primordial power spectrum over axion monodromy after accounting for prior volume and look-elsewhere effects.
  • The 95% CL upper bounds on the oscillation amplitude are constrained to $A_{\mathrm{osc}} < 0.056$ for the conservative dataset and $A_{\mathrm{osc}} < 0.062$ for the aggressive combination.
  • Demonstrates that the CLiENT likelihood emulator accurately learns narrow likelihood peaks in non-trivial parameter spaces, reproducing true log-likelihood values with RMS errors of 0.07 to 0.16 across posterior samples.

Abstract

We investigate features in the primordial power spectrum from inflation, specifically those predicted by the axion monodromy model, using recent observational data. In order to compute posteriors, profile likelihoods and Bayesian evidence accurately for the complex likelihood surfaces arising in this model we have used the CLiENT emulator framework. As our data combinations we use both a "conservative" set consisting of Planck PR4 temperature, polarisation and lensing data together with DESI DR2 BAO and Pantheon+ supernovae, and a more "aggressive" set which also includes small-scale CMB data from ACT and SPT. We find no robust oscillatory feature in current data. With the conservative data the best fit lies at a frequency $\omega \simeq 51$ and lowers $\chi^2$ by 10.1 relative to a featureless spectrum. However, this feature is disfavoured by the more aggressive data combination, whose best fit lies at $\omega \simeq 31$ and lowers $\chi^2$ by 7.4. Fits to featureless data are known to give similar improvements, and the Akaike information criterion, the Bayes factors and the look-elsewhere effect all indicate that neither improvement is significant. The amplitude is limited to $A_\mathrm{osc} < 0.056$ and $A_\mathrm{osc} < 0.062$ (95%) for the two datasets. The emulators reproduce the true log-likelihood at samples of their posteriors with rms errors of 0.07 and 0.16, and the profile likelihoods are calculated with the true likelihood, using the profile capability of CLiENT.

Little Red Dots at Cosmic Noon: Spectroscopy Reveals Declining Abundance but a Non-Evolving Luminosity Function Shape

cross-listYilun Ma, Daniel J. Lyon, Jenny E. Greene, Alberto Torralba, Jorryt Matthee, Ivo Labbe, Andy D. Goulding, David J. Setton, Marianna Annunziatella, Aayush Desai, Eiichi Egami, Xiaohui Fan, Karl Glazebrook, Vasily Kokorev, Jiaxuan Li, Xiaojing Lin, Danilo Marchesini, Themiya Nanayakkara, Luke Robbins, Anna Sajina, Marcin Sawicki
arXiv:2611.26685 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.GA, astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.HE

"Little Red Dots" (LRDs) are a surprisingly abundant population of compact ($r_e \lesssim 200\text{ pc}$), broad-line active galactic nucleus (AGN) candidates discovered by JWST at high redshifts ($z > 4$). They display a characteristic V-shaped spectral energy distribution (a faint blue UV continuum coupled with an extremely red optical continuum) but lack traditional AGN features like strong X-ray emission or dusty torus dust emission. To determine how the abundance and optical luminosity function of LRDs evolve down to "cosmic noon" ($z \sim 2\text{–}3$), the authors searched nearly $20\text{ deg}^2$ of wide-field multiwavelength imaging from the Hyper Suprime-Cam Subaru Strategic Program (HSC-SSP). They identified 266 photometric candidates and performed spectroscopic follow-up on 24 targets with Keck/NIRES, VLT/X-shooter, and Magellan/FIRE to confirm broad $\mathrm{H}\alpha$ emission and estimate sample purity. They found a dramatic drop in LRD number density by a factor of $\sim 35$ from $z \sim 5$ to $z \sim 2$, while the turnover ("knee") of their optical luminosity function remained stationary, implying that the physical mechanism driving LRDs becomes rarer over cosmic time without changing its characteristic energy scale.

Main conclusions
  • Identified 266 photometric LRD candidates at $1.7 < z < 3.7$ across $20\text{ deg}^2$ of HSC-SSP fields, with ground-based spectroscopic follow-up revealing a photometric sample purity of $\sim 25\text{–}30\%$.
  • Measured an LRD number density of $6.8 \times 10^{-7}\text{ cMpc}^{-3}$ ($M_{5500} < -20.5$) at $2.10 < z < 2.55$, confirming a $\sim 35$-fold decline from their nearly flat abundance at $4 < z < 8$.
  • Discovered that the optical luminosity function knee for LRDs stays constant at $M_{5500} \approx -22\text{ mag}$ across $z \sim 2\text{–}5$, falling significantly below the quasar luminosity function knee ($M_{5500} \sim -25$).
  • Indicates that while the physical conditions required to form LRD central engines become increasingly rare at lower redshifts, the intrinsic characteristic mass scale of the central engine does not evolve.

Abstract

Little red dots (LRDs) are an extremely abundant population of red, compact, broad-line objects primarily found in the early universe. At $5<z<8$, they are at least 10 times more abundant than UV-selected quasars. Yet, how this population evolves towards lower redshift is unclear. In this work, we present our search for LRDs at cosmic noon ($2\lesssim z\lesssim 4$) in nearly 20 deg$^2$ of the Deep/UltraDeep fields of the Hyper Suprime-Cam Subaru Strategic Program (HSC-SSP). Combining multiwavelength wide-field imaging surveys from 0.3 to 4.5 $\mu$m, we identify 266 photometric LRD candidates. With spectroscopic follow-up from Keck/NIRES, VLT/X-shooter, and Magellan/FIRE on 24 candidates, we identify a sample of four LRDs with broad H$\alpha$ emission. The knee of the LRD optical luminosity function also remains consistently at $M_{5500}\approx-22$ mag across $2<z<5$, below that of quasars. We further speculate that only the abundance of LRDs, but not the shape of their optical luminosity function, evolves with redshift, and the Roman Space Telescope will be able to test this decisively with a substantially larger sample of low-redshift LRDs.

Physics-Guided Recursive Self-Improvement of Amortized Bayesian Inference Across Physics

Relatedcross-listBo Liang, Mingtao Chen, Minghui Du, Ziren Luo, Guohua Wang, Peng Xu, Zhichao Hu, Maxm Pan
arXiv:2611.27006 · PDF · HTML · physics.comp-ph, astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.EP, astro-ph.IM

In many physical domains, parameter estimation via traditional Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods is severely bottlenecked by narrow, highly curved posterior geometries induced by conservation laws or parameter degeneracies. To solve this, the authors introduce PhyRSI (Physics-Guided Recursive Self-Improvement), a framework where an AI agent interacts with a physics simulator and an exact likelihood function to automatically craft and refine an amortized Bayesian inference pipeline. The agent discovers domain-adapted physical coordinate transformations, local charts, and factorized network architectures, using exact likelihood importance weights (such as effective sample size) as a self-improving feedback score. Across six diverse physical fields—including exoplanet orbits, X-ray spectra, and neutron star equations of state—PhyRSI produces likelihood-reweighted posteriors for 36 real observational datasets in seconds, matching converged MCMC chains while delivering computational speedups of up to $10^6\times$.

Main conclusions
  • PhyRSI automates the design of amortized Bayesian inference algorithms by evaluating candidates directly against physical likelihood importance weights (effective sample size ESS and Pareto tail index $\hat{k}$).

During inflation, heavy scalar fields can interact and mix with inflaton perturbations, leaving distinct oscillatory features in the primordial bispectrum—a phenomenon known as the cosmological collider physics program. Calculating these primordial correlators analytically is notoriously difficult for loop diagrams or tree diagrams with multiple massive internal lines, such as the triple exchange process where three massive fields meet at a cubic vertex. In this paper, the authors obtain an analytical formula for the tree-level bispectrum of three scalar fields with arbitrary, distinct masses. By expanding the mixed propagator carrying the softest momentum, they reduce the triple-exchange time integral to a set of two-propagator integrals governed by inhomogeneous differential equations, which are solved analytically in terms of hypergeometric functions using boundary conditions from a Laplace-representation technique. This provides a convergent double-series representation valid across all triangle configurations (squeezed, equilateral, and folded) and yields explicit expressions for squeezed-limit oscillations.

Main conclusions
  • Derived the first convergent, analytical double-series expression for the primordial bispectrum from triple massive exchanges of three scalar fields with generally unequal masses.
  • Showed that the calculation reduces to two-propagator differential equations whose analytical boundary conditions at equal momentum are determined via a Laplace representation of Schwinger-Keldysh propagators.
  • Demonstrated that in the squeezed limit ($k_1 \ll k_3$), each massive field induces an oscillatory signature in $\log(k_1/k_3)$ with a frequency determined by its own mass $\mu_i$, leading to modulated beat-pattern oscillations when multiple distinct masses are present.
  • Obtained a closed-form expression for the equal-mass squeezed-limit amplitude that eliminates infinite series, matching independent prior derivations.

Among all tree-level contributions to the primordial bispectrum, the triple exchange of massive scalars is the most challenging. Ohmic warm inflation

cross-listAnish Ghoshal, Alessandro Strumia
arXiv:2611.27986 · PDF · HTML · hep-ph, astro-ph.CO, hep-th

Warm inflation assumes that the inflaton field continuously dissipates energy into a surrounding thermal bath during inflation, keeping the temperature high without requiring a separate reheating phase. The authors re-examine the popular 'Warm Little Inflaton' (WLI) framework—which uses a symmetry mechanism to protect the inflaton potential while providing friction via mass-modulated fermions—and uncover severe conceptual flaws. Specifically, they show WLI suffers from uncancelled chiral gauge anomalies and that its friction was over-estimated by orders of magnitude because the inflaton rotates faster than particle collision rates (invalidating the standard quasi-static approximation). To solve this, the authors construct an anomaly-free model using a extra-dimensional $U(1)$ gauge theory (or a ring of $N$ gauge groups), where the inflaton is a Wilson line protected from quantum corrections, and show that in the continuum limit friction naturally emerges as Ohmic dissipation of a fermionic plasma driven by a 5D electric field.

Main conclusions
  • Identifies critical flaws in Warm Little Inflation, showing that occupation relaxation supplies at most 1–12% of the power required to sustain the thermal bath because mass modulation outruns particle collisions.
  • Resolves gauge anomalies and thermal mass mixing by assigning opposite chiral charges and independent bath couplings to the active fermions.
  • Formulates an anomaly-free extra-dimensional model (and its $N$-site lattice ring representation) where the inflaton is a Wilson line whose potential is protected by locality in field space for $N \ge 5$.
  • Shows that in the continuum limit ($N \to \infty$), the inflaton's velocity translates to an electric field along the compact dimension, turning the friction into standard Ohmic dissipation capable of sustaining warm inflation consistently with CMB constraints.

Abstract

We show that Warm Little Inflation is anomalous, lacks a suitable inflaton potential, and cannot sustain the thermal bath, as the quasi-static approximation over-estimated the dissipated power. To address these issues, we propose a new friction mechanism and new anomaly-free models based on one fermion charged under a U(1) gauge group in one extra dimension. The four-dimensional lattice version is a ring of $N$ U(1) groups with one fermion at each site, reducing to an anomaly-free version of the warm little inflaton model for $N=2$.
