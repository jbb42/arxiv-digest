Field-level modeling of halo and galaxy alignments

Highly relevantNickolas Kokron, Mikhail M. Ivanov, James M. Sullivan
arXiv:2610.36027 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.GA
Why: This paper directly extends field-level Lagrangian bias expansion and EFT modeling—central to your research profile—to spin-2 shape fields for intrinsic alignment predictions in Euclid and FLAMINGO simulations.

Intrinsic alignment (IA) of galaxy and halo shapes represents a major systematic contaminant for weak lensing measurements in cosmological surveys like Euclid, yet standard phenomenological models (e.g., NLA or TATT) fail to capture all higher-order field-level correlations. To address this, the authors construct a full field-level Effective Field Theory (EFT) framework for spin-2 shape fields up to third order in perturbation theory using a shifted Lagrangian operator basis. They prove that a theoretical redundancy reduces the independent third-order shape basis from 11 to 10 operators, and construct an optimal generalized least-squares (GLS) estimator with analytic covariance matrices to extract shape bias parameters without cosmic variance. Applying their estimator to dark matter halos in Quijote and hydrodynamical galaxy samples in FLAMINGO, they show that the field-level EFT describes shape helicity fields significantly better than NLA or TATT and enables direct calibration of linear and non-linear alignment priors for weak lensing analyses.

Main conclusions
  • Uncovered a operator redundancy in the 3rd-order spin-2 shape bias expansion, reducing the required basis from 11 to 10 independent cubic operators.
  • Derived an optimal generalized least-squares field-level estimator and analytic covariance matrix that cancels cosmic variance when measuring spin-2 shape bias parameters in simulations.
  • Demonstrated that the field-level EFT achieves a higher cross-correlation with halo shape helicity fields in N-body simulations than standard NLA or TATT models.
  • Detected linear and non-linear alignment coefficients (including density-weighted and tidal torquing contributions) at high significance for LRGs, ellipticals, and spiral galaxies in FLAMINGO, while finding zero alignment for ELGs.

Abstract

We develop an effective field theory (EFT) of intrinsic alignments at the level of spin-2 field fluctuations of large-scale structure tracers, such as the shapes of galaxies and dark matter halos. Our perturbative model includes the non-linear treatment of the Zel'dovich displacement, and contains all necessary shape operators through the third order. We apply our field-level model to the full 3D distribution of galaxy shapes, whose sky projections are a known contaminant of cosmic shear measurements. Our field-level EFT model achieves a better description of the helicity fields of dark matter halo shapes than the standard non-linear alignment (NLA) and tidal torquing + tidal alignment (TATT) models in the literature, as evidenced by improved correlations with the simulations' initial conditions. We perform precision measurements of field-level bias parameters by means of a generalized least-squares problem, for which we derive an optimal estimator and analytic covariance matrices. We measure the full set of linear, quadratic, and cubic shape bias coefficients as a function of halo mass in the $\texttt{Quijote}$ N-body simulations. We then study the intrinsic alignments of galaxies in the $\texttt{FLAMINGO}$ hydrodynamical simulation, focusing on observationally relevant samples: spirals, ellipticals, luminous red galaxies (LRG) and emission-line galaxies (ELG). We detect linear and nonlinear alignment coefficients at high significance for LRG, spiral and elliptical galaxies. We also find evidence for non-zero density-weighted tidal bias coefficient of the simulated ELG. This work supports the notion that EFT-based models of alignments will be paramount in optimally extracting constraints from future shape correlations, and lays the foundation for the development of simulation-based priors for future galaxy shape samples.

Why: Directly relevant to your profile as it introduces a GPU-accelerated relativistic N-body simulation code (extending gevolution/AsGRD) for scalar-coupled dark sectors, explicitly studying GR metric potential slip and non-linear backreaction.

Mass-varying neutrino (MaVaN) models attempt to address dark energy coincidence and neutrino mass tensions by coupling neutrinos to a quintessence scalar field, but linear theory breaks down due to strong scalar-mediated forces and non-linear 'neutrino lumps'. To model this regime accurately, the authors present GAsGRD, a GPU-accelerated relativistic N-body and lattice code extending `gevolution` and `AsGRD` to handle species-dependent Jordan-frame geodesics and non-linear scalar field evolution within a weak-field metric expansion. By executing full non-linear simulations, they show that scalar backreaction and dynamical field feedback alter lump formation, causing neutrino structures to become highly dynamic and spinning rather than catastrophic static collapses. Furthermore, the non-linear coupling generates an oscillating effective dark energy equation of state that crosses the phantom divide ($w < -1$) and produces distinct relativistic signatures such as gravitational potential slip ($ ilde{ u}$-driven anisotropic stress).

Why: The paper presents a major updated local measurement of $H_0$ using ZTF SNe Ia DR2, directly relevant to Jonas's secondary interest in the Hubble tension and SNe Ia distance ladder likelihoods.

Type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia) are a core component of the local distance ladder used to measure the Hubble constant ($H_0$), but standard supernova compilations often rely on heterogeneous datasets subject to complex selection effects like Malmquist bias. The authors perform the first cosmological analysis of 772 SNe Ia from the Zwicky Transient Facility second data release (ZTF DR2), focusing on an untargeted, single-telescope, volume-limited subset ($z < 0.06$) designed to minimize selection biases. Using a Bayesian hierarchical model (Simple-BayeSN) to simultaneously constrain light-curve standardisation parameters, host-galaxy dust extinction distributions, and cosmology, they calibrate the ZTF Hubble-flow SNe against 42 Cepheid-anchored calibrators from the SH0ES program while accounting for photometric calibration corrections and peculiar velocity fields. Their findings yield local $H_0$ measurements consistent with previous distance-ladder studies, indicating that selection effects in SN Ia samples are unlikely to be the primary cause of the Hubble tension.

Main conclusions
  • Inferred $H_0 = 71.6 \pm 1.5 \text{ km/s/Mpc}$ assuming a flat prior on deceleration parameter $q_0$, and $72.3 \pm 1.1 \text{ km/s/Mpc}$ when incorporating a Pantheon+ $\Omega_{m0}$ prior.
  • The result stands at a $2.7\sigma$ to $4.1\sigma$ tension relative to the Planck $\Lambda$CDM CMB inference ($67.4 \pm 0.5 \text{ km/s/Mpc}$), confirming the discrepancy in a volume-limited sample resistant to selection bias.
  • Uncertainties in $H_0$ are dominated by the limited number of Cepheid calibrator hosts (42) rather than the ZTF Hubble-flow sample size or photometric zero-point corrections.
  • Results remain robust under various systematic variations (host-mass step, multi-population stretch, peculiar velocity field models), though simplified host-dust models assuming a fixed $R_V$ shift $H_0$ by up to $0.8 \text{ km/s/Mpc}$.

Abstract

Type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia) are instrumental for a precise measurement of the Hubble constant ($H_0$) from the local distance ladder, yet the data used hitherto for this purpose are inhomogeneous and subject to severe selection effects. We present the first, preliminary, cosmological analysis of 772 SNe Ia from the Zwicky Transient Facility's second data release: an untargeted single-telescope sample whose volume-limited subset (z<0.06) is largely free of Malmquist bias. Why: The paper applies reconstruction techniques and simulation-based validation methods highly relevant to the student's work on non-linear modelling and Euclid-era survey analysis.

This paper extends standard Zel'dovich BAO reconstruction—a technique used to sharpen the acoustic peak and reduce non-linear shifts in galaxy surveys—to the Lyman-alpha forest. The authors apply this method to idealized mocks based on AbacusSummit N-body simulations, accounting for the unique redshift-space distortions and flux-based density response of the forest. By estimating the large-scale displacement field either from the forest itself or from external high-redshift tracers, they demonstrate that reconstruction can significantly improve the precision of radial and transverse BAO distance measurements at $z hickapprox 2.5$. This work provides a crucial methodological framework for future high-redshift surveys like DESI-II to tighten constraints on the expansion history.

Main conclusions
  • Reconstruction reduces the non-linear radial BAO shift from $+0.3\%$ to $+0.1\%$, effectively mitigating systematic biases in distance measurements.
  • In fully sampled mocks, reconstruction improves the precision of radial ($α_{\parallel}$) and transverse ($α_{\perp}$) scaling parameters by approximately $25\%$ and $15\%$, respectively.
  • For a DESI-like survey density ($50\, \text{deg}^{-2}$), the method yields comparable gains, with the figure of merit improving by a factor of $\approx 1.65$.
  • Using external tracers (like Lyman-break galaxies) with $\bar{n} \geq 10^{-3}\, h^3 \text{Mpc}^{-3}$ to estimate displacements is as effective as self-reconstruction, offering a robust path forward that avoids flux-specific systematics.

The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) measures baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) in the Lyman-$\alpha$ (Lyman-alpha) forest and provides a robust probe of the expansion history in the matter-dominated era. Yet, non-linear structure formation broadens and shifts the BAO peak. This paper investigates the minimum cosmic reheating temperature ($T_{ m RH}$) allowed by Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN) and CMB data, focusing on scenarios where a long-lived particle decays into both electromagnetic species and neutrinos. While purely electromagnetic reheating is constrained to $T_{ m RH} o 5 ext{--}6$ MeV due to neutrino depletion and subsequent helium overproduction, the authors demonstrate that mixed decays can satisfy observational constraints down to $T_{ m RH} o 0.4$ MeV. By directly injecting neutrinos, these models compensate for the neutrino deficit, allowing for a lower reheating temperature that remains consistent with primordial light element abundances and $N_{ m eff}$. This mechanism has significant implications for neutrino mass density, sterile neutrino production, and the interpretation of cosmological tensions.

Main conclusions
  • Mixed decay channels allow for a minimum reheating temperature of $T_{ m RH} o 0.4$ MeV, an order of magnitude lower than the bound for purely electromagnetic reheating.
  • Late reheating significantly alters the relic neutrino momentum distribution, which can reduce the present-day active neutrino mass density by up to a factor of five for normal mass ordering.
  • The altered neutrino mass density and non-thermal spectra induce percent-level changes in the matter power spectrum, which may be detectable by upcoming surveys like Euclid.
  • Late reheating suppresses the production of sterile neutrinos, potentially reconciling models with large mixings (accessible to laboratory experiments) with cosmological hot dark matter constraints.

This paper investigates the rare phenomenon of Brightest Cluster Galaxies (BCGs) with 'blue cores,' which serve as optical indicators of recent star formation triggered by the cooling of the hot Intracluster Medium (ICM). Using X-ray data from the eROSITA All-Sky Survey (eRASS:5) combined with optical imaging from the Legacy Surveys, the authors identified 379 blue-core BCGs out of a sample of over 11,000 clusters at $z < 0.6$.   • The blue continuum excess correlates strongly with H$\alpha$ emission, suggesting young stellar populations comprising 0.1–1% of the BCG's stellar mass.

Abstract

Blue cores in brightest cluster galaxies (BCGs) trace recent central activity associated with cooling cluster atmospheres. We search for blue cores at $z<0.6$ in five eROSITA all-sky surveys using X-ray data, optical imaging, and optical spectroscopy. We identify 379 blue-core BCGs among 11352 systems (3.3%), although contamination increases toward higher redshift. Of these, 98 (0.86%) are X-ray bright. Their defining signature is a central $g-r$ color at least 0.2 mag bluer than the reference population within $a=20$ kpc before returning to the normal BCG envelope. Ha emission equivalent width correlates strongly with the blue continuum excess, consistent with young stellar components comprising ~0.1-1% of pre-existing stellar mass. Line ratios indicate widespread LINER-like excitation, with a larger star-forming contribution in blue Ha emitters but substantial non-stellar excitation. This activity is closely linked to the thermodynamics of the host-cluster gas. At $0.1R_{500}$, cooling times decrease from BCGs without significant Ha emission to red and then blue Ha emitters. Blue BCGs preferentially occur in clusters with $L_{\rm X,300}>10^{44}$ erg s$^{-1}$; red Ha emitters also favor higher X-ray luminosities. The blue-core fraction rises with optical richness. For systems with $t_{\rm cool}(0.1R_{500})<20$ Gyr, doubling richness increases the odds of hosting a blue-core BCG by $2.45\pm0.39$. This indicates that cluster scale carries information beyond cooling time, plausibly through the amount of gas available for cooling and condensation. The scarcity of blue BCGs without significant Ha emission disfavors isolated short bursts followed by long Ha-quiet fading phases. It can instead be explained by sustained or closely recurrent star formation over timescales much longer than 100 Myr, or by Ha persisting through additional excitation mechanisms as the young stellar component fades.

Abstract

We present a deep low-frequency radio point-source catalogue centred on the North Celestial Pole (NCP) and extending $5^\circ$ in radius, derived from a single 24-h observation with the 21 CentiMeter Array (21CMA). Covering 75-175 MHz, the catalogue characterises extragalactic foreground point sources for Epoch of Reionization (EoR) studies. This paper investigates the potential for the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) to measure the mass of ultralight dark matter (ULDM) particles by observing Extreme-Mass-Ratio Inspirals (EMRIs)—systems where a stellar-mass compact object orbits a massive black hole. In these systems, ULDM can form a dense 'spike' that behaves as a collisionless fluid on the scale of the primary black hole but exhibits wave-like effects (such as modified dynamical friction and accretion) on the scale of the smaller companion. By incorporating these wave-dependent effects into relativistic waveforms, the authors demonstrate that the resulting phase shifts in the gravitational wave signal can be used to break the degeneracy between the dark matter distribution and the particle's microscopic mass.

Main conclusions
  • LISA could measure ULDM particle masses in the range $10^{-14}$ to $10^{-11}$ eV to within an order of magnitude for systems with $M \sim 10^5 M_\odot$.
  • The dark matter particle mass $m_\phi$ and the spike normalization $\bar{\rho}$ are correlated, but the unique radial dependence of wave-driven torques allows for their joint inference.
  • Sensitivity is highest for low-mass primaries ($M \lesssim 2 \times 10^5 M_\odot$) where environmental effects are stronger relative to gravitational radiation reaction at larger radii.
  • Extending LISA's observation time from 4 to 8 years significantly lowers the host halo density required to make a definitive mass measurement.
  • For masses above $10^{-11}$ eV, the interaction approaches the collisionless limit, allowing for precise density constraints but only lower bounds on the particle mass.

Abstract

It is commonly assumed that the mass of dark matter particles cannot be determined from their gravitational effects when dark matter behaves as collisionless matter. Here we show that extreme-mass-ratio inspirals observable by millihertz gravitational-wave observatories such as the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) could measure the mass of ultralight dark matter particles in sufficiently dense dark matter spikes. The separation of scales between the two black holes allows ultralight dark matter to form a collisionless density spike around the massive primary while retaining wave effects in its interaction with the stellar-mass companion. We incorporate wave-dependent accretion and dynamical friction into relativistic inspiral waveforms and jointly infer the dark matter particle mass, spike normalisation and binary parameters from simulated LISA signals. For massive black holes at the low-mass end of the LISA population ($M\sim 10^5 M_\odot$), our forecasts show that particle masses spanning $10^{-14}-10^{-11}\,\mathrm{eV}$ could be measured to within an order of magnitude.

This paper addresses the 'baryon-magnetism degeneracy' in galaxy clusters and groups, where Faraday rotation measures (RM) alone cannot distinguish between strong magnetic fields in diffuse gas and weak fields in dense gas. The authors break this degeneracy by combining $RM^2$ stacks from the SPICE-RACS survey with independent electron density profiles derived from Fast Radio Burst (FRB) dispersion measures and the kinematic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (kSZ) effect. By further incorporating thermal pressure profiles from tSZ-shear cross-correlations, they provide new constraints on the magnetic field strength and the plasma $\beta$ (the ratio of thermal to magnetic pressure) in massive halos. This is particularly relevant for understanding the 'tSZ deficit'—a discrepancy where observed tSZ signals are lower than predicted—which may be partially explained by non-thermal pressure support from magnetic fields.

  • Derived 95% lower limits on the plasma $\beta$ of $>0.31$ for groups and $>11$ for clusters, suggesting magnetic pressure in clusters is at most $\approx 10\%$ of thermal pressure.

Abstract

Magnetic fields are vital in determining the physical conditions of baryons in massive halos. They are, however, notoriously difficult to observe. This impacts problems from our understanding of galaxy formation, to the observed deficit in the amplitude of thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (tSZ) power spectrum that affects cosmological inferences. Although Faraday rotation measures (RMs) offer a direct probe of magnetic fields, it weights magnetic fields by the free-electron density. Several probes of free electrons, including fast radio burst (FRB) dispersion measures (DMs) and kinematic SZ (kSZ) effect, are now maturing into precision tools. We combine RM$^2$ stacks from SPICE-RACS around DESI galaxy groups and ROSAT clusters with optical depth profiles constrained jointly by the FRB DM-redshift relation and the kSZ effect, and obtain 95% upper limits on the rms line-of-sight magnetic field of $<3.9$-$11.6~\mu$G ($<2.3$-$6.4~\mu$G) in groups (clusters) for a 10~kpc coherence length. Combining these with thermal pressure profiles inferred from tSZ-shear cross-correlations, we obtain 95% lower limits on the plasma $\beta$ of $>0.31$ ($>11$) in groups (clusters). Although current constraints are only limits, we forecast a first detection with larger datasets from the Deep Synoptic Array (DSA). We present the first forecast of the DSA RM grid density ($\sim365$ RMs deg$^{-2}$), which when combined with 10$^4$ FRBs would measure the field in groups to $3$-$13\%$ precision, compared to current constraints of $38$-$75\%$.
