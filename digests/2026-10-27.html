FastPM: Fast Pretrained Model for Cosmological Simulation-Based Inference

RelevantMasaya Yamamoto, Adrian E. Bayer
arXiv:2610.33955 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.IM
Why: Directly demonstrates multi-fidelity transfer learning from approximate PM simulations (FastPM, closely analogous to COLA) to high-fidelity N-body/hydro simulations for non-linear power spectrum inference and SBI.

Simulation-based inference (SBI)—where neural networks learn posterior distributions directly from forward simulations without assuming a Gaussian likelihood—is a powerful way to extract non-linear cosmological information on small scales ($k \le 5\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$), but training these networks normally requires thousands of computationally expensive N-body or hydrodynamical simulations. To overcome this bottleneck, the authors use transfer learning: they first pretrain a neural posterior estimator (a normalizing flow paired with an MLP embedding network) on thousands of cheap, approximate Fast Particle-Mesh (FastPM) gravity-only simulations to infer $\Omega_m$ and $\sigma_8$ from the matter power spectrum at $z=0$. They then fine-tune this pretrained network using a small fraction of expensive, high-fidelity N-body and IllustrisTNG hydrodynamical simulations from the CAMELS suite (which also marginalize over four stellar and AGN feedback parameters). This multi-fidelity strategy works remarkably well even when transferring from pure gravity to full hydrodynamics, slashing total computational costs by up to an order of magnitude while achieving tighter and better-calibrated posteriors than training on high-fidelity simulations alone.

Main conclusions
  • Pretraining on just 2,000 ultra-fast 20-step FastPM simulations (costing ~2,000 CPU hours total, less than a single CAMELS hydro run at 3,000 CPU hours) cuts the number of CAMELS simulations needed to reach a benchmark accuracy ($\mathrm{NLTP} = 1$) by 80–90%, reducing the total end-to-end simulation compute budget by $8.8\times$ (N-body), $6.8\times$ (hydro CDM), and $4.7\times$ (hydro total matter).
  • With larger pretraining sets ($10^4$ FastPM runs), the required number of high-fidelity CAMELS simulations drops to just 5–15% of the baseline trained from scratch, saving up to an order of magnitude ($\sim 10\times$) in total CPU hours.
  • Transfer learning succeeds even when bridging the physics gap to full hydrodynamics: although gravity-only FastPM lacks baryonic feedback (which suppresses small-scale power and is degenerate with $\sigma_8$), pretraining captures the underlying gravitational cosmological dependence and avoids negative transfer.
  • Pretraining on $10^4$ higher-accuracy (200-step) FastPM runs achieves a lower asymptotic loss ($\mathrm{NLTP} \approx 0.5$–$0.6$) than training on up to 700 CAMELS runs alone ($\mathrm{NLTP} \approx 0.7$), while maintaining well-calibrated posteriors (verified via TARP coverage tests) with as few as $\ge 50$ high-fidelity runs.

Abstract

Modern cosmology increasingly relies on large ensembles of expensive simulations to model the non-linear Universe and extract information from upcoming surveys. However, generating high-fidelity simulations—whether gravity-only N-body or full hydrodynamical—requires substantial computational resources. In this work, we demonstrate that transfer learning from fast low-fidelity simulations can dramatically reduce the cost of training machine learning models, in particular for simulation-based inference (SBI). We train a Fast Pretrained Model (FastPM) on a set of Fast Particle-Mesh (FastPM) simulations to infer $\Omega_m$ and $\sigma_8$ from the power spectrum, and finetune on a smaller set of high-fidelity CAMELS N-body and hydrodynamical simulations. To reach a fixed posterior accuracy, the pretrained model requires only 5–15% of the high-fidelity simulations compared to the number of simulations needed when training on CAMELS alone, reducing the total training simulation compute cost, including that of the pretraining simulations, by up to an order of magnitude in CPU hours. Remarkably, the gains persist for the total matter power spectrum of full hydrodynamical simulations: although FastPM contains no baryonic physics, pretraining on FastPM runs that together cost less than a single hydrodynamical simulation still reduces the total training simulation compute by a factor of $\sim$5. Moreover, pretraining on more accurate FastPM runs yields more accurate posteriors than training on CAMELS alone achieves with any number of simulations we consider. These results demonstrate that transfer learning from fast simulations is a simple and powerful route to high-fidelity SBI, and highlight the promise of simulation-based foundation models in cosmology.

Comment on "Test of the Gravitational Force Law on Cosmological Scales Using the Kinematic Sunyaev–Zeldovich Effect”

RelevantAlessandro Melchiorri
arXiv:2610.34437 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: Directly addresses cosmological tests of modified gravity and highlights a crucial mathematical pitfall—that spatial/pair averaging does not commute with non-linear field transformations—which is closely related to both modified-gravity LSS modelling and averaging/backreaction in inhomogeneous cosmology.

The pairwise kinematic Sunyaev–Zeldovich (kSZ) effect—tiny temperature shifts in the CMB caused by cosmic microwave photons scattering off the hot gas in galaxy clusters moving toward each other—tracks the mean gravitational infall velocity of galaxy pairs and can test gravity on $30\text{–}230\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ scales. This comment refutes a recent Physical Review Letters paper by Gallardo et al. (2026) that claimed to rule out a deep-MOND gravitational force law ($g \propto r^{-1}$, as opposed to Newtonian $g \propto r^{-2}$) at $3.3\sigma$ using ACT CMB maps and SDSS BOSS galaxies. Melchiorri points out a fundamental mathematical error: because a non-linear function of an average is not the average of the function ($\langle g^{n/2}\rangle \neq \langle g\rangle^{n/2}$), raising the pair-averaged Newtonian acceleration template to the power $n/2$ fails to represent a pointwise modified force law, where a halo's total acceleration is dominated by its broader cosmic environment rather than a single distant partner. By analytically applying Gaussian integration by parts to a linear density field and verifying with numerical grid realizations of density peaks, the author shows that the properly pair-averaged $r^{-n}$ force template has the exact same radial shape as the Newtonian template, meaning the kSZ data cannot distinguish $n=1$ from $n=2$ once the unknown cluster optical depth amplitude is marginalized over.

Main conclusions
  • The previous claim by Gallardo et al. (2026) that pairwise kSZ measurements constrain the gravitational force law index to $n=2.1\pm 0.3$ and exclude deep MOND ($n=1$) at $3.3\sigma$ rests on taking the non-linear power after pair-averaging ($\langle g_N \rangle^{n/2}$) rather than averaging the pointwise force law ($\langle g_N^{n/2} \rangle$).
  • Using Stein's identity (Gaussian integration by parts), the author proves analytically that for a Gaussian linear density field with linearly biased tracers, the correctly averaged pairwise acceleration template for any force index $n > -2$ is strictly proportional to the Newtonian ($n=2$) radial profile, differing only by an overall constant $C_n \propto \frac{n+4}{6}\langle |\mathbf{g}|^{n/2-1}\rangle$.
  • In numerical Gaussian realizations with non-linear peak-selected tracers ($\nu > 1, 1.5, 2$), the ratio between the properly averaged deep-MOND ($n=1$) and Newtonian ($n=2$) pairwise profiles stays constant across $25\text{–}225\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ to within $0.5\text{–}2\%$ (compared to a factor of $3.3$ variation in the original paper's template).
  • Because the overall template amplitude is degenerate with the uncalibrated mean optical depth $\bar{\tau}$ of the galaxy clusters, refitting the ACT + BOSS data with the correctly averaged $n=1$ template yields $\chi^2 = 20.1\text{–}20.2$, identical to the Newtonian fit ($\chi^2 = 20.1$) and erasing the claimed $3.3\sigma$ exclusion ($\chi^2 = 33.8$) as well as the $10\sigma$ forecast for future surveys.

Abstract

Gallardo et al. [Phys. Rev. Lett. 136, 151002 (2026)] constrain the index of the force law $g\propto r^{-n}$ with pairwise kinematic Sunyaev–Zeldovich measurements, $n=2.1\pm0.3$, and exclude the deep-MOND value $n=1$ at $3.3\sigma$. Their templates for $n\neq2$ [their Eq. (8)] raise the pair-averaged Newtonian acceleration to the power $n/2$, whereas the pair average of the pointwise law [their Eq. (30) for deep MOND] is the average of the transformed field; the step that identifies the two attributes to the three-point function a correction that is of second order and not small. What a self-consistent MOND cosmology predicts for this observable is a separate question, which the Letter does not address.

Correlations between Dynamical Dark Energy and Neutrino Parameters

RelevantChing-Yu Leung, WangZheng Zhang, Ming-Chung Chu
arXiv:2610.34445 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: Directly investigates parameter degeneracies in dynamical dark energy ($w_0w_a$) fits to Planck CMB, DESI BAO, and Pantheon+ SNe datasets, and highlights the necessity of non-linear structure growth measurements (like those from Euclid) to break background expansion degeneracies.

Recent joint analyses of DESI Baryon Acoustic Oscillations (BAO), Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB), and Type Ia supernovae (SNe) have shown a $2.8\text{–}4.2\sigma$ preference for dynamical dark energy ($w_0w_a\text{CDM}$) over $\Lambda\text{CDM}$, but this apparent evolution could be mimicking unmodeled physics in the neutrino sector. In particular, allowing relic neutrinos to have both a finite total mass $M_\nu$ and a non-zero chemical potential $\mu_\nu$—parameterized by the dimensionless neutrino degeneracy parameter $\xi_\nu \equiv \mu_\nu / T_\nu$, which measures the lepton asymmetry between neutrinos and antineutrinos—alters the cosmic neutrino energy density and expansion history. The authors use CLASS and Cobaya to run MCMC fits on Planck CMB temperature, DESI BAO, and Pantheon+ SNe data (intentionally omitting CMB polarization due to previously reported tensions in this model), jointly varying $(w_0, w_a)$ alongside $M_\nu$ and the third mass eigenstate degeneracy $\xi_{\nu_3}$. They also perform a semi-analytical perturbation analysis holding the CMB acoustic scale $\theta_*$ and angle-averaged BAO scales fixed, proving that geometric distance constraints alone create a strong degeneracy between evolving dark energy and degenerate massive neutrinos.

Main conclusions
  • Fitting Planck CMB temperature, DESI BAO, and Pantheon+ SNe together under a standard $w_0w_a\text{CDM}$ model yields $(w_0, w_a) = (-0.769 \pm 0.079, -0.68^{+0.31}_{-0.27})$, representing $2.9\sigma$ and $2.2\sigma$ deviations from a cosmological constant.
  • When simultaneously varying neutrino parameters (applying Gaussian priors from previous CMB+lensing+BAO fits), the data accommodate a large total neutrino mass $M_\nu = 0.41 \pm 0.13\text{ eV}$, a third-eigenstate degeneracy parameter $\xi_{\nu_3} = 1.14^{+0.18}_{-0.13}$, and a higher Hubble constant $H_0 = 69.7 \pm 1.1\text{ km s}^{-1}\text{ Mpc}^{-1}$, while slightly shifting $(w_0, w_a)$ toward $(-0.796 \pm 0.079, -0.56^{+0.34}_{-0.30})$.
  • A semi-analytical Taylor expansion around fixed CMB acoustic scale $\theta_*$ and BAO angles closely reproduces the 2D MCMC posteriors, demonstrating that the tight constraint on $\theta_*$ is the primary driver of the correlation between $w_a$ and $\xi_{\nu_3}$ at fixed $M_\nu$ and $w_0$.
  • Because background geometric probes (CMB acoustic peaks, BAO, and SNe) cannot break the degeneracy between evolving dark energy and non-zero neutrino chemical potentials, precise measurements of large-scale structure growth will be required to distinguish between the two sectors.

Recent Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) data from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI), fitted jointly with Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) and Type Ia Supernovae (SNe) data, favor dynamical dark energy (DDE) over the standard $\Lambda$CDM model. Cracking into RACS - I. Measuring the cosmic dipole in the RACS-low3 and RACS-mid1 radio surveys with simulation-based inference

RelevantOliver T. Oayda, Mali Land-Strykowski, Stefan W. Duchesne, Emil Lenc, Tara Murphy, Geraint F. Lewis
arXiv:2610.35207 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: Directly applies simulation-based inference (SBI) to test the cosmological principle and large-scale isotropy via the cosmic dipole tension, connecting to both SBI methodologies and observational tests of inhomogeneous/anisotropic cosmologies.

Under the cosmological principle, distant galaxies should be isotropically distributed in the cosmic rest frame, meaning our solar system's motion through space should imprint a kinematic dipole in galaxy number counts across the sky (the Ellis–Baldwin test) that matches the direction and speed ($v \approx 370\text{ km s}^{-1}$) of the CMB temperature dipole. However, radio and infrared surveys consistently find a number-count dipole several times larger than expected—a puzzle known as the "cosmic dipole tension." In this paper, the authors measure the radio galaxy dipole in two epochs of the Rapid ASKAP Continuum Survey (RACS-low3 at $943.5\text{ MHz}$ and RACS-mid1 at $1367.5\text{ MHz}$) using Simulation-Based Inference (SBI), where a neural network learns the likelihood from forward-simulated sky maps. Crucially, their forward model incorporates pseudo-clustering of sources and a newly identified instrumental systematic: when the telescope's phased-array feed (PAF) receivers heat up during hot daytime observations, reported source flux densities are systematically suppressed. This work matters because it demonstrates how forward modelling and SBI can disentangle subtle hardware and survey systematics from genuine cosmological signals in large-scale structure tests.

Main conclusions
  • Even after correcting for instrumental systematics, RACS-low3 exhibits a dipole direction consistent with the CMB ($\ln\mathcal{B} = 2.3 \pm 0.5$) but an inferred observer velocity $4.03^{+0.72}_{-0.71}$ times the CMB expectation ($\approx 1490\text{ km s}^{-1}$ vs. $370\text{ km s}^{-1}$), reinforcing the cosmic dipole tension.
  • Accounting for the receiver temperature response is critical for RACS-low3 ($\ln\mathcal{B} = -37.6 \pm 1.1$ when omitted); ignoring it artificially inflates the inferred velocity to $9.57^{+0.46}_{-0.43}$ times the CMB value because hot daytime observations systematically suppress flux counts.
  • RACS-mid1 yields a dipole amplitude roughly $5$ times the CMB expectation (or $3.42^{+0.96}_{-0.98}$ times when fixed to the CMB direction), but its direction is strongly pulled toward the north equatorial pole ($\ln\mathcal{B} = -9.6 \pm 0.5$ against alignment with the CMB) due to remaining unmodelled declination-dependent striping systematics.
  • Models assuming pure Poisson noise are decisively ruled out ($\ln\mathcal{B} = -83.2$ for low3 and $-130.5$ for mid1), proving that accounting for source overdispersion/clustering (such as multi-component radio galaxies split by source-finders) is essential for accurate one-point statistics.

Abstract

We measure the dipole in the count of radio galaxies over the sky as seen by the Australian SKA Pathfinder (ASKAP) in the Rapid ASKAP Continuum Survey (RACS). Our analysis focuses on the third observational epoch in the 'low' passband (943.5 MHz; RACS-low3) and the first epoch in the 'mid' passband (1367.5 MHz; RACS-mid1). We find strong evidence for a systematic relation between flux density and the temperature at the time of observation, whereby reported flux densities are suppressed to a greater degree when the ASKAP antennas are warmer. We include this temperature response in our forward model as part of our Simulation-based Inference (SBI) pipeline, generating data realisations that are then used by a neural network to learn the likelihood function. Despite this model, RACS-low3 and RACS-mid1 both exhibit a dipole inconsistent with the kinematic dipole of the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB), adding further evidence for the 'cosmic dipole tension'. For RACS-low3, the dipole agrees in direction but has an amplitude four times in excess, while the dipole for RACS-mid1 is offset from the CMB and has an amplitude roughly five times in excess. Our findings underscore the importance of interrogating the data for systematic effects and showcase the power of SBI in handling them.

Exploring the Limits: Neutrino Mass and Interacting Dark Sector in the DESI Era

Relevantcross-listWeiqiang Yang, Sibo Zhang, Supriya Pan, Luis A. Anchordoqui, Eleonora Di Valentino
arXiv:2610.33729 · PDF · HTML · hep-ph, astro-ph.CO
Why: Directly combines Planck CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, and SN Ia compilations (PantheonPlus, Union3, DES-Dovekie) to test beyond-ΛCDM dark energy extensions, which matches your medium-relevance interest in cosmological likelihood combinations and DESI-era dynamical dark energy.

When recent DESI baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) measurements are combined with CMB and Type Ia supernova data under $\Lambda$CDM, the inferred upper bound on the sum of neutrino masses ($\sum m_\nu < 0.064\,\mathrm{eV}$) is pushed right up against—or below—the minimum mass required by particle physics oscillation experiments ($\sum m_\nu \ge 0.057\,\mathrm{eV}$), and even favors an unphysical negative effective mass when the positivity prior is relaxed due to a geometric mismatch in the inferred matter density across cosmic time. Because $w_0w_a$CDM dynamical dark energy is known to relax this bound by altering the expansion history, the authors test whether interacting dark sector models—where cold dark matter exchanges energy with vacuum energy ($w_x = -1$) or constant-$w_0$ dark energy at a rate $Q = 3\xi \mathcal{H}\rho_x$—can similarly resolve the neutrino mass tension. Using MCMC analyses with CLASS and Cobaya across Planck PR4 CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, and three supernova compilations (PantheonPlus, Union3, and DES-Dovekie), they constrain both fixed-mass and free effective neutrino mass ($\sum m_{\nu,\mathrm{eff}} \in [-1.5, 1.5]\,\mathrm{eV}$) scenarios. They show that while dark-sector interactions can mimic late-time dynamical dark energy and improve $\chi^2$, these constant-$w$ interacting models completely fail to restore positive neutrino masses compatible with laboratory lower limits.

Main conclusions
  • Across all three interacting dark sector models (interacting vacuum with $w_x = -1$, and constant-$w_0$ phantom or quintessence regimes) and all dataset combinations, relaxing the positivity prior yields a preference for unphysical negative effective neutrino masses (e.g., $\sum m_{\nu,\mathrm{eff}} \approx -0.125$ to $-0.182\,\mathrm{eV}$ in the interacting vacuum case), and none of the $95\%$ confidence intervals reach the minimum terrestrial oscillation bound of $\sum m_\nu \ge 0.057\,\mathrm{eV}$.
  • Allowing $\sum m_{\nu,\mathrm{eff}}$ to vary freely alongside the dark-sector interaction boosts the statistical preference for a nonzero coupling $\xi$ up to $\sim 2\text{–}3\sigma$ (with $\xi < 0$, indicating energy transfer from dark energy to dark matter) and improves the fit by $\Delta \chi^2_{\mathrm{min}} \approx -5.4$ to $-5.9$ when supernovae are included, yet the preferred neutrino mass remains firmly negative.
  • Phenomenological energy transfer proportional to the Hubble expansion rate ($Q = 3\xi \mathcal{H}\rho_x$) with a constant dark energy equation of state cannot replicate the high-redshift ($z > 2.33$) dark energy suppression that enables $w_0w_a$CDM to resolve the geometric distance mismatch and relax cosmological neutrino mass bounds.

We constrain three interacting dark matter–dark energy scenarios using cosmic microwave background measurements, DESI DR2 baryon acoustic oscillations, and the PantheonPlus, Union3, and DES-Dovekie Type Ia supernova compilations. Spiralling into Dark Energy: Axio-Dilaton Attractors in Light of Current Data

Relevantcross-listMario Ramos-Hamud
arXiv:2610.34380 · PDF · HTML · hep-th, astro-ph.CO, hep-ph
Why: Directly addresses dynamical dark energy models and likelihood constraints using DESI DR2 BAO, Pantheon SNe, and Planck data, which is a key medium-relevance area in your profile.

Recent DESI BAO measurements hinting at evolving dark energy motivate looking beyond simple single-field quintessence, especially since string theory naturally pairs scalar moduli (dilatons, which control compactification volume or coupling strength) with massless axions in a curved field space. In such an axio-dilaton system, the non-trivial kinetic coupling between the two fields generates a centrifugal-like force in field space that pushes the dilaton uphill, slowing its roll and driving cosmic acceleration even when the potential is steep. The author performs a dynamical systems analysis of an exponential dilaton potential modified by renormalization-group polynomial prefactors (up to quadratic order, $n=0,1,2$) and confronts the background expansion with DESI DR2 BAO, binned Pantheon SNe Ia, and Planck 2018 distance priors. This provides a string-motivated realization of thawing/evolving dark energy that slightly outperforms $\Lambda$CDM without requiring unnatural flat potentials or phantom instabilities.

Main conclusions
  • Non-geodesic late-time attractors yield a dark energy equation of state $w_\phi = (\lambda - 2\xi)/(\lambda + 2\xi)$, which approaches $-1$ without ever crossing into the phantom regime ($w_\phi < -1$) provided the kinetic coupling dominates the potential slope ($\xi \gg \lambda$).
  • Adding a linear polynomial prefactor ($n=1$) leaves the late-time attractors unchanged, while a quadratic Albrecht–Skordis correction ($n=2$) introduces a stable de Sitter minimum ($w_\phi = -1$) when $A\lambda^2 < 1$.
  • Combining DESI DR2 BAO, Pantheon SNe, and Planck distance priors constrains $\lambda \gtrsim 7\text{–}9$ and $\log_{10}\xi \gtrsim 2.5$ (95% CL), driven by early dark energy bounds (which prevent $\lambda$ from being too small) and the need for late-time acceleration ($\xi \gg \lambda$).
  • The axio-dilaton models improve the fit over $\Lambda$CDM by $\Delta\chi^2_{\rm min} \approx -4$ to $-6$ for DESI DR2 BAO alone and $\Delta\chi^2_{\rm min} \approx -1.2$ for the full dataset combination, though the polynomial correction order $n$ remains unconstrained.

Constraining the Inner Dark Matter Profiles of Strong-Lensing Galaxy Clusters

Emma M. Strickland, Zelie Goldberg Little, Jackson H. O'Donnell, Tesla E. Jeltema
arXiv:2610.33370 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

Collisionless cold dark matter (CDM) predicts that dark matter halos have 'cuspy' inner density profiles where density rises steeply toward the center ($\rho \propto r^{-1}$), whereas alternative models like self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) transfer heat inward to form flatter central 'cores'. Testing this in galaxy clusters requires disentangling the dark matter halo from the central brightest cluster galaxy (BCG) and avoiding unrelaxed, merging clusters whose complex substructure biases mass reconstructions. The authors model three strong-lensing galaxy clusters from the Dark Energy Survey ($z \approx 0.4 - 0.6$) that exhibit near-ring giant arcs in Hubble Space Telescope imaging, using PyAutoLens and nested sampling (dynesty) to fit a generalized Navarro-Frenk-White (GNFW) halo profile alongside separate BCG and member-galaxy mass components. They combine this with Chandra X-ray observations to verify that the hot intracluster gas is smooth and symmetric—confirming the clusters are dynamically relaxed—and to obtain independent temperature-based mass estimates, demonstrating that these cluster cores are fully consistent with standard collisionless CDM.

Main conclusions
  • The recovered inner dark matter slopes for the three clusters are $\gamma^{\mathrm{DM}} = 0.85^{+0.09}_{-0.08}$, $0.89^{+0.16}_{-0.17}$, and $1.05^{+0.18}_{-0.19}$, all statistically consistent with a standard NFW cusp ($\gamma^{\mathrm{DM}} = 1$) and showing no evidence for flat cores from self-interacting dark matter.
  • Strong lensing and Chandra X-ray temperatures ($T_X = 2.7 - 6.2\text{ keV}$) yield mutually consistent total cluster halo masses in the range $M_{200m} \approx (1 - 6) \times 10^{14}\, M_{\odot}$ with concentrations $c_{200m} \approx 8 - 10$.
  • The X-ray emission is symmetric and lacks substructure, while the lens models require minimal external shear ($|\gamma^{\mathrm{ext}}| = 0.04 - 0.09$, consistent with zero for two clusters), confirming these systems are dynamically relaxed and well-described by a single spherical halo plus galaxy components.

Emission line stacking on cosmological scales: equivalence to two-point statistics and modelling of the observational effects

RelatedZhaoting Chen, José Luis Bernal
arXiv:2610.33603 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.GA

Line intensity mapping (LIM) measures the integrated emission of spectral lines—such as the 21 cm hyperfine transition of neutral hydrogen ($\text{H}\textsc{i}$)—across large cosmic volumes without resolving individual galaxies. To boost the signal-to-noise ratio, analysts often "stack" (co-add) 3D sub-volumes (cubelets) of the intensity map centered on known galaxy positions from an overlapping optical survey; however, because LIM telescopes have coarse angular resolution (arcminutes to degrees), this stacked signal blends the intrinsic emission of the target galaxies with large-scale clustering from neighboring structures. The authors prove mathematically that 3D emission line stacking is formally equivalent to a quadratic estimator for the two-point cross-correlation function in configuration space (or the inverse Fourier transform of the cross-power spectrum) between the intensity map and the galaxy overdensity field. Using this formalism, they incorporate linear observational effects—including beam smoothing, galaxy selection functions, survey masks, and principal component analysis (PCA) foreground cleaning—directly into the estimator's window and transfer functions, validating their analytical model against lognormal mock $\text{H}\textsc{i}$ simulations. This equivalence eliminates the need for expensive forward modelling of galaxy profiles and drastically speeds up both stacking measurements and covariance estimation for large upcoming datasets like MeerKLASS and the Square Kilometre Array Observatory (SKAO).

Main conclusions
  • 3D emission line stacking around catalogued galaxies is mathematically identical to the configuration-space two-point cross-correlation function $\xi[L, \delta](\bm{s})$ (and the inverse Fourier transform of the cross-power spectrum $P(\bm{k})$) between the intensity map and the galaxy overdensity field, reducing to the average source emission profile only in the shot-noise limit.
  • All linear observational and data-processing operations—such as PCA foreground cleaning, beam smoothing, survey footprint masking, galaxy selection weights, and aperture tapering—can be absorbed directly into the window function and transfer function of a quadratic cross-power spectrum estimator.
  • Validated on lognormal mock $\text{H}\textsc{i}$ intensity mapping simulations (including thermal noise, beam smoothing, and foreground removal), the quadratic estimator's analytical predictions and covariances match direct 3D and 1D aperture-tapered stacking measurements well within $1\sigma$.
  • Replacing source-by-source cubelet stacking with Fourier-space cross-power spectrum estimation eliminates the need for expensive forward modelling and provides a major computational speed-up for cross-correlating upcoming high-density galaxy surveys (like DESI) with large LIM datasets (like MeerKLASS and SKAO).

We also examine potential systematic effects, including the use of subhalos versus stellar particles, the choice of cluster center, and the 3D shape of the halo. We then apply the PEEL model to a stacked sample of nine massive clusters from the MAssive Cluster survey with Hectospec (MACH). The observed PEEL profile is much flatter than the simulated 3D profile, indicating that the simulated clusters have a more centrally concentrated stellar mass distribution, mainly because of an excess of extremely massive galaxies in their cores. Combining the PEEL profile with the total mass profile from the caustic technique, we derive the stellar mass fraction profile of the observed clusters. The stellar mass fraction remains nearly constant at $0.009 \pm 0.002$ within $R_{200}$, consistent with previous observations and with the simulated clusters. The PEEL model provides a practical tool for measuring the 3D stellar mass profiles of large cluster samples from future wide-field spectroscopic surveys.
