A Positive Turn: Coupled Quintessence and Cosmological Neutrino Masses

RelevantTian-Nuo Li, William Giarè, Guo-Hong Du, Eleonora Di Valentino, Luis A. Anchordoqui, Shao-Jiang Wang, Xin Zhang, Rong-Gen Cai
arXiv:2611.14584 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-ph, hep-th
Why: Relevant (score 2) because it explores dynamical dark energy (coupled quintessence) and joint cosmological likelihoods (CMB + DESI DR2 BAO + SN Ia) to resolve the cosmological neutrino mass tension, directly touching on the profile's medium-relevance interests in quintessence and DESI dark energy constraints without involving N-body simulations or emulators.

Combining primary CMB and lensing data with DESI baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) measurements within flat $\Lambda$CDM leads to a geometric discrepancy that drives cosmological upper bounds on the sum of neutrino masses ($\sum m_\nu$) down to unphysically tight or negative effective values, in $>3\sigma$ tension with terrestrial oscillation lower limits ($\ge 0.06\ \mathrm{eV}$ for normal ordering). While phenomenological dynamical dark energy (such as CPL $w_0 w_a$) can alleviate this tension, it typically demands crossing the phantom divide ($w < -1$), which is problematic for simple canonical scalar field theories. In this work, the authors implement a coupled quintessence (CQ) model—wherein cold dark matter exchanges energy and momentum with a canonical scalar field through an exponential coupling $m_{\rm c}(\phi)\propto e^{-\beta\phi/M_{\rm Pl}}$—into CAMB and Cobaya to test whether dark-sector interactions can naturally restore consistency with neutrino physics using Planck, ACT DR6, SPT-3G, DESI DR2 BAO, and DES-Dovekie supernova data. They find that energy flow from dark matter to dark energy dilutes the late-time matter density and alters perturbation growth, relaxing neutrino mass constraints to be compatible with normal ordering within $2\sigma$ without any phantom crossing. This demonstrates that dark-sector interactions provide a well-grounded physical mechanism capable of reconciling cosmological datasets with laboratory neutrino mass bounds.

Main conclusions
  • Restoring consistency with neutrino oscillations: For the joint CMB + DESI DR2 + DES-Dovekie dataset, coupled quintessence (CQ) yields $\sum m_{\nu,\mathrm{eff}} = -0.017^{+0.041}_{-0.063}\ \mathrm{eV}$, with profile likelihood $\Delta\chi^2 < 3.84$ and $9.5\%$ of the posterior above the normal ordering benchmark ($0.06\ \mathrm{eV}$), easing the $>3\sigma$ tension in $\Lambda$CDM down to within $2\sigma$.
  • Competitive with CPL without phantom crossing: CQ achieves neutrino mass limits comparable to the phenomenological CPL ($w_0, w_a$) parametrization (which yields $\sum m_{\nu,\mathrm{eff}} = -0.005^{+0.043}_{-0.059}\ \mathrm{eV}$) through a physical, single canonical scalar field without requiring the equation of state to cross the phantom divide ($w < -1$).
  • Coupling parameters and fit improvement: The joint data prefer energy transfer from cold dark matter to dark energy with coupling strength $\beta = 0.041^{+0.015}_{-0.012}$ and inverse power-law slope $\alpha = 0.39^{+0.17}_{-0.19}$, improving the maximum a posteriori fit relative to $\Lambda$CDM+$\sum m_{\nu,\mathrm{eff}}$ by $\Delta\chi^2_{\mathrm{MAP}} = -5.98$ (primarily from SNe and BAO).
  • Interplay of geometry and perturbation dynamics: Alleviating neutrino mass bounds requires a constructive combination of altered background expansion, CMB acoustic calibration, and clustering/lensing suppression; other interacting dark energy models tested in the study instead push effective neutrino masses to more negative values.

Abstract

In $\Lambda$CDM, the CMB and DESI BAO upper limits on the total neutrino mass are in tension with the lower bounds from oscillation experiments, and analyses allowing negative effective masses favor negative values. We investigate whether a coupled quintessence (CQ) model in which dark matter interacts with a canonical scalar field $\phi$ through an exponential mass coupling $m_{\rm c}(\phi)\propto e^{-\beta\phi/M_{\rm Pl}}$ can alleviate this tension. Combining CMB, DESI, and supernova data, we find that CQ yields neutrino-mass constraints comparable to those obtained in CPL and consistent with oscillation experiments within $2\sigma$ for normal ordering, without requiring the scalar-field equation of state to cross the phantom divide. We further show that it alleviates the tension more effectively than alternative interacting dark energy models that instead favor more negative effective masses. This comparison shows that whether dark sector interactions can relax neutrino mass bounds ultimately depends on the interplay of their effects on primary CMB anisotropies, geometry, growth, and lensing.

From $3\times2$pt to $4\times3$pt: calibrating galaxy bias and intrinsic alignments with galaxy clustering and weak lensing bispectra

RelevantRaphaël Kou, Antony Lewis
arXiv:2611.15471 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: While the paper uses standard Eulerian perturbation theory and TATT rather than your core focus on hybrid Lagrangian bias expansions, COLA, or modified gravity, it provides highly relevant methodology and forecasts for Euclid galaxy clustering and weak lensing theory predictions.

In standard cosmic shear and galaxy clustering analyses (the "$3\\times2$pt" framework), extracting cosmological parameters from mildly non-linear scales is severely limited by degeneracies with astrophysical nuisance parameters, such as non-linear galaxy bias and intrinsic galaxy alignments (where tidal gravitational fields align galaxy shapes and mimic lensing shear). To break these degeneracies, the authors introduce a unified "$4\\times3$pt" framework combining 1-loop power spectra with tree-level bispectra for all auto- and cross-correlations ($GGG, GGL, GLL, LLL$) alongside the Tidal Alignment and Tidal Torquing (TATT) model. Performing Fisher forecasts for a Euclid-like survey with conservative scale cuts, they find that adding bispectra improves the dark energy figure of merit by $\\sim 30\\%$ in combination with CMB data, predominantly by calibrating second-order galaxy bias parameters. To scale the computationally demanding bispectrum covariance to ten tomographic bins, they introduce a filtered skew-spectrum compression scheme that preserves most of this constraining power while also demonstrating how cross-bispectra help separate intrinsic alignments from baryonic feedback.

Main conclusions
  • Substantial Dark Energy Gain: Adding tree-level $4\\times3$pt bispectra ($GGG, GGL, GLL, LLL$) to 1-loop $3\\times2$pt power spectra improves the dark energy figure of merit (FoM) by $\\approx 30\\%$ for a Euclid-like survey combined with CMB data (evaluated across 3 to 5 tomographic bins).
  • Galaxy Bias Calibration as the Main Driver: The improvement is primarily driven by three-point statistics breaking degeneracies between linear ($b_1$) and second-order ($b_2, b_s$) galaxy bias parameters—which enter bispectra at tree level rather than through loop integrals—with galaxy auto-bispectra ($GGG$) alone providing $56\\text{–}65\\%$ of the FoM increase.
  • Disentangling Intrinsic Alignments and Baryonic Feedback: Cross-bispectra involving two galaxy fields and one lensing field ($GGL$) calibrate second-order Tidal Alignment and Tidal Torquing (TATT) parameters, partially breaking degeneracies with baryonic feedback (AGN heating temperature $T_{\\mathrm{AGN}}$) that emerge once the matter power spectrum amplitude is anchored by the CMB.
  • Scalable Skew-Spectrum Compression: To overcome the prohibitive covariance size of full bispectra across 10 tomographic bins, the authors compress the data vector into filtered skew-spectra, retaining most of the constraining power with a $20\\%$ FoM improvement with CMB (and $37\\%$ without CMB).

Abstract

Combining galaxy clustering and weak gravitational lensing beyond two-point statistics offers a way to extract additional cosmological information while calibrating astrophysical nuisance parameters. We investigate this approach by combining 1-loop power spectra with tree-level bispectra, including all auto- and cross-bispectra of galaxy clustering and weak lensing, a combination that we refer to as $4\times3$pt by analogy with the well-known $3\times2$pt analysis. Modelling two- and three-point statistics consistently, with galaxy bias and intrinsic alignments described by the TATT model, we perform Fisher forecasts of dark energy constraints for a Euclid-like survey, with conservative scale cuts. When combined with CMB data, adding $4\times3$pt bispectra to the $3\times2$pt improves the dark energy figure of merit by about $30\%$, a gain that remains stable when using the three, four or five lowest redshift bins of our ten. To extend the analysis to ten tomographic bins, we compress the bispectra into skew-spectra, which improve the figure of merit by $20\%$ with the CMB and by $37\%$ without. We show that most of this improvement comes from the calibration of second-order galaxy bias parameters by three-point statistics, mainly by the galaxy-auto and the GGL bispectra. We also find that baryonic feedback is correlated with second-order intrinsic alignment parameters once the amplitude of the matter power spectrum is constrained by the CMB, and that bispectra reduce this correlation by calibrating intrinsic alignments, almost entirely through the GGL bispectra, a mechanism that could become more valuable with more flexible baryonic feedback models. These results demonstrate the potential of combined galaxy clustering and weak lensing bispectra to improve cosmological constraints from stage-IV galaxy surveys through the calibration of galaxy bias and intrinsic alignments.

Measuring the growth index in the $w_0w_a$CDM background

RelevantÍcaro. B. S. Cortês, Léo G. Medeiros, Ronaldo C. Batista
arXiv:2611.15881 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: The paper analyzes cosmological likelihoods combining Planck CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, Pantheon+, and growth data in the context of dynamical dark energy ($w_0w_a$CDM) and the $S_8$/growth tension, which aligns with your medium-relevance interests, though it relies on linear phenomenological growth modeling rather than non-linear simulations or emulators.

In cosmological perturbation theory, the linear growth rate of matter fluctuations is commonly parameterized as $f(a) \equiv d\ln\delta_m/d\ln a \simeq \Omega_m(a)^\gamma$, where standard General Relativity with a cosmological constant predicts a growth index of $\gamma \simeq 0.55$, with higher values indicating slower structure growth. Recent analyses assuming $\Lambda$CDM have found a '$\gamma$ tension' ($\gamma \approx 0.63$, excluding GR at $>3.5\sigma$), closely mirroring the low-redshift $S_8$ tension, while DESI BAO data simultaneously hints at dynamical dark energy parameterized by an evolving equation of state $w(a) = w_0 + w_a(1-a)$. Here, the authors implement a modified version of CAMB with the Parametrized Post-Friedman framework to test whether adopting a flexible $w_0w_a$CDM background relieves this growth anomaly when fit against Planck 2018 CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, Pantheon+ supernovae, and $f\sigma_8$ measurements. They find that dynamical dark energy leaves the inferred growth index virtually unchanged at $\gamma = 0.633 \pm 0.024$ ($3.4\sigma$ tension with GR), demonstrating that the preference for suppressed growth is robustly localized to the $f\sigma_8$ data rather than background geometry or CMB lensing anomalies.

Main conclusions
  • Allowing a dynamical dark energy background ($w_0w_a$CDM) does not resolve the growth index tension: combining Planck 2018, $f\sigma_8$, DESI DR2 BAO, and Pantheon+ yields $\gamma_{w_0w_a} = 0.633 \pm 0.024$ (compared to $\gamma_\Lambda = 0.635 \pm 0.024$), ruling out standard General Relativity ($\gamma \simeq 0.55$) at $3.4\sigma$ ($3.5\sigma$ in $\Lambda$CDM).
  • The discrepancy is almost entirely driven by the $f\sigma_8$ dataset (from redshift-space distortions and peculiar velocities), which prefers suppressed growth; replacing full Planck CMB data with CMB geometric priors that exclude lensing and ISW still favors $\gamma > 0.55$ at $2.8\sigma$, ruling out CMB lensing systematics as the sole driver.
  • Bayesian model comparison strongly favors the free-growth model over fixed GR ($\Delta\chi^2 = -10.6$ and Bayes factor $\log_{10}BF_{10} = 1.15$ for the full dataset combination), with growth index $\gamma$ displaying minimal parameter degeneracy with $w_0$, $w_a$, or $\Omega_m$.
  • Accounting for clustering dark energy ($c_s^2 = 0$) shifts the theoretical expectation to $\gamma \approx 0.564$, only modestly reducing the tension to $2.8\sigma$; physically, a value as high as $\gamma \sim 0.63$ implies a weakened effective gravitational coupling ($G_{\rm eff} < G_{\rm N}$) at late times, challenging standard modified gravity models like $f(R)$ that typically predict enhanced growth.

Shear, vorticity, and $f(R)$ theories

RelevantAntonino Del Popolo
arXiv:2611.16026 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
Why: The paper models non-linear collapse and halo abundances in Hu–Sawicki $f(R)$ gravity (F4/F5/F6) with chameleon screening—a core model in your research profile—but relies on semi-analytical spherical collapse and turnaround radius tests rather than your primary methods of N-body/COLA simulations and emulators.

In standard non-linear structure formation, the spherical collapse model describes how an expanding overdensity slows down under gravity, halts at a maximum turnaround radius ($R_t$), and collapses into a virialized halo once it exceeds a critical linear threshold ($\delta_c$). In modified gravity theories such as Hu–Sawicki $f(R)$, an additional scalar degree of freedom enhances gravity via a fifth force, which is dynamically suppressed in high-density regions through the chameleon screening mechanism. In this paper, the author develops an Extended Spherical Collapse Model (ESCM) that incorporates realistic environmental tidal shear, vorticity, and chameleon screening for the F4, F5, and F6 variants of Hu–Sawicki gravity to calculate corrections to $\delta_c(M, z)$, the turnaround overdensity $\delta_t$, the turnaround radius $R_t$, and the resulting excursion-set halo mass function. The author demonstrates that while shear and vorticity provide significant dynamical support that delays collapse, existing $\sim 20\%$ observational uncertainties on cluster turnaround radii prevent $R_t$ from distinguishing viable $f(R)$ models (such as F5) from General Relativity, leaving high-redshift halo abundance as the primary discriminative probe.

Main conclusions
  • Impact of shear and vorticity vs modified gravity: Non-radial shear and vorticity from surrounding tidal fields act as effective rotational support opposing collapse, increasing the linear collapse threshold $\delta_c$ by $\sim 20\%$ relative to the standard spherical collapse model; this stabilizing effect dominates over the gravitational boost from $f(R)$, which only modifies $\delta_c$ by $\sim 1.7\text{–}2.5\%$.
  • Turnaround density and radius variations: In the extended spherical collapse model, the turnaround overdensity $\delta_t$ deviates from GR by $>20\%$ for F4 ($|f_{R0}|=10^{-4}$), $\sim 10\%$ for F5 ($|f_{R0}|=10^{-5}$), and vanishes for F6 ($|f_{R0}|=10^{-6}$), while the turnaround radius $R_t$ shifts by $\sim 6.5\%$ (F4), $\sim 3\%$ (F5), and $\sim 0\%$ (F6).
  • Turnaround radius ruled out as an observational test: Because current cluster observations already constrain $|f_{R0}| \lesssim 10^{-4.5}$ (ruling out F4) and F6 is virtually indistinguishable from GR, only F5 is theoretically viable to test; however, typical observational uncertainties on measured cluster turnaround radii are $\sim 20\%$, rendering $R_t$ incapable of discriminating $f(R)$ from GR.
  • High-redshift halo mass function remains viable: In contrast to turnaround radii, the excursion-set halo mass function computed with the mass- and redshift-dependent collapse barrier exhibits enhanced halo abundance in $f(R)$ that grows with redshift, maintaining cluster counts as a promising probe for future surveys.

In hierarchical $\Lambda$CDM cosmology, satellite dwarf galaxies orbiting hosts like the Milky Way (MW) and Andromeda (M31) are expected to frequently fall in together in small groups before tidal forces gradually disperse them. To determine whether observed close pairs are gravitationally bound binaries or transient phase-space alignments, the authors compared 3D positions, line-of-sight velocities, and proper motions from the Local Volume Database against isotropic distributions and Local Group analogues from the HESTIA constrained cosmological hydrodynamical simulations. They find that the MW displays a marked excess of spatial pairs (pairing efficiency $\sim 53\%$) relative to both isotropic random draws and simulated analogues ($\sim 17\text{–}27\%$), whereas M31 remains broadly consistent with an isotropic population ($\sim 28\%$). Crucially, combining spatial proximity with full phase-space kinematics and gravitational binding estimates reveals that only a small fraction of candidate pairs are mutually bound, demonstrating that the MW's pair excess primarily reflects recent group accretion events (such as the Magellanic system) rather than stable, long-lived binary dwarfs.

Main conclusions
  • Milky Way spatial pair excess: The Milky Way exhibits a high satellite nearest-neighbour pairing efficiency of $\sim 53\%$ (retaining $\sim 52\%$ after applying a completeness cut of $M_\star > 10^4\,\mathrm{M}_\odot$), roughly double the $\sim 17\text{–}27\%$ found in the HESTIA cosmological analogues, demonstrating significant spatial clustering over isotropic expectations.
  • Contrast with Andromeda (M31): In contrast, M31's satellite system has a pairing efficiency of $\sim 28\%$, consistent with an isotropic distribution within distance uncertainties, highlighting that satellite clustering properties are host-specific and sensitive to stochastic accretion histories.
  • Scarcity of gravitationally bound pairs: Only $\sim 10\%$ of simulated HESTIA pairs and $\sim 38\text{–}43\%$ of observed MW candidate pairs satisfy mutual gravitational binding criteria ($b \equiv 2GM / (\Delta r \Delta v^2) \ge 1$), meaning relative kinetic energy dominates in the vast majority of associations.
  • Transient remnants over long-lived binaries: Requiring spatial proximity, velocity coherence, and gravitational binding simultaneously leaves only $\sim 19\text{–}29\%$ of MW pairs and $\sim 8\text{–}11\%$ of simulated pairs intact, demonstrating that most associations are unbound remnants of recent group infall (e.g., alongside the LMC) or chance alignments rather than long-lived binary dwarfs.

The astrometric precision of incoherent pulsar timing arrays

arXiv:2611.17069 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.IM, astro-ph.CO

Pulsar timing arrays (PTAs) detect nanohertz gravitational waves (GWs) from supermassive black hole binaries by measuring correlated pulse arrival times across Galactic millisecond pulsars, but because pulsar distances are typically known with uncertainties much larger than a nanohertz wavelength, current single-source searches must operate "incoherently" by utilizing only the Earth term. Treating PTAs as an imaging telescope, the authors derive the intrinsic astrometric precision—how accurately an incoherent PTA can localize an individual GW point source on the sky—using matched-filter map-making simulations across varying signal-to-noise ratios (SNR). They demonstrate analytically and numerically that the singular core of the quadrupolar response function yields a localization error that scales as $\propto 1/[\mathrm{SNR}\sqrt{\ln\mathrm{SNR}}]$, outperforming a standard Gaussian beam by a factor of roughly $1.4$ at an SNR of 6. While an idealized, uniform pulsar array achieves an intrinsic sky-averaged precision of $3.3^\circ$ at $\mathrm{SNR}=6$, the realistic, non-uniform IPTA DR2 array achieves a median precision of $8.9^\circ$ (though beating the uniform limit in the $14\%$ of the sky with the highest pulsar density). Ultimately, this work establishes the fundamental localization limits of incoherent PTAs and highlights that achieving the arcminute precision needed for host-galaxy identification will strictly require measuring precise pulsar distances to unlock coherent phase modeling.

Main conclusions
  • Sharper SNR scaling from singular response: The median astrometric error of an incoherent PTA scales as $\gamma_{\rm med} \approx \sqrt{\ln 2 / 3} \, / [\mathrm{SNR}\sqrt{\ln\mathrm{SNR}}] \approx 27.5^\circ / [\mathrm{SNR}\sqrt{\ln\mathrm{SNR}}]$; the logarithmic enhancement stems from the singular core of the quadrupolar response kernel, delivering an angular error $\approx 1.4$ times smaller at $\mathrm{SNR}=6$ than a conventional Gaussian beam ($1/\mathrm{SNR}$).
  • Intrinsic array precision: For an idealized, dense, uniform array (4800 pulsars), the intrinsic sky-averaged astrometric precision is $3.3^\circ$ at map $\mathrm{SNR}=6$ (equivalent to a $5.2\sigma$ peak detection, comparable to current PTA sensitivities).
  • Realistic IPTA DR2 performance: With the current, sparsely distributed pulsars in IPTA DR2, the sky-median precision degrades to $8.9^\circ$ at $\mathrm{SNR}=6$ due to non-uniform sky coverage.
  • Sky-density trade-off: In the $14\%$ of the sky where pulsar density is highest, IPTA DR2 outperforms the uniform limit ($< 3.3^\circ$), but this comes at the expense of substantially poorer localization across the remaining $86\%$ of the sky.
  • Need for distance coherence: Incoherent searches (Earth-term only) are fundamentally limited to degree-scale localization; reaching the sub-degree or arcminute precision required for host-galaxy identification will necessitate measuring pulsar distances to subparsec accuracy to model pulsar terms coherently.

To measure galaxy redshifts and map large-scale cosmic structure, Euclid relies on slitless near-infrared spectroscopy from its NISP instrument. Because slitless spectroscopy disperses light across the entire field of view without fixed entrance slits, spectra heavily overlap and cannot be self-calibrated from science frames alone. In this Data Release 1 (DR1) paper, the Euclid Collaboration presents the dedicated SIR (Spectral near InfraRed) calibration pipelines responsible for global geometric alignment, wavelength solutions, and spectrophotometric flux calibration across the 16-detector focal plane. Evaluating these pipelines against commissioning data, the authors demonstrate that the resulting calibrations meet all baseline mission requirements across more than 170 million validated spectra, while outlining key refinements—such as accounting for detector ice accumulation and mechanical wheel jitter—for DR2.

Main conclusions
