Nonstandard Dark Matter at Nonlinear Scales

RelevantRafael C. Nunes
arXiv:2611.08636 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, hep-ph
Why: The paper uses particle-mesh N-body simulations to predict non-linear density and velocity power spectra for beyond-$\Lambda$CDM models (IDE and non-zero dark matter equation of state), closely matching Jonas's focus on non-linear structure predictions for Euclid.

Interpreting non-linear large-scale structure (LSS) data from surveys like Euclid requires accurate non-linear predictions for models extending standard $\Lambda$CDM. In this paper, the author extends the particle-mesh $N$-body code pmwd to simulate two non-standard dark matter scenarios: interacting dark energy (IDE), where energy-momentum is exchanged between dark matter and dark energy via $Q = \xi \mathcal{H} \rho_x$, and dark matter with a non-zero equation-of-state ($w_{\rm DM} \neq 0$). Using fixed-and-paired initial conditions and a Monte-Carlo Voronoi velocity estimator, the study measures the non-linear matter power spectrum $P_{\delta\delta}(k)$ as well as the velocity divergence spectra $P_{\theta\theta}(k)$ and $P_{\delta\theta}(k)$ up to $k \sim 3.8\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$. The findings demonstrate that IDE models induce dramatic scale-dependent deviations from $\Lambda$CDM, whereas small intrinsic DM pressure effects remain subtle, highlighting where non-linear emulators and theoretical pipelines need calibration.

Main conclusions
  • Interacting dark energy with coupling $\xi \in [-0.1, -0.05]$ produces large, scale-dependent deviations of $50\%$ to $100\%$ in $P_{\delta\delta}(k)$ relative to $\Lambda$CDM across scales up to $k \sim 1\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$, driven by modified linear growth and altered non-linear mode coupling.
  • The normalized velocity divergence auto- and cross-power spectra ($P_{\theta\theta}$ and $P_{\delta\theta}$) provide complementary sensitivity to dark sector interactions, breaking degeneracies with density field observables at non-linear scales.
  • A non-zero dark matter equation of state $w_{\rm DM} = 10^{-3}$ (with $c_s^2 = 10^{-6}$) produces remarkably small shifts confined within $\pm 5\%$ across all probed linear and non-linear scales.
  • Robust cosmological constraints on non-standard dark sector models from forthcoming LSS surveys like Euclid and DESI will require dedicated non-linear emulation or extended perturbation theory frameworks.

Abstract

We investigate how two extensions of the standard $\Lambda$CDM scenario affect the evolution of the matter density and velocity divergence fields, from the linear regime to deeply nonlinear scales, using $N$-body simulations. We consider two well-motivated scenarios in which the canonical properties of dark matter (DM) are modified. In the first scenario, DM interacts with dark energy (DM–DE interaction). In the second scenario, we allow the DM equation-of-state parameter to deviate from the canonical pressureless limit, $w_{\rm DM}=0$. For the coupled DM–DE scenario, we find significant and scale-dependent deviations from $\Lambda$CDM of order $50$–$100\%$ in the matter power spectrum, persisting throughout the nonlinear regime, with the velocity divergence spectra providing complementary and partially independent sensitivity. For the $w_{\rm DM} \neq 0$ scenario, we find systematic but remarkably small deviations confined within $5\%$ across all scales probed. Our results identify which scales carry the greatest discriminatory power between these nonstandard scenarios and $\Lambda$CDM. We show that testing these models with current and forthcoming LSS surveys, through the analysis of density and/or velocity fields, will require a deeper theoretical understanding and accurate nonlinear modelling before robust constraints can be derived from observational data.

Primordial non-Gaussianity as a large-scale cosmic-web bias

RelevantKilian Peraza Vera, Francisco-Shu Kitaura, Francesco Sinigaglia
arXiv:2611.09362 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-th
Why: The paper introduces a novel, fast emulator technique for PNG that is highly relevant to the student's work on beyond-ΛCDM emulators and hybrid-bias models for Euclid.

This paper reformulates the scale-dependent bias signature of local primordial non-Gaussianity (PNG), typically expressed as $\Delta b(k) \propto f_{\rm NL} k^{-2}$, as a nonlocal environmental bias effect. The authors define a Hessian field $\mathcal{H}_{ij} = \partial_i \partial_j \nabla^{-4} \delta$ whose trace naturally reproduces the $k^{-2}$ enhancement, allowing them to partition the cosmic web into four large-scale environments (knots, filaments, sheets, and voids). By assigning specific weights to these environments, they demonstrate that existing Gaussian halo catalogs can be post-processed to imprint an effective $f_{\rm NL}$ signature without needing to re-run expensive $N$-body simulations. This approach provides a computationally efficient method for generating PNG mocks and suggests a new observational statistic based on environmental tracer occupation that is complementary to standard power-spectrum analysis.

Main conclusions
  • The leading-order PNG bias can be accurately emulated by weighting tracers based on four large-scale environments defined by the Hessian of the density field.
  • A clear hierarchy exists in the environment weights: for $f_{\rm NL} > 0$, knots receive the highest weights, while for $f_{\rm NL} < 0$, the hierarchy reverses, with voids receiving the highest weights.
  • Including the standard tidal-field tensor $\mathcal{T}_{ij}$ as an additional weighting basis improves the reconstruction of the PNG signature at higher wavenumbers.
  • The method allows for the fast creation of PNG-like mock catalogs from existing Gaussian simulations while preserving the original small-scale halo dynamics and clustering.

Abstract

Primordial non-Gaussianity is a unique probe of the physics of inflation, but its large-scale-structure signature is difficult to measure because it appears on the largest, most cosmic-variance-limited modes. The standard view of local-type primordial non-Gaussianity is isotropic: it induces a scale-dependent correction to tracer bias, $\Delta b(k)\propto f_{\rm NL}k^{-2}$, on large scales. Here we show that the same large-scale response can be reformulated as an anisotropic, nonlocal environmental-bias effect. Motivated by the spectral cosmic-web hierarchy of Kitaura and Sinigaglia, we define a Hessian field $\mathcal{H}_{ij}(\bf{x})\equiv\partial_i\partial_j\nabla^{-4}\delta(\bf{x})$, whose trace obeys $\mathrm{Tr}\mathcal{H}=\nabla^{-2}\delta$ and therefore carries the same $k^{-2}$ enhancement that appears in the Dalal et al. scale-dependent-bias term. Diagonalizing $\mathcal{H}_{ij}$ defines four large-scale web environments, analogous to knots, filaments, sheets, and voids, although operating on scales much larger than the conventional nonlinear cosmic web. We assign one weight to each environment and test the construction with the AbacusPNG suite of local-PNG $N$-body simulations, using $f_{\rm NL}=\{-100,-30,30,100\}$. We show that these four weights reproduce the large-scale $k^{-2}$ rise of the tracer bias. We further show that existing halo catalogs without PNG can be post-processed to imprint an effective $f_{\rm NL}$ signature. Beyond mock generation, the resulting cosmic-web response motivates an observational statistic based on changes in tracer occupation of large-scale environments relative to a matched Gaussian reference, complementary to the conventional low-$k$ power spectrum.

Through Ups and Downs: the Transient Lensing Behaviour of SIDM halos undergoing Core Collapse

RelevantFrank C. van den Bosch, Yarone M. Tokayer
arXiv:2611.09546 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.GA
Why: The paper provides an emulator for non-linear density evolution in beyond-ΛCDM models (SIDM), which is highly relevant to the student's work on emulators and non-linear theory predictions for Euclid.

This paper investigates the gravitational lensing properties of self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) haloes as they undergo gravothermal core collapse. While previous studies suggested that core collapse consistently enhances lensing efficacy, the authors demonstrate that this effect is transient and non-monotonic: lensing strength peaks during the early stages of collapse but declines significantly as the halo enters the short-mean-free-path (SMFP) regime. By developing an emulator based on a gravothermal fluid code, the authors show that the resulting 'core-within-a-core' structure is poorly approximated by generalized NFW profiles with steep central cusps. The results indicate that SIDM haloes are only optimal lenses for a brief period when their core surface density is near critical, highlighting the need for explicit, time-resolved modelling of SIDM density profiles in strong lensing studies.

Main conclusions
  • Lensing efficacy is non-monotonic, peaking during early core collapse and declining as the inner core mass and radius shrink in the SMFP regime.
  • Generalized NFW profiles with cusps $\gamma \geq 2$ are poor proxies for core-collapsed SIDM haloes and consistently overestimate their lensing efficacy.
  • SIDM haloes are only significantly more efficient lenses than NFW counterparts for a narrow time window when the core surface density $\Sigma_c \simeq 0.6 \Sigma_{\text{crit}}$.
  • The radial eigenvalue at the Einstein radius becomes highly sensitive to the collision cross-section during the late stages of collapse, breaking self-similarity.

Abstract

Self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) haloes can become extremely dense following gravothermal core collapse, motivating their use as explanations for unusually concentrated gravitational lenses. Using an emulator calibrated on gravothermal fluid calculations, we investigate whether increasing central density necessarily strengthens lensing. We follow initially Navarro-Frenk-White (NFW) haloes through core formation and collapse deep into the short-mean-free-path regime, and compute finite-source magnifications, critical curves, and source-plane lensing cross sections for isolated spherical haloes with elastic, velocity-independent self-interactions, including external shear in selected calculations. Lensing strength evolves non-monotonically: it initially decreases during core formation, rises during early collapse, and generally declines at later stages despite continued growth of the central density. This decline accompanies the emergence of a dense inner core whose mass and radius decrease within a more extended, lower-density core. Time-averaged enhancement relative to NFW haloes depends on the lensing statistic, halo concentration, and interaction cross section. Generalized NFW haloes with central cusps $\rho \propto r^{-\gamma}$ with $\gamma \geq 2$ always have larger lensing efficacies than core-collapsed SIDM haloes of equal mass and are therefore poor proxies, including in terms of the demographics (size and radial thickness) of arcs and Einstein rings. The core collapse stage at which SIDM haloes are optimal lenses coincides with the epoch at which the characteristic core surface density is close to critical, which typically occurs during the early stages of core collapse. These results demonstrate that central density alone is insufficient to characterize SIDM lensing and that its evolving mass distribution must be modelled explicitly.

Lessons from double-plane lenses for galaxy profiles, the Hubble tension, and the mass-sheet degeneracy

RelevantDaniel P. Johnson, Martin Millon, Pierre Fleury, Giacomo Queirolo, Tian Li, Wolfgang J. R. Enzi, Daniel J. Ballard, Duncan Bowden, Thomas E. Collett, Daniel Gilman
arXiv:2611.09856 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: The paper addresses the mass-sheet degeneracy and lens modelling systematics, which are critical for the student's work on Euclid-based non-linear theory predictions and beyond-ΛCDM simulations.

Double-plane lenses (DPLs), where a single foreground galaxy lenses two background sources at different redshifts, are powerful cosmological probes but remain limited by the mass-sheet degeneracy (MSD). This degeneracy prevents an unambiguous determination of the lens mass profile and the Hubble constant $H_0$ from lensing observables alone. The authors demonstrate that DPLs are sensitive to a specific combination of mass-sheet components across both lens planes, which can be constrained by comparing the measured cosmological scaling factor $\eta$ to a fiducial expansion history. This approach allows for breaking the MSD without requiring independent velocity dispersion measurements or a prior on $H_0$, and the authors show that future Euclid and LSST samples will enable robust population-level constraints on these mass-sheet components.

Main conclusions
  • DPLs allow for the measurement of a combined mass-sheet parameter $\zeta_{12}$ across two lens planes, which can be used to break the internal mass-sheet degeneracy.
  • For systems with $\eta \gtrsim 1.5$, the inclusion of $\zeta_{12}$ constraints can improve $H_0$ measurements from time-delay lenses by up to $\sim 50\%$.
  • Analysis of existing DPLs (J1721, J0946, AGEL0353) provides initial constraints on $\zeta_{12}$ that mildly disfavour the assumption of simple power-law mass profiles in both planes.
  • Future Euclid and LSST samples are expected to provide $\mathcal{O}(100-1000)$ DPLs, enabling precise population-level constraints on the mass-sheet components of lens galaxies.

Abstract

Double-plane lenses, like single-plane lenses, are subject to the mass-sheet degeneracy, a fundamental limit to our knowledge of the deflector mass profiles from lensing. However, with a prior on the relative expansion history, unlike in the single-plane case, this degeneracy can be constrained with the modelling of lens images, without the need for time delays, velocity dispersion measurements or a prior on the Hubble constant $H_0$. We show that double-plane lenses are sensitive to a specific combination of the mass-sheet components of the two lens planes. Using literature values of the cosmological scaling factor from the double-plane lenses J0946 and AGEL0353, a measurement from the point-source images of J1721 (the “Einstein zig-zag” lens), and a reference value of $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$, we show the constraints that can be placed on the mass-sheet component of each of these systems. We show that the additional information brought by images of a second source can improve measurements of $H_0$ from individual time-delay lenses by up to $\sim50\%$, depending on certain key assumptions. Using forecasted Euclid and LSST double-plane lens samples, we show that these samples will enable strong constraints on the population of mass sheets in lens galaxies. Such population constraints will have implications for a range of strong-lensing science applications, in particular in constraining $H_0$, dark energy, and the dark matter profiles of galaxies.

Violent relaxation redux

Relevantcross-listMichael L. Nastac, Yonadav Barry Ginat, Robert J. Ewart, Michael Barnes, Alexander A. Schekochihin
arXiv:2611.09531 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.GA, astro-ph.CO, cond-mat.stat-mech, nlin.CD, physics.plasm-ph
Why: The paper provides a theoretical framework for understanding the non-linear relaxation of collisionless systems, which is highly relevant to the student's work on N-body simulations and the validity of approximate methods like COLA in capturing the final state of dark matter halos.

This paper re-examines the long-standing problem of 'violent relaxation' in collisionless self-gravitating systems, such as merging galaxies or dark matter halos. The authors argue that while Lynden-Bell's original statistical mechanics approach is fundamentally correct, it fails to predict final states because it assumes exact conservation of phase-space volume (Casimir invariants). By introducing a theory of 'gravitational turbulence' that drives chaotic phase-space mixing, they show that this conservation is broken on a timescale $\tau_{\text{diss}} \sim \tau_{\text{dyn}} \log N$. This allows the system to relax adiabatically through a sequence of Lynden-Bell equilibria toward a final 'ground state' (minimum energy, maximum entropy), providing a new framework for understanding the universal structure of dark matter halos.

Main conclusions
  • Violent relaxation is not a single event but a process where the system evolves through a sequence of Lynden-Bell equilibria as phase-space volume conservation is broken.
  • The dissipation of Casimir invariants occurs on a timescale $\tau_{\text{diss}} \sim \tau_{\text{dyn}} \log N$, which is much faster than two-body collisional relaxation but slower than the dynamical time.
  • The system's long-term evolution tends toward a 'ground state' (Gardner distribution), where the energy of the system approaches the minimum possible energy allowed by its current waterbag content.
  • The observed universal equilibria of galaxies and halos may be understood as attractors in this turbulent relaxation process, with the 'incomplete' nature of the relaxation explained by the decay of potential fluctuations before the absolute ground state is reached.

Abstract

Collisionless self-gravitating systems, such as merging galaxies, have long seemed an exception to the statistical-mechanical principle that chaotic many-body systems relax to maximum-entropy states: Lynden-Bell's statistical mechanics of their "violent relaxation," constrained by exact conservation of phase-space volume – equivalently, of infinitely many collisionless "Casimir" invariants – has appeared not to be a good description. We propose that violent relaxation does give rise to Lynden-Bell's maximum-entropy equilibria, but that both are underpinned by gravitational turbulence, in which large-scale gravitational fluctuations drive chaotic phase-space mixing of the particles' distribution function. We derive the universal small-scale (sub-Jeans) spectra of this turbulence and show that it leads to phase-space-volume conservation being broken on a timescale only logarithmically longer, in the particle number N, than the dynamical (violent-relaxation) time. This timescale separation lets the system relax "adiabatically" through a sequence of Lynden-Bell equilibria, each corresponding to instantaneous values of the Casimirs. At long times, it approaches an equilibrium close to its "ground state": a distribution whose Casimirs have adjusted so that the system's energy is now its minimum possible energy. Thus, the system's universal tendency is toward a minimum-energy, maximum-entropy state. We corroborate this picture with 1D N-body simulations and hypothesize that it can explain the universal equilibria of galaxies and dark-matter halos.

DarkMatterFM-Registry: A Machine-Readable Registry of Dark-Matter Models Extracted from the arXiv Literature

RelatedIoana A. Zelko, Jo Bovy, Nolan Koblischke
arXiv:2611.08583 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.IM, hep-ph

The literature on alternative dark matter (DM) candidates—such as warm, self-interacting, or decaying dark matter—is scattered across thousands of papers with varied parameterizations and assumptions. To organize this ecosystem, the authors developed DarkMatterFM-Registry, an automated pipeline using large language models (LLMs) to extract structured 'model cards' for 6,145 DM models from over 18,000 arXiv papers. They evaluated the theory-to-observable computational pathways (e.g., relic abundance solvers, Boltzmann codes, likelihoods) for the top 2,004 prioritized models to map out missing software infrastructure. The resulting registry reveals that progress in testing alternative DM models is primarily bottlenecked by missing shared software tools—such as a unified non-cold DM Boltzmann code and standardized Lyman-$\alpha$ forest likelihoods—rather than missing theoretical derivations.

Main conclusions
  • Constructed a validated, machine-readable registry of 6,145 dark matter models extracted from arXiv, with 97.8% passing strict JSON schema checks and an 81% section accuracy rate in expert LLM evaluations.
  • Enriched 2,004 high-priority models with detailed observational constraints, regime assumptions, potential automated-reasoning failure modes, and theory-to-observable computational routes.
  • Identified missing shared computational infrastructure—rather than uncalculated theory—as the major bottleneck preventing observational testing for hundreds of non-cold dark matter models.
  • Produced a prioritized community roadmap of 25 projects, highlighting universal non-cold dark matter Boltzmann solvers, relic abundance model libraries, and Lyman-$\alpha$ forest likelihoods as the highest-impact software gaps.

Abstract

To use a dark-matter model one needs to know what defines it, which observables it affects, which codes and likelihoods evaluate it, and under what assumptions a published bound applies. That information is scattered across tens of thousands of papers. We present DarkMatterFM-Registry, a machine-readable registry of dark-matter models extracted from arXiv full texts by a staged large-language-model (LLM) pipeline: 6,145 structured model cards, validated against a seven-file JSON Schema suite, ranked by expected value for observational follow-up, and enriched for the top ~2,000 with observables, constraints, and theory-to-observable routes. Schema checks pass 97.8% of cards, and an LLM-judged accuracy study on a stratified card sample finds 81% of section verdicts correct, 18% partially correct, and none hallucinated. As a first application we turn the registry into a priority-weighted roadmap of 25 concrete projects the community could build. The roadmap is built by judging every computational step of every enriched model's theory-to-observable routes as available, partial, or missing, and aggregating the resulting typed gaps weighted by model priority. We find that what blocks the most high-priority models is not missing theory but missing shared infrastructure: model files for existing relic-abundance solvers, a general non-cold Boltzmann-code framework, reusable indirect-detection likelihoods, and a Lyman-$\alpha$ forest likelihood for non-cold dark matter (the largest gap for structure-formation probes). The model cards, schemas, roadmap, and ranking are released making the project reproducible and the cards available for the community.

Primordial or Post-Inflationary: Distinguishing the Origin of Scale-Dependent Features in the CMB

RelatedMatteo Braglia, Wenzer Qin, Xingang Chen, Tracy R. Slatyer
arXiv:2611.08585 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-ph

Scale-dependent residuals in the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) anisotropies could originate either from primordial inflationary dynamics (such as sharp features or resonant oscillations) or from post-inflationary energy injections and transient Early Dark Energy (EDE). To test whether these distinct physical mechanisms can be confused with one another, the authors perform a systematic comparison using theoretical feature models, Principal Component Analysis (PCA), and a joint nested sampling likelihood analysis of Planck CMB data. They find that post-inflationary mechanisms primarily manifest either as overall damping (via modifications to optical depth) or phase shifts at the characteristic BAO acoustic scale, whereas primordial features produce oscillations at arbitrary frequencies across scale space. Consequently, their signatures in the CMB angular power spectrum are nearly orthogonal, allowing current and future CMB data to cleanly distinguish primordial features from post-inflationary energy or expansion rate modifications.

Main conclusions
  • Primordial features (linear or logarithmic oscillations) and post-inflationary modifications (energy injection, transient EDE) produce qualitatively distinct patterns in CMB power spectra, with post-inflationary effects largely restricted to overall damping or phase shifts at the BAO scale.
  • Principal Component Analysis demonstrates that the dominant eigenmodes describing primordial features and post-inflationary physics in parameter space are nearly orthogonal.
  • Full Bayesian likelihood analyses of Planck (CamSpec NPIPE) data confirm that posteriors on primordial feature parameters are unaffected when jointly fitting for post-inflationary feature parameters, and vice versa.
  • A minor partial degeneracy only occurs if a primordial feature frequency fine-tunes to match the BAO sound horizon scale, but transfer function deformations still preserve distinct signatures.

Abstract

Physics beyond the Standard Model, whether of primordial or post-inflationary origin, can leave scale-dependent signals in the anisotropies of the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB). In this work, we study how distinguishable these broad classes of signals are and quantify their level of degeneracy. In this initial investigation, for primordial features, we limit ourselves to those of the “sharp” and “resonant” kinds; for post-inflationary features, we consider two qualitatively distinct models: exotic energy injection, which deposits energy into the primordial plasma and modifies the global thermodynamic history, and early dark energy (EDE), which affects the CMB through the background expansion and gravitational perturbations. By studying representative examples, we find that while primordial features can leave oscillatory signatures in the CMB power spectrum with essentially arbitrary frequency and running, the post-inflationary models considered here produce qualitatively different patterns that cannot generically reproduce the characteristic running of primordial feature oscillations. We verify these findings first with a principal component analysis, and show using only the most significant principal components that there are no significant degeneracies between the primordial and post-inflationary features considered here, and secondly with a full likelihood analysis, which shows that the resulting posteriors do not change significantly when considering individual classes of models or combinations of primordial/post-inflationary features. While the two post-inflationary models probe distinct physical mechanisms and both are implemented in a simplified, phenomenological fashion, the methods used in this work can be generalized to include more realistic signals or other classes of signals.

Anisotropic three-point statistics of the peculiar velocity and galaxy density fields: modelling in Fourier and configuration space

RelatedM. Guidi, A. Farina, A. Nusser, E. Branchini
arXiv:2611.08589 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

Peculiar velocity fields probe the total matter distribution directly via the equivalence principle, offering a way to break degeneracies between the growth rate $f\sigma_8$ and galaxy bias parameters. This paper presents a tree-level Eulerian perturbation theory framework in redshift space for the anisotropic three-point cross-correlation between one line-of-sight peculiar velocity and two galaxy density fields ($\langle v \delta_g \delta_g \rangle$), incorporating quadratic ($b_2$) and tidal ($b_{\mathcal{G}_2}$) galaxy bias along with infrared resummation for BAO damping. The authors decompose the scalar velocity-divergence bispectrum ($\theta\delta\delta$) into spherical harmonics and map it to line-of-sight velocity multipoles via a purely geometric mixing operator that accounts for the non-local wavevector coupling introduced by velocity projection ($1/k_1$). They evaluate the model in both Fourier and configuration space (using 2D FFTLog) at $z=0.9$ and provide estimators for practical density-weighted catalogue measurements.

Main conclusions
  • Developed a tree-level redshift-space perturbation theory model for $\langle v \delta_g \delta_g \rangle$ using triple-product spherical harmonics in both Fourier and configuration space.
  • Demonstrated that line-of-sight velocity projection acts via a precomputable geometric angular mixing matrix that is independent of cosmology and separates cleanly from the $\theta\delta\delta$ bispectrum tables.
  • Found that dipole terms ($L=1$) strongly dominate the total signal, accounting for $94\%$ of the squared amplitude in Fourier space and $95\%$ in configuration space at $z=0.9$.
  • Showed that BAO features in configuration space depend heavily on triangle orientation and which specific triangle side approaches the acoustic scale ($r \sim 100\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}$).
  • Linked the theoretical $v\delta\delta$ model directly to density-weighted momentum statistics measured from velocity catalogues and formulated explicit multipole estimators.

Abstract

By the equivalence principle, large-scale peculiar velocities provide a kinematical probe of the matter distribution independent of the biased galaxy distribution. Their three-point correlation with galaxies assesses the nonlinear coupling between cosmic flows and the surrounding density field. We develop a tree-level model of the anisotropic correlation between one line-of-sight peculiar velocity and two galaxy densities in redshift space, including quadratic and tidal galaxy bias. Starting from the corresponding velocity-divergence bispectrum, we derive predictions in both Fourier and configuration space through a spherical-harmonic expansion. We test the Fourier-space calculation against a closed-form expression and assess the numerical convergence of the configuration-space predictions. For a fiducial model, the signal is dominated by a dipolar dependence on triangle orientation relative to the line of sight, while subdominant multipoles distinguish the responses to growth and galaxy bias. In configuration space, the baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) feature depends on which triangle side crosses the acoustic scale and on the velocity projection. We also relate the model to the density-weighted statistic measured by peculiar-velocity catalogues and formulate estimators for its observable multipoles. This framework supports future measurements and covariance analyses needed to establish the additional cosmological information carried by velocity-density three-point correlations.

We present the strong lens mass modeling result of 60 galaxy clusters targeted by Vast Exploration for Nascent, Unexplored Sources (VENUS), the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) multi-epoch Treasury program. Short-term optical monitoring of quadruply imaged quasars

L. J. Goicoechea, V. N. Shalyapin, A. Esteban-Gutiérrez, R. Gil-Merino, M. Serra-Ricart
arXiv:2611.08633 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

Gravitationally lensed quadruply-imaged quasars (quads) provide powerful probes of dark matter profiles, cosmic expansion, and accretion disk structure via time-delay cosmography and microlensing. This paper presents a proof-of-concept showing that short-term (~6-month) optical monitoring with modest 2m-class ground-based telescopes (the Liverpool Telescope and TTT3) can yield valuable physical constraints on quad systems. Using $g$- and $r$-band light curves of the Einstein Cross (QSO 2237+0305) and the Andromeda Parachute (PS J0147+4630), the authors model microlensing variability and measure inter-image time delays. For QSO 2237+0305, microlensing difference light curves constrain the $g$-band source half-light radius to $R_s = 0.25 \pm 0.16 R_{\rm E}$ (3.5–16 light-days). For PS J0147+4630, short time delays between the arc images ($\Delta t_{\rm AB}$ and $\Delta t_{\rm AC}$) were measured to within $\sim 2$ days ($2\sigma$), favoring a galaxy mass density profile slightly shallower than isothermal (power-law index $\gamma \approx 1.8-1.9$).

This paper investigates the 'missing mass' problem in galaxy clusters within the framework of Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND). Abstract

First proposed by Milgrom in 1983 as an alternative to the standard Cold Dark Matter (CDM) paradigm, Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND) has gained renewed momentum in light of persistent non-detections of exotic dark matter particles and growing tensions facing CDM on galactic scales. While MOND perfectly reproduces galactic rotation curves, it encounters notable challenges in explaining the equilibrium of galaxy clusters. This paper introduces a 'sourced oscillatory' (SO) dark energy model where the equation of state $w_{ m de}$ evolves according to a harmonic oscillator equation driven by a cubic term in e-folds. Unlike the standard CPL parametrization, which can behave unphysically when extrapolated, this model predicts a transient period of cosmic acceleration that eventually transitions to a decelerated, asymptotically static universe. This paper investigates the theoretical requirements for generating a parity-violating (PV) signal in the primordial trispectrum, the four-point correlation function in Fourier space. Using a model-independent perturbative approach, the authors evaluate all possible Wick-contraction configurations for third- and second-order density kernels. They demonstrate that under the assumption of statistical isotropy, only the non-self-contraction configuration of the third-order kernel possesses the necessary degrees of freedom—specifically, three independent wave-vectors—to support a parity-odd contribution. This work provides a crucial theoretical foundation for interpreting potential PV signals in large-scale structure surveys like Euclid, as it identifies which inflationary kernels are capable of producing observable signatures.

Main conclusions
  • The third-order density kernel's non-self-contraction configuration is the lowest-order perturbative structure capable of supporting a parity-violating trispectrum.
  • All four evaluated second-order kernel contraction configurations are parity-even, as they lack the necessary independent wave-vectors to form the scalar triple product required for parity violation.
  • The self-contraction configuration of the third-order kernel is parity-even because it reduces to a dependence on a single wave-vector, which must vanish under statistical isotropy.

Abstract

Non-Gaussian perturbations generated during inflation can often be described perturbatively via integrals of kernels acting on Gaussian fields. In this work, we investigate, in a model-independent manner, whether these kernels can produce parity-violating (PV) contributions to the trispectrum, and which Wick's theorem contraction configurations of the Gaussian fields lead to them. Abstract
