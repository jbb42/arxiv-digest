Disentangling constraints on $S_8$ from the high and low redshift CMB

RelevantJoann Jones, Ualype de Andrade Uchôa, Scott Dodelson
arXiv:2610.37752 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: Directly addresses the $S_8$ tension and structure growth across redshifts by separating early-time recombination constraints from intermediate-redshift CMB lensing, which is central context for testing beyond-$\Lambda$CDM growth with surveys like Euclid.

The $S_8$ tension—where galaxy weak lensing surveys measure weaker late-time matter clustering ($S_8 \equiv \sigma_8 (\Omega_m/0.3)^{0.5}$) than predicted by the cosmic microwave background (CMB)—is usually framed as a discrepancy between early-universe ($z_* \simeq 1100$) and late-universe ($z \lesssim 1$) physics. However, standard CMB temperature and polarization power spectra (2-point functions) are gravitationally lensed by matter along the line of sight at intermediate redshifts ($0.7 < z < 5$, peaking at $z \sim 2$), which smooths the acoustic peaks and mixes low-redshift clustering information into the supposedly primordial CMB constraint. The authors cleanly separate the primordial ($z_*$) and lensing ($z^{\text{CMBL}}$) contributions in combined Planck 2018, ACT DR6, and SPT-3G D1 data by decomposing the lensing potential power spectrum $C_L^{\Phi\Phi}$ into principal components (PCs) and marginalizing over the peak-smoothing modes, while separately extracting intermediate-redshift clustering from the 4-point lensing reconstruction. Thanks to the high angular resolution of ACT and SPT-3G, shifts in standard $\Lambda$CDM parameters cannot mimic the oscillatory lensing smoothing pattern (leaving only a 2–3% overlap), making it possible to isolate pure recombination-era predictions and pinpoint whether the $S_8$ tension arises between early and late times or between intermediate-redshift CMB lensing and other probes.

Main conclusions
  • With a broad neutrino mass prior ($0.06 < \sum m_\nu < 0.6\,\text{eV}$), the unlensed primordial CMB ($z_* \simeq 1100$) yields $S_8^{z_*} = 0.795 \pm 0.022$, which agrees to within $0.3\sigma$ with the low-redshift DES Year 6 $3\times 2\text{pt}$ measurement ($S_8^{\text{GS}} = 0.789 \pm 0.012$ at $0.3 < z < 1$).
  • CMB lensing from the 4-point reconstruction alone ($0.7 < z < 5$) prefers a higher clustering amplitude of $S_8^{4\text{-pt}} = 0.836 \pm 0.033$ (or $S_8^{\text{CMBL}} \equiv \sigma_8(\Omega_m/0.3)^{0.25} = 0.814 \pm 0.012$), which pulls the full lensed CMB 2-point constraint up to $S_8 = 0.836 \pm 0.013$ (a $2.6\sigma$ discrepancy with DES Y6).
  • Both the 2-point peak smoothing and 4-point lensing reconstruction show consistent $>2\sigma$ excesses in the primary lensing principal component $\Theta^{(1)}$ relative to the $\Lambda$CDM prediction from the unlensed CMB ($2.4\sigma$ and $2.2\sigma$, respectively).
  • The interpretation of these discrepancies depends strongly on the neutrino mass prior: tightening the prior to $\sum m_\nu < 0.1\,\text{eV}$ reduces neutrino suppression of structure growth, raising the unlensed CMB prediction to $S_8^{z_*} = 0.822 \pm 0.018$—which resolves the excess CMB lensing tension (down to $0.4\sigma$–$1.4\sigma$) while only modestly increasing the difference with DES Y6 to $1.3\sigma$.

Abstract

The clustering of matter in the universe is quantified by $\sigma_8$, the RMS fluctuations today in linear theory in spheres of radius $8h^{-1}$ Mpc, where $h$ parametrizes the Hubble constant. Surveys of gravitational lensing and galaxy clustering best constrain $S_8\equiv (\Omega_m/0.3)^{0.5} \sigma_8$, where $\Omega_m$ is the energy density of matter in units of the critical density. Comparing this late time measurement to the value of $S_8$ inferred from the cosmic microwave background (CMB), one can test the $\Lambda$CDM prediction for the growth of clustering from the primordial universe to now. Several analyses report hints that CMB measurements of $S_8$ are larger than those from galaxy surveys. However, CMB measurements have information from both high and low redshift due to lensing, and these tests traditionally conflate the two. Here, we present a technique to extract constraints on the cosmological parameters from each redshift component separately, enabling a high-z vs. low-z clustering comparison that was not previously possible. We marginalize over lensing information using a principal component analysis, and show that the primordial CMB from combined Planck 2018, ACT DR6, and SPT-3G D1 prefers $S_8= 0.795\pm0.022$, in agreement with the recent determination from the Dark Energy Survey (DES) of $S_8=0.789\pm0.012$. The clustering inferred from CMB lensing is larger. However, both the level of excess lensing and increase in agreement with DES depends on the prior on the sum of neutrino masses, $\sum m_\nu$. We find that removing lensing brings the CMB towards agreement with DES in any case.

The Redshift-Space Cluster-Galaxy Cross-Correlation as a Probe of Gravity on Cosmic Scales

RelevantNicole Marcelina Gountanis, David H. Weinberg, Andrés N. Salcedo, Claire Lamman
arXiv:2610.37782 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
Why: Directly targets tests of screened modified gravity ($f(R)$, Galileon) on quasi-linear scales using $N$-body simulations and motivates building emulators for cluster-galaxy cross-correlations in modified gravity.

In viable modified gravity theories like $f(R)$ or Galileon models, screening mechanisms suppress fifth forces inside high-density virialized cluster cores, but screening weakens in the surrounding quasi-linear infall regions, boosting the velocities of galaxies falling toward the cluster by $10\text{–}30\%$ compared to General Relativity (GR). Because peculiar velocities distort galaxy positions along the line of sight in spectroscopic surveys—creating small-scale "Finger-of-God" elongation and larger-scale coherent squashing/caustics—this infall velocity field is encoded in the 2D redshift-space cluster–galaxy cross-correlation function, $\xi_{\rm cg}^{\rm s}(r_p, r_\pi)$. The authors perform a simulation-based Fisher matrix forecast using the AbacusSummit $N$-body suite at $z=0.3$ to test how well the DESI Bright Galaxy Survey (BGS) can constrain a phenomenological velocity-rescaling parameter $F_{\rm G}$ (where $F_{\rm G}=1$ is GR), while simultaneously marginalizing over Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD) parameters, velocity bias, cluster mass–observable scatter, $\Omega_m$, and $\sigma_8$. This provides a concrete roadmap for testing gravity in the unscreened infall regime and enables a cluster-scale gravitational slip test when combined with weak lensing.

Main conclusions
  • DESI BGS ($0.25 < z < 0.35$) can constrain the velocity-scaling parameter $F_{\rm G}$ to $\sigma(F_{\rm G}) = 0.033$ (a $3.3\%$ measurement of the infall velocity amplitude), improving over previous SDSS constraints by nearly an order of magnitude and allowing typical $f(R)$ and Galileon models ($F_{\rm G} \approx 1.1\text{–}1.3$) to be distinguished from GR at $\gtrsim 4\sigma$.
  • The $F_{\rm G}$ constraint is largely immune to degeneracies with galaxy HOD and velocity-bias parameters, with the primary limiting factor being the degeneracy with the cluster mass–observable scatter $\sigma_{\log M_{\rm c}}$ (fixing this scatter improves $\sigma(F_{\rm G})$ by $\sim 23\%$, while perfectly knowing the full galaxy–halo connection lowers it to $\approx 0.015$).
  • The same cluster–galaxy cross-correlation data vector tightly constrains background and growth parameters to $\sigma(\Omega_m) = 0.010$ and $\sigma(\sigma_8) = 0.010$ from distinct spatial and anisotropic clustering signatures, with only moderate correlation with $F_{\rm G}$.
  • Directly modeling the full 2D correlation function $\log \xi_{\rm cg}^{\rm s}(r_p, r_\pi)$ captures nearly all the velocity information of the 1D radial infall profile while simultaneously breaking degeneracies with astrophysical nuisance parameters.

Abstract

Testing General Relativity (GR) on cosmological scales requires probes that are directly sensitive to gravitational dynamics. We present a Fisher matrix forecast of how well the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) Bright Galaxy Survey (BGS) can constrain departures from GR through the cluster infall velocity field, using the redshift-space cluster-galaxy cross-correlation function $\xi^{\rm s}_{\rm cg}(r_p, r_\pi)$ as the primary observable. We construct mock catalogs from the AbacusSummit N-body simulations, using a Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD) model for galaxies and a log-normal mass-observable relation for cluster selection. We parameterize departures from GR with a single multiplicative factor $F_{\rm G}$ that rescales halo infall velocities relative to the GR prediction. Marginalizing over the HOD, mass-observable, and cosmological parameters, we find that DESI BGS can constrain $F_{\rm G}$ to $\sigma(F_{\rm G}) = 0.033$ at $z \sim 0.3$. This precision is sufficient to distinguish representative $f(R)$ and Galileon gravity models from GR at $\gtrsim 4\sigma$. The strongest limitation is a degeneracy between $F_{\rm G}$ and the scatter in the cluster mass-observable relation, while the constraint shows no strong degeneracy with any individual HOD parameter. Perfect knowledge of the galaxy-halo connection would improve the constraint to $\sigma(F_{\rm G}) \approx 0.015$, and including the galaxy autocorrelation function in the data vector could recover part of this improvement. The same data vector yields tight constraints on the cosmological parameters, $\sigma(\Omega_m) = 0.010$ and $\sigma(\sigma_8) = 0.010$, only moderately correlated with $F_{\rm G}$. These results demonstrate the potential of DESI BGS cluster-galaxy correlations to test gravity on quasi-linear scales, complementary to existing modified gravity probes and to weak-lensing cluster mass profiles.

Probing Cosmology with Diffusion Models: Memorization, Generalization, and Parameter Estimation

RelevantJiaming Pan, Nicholas Kern
arXiv:2610.37825 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: Directly addresses the validation, training-set sizing, and statistical calibration of machine-learning emulators and field-level cosmological surrogates, which is a key methodological area in your profile.

Generative AI models—specifically diffusion models, which learn to generate complex images by iteratively removing noise—are increasingly used as fast field-level emulators for expensive cosmological simulations. However, if a neural network is trained on too few simulations relative to its size, it can simply memorize the training maps rather than learning the underlying physics needed to generate genuinely new realizations. To investigate how this affects cosmological parameter inference, the authors train conditional attention-UNet diffusion models of varying capacities on $2^6$ to $2^{15}$ 2D neutral hydrogen ($\mathrm{H\,I}$) maps from the CAMELS IllustrisTNG hydrodynamical suite, conditioned on $\Omega_m$ and $\sigma_8$. They evaluate the generated fields using three criteria: feature-space similarity to the training set (to detect memorization), 1-point pixel histograms and 2-point power spectra, and posterior calibration when inferring $\Omega_m$ via a separate neural regressor. Crucially, they show that a memorized diffusion emulator can pass visual inspections as well as 1-point and power-spectrum tests with flying colors, yet produce severely under-dispersed, overconfident parameter posteriors because different random seeds collapse onto the same memorized training fields.

Main conclusions
  • A diffusion model trained on a small dataset ($N_{\rm 2D} = 2^6$ to $2^8$ slices) almost entirely memorizes its training images (median pixel cosine similarity $> 0.994$), yet still accurately reproduces the true 1-point pixel histogram and 2D power spectrum—showing that standard summary-statistic checks cannot detect memorization.
  • Because a memorized model outputs nearly identical fields regardless of the initial random noise seed, using it for parameter inference yields confidence intervals for $\Omega_m$ that are $\sim 2.5\times$ narrower than ground truth (covering only 9/32 and 16/32 of held-out cosmologies at nominal 68% and 95% confidence, compared to 27/32 and 32/32 for real simulation slices).
  • As training set size increases, models undergo a sharp transition from memorization to generalization at a critical dataset size that scales with neural network capacity (e.g., jumping from a sample novelty fraction $G=0.34$ at $N_{\rm 2D}=512$ to $G=1.0$ at $N_{\rm 2D}=1024$ for UNet-128, while the larger UNet-256 only reaches $G=0.84$ at $N_{\rm 2D}=1024$).
  • Right at the memorization-to-generalization transition ($N_{\rm 2D} = 2^{10}$), summary statistics temporarily degrade (losing $\sim 1/3$ of large-scale power and high-density tail pixels), before recovering both accurate power spectra and well-calibrated 68% posterior coverage (24–25 out of 32 test cosmologies) once well into the generalized regime ($N_{\rm 2D} \ge 2^{12}$).

Abstract

Diffusion models are increasingly used as surrogates for expensive cosmological simulations, but simple metrics like reconstruction fidelity do not guarantee a surrogate can be trusted for scientific inference. To explore this, we train diffusion models on HI fields from the CAMELS IllustrisTNG simulations and test how three properties change with training-set size: memorization of individual training examples, agreement with one- and two-point statistics (the pixel-value distribution and the power spectrum), and estimation of cosmological parameters from the generated fields. At fixed model size, generative models trained with smaller, less diverse training data produced memorized samples. While this is a well known phenomenon, we show that this leads to miscalibrated parameter densities for cosmological inference tasks. At fixed size, there is a training set size with enough information content to enable a model to generalize, at which point posterior density estimation with neural surrogates can be well-calibrated. We demonstrate that a neural surrogate can therefore pass visual, one-point and power-spectrum convergence tests while still resulting in overconfident parameter estimation in a cosmological inference pipeline. Thus, tests of model generalization should be an additional check for inference pipelines adopting neural surrogates.

Early Dark Energy Crowds Out Interacting Dark Energy

RelevantWilliam Giarè, Jeremy Sakstein
arXiv:2610.37836 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: Directly investigates dynamical and interacting dark energy extensions, the Hubble tension, and cosmological likelihood combinations of Planck/ACT/SPT CMB, DESI BAO, and DES 5-year SNe Ia.

Proposed solutions to the Hubble tension typically rely on either early-time new physics like Early Dark Energy (EDE)—a scalar field that briefly boosts the pre-recombination expansion rate to shrink the sound horizon—or late-time mechanisms like Interacting Dark Energy (IDE), where cold dark matter transfers energy to dark energy. Because DESI BAO and supernova data recently hinted at non-standard late-time dark energy dynamics, it is natural to ask whether combining EDE with late-time IDE can work together to fully resolve the $H_0$ tension. The authors test a hybrid model containing an axion-like EDE scalar field, a cosmological constant $\Lambda$, and an independent dark energy fluid exchanging energy with cold dark matter ($Q_c^\nu = \xi H \rho_{\rm fld} u_c^\nu$) against CMB (Planck, ACT DR6, SPT-3G), DESI BAO, recalibrated DES 5-year supernovae (DES-Dovekie), and SH0ES data, using both MCMC sampling and profile likelihoods. They find that EDE effectively "crowds out" the interacting dark sector: because the CMB tightly anchors the early-time physical matter density, decaying dark matter into dark energy alongside an EDE-boosted $H_0$ forces the present-day matter fraction $\Omega_{m,0}$ to drop to values strongly disfavored by Type Ia supernovae.

Main conclusions
  • In a joint fit to CMB (Planck + ACT DR6 + SPT-3G), DESI BAO, DES-Dovekie SNe Ia, and SH0ES, the best-fit model collapses to the non-interacting EDE-only limit ($\xi = -3.5 \times 10^{-4}$, $\Delta\chi^2_{\rm tot} = -0.02$), yielding $H_0 = 71.51 \pm 0.71\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ ($70.88\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ at best fit) and restricting the interaction-induced matter density shift to $\Delta\Omega_m^{\rm IDE} < 0.0217$ (95% CL).
  • Once EDE raises $H_0$, only a small statistical reward remains from the SH0ES prior ($\Delta\chi^2 \simeq -4$), which is rapidly outweighed by a severe degradation in the fit to SNe Ia data ($\Delta\chi^2 \simeq +11$ for even modest interactions).
  • Physically, because the CMB fixes the early-time physical matter density $\omega_{m,\rm early}^{\rm eff}$, transferring energy from CDM to dark energy lowers the present-day matter fraction $\Omega_{m,0}$—an effect compounded by the higher $H_0$ from EDE—pushing $\Omega_{m,0}$ below the range permitted by supernovae.
  • Allowing the interacting fluid's equation of state to evolve via a $w_0w_a$ (CPL) parametrization does not rescue the interaction ($\Delta\chi^2_{\rm tot} = -2.37$ vs. $-2.41$ without interaction), because perturbative stability restricts this energy-transfer branch to the non-phantom regime ($w(a) \geq -1$), whereas the DESI-preferred phantom-crossing solution ($w_0 = -0.84, w_a = -0.55$, $\Delta\chi^2_{\rm tot} = -8.88$) actually lowers $H_0$ to $70.33\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$.

Abstract

We investigate whether interacting dark energy can provide additional relief to the Hubble tension once early dark energy (EDE) is present. Considering a cosmology containing EDE, an independent late-time dark energy fluid with an equation of state fixed at $w\simeq-1$ interacting with cold dark matter, and a cosmological constant, we find that the data strongly drive the model toward the non-interacting limit. The best fit effectively reduces to the standard EDE cosmology, where a non-zero EDE component provides most of the required increase in $H_0$, leaving little statistical gain for the interaction. Although energy transfer from dark matter to dark energy can raise $H_0$ further, this limited gain is rapidly outweighed by the deterioration of the fit to low-redshift observations. In particular, since the CMB tightly constrains the early-time physical matter density, any additional energy transfer requires a lower present-day matter abundance. Such low values are strongly disfavored by Type Ia supernovae. We show that this limitation becomes particularly severe once EDE has already raised $H_0$ and that the same mechanism extends to a broad class of post-recombination interactions. Allowing a dynamical dark energy equation of state of the Chevallier-Polarski-Linder form does not alter our conclusions.

Why: Provides the flagship DESI DR1 observational constraints on the cosmic homogeneity scale and explicitly tests whether standard random-catalogue estimators can detect large central vs. off-centre voids and fractal universes, which is directly relevant to your work on inhomogeneous cosmologies and tests of the cosmological principle.

A foundational assumption of standard cosmology is the cosmological principle—that the Universe becomes statistically homogeneous and isotropic on sufficiently large scales—which is crucial to test against inhomogeneous models and cosmic backreaction scenarios. To quantify where this transition occurs, the authors analyse nearly 5 million galaxies and quasars across six redshift bins ($0.1 < z < 2.1$) from DESI Data Release 1 using the correlation dimension $D_2(r)$, which measures the logarithmic slope of the average number of galaxies inside spheres of radius $r$ (normalized by unclustered random catalogues) and approaches $3$ in a uniform distribution. Defining the homogeneity scale $R_H$ as the radius where $D_2(r)$ reaches within $1\%$ of $3$ ($D_2 = 2.97$), they combine direct spline fits with perturbation-theory clustering models calibrated on AbacusSummit and EZmock simulations to disentangle galaxy bias and fiber-collision systematics from the underlying dark matter distribution. Furthermore, by testing their pipeline on toy inhomogeneous universes (including fractal and giant-void models), they demonstrate that using random catalogues does not artificially force homogeneity unless an observer sits closely at the centre of a spherical structure, providing the most precise confirmation of the cosmological principle across cosmic time to date.

Main conclusions
  • The galaxy homogeneity scale $R_H$ (where the correlation dimension reaches $D_2 = 2.97$) clearly transitions to homogeneity across all six DESI DR1 tracer bins ($0.1 < z < 2.1$), ranging from $93.8^{+0.9}_{-0.7}\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}$ to $142.6^{+1.4}_{-1.4}\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}$ depending on redshift and galaxy bias.
  • Reconstructing the underlying dark matter distribution yields a matter homogeneity scale $R_H^{\mathrm{matter}}$ that decreases monotonically with redshift—from $67.7^{+1.1}_{-1.0}\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}$ at $z_{\mathrm{eff}}=0.295$ down to $40.0^{+0.2}_{-0.2}\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}$ at $z_{\mathrm{eff}}=1.491$ (NGC)—in tight agreement with $\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}$ gravitational growth predictions.
  • A systematic offset of up to $\sim 10\%$ in the raw galaxy homogeneity scale between the North and South Galactic Caps is reproduced in AbacusSummit mocks and traced to small-scale fiber assignment incompleteness; it is largely absorbed by bias, shot-noise, and counterterm parameters when reconstructing the matter homogeneity scale.
  • Tests on inhomogeneous toy models (fractal Swiss-cheese, Gaussian, and $800\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}$ Giant Void distributions) prove that normalising counts with redshift-shuffled random catalogues still detects large-scale inhomogeneity unless the observer sits precisely at the centre of an isotropic void/overdensity, refuting recent claims that DESI DR1 shows no transition to homogeneity within $400\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}$.

Abstract

We present measurements of the scale of cosmic homogeneity using the first data release (DR1) of the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI), the largest publicly available spectroscopic extragalactic survey to date. Using a counts-in-spheres estimator applied to nearly 5 million galaxies and quasars across six independent redshift bins spanning $0.1 < z < 2.1$, we measure the correlation dimension $D_2(r)$ and define the homogeneity scale $R_H$ as the point at which the galaxy distribution reaches within 1% of the homogeneous asymptotic value. We find that all tracers clearly transition to homogeneity, with the galaxy homogeneity scale ranging from $93.8^{+0.9}_{-0.7}$ to $142.6^{+1.4}_{-1.4}$ $h^{-1}$Mpc depending on the tracer redshift, galaxy bias, and modelling used to extract the scale. Testing Extended Exponential Infrared Teleparallel Gravity with CMB, DESI, and PantheonPlus

RelevantMahmoud Hashim, Jackson Levi Said, Waleed El Hanafy, Eleonora Di Valentino
arXiv:2610.38684 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
Why: Tests a modified gravity model at the background and linear perturbation levels using combined Planck/ACT/SPT CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, and Pantheon+ likelihoods, directly touching on dynamical effective dark energy and the Hubble tension.

Instead of attributing late-time cosmic acceleration to a cosmological constant $\Lambda$, teleparallel gravity reformulates gravity in terms of spacetime torsion (twisting) rather than curvature, and $f(T)$ theories generalize the torsion scalar $T$ in the action—much like $f(R)$ generalizes the Ricci scalar—so that late-time acceleration emerges geometrically while keeping the field equations second-order. In this paper, the authors study the Extended Exponential Infrared (EExpIR) $f(T) = T \exp[\beta(T_0/T)^b]$ model, which acts like standard General Relativity at high redshift ($T \gg T_0$) but introduces a single extra free parameter $b$ that drives an effective dynamical dark energy component at late times. They first use a dynamical systems phase-space analysis to select the physically viable Lambert-$W$ solution branch and establish priors ($0.5 < b < 1$), then implement both the background and linear perturbation equations (which modify the effective Newton's constant and introduce a difference between the two metric potentials, known as gravitational slip) to constrain the model against combined CMB (Planck, ACT, SPT), DESI DR2 BAO, and Pantheon+ supernova datasets. This provides a clear test of whether infrared torsional modifications can simultaneously fit full CMB angular power spectra and resolve the Hubble tension once low-redshift geometric probes are included.

Main conclusions
  • A dynamical systems / phase-space analysis of the Lambert-$W$ solution branches shows that the principal branch with $0.5 < b < 1$ is the only one that reproduces standard early-time matter domination before transitioning to a stable late-time accelerated attractor (exhibiting a quintessence-to-phantom crossing in $w_{\rm eff}$).
  • Combining CMB (Planck, ACT, SPT), DESI DR2 BAO, and SH0ES-calibrated Pantheon+ supernovae constrains the deformation parameter to $b = 0.573^{+0.016}_{-0.060}$ and pushes the inferred Hubble constant up to $H_0 = 70.67^{+0.31}_{-0.35}\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ (at 68% CL), partially easing the Hubble tension compared to $\Lambda$CDM.
  • At the linear perturbation level, torsional modifications alter the effective gravitational coupling ($Q = 1/f_T$) and generate a non-vanishing gravitational slip on ultra-large scales, producing a slight enhancement of the CMB temperature power spectrum at $\ell \lesssim 5$ while remaining nearly indistinguishable from $\Lambda$CDM at higher multipoles.
  • Despite the higher $H_0$, the late-time expansion history is too rigid when tested against low-redshift geometric probes: Bayesian evidence reveals that EExpIR $f(T)$ is statistically indistinguishable from $\Lambda$CDM using CMB data alone, strongly disfavored by CMB+DESI+Pantheon+ without SH0ES, and weakly disfavored when the SH0ES prior is included.

Abstract

We place cosmological constraints on an extended exponential infrared (EExpIR) $f(T)$ teleparallel gravity model using current observational datasets. The extension is characterized by an additional parameter, $b$, which governs the late-time cosmological dynamics. Owing to the existence of two solution branches, phase-space analysis is employed to select the cosmological branch and to establish an appropriate prior range for the new parameter. Theoretical Consistency and Observational Constraints on Entropic Cosmology

RelevantYunliang Ren, Xiaofeng Yang, Xuwei Zhang, Shuangnan Chen, Yangjun Shi, Hoernisa Iminniyaza
arXiv:2610.40205 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
Why: This paper tests dynamical dark energy alternatives ("acceleration without $\Lambda$") against DESI DR2 BAO, Pantheon+, and Planck CMB distance priors, directly matching the medium-relevance criteria in your profile.

Entropic cosmology attempts to explain late-time cosmic acceleration without a cosmological constant by treating the cosmic horizon as a thermodynamic boundary with Bekenstein-Hawking entropy and temperature, which generates an outward "entropic force" modifying the Friedmann equations. Motivated by recent DESI DR2 hints of evolving dark energy, the authors test three effective entropic driving terms—proportional to $H^2$ (Model A), $H^2 + \dot{H}$ (Model B), and $H^2 + \dot{H} + H^4$ (Model C)—against three theoretical criteria: standard early-universe expansion, the Trans-Planckian Censorship Conjecture (TCC, a quantum-gravity condition requiring sub-Planckian fluctuations to never cross the Hubble horizon, which restricts eternal acceleration), and "Quintom-B" crossing (where the effective dark energy equation of state $w_{\rm DE}$ crosses $-1$ from below to above). They then confront the background expansion histories of these models with Pantheon+ Type Ia supernovae, DESI DR2 BAO, and Planck 2018 CMB distance priors. This study is important because it shows that while horizon thermodynamics can mimic dynamical dark energy, the parameter space favored by late- and early-time distance measurements is in tension with the theoretical requirements for a self-consistent cosmic history.

Main conclusions
  • The simplest entropic model (Model A, driving term $\propto H^2$) is statistically preferred by Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) due to having fewer parameters, but fails to simultaneously satisfy early-universe matter/radiation domination and the Trans-Planckian Censorship Conjecture (TCC).

In galaxy clusters, jets from central supermassive black holes inflate relativistic plasma bubbles that push aside the hot intracluster gas; observers usually measure the mechanical energy of this "AGN feedback" by spotting the resulting dark depressions ("X-ray cavities") in soft X-ray images. At high redshift ($z \gtrsim 1$), however, the cosmic microwave background (CMB) energy density is much higher, scaling as $(1+z)^4$, so relativistic electrons and positrons inside the bubble rapidly inverse-Compton (IC) scatter CMB photons directly into the observed 0.5–2 keV X-ray band. The authors build an analytical model that couples radiative lepton cooling, bubble volume and enthalpy ($4pV$) evolution, and soft X-ray surface-brightness contrast, testing it against the measured atmosphere of the $z=1.16$ cluster SPT-CL J2215$-$3537. They show that a tiny fraction of bubble pressure carried by relativistic leptons can produce enough IC X-ray glow to completely fill in and hide the thermal X-ray depression without noticeably reducing the bubble's mechanical energy. This means high-redshift X-ray cavity surveys can miss mechanically important AGN bubbles simply because their own CMB-boosted IC emission masks the cavity shadow.

Main conclusions
  • In the $z=1.16$ cluster SPT-CL J2215$-$3537, relativistic leptons need to carry only 0.6–1.2% of the total bubble pressure (for a power-law index $s=2.5$ and low-energy cutoff $100 \le \gamma_{\min} \le 1000$)—or 0.2–3.7% across the full tested range of spectra and atmospheric uncertainties—to completely fill in the 0.5–2 keV X-ray cavity deficit via IC/CMB emission.
  • Even if this radiating lepton component loses all of its energy to cooling, the bubble's total mechanical enthalpy ($H = 4pV$) drops by less than 3% relative to an adiabatic bubble, meaning a cavity can remain energetically powerful while becoming completely invisible as an X-ray depression.
  • The main physical uncertainty is the low-energy cutoff of the electron spectrum: lowering $\gamma_{\min}$ to 10 with a steep slope ($s=3$) places most of the lepton pressure in electrons too low-energy to upscatter CMB photons into the soft X-ray band, raising the required pressure fraction for fill-in to 18.9%.
  • For a fixed cluster atmosphere and lepton spectrum, the radiating pressure fraction required to erase the X-ray cavity scales as $(1+z)^{-4}$, making IC fill-in a rapidly growing selection effect for high-redshift AGN feedback surveys.

Abstract

At high redshift, the cosmic microwave background (CMB) both cools relativistic leptons and provides seed photons for inverse-Compton (IC) X-ray emission. These two effects link the particle content of a bubble directly to the soft X-ray depression used to identify a cavity. We derive a physical criterion connecting lepton cooling, bubble volume, enthalpy evolution, and 0.5–2 keV X-ray visibility, while separating intrinsic filling of the X-ray depression from detector-dependent cavity recovery. For uncooled electron populations described by a power law with spectral index $s=2.5$, minimum Lorentz factor $100\leq\gamma_{\min}\leq1000$, and maximum Lorentz factor $\gamma_{\max}=10^5$, the measured atmosphere of the testbed high-redshift cluster SPT-CL J2215$-$3537 requires 0.6–1.2% of the pressure in radiating leptons to fill the soft-band deficit. Across the full tested ranges of $s$, $\gamma_{\min}$, and atmospheric properties, the required fraction is 0.2–3.7%. Complete loss of this component reduces the enthalpy by less than 3% relative to an otherwise identical adiabatic bubble at the same pressure. A low-cutoff control with $\gamma_{\min}=10$ and $s=3$ instead requires 18.9%, identifying the poorly constrained low-energy spectrum as the main physical uncertainty. For a fixed atmosphere and spectrum, the required fraction scales as $(1+z)^{-4}$. The declining-pressure tracks show that the X-ray deficit can cross the adopted detection threshold while the enthalpy deficit remains below 0.4%; the time of this transition depends on the assumed pressure history. Thus, high-redshift cavity visibility depends on both the surrounding atmosphere and the pressure-carrying particle spectrum. This criterion provides the physical basis for an instrument-specific recovery analysis.
