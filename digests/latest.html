Horndeski Gravity as an Effective Dark Energy Fluid in Cosmological N-body Simulations

Highly relevantVicente Pedreros Aballay
arXiv:2611.06330 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: The paper directly addresses the implementation of beyond-ΛCDM models (Horndeski) in N-body simulations, which is central to the student's work on Euclid-related simulation emulators and modified gravity.

This paper presents a new implementation of Horndeski gravity models within the cosmological N-body code CONCEPT, utilizing an effective dark energy fluid approach. By integrating a modified version of the Einstein-Boltzmann solver CLASS (mochi_CLASS), the authors evolve linear dark energy perturbations alongside the N-body simulation to capture the impact of modified gravity on the matter power spectrum. The study validates this framework by simulating Hu-Sawicki f(R) gravity and cubic Galileon models, demonstrating that while the code successfully reproduces linear-regime deviations from General Relativity, it currently lacks a non-linear screening mechanism, which is identified as a key area for future development.

Main conclusions
  • Successfully implemented Horndeski gravity models as an effective fluid in the CONCEPT N-body code.
  • Achieved sub-0.2% agreement with standard ΛCDM simulations when the Horndeski parameters are tuned to recover General Relativity.
  • Observed significant deviations from GR in the matter power spectrum, reaching up to 40% for f(R) and 25% for cubic Galileon models in the linear regime.
  • Identified the need for implementing a Jeans-length-based screening mechanism to accurately capture non-linear gravitational effects.

Abstract

Unraveling the nature of the cosmic expansion, driven by Dark Energy (DE), remains one of the key challenges in modern cosmology. Modified Gravity (MG) models provide an alternative to the standard cosmological constant paradigm. These models predict distinct signatures in the growth of structure, yet analytical predictions are challenging due to the highly non–linear equations involved. To overcome this problem, we study MG as an effective DE fluid. To this end, we have incorporated linear DE perturbations into the N–body code $\texttt{CO}N\texttt{CEPT}$, which relies on the Einstein–Boltzmann solver $\texttt{CLASS}$. We replaced the $\texttt{CLASS}$ version used by $\texttt{CO}N\texttt{CEPT}$ with $\texttt{mochi\_CLASS}$, a patched version that includes Horndeski family models. By writing linear DE perturbations ($\delta\rho_{\rm DE}$, $\delta P_{\rm DE}$, $\theta_{\rm DE}$, and $\sigma_{\rm DE}$) in terms of metric perturbations in the synchronous gauge, the N–body code is able to evolve the effective fluid using the Boltzmann equation. Our current simulations encompass a range of cosmological scenarios —$w_0w_a$CDM, Hu–Sawicki $f(R)$ and cubic Galileon— each extended to include massive neutrinos. For each of the models, we have found deviations from General Relativity close to what is predicted in the linear regime: a sub-$0.2\%$ relative difference for $\Lambda$CDM, up to $40\%$ for $f(R)$ and a sub-$25\%$ for cubic Galileon. Since our code is the first to implement Horndeski gravity into an N–body code, its validation is a non–trivial computational task. Our novel code opens the door to explore a vast number of modified gravity models in the context of current and future cosmological surveys.

Planting neutrino SE-EdS: A < 3% accurate clustering approximation for neutrinos and other hot dark matter

Highly relevantAmol Upadhye, Yvonne Y. Y. Wong
arXiv:2611.06399 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, hep-ph
Why: This paper provides a direct, high-accuracy method for incorporating neutrino clustering into N-body simulations, which is highly relevant to the student's work on fast, accurate non-linear theory predictions and beyond-ΛCDM simulations.

This paper introduces 'SE-EdS', a new, computationally efficient linear approximation for modeling the clustering of massive neutrinos and other hot dark matter (HDM) species. Because neutrinos have significant thermal velocities, they do not cluster like cold dark matter (CDM) on small scales, making them expensive to track in N-body simulations. The authors develop a fitting function for the ratio of the neutrino density contrast to the exact Einstein-de Sitter (EdS) solution, which remains accurate to within 3% across a wide range of cosmological parameters ($\Omega_{m,0}$, $\Omega_{\nu,0}$, $\Omega_{K,0}$, and $w_0$). This method allows researchers to 'paint' neutrino clustering into N-body simulations in Fourier space at negligible cost, providing a robust tool for precision cosmology where neutrino degeneracies are critical.

Main conclusions
  • The SE-EdS approximation achieves <3% accuracy for neutrino clustering across a broad parameter space ($0.2 \leq \Omega_{m,0} \leq 1$, $w_0 \leq -0.6$, and $|\Omega_{K,0}| \leq 0.1$).
  • The method is significantly more accurate than previous 'Super-Easy' (SE) fits in the linear regime ($k \lesssim 0.1 \, h/\text{Mpc}$) and remains robust in the non-linear regime.
  • The approximation is expressed solely in terms of the large-scale growth factor $G(a) = D(a)/a$, making it easily adaptable to arbitrary dark energy models and modified gravity scenarios.
  • The implementation is computationally inexpensive, enabling the inclusion of neutrino effects in large-scale structure simulations without the need for full 6D phase-space tracking.

Abstract

The age of the cosmic neutrino is upon us. Aside from their fundamental importance to particle physics and the recent tensions in their mass scale $M_\nu = \sum m_\nu$, neutrinos' degeneracies with other cosmological parameters in analyses of scale-dependent cosmic clustering will significantly affect the estimates of these parameters, making accurate, inexpensive, easily-implemented approximations essential tools for theoretical cosmology. We present a new linear approximation, SE-EdS, applicable to non-relativistic neutrinos and other hot dark matter, which is exact in the linear Einstein-de Sitter case, and < 3% accurate for a broad range of flat and non-flat cosmologies characterized by parameters $0.2 \leq \Omega_{m,0} \leq 1$, $0 \leq \Omega_{\nu,0} \leq 0.018$, $-0.1 \leq \Omega_{K,0} \leq 0.1$, and $w_0 \leq -0.6$, at all length scales. This broad coverage in $\Omega_{m,0}$ makes SE-EdS ideal for N-body simulations, whose redshift range $z \lesssim 100$ demands accuracy for time-dependent $\Omega_m$ between unity and the preferred late-time values $\sim 0.3$. SE-EdS may be used to paint neutrinos into simulations in Fourier space, where its < 3% error and neutrinos' $\leq$ 3% matter fraction guarantees < 0.1% errors in the total matter density contrast, thereby including neutrino effects upon clustering at negligible computational cost. We quantify the approximation's accuracy; extend it to a broad range of cosmologies; and explore its correspondence with higher-order corrections to existing linear response schemes. Cosmological Backreaction and the Separate Universe Conjecture: Dynamical volume partitioning of Newtonian cosmologies

Highly relevantThomas Buchert, Jan J. Ostrowski, Alfred Bovon
arXiv:2611.06787 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: The paper directly addresses the theoretical foundations of backreaction and inhomogeneous cosmology, which are core components of your PhD project on relativistic/inhomogeneous cosmologies and the validity of approximate simulation methods.

This paper investigates the theoretical foundations of cosmological backreaction within Newtonian gravity by employing an exact, dynamical volume partitioning of the universe into distinct spatial regions, such as overdense and underdense domains. The authors analyze the interaction between these regions and their global average properties, specifically testing the consistency of 'separate universe' conjectures—which treat local regions as independent Friedmann-like domains—against the global constraints imposed by a 3-torus topology. By deriving the Euler-Newton-Jeans system for collisionless matter, the study demonstrates that identifying local expansion rates with global Friedmannian rates is generally inconsistent due to mass flux and backreaction terms arising from the non-commutation of spatial averaging and time evolution. This work provides a rigorous framework for understanding how local structure formation influences global expansion, highlighting the limitations of simplified regional models in capturing the full dynamics of inhomogeneous cosmologies.

Main conclusions
  • Identified that 'separate universe' approximations, such as the Average Expansion Rate Approximation (AvERA), are constrained by the global 3-torus topology mandatory in standard Newtonian simulations.
  • Demonstrated that when simulation cells are treated as non-material control volumes, Reynolds transport introduces mass flux and flux-backreaction terms, preventing them from acting as independent Friedmann domains.
  • Extended the Euler-Newton-Jeans hierarchy to include velocity dispersion, showing that the kinematical backreaction $\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}$ is a fundamental consequence of the non-commutation of averaging and time evolution in inhomogeneous systems.

Abstract

We provide a description of general Newtonian cosmologies for collisionless matter in terms of an exact and dynamical volume partitioning of global realizations of Newtonian models. Employing the formalism for the description of cosmological backreaction, an interaction between the inhomogeneities of the formation of structures in the Universe and their average properties can be analyzed. Our results can be regarded as a tool for many potential applications in the context of Newtonian simulations, but they also provide an exact way to assume analytical properties of the universe models on regional scales in conformity with global integral constraints on their average properties. We discuss approaches in relation to ‘separate Universe conjectures’ in the literature and examine their consistency with the exact partitioning framework. In particular, using the Average Expansion Rate Approximation (AvERA) as an example, we show that identifying locally assigned Friedmannian rates with physical regional expansion rates is constrained by the global Newtonian 3-torus condition. Alternatively, when the simulation cells are interpreted as non-material control volumes, Reynolds transport introduces mass flux and flux-backreaction terms, so that their dynamics is not in general that of independent Friedmann domains. We also discuss the corresponding consistency conditions for stable clustering prescriptions.

An obstruction to cosmic acceleration from shift-symmetric dark sector interactions

RelevantJose Beltrán Jiménez, Luis J. Garay, Erik Jensko, María Pérez Garrote
arXiv:2611.05560 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-th
Why: The paper uses EFT of cosmological media to constrain dark sector models, which is highly relevant to the student's work on beyond-ΛCDM simulations and the theoretical foundations of modified gravity/dark sector interactions.

This paper investigates the theoretical viability of dark sector models consisting of a perfect fluid interacting with a superfluid, using an effective field theory (EFT) approach. The authors derive stability conditions for these systems, focusing on the absence of ghosts and Laplacian instabilities in the large-k limit. They demonstrate that such models cannot support a stable slow-roll expansion, nor can they accommodate a stable phantom-to-non-phantom crossing of the dark energy equation of state. These results impose significant constraints on beyond-ΛCDM models, particularly those attempting to explain recent observational hints of evolving dark energy.

Main conclusions
  • Slow-roll expansion is incompatible with stability in this class of interacting dark sector models, as the adiabatic sound speed squared $c_a^2$ becomes negative.
  • Stable phantom-divide crossing is prohibited for the total medium; the effective dark energy component can only transition from non-phantom to phantom, contradicting the direction preferred by some recent DESI-based analyses.
  • The EFT framework provides a robust dictionary between fluid-superfluid interactions and thermodynamic variables, allowing for the systematic testing of theoretical stability in modified dark sector scenarios.

This paper addresses the challenge of applying self-supervised image denoising to low-signal-to-noise (SNR) astronomical data, where standard methods like Noise2Void (N2V) often introduce systematic flux biases that compromise downstream scientific analysis. The authors demonstrate that standard N2V significantly underestimates the flux of faint galaxies, and they propose a two-part solution: replacing the final layer with a bounded-output activation to prevent negative flux predictions, and fine-tuning the model using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) with flux-aware loss functions. By incorporating differentiable aperture-photometry into the training loop, the authors ensure that the denoised images preserve the original photometric scale while significantly reducing random flux scatter in repeated observations. This approach provides a robust framework for improving image quality in deep surveys without sacrificing the integrity of photometric measurements.

Main conclusions
  • Standard N2V denoising introduces a strong, magnitude-dependent flux bias, systematically underestimating the flux of faint galaxies.
  • The proposed bounded-output architecture effectively eliminates the systematic flux loss, restoring the median flux ratio to near unity for faint sources.
  • LoRA-based fine-tuning with flux-aware losses reduces random flux scatter by approximately 47% in repeated PAUS exposures.
  • The method successfully preserves spectral features, such as the $D4000$ break, while reducing measurement scatter by 33–37% in faint galaxy samples.

Abstract

Tip of the red giant branch (TRGB)-based determinations of the Hubble constant ($H_0$), the current expansion rate of the Universe, depend on choices made in selecting and analyzing stellar photometry and in assembling the sample of calibrator galaxies hosting Type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia). The CosmoVerse Data Challenge invites participants to analyze public photometric catalogs within a shared distance-ladder framework, opening each step of the measurement to the community and testing how these choices propagate into $H_0$. Dynamics of self-gravitating halos with decaying dark matter

Why: The paper develops a fast emulator for non-linear halo structure in a beyond-ΛCDM model, which is highly relevant to the student's work on emulators and non-linear modelling for Euclid.

This paper investigates the structural evolution of dark matter halos in models where dark matter particles decay into a slightly less massive daughter particle and a massless particle, imparting a small 'kick' velocity. Using the NSphere code, which exploits spherical symmetry to efficiently evolve halos, the authors simulate the resulting halo heating and expansion across a range of decay lifetimes and kick velocities. They find that these decays lead to core formation and a reduction in central density, and they develop a PCA-based emulator that maps decay parameters to the relaxed density profile with ~1% accuracy. This work provides a computationally efficient tool to predict non-linear halo-scale signatures of decaying dark matter, which can be used to test these models against upcoming high-resolution observations from surveys like Euclid and the Rubin Observatory.

Main conclusions
  • Decaying dark matter (DDM) causes halo heating and expansion, leading to a shallower inner density profile (core formation) and a reduction in enclosed mass.
  • The structural response is primarily driven by the kick velocity $v_{ m kick}$, with the mass deficit at $8r_s$ scaling approximately as $1 - 2.2(v_{ m kick}/v_s)^2$.
  • The emulator provides percent-level accuracy for density profiles within the domain $0.1 ext{–} 7.5 r_s$ for $v_{ m kick} ext{ in } [0.1, 0.5] v_s$.
  • Decay dynamics tend to erase the memory of initial orbital anisotropy, driving halos toward a common, mildly radially biased state ($eta ext{ ~ } 0.15$) regardless of initial conditions.

Abstract

We study the evolution of self-gravitating spherical dark matter halos composed of dark matter particles that decay to a slightly less massive particle and a massless particle. The small kick velocity imparted to the daughter particle upon decay heats the halo. If the kick velocity is small compared with the characteristic velocity, the halo remains bound but puffs out.
