Breaking dark degeneracies in $k$-essence models with redshift-space distortions

RelevantRebecca Briffa, Lehel Csillag, Erik Jensko
arXiv:2611.07033 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: The paper investigates linear growth of structure and parameter degeneracies in dynamical dark energy ($k$-essence) models using DESI DR2 BAO, SNe Ia, and RSD growth data.

In dynamical dark energy models where the scalar field evolves through an effective pressureless phase ($w_\phi \approx 0$), expansion history measurements alone often suffer from severe parameter degeneracies between the dark energy sector parameters and the matter density $\Omega_{m,0}$. To address this, the authors test whether redshift-space distortion (RSD) growth-rate measurements ($f\sigma_8$) can break these degeneracies in two kinetic scalar field models: the ghost condensate and the dilatonic ghost condensate $k$-essence models. Using MCMC sampling with observational data from Pantheon+ supernovae, DESI DR2 BAO, cosmic chronometers, and RSD growth rates, they enforce physical stability priors to strictly exclude ghosts ($Q_s > 0$) and Laplacian/gradient instabilities ($c_s^2 \ge 0$). Additionally, because $k$-essence fields can have very small sound speeds ($c_s^2 \to 0$), the authors quantitatively verify that the quasi-static approximation remains valid on linear RSD scales due to background suppression of dark energy perturbations.

Main conclusions
  • Enforcing non-ghost ($Q_s > 0$) and gradient stability ($c_s^2 \ge 0$) theoretical priors restricts the scalar field to stable non-phantom evolution ($-1 \le w_\phi < 1/3$) and subluminal propagation speeds ($0 \le c_s^2 < 1/3$).
  • RSD growth-rate measurements ($f\sigma_8$) significantly break the background parameter degeneracy between $\Omega_{m,0}$ and dark energy parameters, proving especially crucial for the dilatonic ghost condensate model.
  • The quasi-static approximation for matter perturbations remains valid on linear RSD scales ($k \sim 0.01 - 0.02 \, h\text{Mpc}^{-1}$) despite vanishingly small scalar sound speeds, as dark energy density perturbations remain strongly suppressed by $\Omega_\phi(1+w_\phi) \ll 1$.
  • Both ghost condensate models prefer slightly higher central values for the clustering amplitude $S_8$ than $\Lambda$CDM, though model selection metrics (BIC) still favor standard $\Lambda$CDM due to its fewer free parameters.

Dynamical dark energy models are often difficult to constrain with background observations alone due to parameter degeneracies. This is especially true when the dark energy sector evolves through an effective pressureless matter phase, leading to degeneracies between the model parameters and the matter density parameter. In this work, we show that redshift-space distortion (RSD) measurements help break this degeneracy when combined with expansion history probes including Pantheon$^{+}$ supernovae and DESI DR2 BAO measurements. Viable Phantom Crossing in Softly Broken Shift Symmetric Gravity

RelevantRodrigo Calderon, Eric V. Linder
arXiv:2611.07035 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: It offers a viable Horndeski modified gravity explanation for the evolving dark energy / phantom crossing signal seen in DESI DR2 and Planck, with clear implications for linear growth ($G_\mathrm{eff}$).

Recent observational hints from DESI BAO and supernova compilations suggest that dark energy may cross the phantom divide ($w = -1$), transitioning from $w < -1$ at $z \sim 1$ to $w > -1$ today, which is impossible to realize with standard minimally coupled quintessence without introducing ghosts. The authors propose 'Softly Broken Shift Symmetric' (SBSS) gravity within luminal Horndeski theory, where shift symmetry holds at high energies to protect against quantum corrections but is softly broken at late times via exponential field modulations in the kinetic ($K$) and braiding ($G_3$) terms. They analyze the autonomous dynamical system, show that the model naturally transitions through $w = -1$ while remaining stable and ghost-free, and fit the background cosmology to DESI DR2, Union3.1 supernovae, and Planck 2018 CMB data.

Main conclusions
  • SBSS gravity provides a healthy, ghost-free physical mechanism for crossing the phantom divide around $z \approx 0.5$ without requiring a potential or cosmological constant.
  • Fitting DESI DR2 BAO, Union3.1 SNe, and Planck 2018 CMB yields an improvement of $\Delta\chi^2 \approx -3$ and a log Bayes factor preference $\Delta\ln Z \approx +3.1$ relative to $w_0w_a$CDM for the same number of effective dark energy parameters ($N_\mathrm{par} = 2$).
  • Because the theory maintains minimal tensor coupling, gravitational slip remains unity ($\eta = 1$), but kinetic braiding generates a time-dependent $G_\mathrm{eff}(z)$ that predicts testable deviations in linear structure growth.

Why: The paper uses a multifluid perturbation framework and explores non-standard dark energy models with clustering properties, which is highly relevant to the student's work on beyond-$\Lambda$CDM emulators and growth of structure.

This paper presents a multi-probe observational analysis of a tachyonic Dirac-Born-Infeld (DBI) dark energy model, a type of k-essence theory where a noncanonical kinetic term leads to a time-dependent equation of state and a subluminal sound speed. Unlike the cosmological constant or standard quintessence, this model allows for dark energy clustering on subhorizon scales, which the authors test using a combination of CMB distance priors, DESI DR2 BAO, cosmic chronometers, $f\sigma_8$ growth data, and multiple Type Ia supernova compilations. The authors use nested sampling to constrain the model parameters, finding that while the DBI scenario is statistically competitive with $\Lambda$CDM, it is restricted by a theoretical no-go theorem that prevents it from crossing the phantom divide ($w=-1$), limiting its ability to fully capture recent DESI DR2 preferences for evolving dark energy.

  • The DBI model remains consistent with $\Lambda$CDM within $2\sigma$ for standard parameters, though it prefers a slightly lower $H_0$ and higher $\Omega_{m0}$.

We present a multi-probe observational analysis of a clustering tachyonic Dirac-Born-Infeld (DBI) dark energy model. Its noncanonical kinetic term gives a subluminal sound speed, so dark energy perturbations can grow on subhorizon scales. The box within the Universe: anisotropies in periodic cosmological simulations I. Dark-matter-only simulations

RelevantGábor Rácz, Till Sawala
arXiv:2611.08223 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: Co-authored by collaborator Gábor Rácz, this paper focuses on fundamental $N$-body simulation methodology, volume artifacts, and non-linear mode coupling relevant for simulating structures beyond standard setup assumptions.

Cosmological $N$-body simulations almost universally employ periodic boundary conditions on a cubic three-torus ($T^3$) to approximate an infinite universe. However, this topology breaks continuous $SO(3)$ rotational symmetry down to the discrete octahedral point group $O_h$, introducing an intrinsic, box-aligned gravitational force anisotropy that invalidates total angular momentum conservation. In this paper, the authors evaluate an ensemble of 498 dark-matter-only simulations with box sizes ranging from $L=6.25\text{ Mpc}$ to $100\text{ Mpc}$ at fixed mass and force resolution to quantify how these boundary artifacts distort cosmological statistics. They demonstrate that geometry-induced anisotropic tidal fields couple through non-linear evolution, propagating directional biases to scales much smaller than the box size and corrupting halo abundances, shapes, alignments, and filament orientations in small periodic volumes.

Main conclusions
  • Cubic periodic boundary conditions ($T^3$ topology) reduce full continuous rotational symmetry to the discrete octahedral group $O_h$, leading to non-conservation of total angular momentum due to net torques from periodic images.
  • Non-linear mode coupling propagates the box-aligned anisotropic gravitational tidal field down to small physical scales, systematically biasing halo shapes, alignments, merger histories, and filament orientations.
  • Small periodic boxes ($L \lesssim 25\text{ Mpc}$), commonly used in modern hydrodynamical galaxy-formation runs, suffer from significant macro-environment orientation biases at late times ($z=0$).
  • To suppress periodic-box anisotropy effects below the percent level at $z=0$, standalone simulations require box sizes of $L \ge 100\text{ Mpc}$, or should employ cosmological zoom-in techniques inside a large parent volume.

Abstract

The vast majority of cosmological simulations employ periodic boundary conditions to achieve statistical homogeneity and to mimic the infinite spatial extent of our Universe. In a cubic periodic domain the Green function breaks full rotational invariance and introduces a geometry-induced anisotropy in the gravitational interaction. We quantify how this intrinsic anisotropy affects cosmic structure growth and investigate the extent to which it biases the commonly used large-scale structure and halo-based observables in dark-matter-only simulations. We analyse an ensemble of 498 cosmological dark-matter-only $N$-body simulations in cubic $T^3$ topological manifolds, evolved to $z=0$, spanning box sizes from $L=6.25\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ to $L=100\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ at fixed mass and force resolution. Our results demonstrate that periodic-box anisotropy is a non-negligible source of systematics for small simulation volumes, including those commonly employed in modern hydrodynamical simulations.

Beyond-Standard-Model neutrino self-interactions ($ u$SI) delay the onset of neutrino free-streaming in the early Universe, leaving scale-dependent signatures of enhancement and suppression in the matter power spectrum $P(k)$ at high wavenumbers. Because hierarchical structure formation implies that high-redshift galaxies inhabit low-mass halos, measurements of galaxy abundances at early times offer a sensitive probe of small-scale power spectrum modifications. In this paper, the authors combine Planck CMB data with JWST high-redshift ($z \sim 9\text{–}25$) UV luminosity functions to place bounds on the effective neutrino interaction coupling $G_{\mathrm{eff}}$ for both single-flavor and three-flavor interaction scenarios. By modeling the halo mass function and galaxy-halo connection using non-linear modeling tools, they demonstrate that JWST observations significantly tighten limits on neutrino self-interactions compared to CMB data alone.

Main conclusions
  • Combining JWST UV luminosity functions with Planck CMB data strengthens bounds on neutrino self-interaction strength $G_{\mathrm{eff}}$ compared to Planck alone.
  • For single-flavor interacting neutrinos ($1I+2f$), the 95% C.L. upper bound on $\log_{10}(G_{\mathrm{eff}}/\mathrm{MeV}^{-2})$ improves from $-2.00$ (Planck) to $-2.75$ (Planck + JWST).
  • For three-flavor interacting neutrinos ($3I+0f$), the 95% C.L. upper bound improves from $-2.85$ (Planck) to $-3.41$ (Planck + JWST).

Algebraic classification of single-field inflationary dynamics

Jinn-Ouk Gong, Wei-Chen Lin
arXiv:2611.07331 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

In single-field inflation, models are traditionally classified assuming slow-roll conditions, but non-attractor regimes like ultra-slow-roll (USR) can lead to non-trivial evolution of primordial perturbations. The authors develop an exact algebraic framework for canonical single-field background dynamics by expressing the inflaton velocity as a depressed cubic equation. From its root structure, they identify an algebraic branch-swap boundary as well as a strict physical bound for downhill inflaton motion ($\\eta > -6 + 2\\epsilon$). Independently, using the conserved momentum conjugate to the comoving curvature perturbation $\\mathcal{R}$ on super-horizon scales, they formulate a physical criterion for the growth of the gauge-invariant curvature difference $|\\zeta - \\mathcal{R}|$. Combining these criteria yields a unified dynamical chart in slow-roll parameter space, allowing one to map out the dynamical trajectory of an inflationary model across different background and perturbation regimes.

Main conclusions
  • The inflaton velocity satisfies an exact depressed cubic equation, whose discriminant structure reveals a branch-swap boundary at $\\eta_{\\text{swap}} = -6(1-\\epsilon)$ where physical trajectories switch between lower- and higher-velocity algebraic branches.
  • Monotonic downhill inflaton motion requires $\\eta > -6 + 2\\epsilon$, establishing ultra-slow-roll (USR) on a flat potential as the limiting boundary of maximally damped downhill kinetics.
  • Super-horizon growth of the gauge-invariant curvature difference $|\\zeta - \\mathcal{R}|$ is dictated by $\\Gamma_{\\mathcal{R}} = 3 - \\epsilon + \\eta < 0$, which is physically and dynamically distinct from both the background branch-swap boundary and the USR limit.
  • The authors construct a 2D dynamical chart in $(\\epsilon, \\chi)$ space to trace inflationary trajectories, demonstrating that curvature perturbation growth can begin well before entering higher-velocity or USR regimes.

Abstract

We develop an algebraic classification of canonical single-field inflationary dynamics using an exact depressed cubic equation for the inflaton velocity. Its root structure identifies a branch-swap boundary distinct from the limiting boundary of downhill motion, where ultra-slow-roll inflation naturally emerges. Independently, the conservation of momentum conjugate to the comoving curvature perturbation in the long-wavelength limit provides a criterion for the growth or decay of the gauge-invariant difference between comoving and uniform-density curvature perturbations. Combining these criteria, we construct a unified dynamical chart in terms of the Hubble and potential slow-roll parameters, illustrating its applicability to constant-roll inflation and simple toy models. Our classification shifts the emphasis from assigning models to fixed categories toward understanding the dynamical regimes traversed by inflationary trajectories and their implications for primordial perturbations.

RelatedLei Zu, Kazunori Kohri
arXiv:2611.07574 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.HE, hep-ph

Primordial black holes (PBHs) in the supermassive regime ($M_{\rm PBH} \gtrsim 10^8 M_\odot$) act as early gravitational seeds, accreting surrounding dark matter to form dense dark matter spikes known as 'dressed PBHs'. Rather than relying on the standard scale-independent Poisson shot-noise effect, these localized, highly compact structures modify the non-linear matter power spectrum primarily through an enhanced 1-halo term on small scales. The authors incorporate dressed PBHs into the halo-model framework under optimistic (Case 1) and conservative (Case 2) dark matter accretion prescriptions with spatial mass compensation. Using cosmic shear correlation functions from the Dark Energy Survey Year 3 (DES Y3), they run MCMC chains to derive upper limits on the PBH dark matter fraction $f_{\rm PBH}$ across $M_{\rm PBH} \in [10^8, 10^{18}] M_\odot$, and forecast future sensitivities for the Rubin Observatory's LSST.

Main conclusions
  • Incorporated dark matter accretion spikes around supermassive PBHs into a compensated 1-halo model framework to capture non-linear small-scale $P(k)$ enhancements.
  • Used DES Y3 cosmic shear data to place 95% C.L. upper limits on $f_{\rm PBH}$, excluding $f_{\rm PBH} \gtrsim 10^{-4}$ for $M_{\rm PBH} \gtrsim 10^{10} M_\odot$ under an optimistic accretion scenario (Case 1).
  • Conservative accretion assumptions (Case 2, halting DM accretion at $z=30$) yield less stringent bounds, particularly for $M_{\rm PBH} < 10^{12} M_\odot$.
  • Forecasts using mock Rubin Observatory (LSST) weak lensing data indicate an improvement in constraint sensitivity on $f_{\rm PBH}$ by about two orders of magnitude.

Local measurements of the Hubble constant ($H_0$) using Type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia) rely on anchoring distant Hubble-flow SNe to nearby calibrator SNe on a unified photometric zero-point scale. Recently, Karchev et al. (2026) claimed an independent determination of $H_0$ by combining the Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF) DR2 SN Ia dataset with SH0ES calibrators.   • ZTF DR2 remains valid for internal relative studies (e.g., SNe Ia population properties), but robust cosmological constraints require the forthcoming fully calibrated ZTF data release.

GradFlex: a Gradient-Based Method for Weak Lensing Flexion Measurement

RelatedJiarui Sun, Jun Zhang, Toshifumi Futamase
arXiv:2611.07824 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO

Gravitational lensing flexion represents the second-order spatial gradient of weak lensing convergence and shear, probing small-scale mass variations like subhalos in galaxy clusters. Detecting flexion from galaxy images is notoriously difficult due to faint signals, point spread function (PSF) convolution, noise, and complex galaxy morphologies. The authors present GradFlex, a gradient-based method that extracts first (spin-1) and second (spin-3) flexion from surface brightness gradient fields in Fourier space without assuming a specific PSF model or parametric light profile. Using surface brightness conservation and ensemble-averaged spin statistics with derived response factors, the method isolates flexion signals from galaxy shape noise. Tests on simulated regular (Sérsic) and irregular galaxies demonstrate that GradFlex recovers reduced flexion accurately in the weak-distortion regime and remains stable against noise and centroiding uncertainties.

Main conclusions
  • GradFlex provides a model-independent estimator for reduced flexion ($F$ and $G$) based on ensemble averages of surface brightness gradient products in Fourier space.
  • Simulation tests confirm accurate signal recovery in the weak-lensing regime, though systematic biases occur near halo centers where higher-order terms like shear-flexion couplings become non-negligible.
  • The method demonstrates strong resilience against background noise down to $\text{SNR} \sim 20$ when paired with a Friends-of-Friends based circular masking approach.
  • Centroid shifts between the brightest pixel and flux-weighted center cause minimal impact (under $2\%$ change for regular galaxies) on the extracted flexion.

Focusing on the weakly self interacting regime, we find that this framework can reproduce the theoretically predicted abundances through both short and long lived bound states. While long lived bound states can alleviate the cosmological ${}^7$Li problem, they simultaneously lead to discrepancies with other light element abundances. We show that this tension is resolved by incorporating non thermal nucleosynthesis, which act on these non standard initial light element abundances to alleviate the ${}^7 Li$ tension without violating observational constraints on the other elements. Abstract

We study slow-roll inflation in the context of metric-affine gravity. We consider the most general projectively invariant metric-affine theory at most quadratic in the distortion and linear in curvature with no propagating torsion and non-metricity and couple it non-minimally to a scalar field. We focus on the class of $\xi$-attractors, in which a single function $f(\phi)$ fixes the whole scalar sector. Integrating out the non-dynamical distortion generates a correction to the kinetic term $k(\phi)$ which is $f'(\phi)^2$ times a ratio of polynomials in $f(\phi)$. The new kinetic term can develop poles at finite field values, where the distortion field equations degenerate. We classify the poles and derive the conditions on the effective couplings for poles of order $p=2$ and $p=3$, the maximal order allowed in this setting. We then proceed to study inflation. Second-order poles realize $\alpha$-attractors with $\alpha$ fixed by the residue and are already available in the parity-even sector of the theory. Third-order poles instead require the parity-odd sector: whenever the parity-odd quadratic couplings vanish, the kinetic function splits into two independent terms with poles of order at most $p=2$. Third-order pole-inflation, as we confirm numerically for $f(\phi)\propto\phi^2$, yields $r\propto N_*^{-3/2}$ with a tunable amplitude and $n_s\simeq 1-\tfrac{3}{2N_*}$, a spectral index mildly larger than the $\alpha$-attractor prediction and well centered in the region preferred by the current combination of ACT, SPT and DESI data. This paper investigates the energy exchange between geometry and matter in Einstein-Cartan (EC) cosmology, where torsion is sourced by the spin of matter fields. The authors derive a sector-wise Noether identity showing that energy exchange is proportional to the spin of each individual matter component, implying that spinless sectors—such as minimally coupled photons—must be conserved regardless of the torsion. By analyzing CMB temperature measurements from quasar absorption lines and Sunyaev-Zel'dovich clusters, they demonstrate that models distributing energy exchange universally (including to photons) are strongly disfavored by data. The authors conclude that if torsion exists, it must couple to a spinning dark sector rather than the visible sector to remain consistent with observations.

Main conclusions
  • Minimally coupled spinless fields (like photons) are conserved in Einstein-Cartan theory, meaning their temperature must scale as $T(z) = T_0(1+z)$.

  • The observed CMB temperature evolution is consistent with standard $\Lambda$CDM, placing strict constraints on the coupling of torsion to the visible sector.
  • If torsion is present, it must be sourced by a dark sector with spin, which leaves the expansion history and CMB temperature scaling largely unaffected.
