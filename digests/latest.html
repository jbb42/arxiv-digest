The Universality of Halo Growth and Merger Rates in Modified Gravity Theories

Highly relevantHaicong Xu, Yechun Yong, Feihong He, Zhongxu Zhai, Jiaxin Han, Baojiu Li
arXiv:2611.25472 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
Why: Directly analyses Hu–Sawicki $f(R)$ (F4/F5/F6) and nDGP (N1/N5) $N$-body simulations (ELEPHANT suite), providing both physical insights into halo growth and an important technical caveat on how GR-assumed halo/subhalo unbinding biases mass definitions in modified gravity simulations.

In standard $\Lambda$CDM, dark matter halos grow hierarchically in a remarkably self-similar way: once you scale out a halo's overall mass growth rate, the "specific merger rate" (the number of mergers per unit logarithmic mass gain as a function of progenitor mass ratio $\xi$) is nearly universal across redshift, halo mass, and background cosmology. This paper tests whether that universality survives in modified gravity (MG) theories where fifth forces and environmental screening alter structure formation. Using the ELEPHANT suite of $N$-body simulations—covering General Relativity (GR), Hu–Sawicki $f(R)$ gravity (F4, F5, F6, with chameleon screening), and normal-branch DGP gravity (N1, N5, with Vainshtein screening)—the authors construct halo merger trees with the history-based HBT+ subhalo finder and track both mass accretion histories (MAHs) and merger rates. Measuring how halos assemble and merge in MG is essential for connecting non-linear simulation outputs to galaxy clustering observables and for ensuring halo finders do not introduce hidden GR biases into beyond-$\Lambda$CDM analyses.

Main conclusions
  • Faster halo assembly in modified gravity: Because the fifth force enhances gravitational attraction when unscreened, halos of a fixed $z=0$ mass grow more rapidly at late times in MG than in GR—meaning their high-redshift progenitors were less massive, by up to $\sim 5\%$ in nDGP (N1), $>10\%$ in $f(R)$ F5, and $>15\%$ in F4.
  • Universal specific merger rate: Once normalized by logarithmic halo mass growth ($dN_{\mathrm{merge}} / d\xi / d\log M$), the halo merger rate as a function of progenitor mass ratio $\xi$ is nearly identical across GR, $f(R)$, and nDGP, with negligible dependence on redshift or host halo mass ($M_0 \ge 10^{13.2}\,h^{-1}M_\odot$). This supports the "unbiased accretion" picture where halos sweep up surrounding diffuse matter and smaller halos in fixed proportions regardless of the underlying force law.
  • Subhalo-finder systematic in modified gravity: Defining halo mass via the gravitationally bound particles identified by HBT+ introduces an artificial, redshift-dependent break in merger-rate universality for F4 and F5. Because HBT+ uses a standard Newtonian unbinding routine that ignores the fifth-force binding potential, it systematically underestimates bound subhalo masses in unscreened regimes—a crucial methodological caveat for building halo catalogs in MG simulations.

Abstract

In the standard $\Lambda$CDM framework, the merger rate of dark matter haloes has been found to follow a nearly universal form, with only weak dependence on halo mass and redshift. We examine whether this universality is preserved in modified gravity (MG) models, where the growth of cosmic structure can deviate from that of general relativity (GR). We use the ELEPHANT suite of large $N$-body simulations to study halo merger rates and mass accretion histories in different modified gravity models, including two representative scenarios beyond-GR: the Hu–Sawicki chameleon $f(R)$ model and the normal branch of the Dvali–Gabadadze–Porrati model. For the mass accretion histories, the modified gravitational effect can produce systematic departures from GR, generally corresponding to more rapid halo growth. The deviations are relatively small when the MG effect is weak, while they become more pronounced in models with strong deviation from GR and can exceed $15\%$ in the extreme case. For the halo merger rate, we find that the nearly universal behavior is well preserved across all the gravity models, with only weak dependence on redshift and halo mass, reflecting the unbiased accretion nature of halo growth. However, apparent deviation from this universality can arise if the halo mass is not defined consistently with the modified gravity model.

First $Ω_{\rm m}-w$ Measurement Using Only Fast Radio Burst Observations

RelevantYi-Ying Wang, Shi-Jie Gao, Chi Zhang, Yi-Zhong Fan
arXiv:2611.25312 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.HE
Why: Combines a non-linear matter power spectrum emulator (GokuNEmu) in dynamical dark energy ($w_0 w_a$CDM) space with joint DESI DR2 BAO, Planck CMB, and Pantheon+ likelihoods to extract cosmological constraints from both background expansion and non-linear structure growth.

Fast radio bursts (FRBs) are millisecond-duration extragalactic radio pulses whose signals are dispersed by free electrons along the line of sight; their dispersion measure (DM)—the integrated column density of free electrons—acts as a cosmic distance and structure probe when paired with a host-galaxy redshift. Previous FRB dark energy studies could not constrain the matter density $\Omega_m$ and dark energy equation of state $w$ from FRBs alone because they relied on cosmic-DM probability distributions calibrated at a single fiducial cosmology, ignoring how cosmic-DM variance changes with non-linear structure growth. In this paper, the authors build a self-consistent likelihood for 125 localized FRBs ($0.105 \le z \le 2.148$) by coupling the 10-parameter GokuNEmu non-linear matter power spectrum emulator with an IllustrisTNG/CAMELS-calibrated electron bias model ($P_{ee}(k,z) = b_e^2(k,z) P_{\rm DMO}(k,z)$) to predict how both the mean and the line-of-sight variance of cosmic DM vary across cosmological parameter space. This matters because propagating cosmology into the DM scatter unlocks the first standalone FRB constraints in the $\Omega_m\text{–}w$ plane and reveals a new parameter degeneracy direction that is opposite to that of Type Ia supernovae.

Main conclusions
  • Using 125 localized FRBs alone ($0.105 \le z \le 2.148$) with a BBN prior on $\omega_b$, the authors obtain the first standalone FRB constraints in flat $w$CDM: $\Omega_m = 0.33^{+0.10}_{-0.11}$ and $w = -0.80^{+0.28}_{-0.38}$, consistent with standard $\Lambda$CDM.
  • Because increasing $\Omega_m$ decreases the mean cosmic DM while increasing its clustering variance—whereas increasing $w$ lowers both—FRBs exhibit a positive $\Omega_m\text{–}w$ correlation ($r = +0.54$), which runs nearly orthogonal/opposite to the strong negative degeneracy of Type Ia supernovae ($r = -0.94$).
  • In a flat $w_0 w_a$CDM extension, FRBs alone yield broad constraints ($\Omega_m = 0.33 \pm 0.10$, $w_0 = -0.65^{+0.43}_{-0.57}$, $w_a = -0.78^{+1.26}_{-1.25}$), while combining FRBs with Planck CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, and Pantheon+ SNe Ia yields $w_0 = -0.846^{+0.054}_{-0.053}$ and $w_a = -0.548^{+0.192}_{-0.204}$, corresponding to a $\sim 2.6\sigma$ preference for dynamical dark energy.
  • A key systematic limitation is that the small-scale electron bias $b_e(k,z)$ and ionized fraction are calibrated to fixed IllustrisTNG/CAMELS hydrodynamical prescriptions, meaning future analyses will need joint cosmology-and-baryonic-feedback emulators to marginalize over gas redistribution by AGN and stellar feedback.

Though fast radio bursts (FRBs) are widely advocated as a dark energy probe, no valid $\Omega_m-w$ constraint has been obtained exclusively from FRB data to date. Galaxy Bias and Soft Limits from Inflation

RelevantCharalampos Nikolis, Henrique Rubira
arXiv:2611.26070 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-th
Why: While the focus on primordial non-Gaussianity and inflationary soft limits is outside your core beyond-$\Lambda$CDM gravity focus, the paper is a deep technical advancement in higher-order galaxy bias renormalization, perturbative EFTofLSS, and Lagrangian-to-Eulerian displaced operator expansions relevant to your galaxy bias and non-linear LSS modelling profile.

When inflation produces primordial non-Gaussianity (PNG)—deviations from purely Gaussian initial density fluctuations—long-wavelength gravitational potential modes modulate small-scale structure formation, leaving a distinctive scale-dependent imprint on galaxy clustering (galaxy bias). However, predicting this signal accurately beyond leading order requires "renormalizing" the perturbative galaxy bias expansion, meaning systematically absorbing the unphysical sensitivity of loop integrals to small-scale (ultraviolet) cutoffs into observable bias parameters. In this paper, the authors construct a general multi-loop renormalization and renormalization-group (RG) framework for both deterministic and stochastic galaxy bias operators in the presence of arbitrary higher-order primordial $n$-point functions ($f_{\rm NL}, g_{\rm NL}, h_{\rm NL}, \dots$). By explicitly computing one- and two-loop bias renormalization up to fifth order for local-type PNG, they show that the entire renormalization structure is governed by the "multi-soft limits" (configurations where several wavenumbers are much smaller than the loop momenta) of primordial correlators and perturbative bias kernels. This provides a clean, model-independent bridge between inflationary consistency relations and large-scale structure perturbation theory, allowing future surveys to test complex multi-point inflationary physics consistently at higher loop orders.

Main conclusions
  • The ultraviolet (small-scale) sensitivity of galaxy bias operators factorizes into two independent ingredients: the multi-soft limits of primordial $n$-point functions (which depend on the inflationary model) and the soft limits of perturbative bias kernels (which depend only on gravity and the bias expansion).
  • At $L$ loops, renormalization probes a new connected topology involving a primordial correlator with $L+1$ hard legs; specifically, two-loop renormalization introduces a connected $[3]$ topology probing a hard bispectrum response alongside a disconnected $[22]$ pair-pair topology, while stochastic renormalization at two loops also probes collapsed trispectrum limits ($\tau_{\rm NL}$).
  • Remarkably, for single-source local PNG ($\Delta=0$) up to fifth order in the bias expansion, all generalized two-loop bias kernels reduce to just two kinematic functions—$\gamma(r)$ for the $[22]$ topology and $\tilde{f}(r,\mu)$ for the $[3]$ topology—which correspond to unsymmetrized versions of the functions already known from Gaussian bias renormalization.
  • The authors derive explicit one- and two-loop renormalization group (RG) equations governing how the bias parameters run with the cutoff scale $\Lambda$ in the presence of arbitrary local PNG couplings ($f_{\rm NL}, g_{\rm NL}, h_{\rm NL}, \dots$), demonstrating that leading hierarchical two-loop terms cancel against one-loop-squared contributions.

The LEMAITRE project: An Independent Probe of the $w-w_a$ Tension

RelevantNicolas Regnault (on behalf of the LEMAITRE project)
arXiv:2611.26192 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: This paper directly addresses the observational evidence for dynamical dark energy ($w_0 w_a$) from Type Ia supernovae combined with DESI BAO and Planck CMB, which is a key medium-relevance topic in your profile and motivates beyond-$\Lambda$CDM modelling.

Recent combinations of DESI BAO, CMB, and Type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia) have hinted at a $\sim 3\sigma$ preference for dynamical dark energy ($w_0 > -1, w_a < 0$) over a cosmological constant, but this signal corresponds to a tiny $\sim 0.04\text{ mag}$ shift in the low-redshift ($z \le 0.1$) distance modulus and is highly sensitive to cross-survey photometric calibration systematics. To provide a completely independent test of this tension, this paper introduces the Lemaître project, which assembles $\sim 3300$ previously unused SNe Ia spanning $0.02 < z < 1.1$ from three surveys: ~2800 low-$z$ SNe from the Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF), 172 intermediate-$z$ SNe from the Supernova Legacy Survey (SNLS), and ~300 high-$z$ ($0.6 < z < 1.1$) SNe from Subaru Hyper Suprime-Cam (HSC). The collaboration processes all three datasets uniformly using pixel-level scene-modeling photometry (which forward-models the point-like supernova alongside the underlying host galaxy background), paired with dedicated hardware metrology to map telescope filter bandpasses and anchor fluxes to primary white-dwarf standards (CALSPEC) at the permille level. Finally, they outline a new end-to-end, JAX-based cosmological inference pipeline (NaCl, EDRIS, and cosmologix) that will soon unblind these light curves to clarify whether the $w_0$–$w_a$ anomaly is real physics or a supernova calibration artifact.

Main conclusions
  • Testing the $w_0$–$w_a$ dynamical dark energy tension ($\mathrm{a}\sim 0.04\text{ mag}$ distance modulus shift at $z \le 0.1$) requires controlling low-$z$ distance modulus uncertainties to $\sim 0.004\text{ mag}$, which demands at least 1500 supernovae per bin, flux zero-point calibration to $\sigma_{\mathrm{zp}} \simeq 0.001\text{ mag}$, and filter bandpass accuracy to $\sigma_{\lambda} \simeq 3.6\,\text{\AA}$.
  • The Lemaître dataset comprises $\sim 3300$ unused Type Ia supernovae across $0.02 < z < 1.1$, providing statistical power comparable to DES-5Y with much stronger low-$z$ anchoring via ZTF DR2 ($\sim 2800$ spectroscopically classified SNe).
  • Hardware metrology campaigns—including an in-situ collimated beam projector for ZTF and laboratory optical benches for SNLS and HSC filters—achieved filter wavelength calibrations of $\sim 1\,\text{\AA}$ (ZTF) to $2\text{–}4\,\text{\AA}$ (SNLS) and relative flux zero-point precision of $1\text{ mmag}$ (ZTF) and $2\text{–}5\text{ mmag}$ (SNLS/HSC) on the HST CALSPEC scale.
  • The project has completed its metrology and blinded light-curve extraction, and is currently validating its end-to-end inference chain (including the JAX-based cosmologix code) on simulations before unblinding the cosmological constraints.

Two powers, one scalar field: unified early and late dark energy

RelevantBikash R. Dinda, Roy Maartens
arXiv:2611.26230 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
Why: Directly addresses dynamical dark energy (quintessence), the Hubble and $S_8$ tensions, and joint cosmological likelihood constraints combining CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, and Pantheon+ supernovae.

Early Dark Energy (EDE) models attempt to ease the Hubble tension—the $\sim 5\sigma$ mismatch between early- and late-universe measurements of $H_0$—by introducing a brief spike of dark energy before recombination that shrinks the sound horizon, though standard setups require a separate cosmological constant $\Lambda$ to drive late-time acceleration. In this paper, Dinda and Maartens explore whether a single minimally coupled canonical scalar field (quintessence) can unify both epochs using a "double-power" potential, $V(\phi) = V_0(\phi/M_{\rm Pl})^{\alpha} + V_1(\phi/M_{\rm Pl})^{\beta}$, with a steep positive power ($\alpha = 25$) and an inverse power ($\beta = -1$). Starting frozen at early times, the field rolls down the steep $\alpha$ branch to generate a transient EDE peak before diluting faster than radiation and matter, eventually settling near the potential minimum created by the competing $\beta = -1$ term to drive late-time cosmic acceleration. The authors implement the background and linear perturbation equations in CLASS (using a modified HMcode-2020 for non-linear clustering) and test the model against Planck CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, and SH0ES-calibrated Pantheon+ supernovae. This provides an economical, single-field beyond-$\Lambda$CDM framework with only one extra free parameter that simultaneously nudges both $H_0$ and $S_8$ toward local observations.

Main conclusions
  • A single double-power scalar potential ($V_0 x^{25} + V_1 x^{-1}$ with fixed initial conditions $\phi_{\rm ini} = 2 M_{\rm Pl}$ and $\dot{\phi}_{\rm ini} = 0$) successfully unifies an EDE-like early density peak with late-time acceleration ($z \simeq 0.8$), where the inverse-power term contributes $\sim 96\%$ of the potential energy today.
  • Fitting to CMB + DESI DR2 BAO + Pantheon+/SH0ES shrinks both the comoving sound horizon $r_s(z_*)$ and the distance to last scattering by $\sim 1.9\%$, shifting the inferred Hubble constant up to $H_0 = 69.53 \pm 0.26\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ (compared to $68.45 \pm 0.24\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ in $\Lambda$CDM)—closer to local measurements, though not enough to fully resolve the Hubble tension.
  • Unlike many standard EDE models that worsen the clustering tension by boosting structure growth, this unified model slightly lowers the matter clustering amplitude to $S_8 = 0.8047 \pm 0.0084$ (down from $0.8126 \pm 0.0063$ in $\Lambda$CDM), moving in the direction favored by weak-lensing surveys like DES Year 6.
  • With only one extra free parameter compared to flat $\Lambda$CDM, the model improves the overall fit to the combined datasets by $\Delta \chi^2_{\rm data} = -6.90$ (or $\Delta \chi^2_{\rm objective} = -3.76$ when accounting for Gaussian prior penalties).

Can next generation CMB experiments discriminate between $Λ$CDM and dynamical dark energy? A Bayesian forecast for Simons Observatory and CMB-S4

RelevantAnna Chiara Ferri, Ruchika, Alessandro Melchiorri
arXiv:2611.26305 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: Directly addresses dynamical dark energy ($w_0w_a$CDM) constraints and cosmological likelihood combinations of CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, and Pantheon+ SNe Ia, which are core medium-relevance topics in your research profile.

Recent Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) measurements from DESI, when combined with Planck CMB and supernova data, suggest up to a $4.2\sigma$ preference for dynamical dark energy—where the equation of state evolves over time via the Chevallier–Polarski–Linder (CPL) parameterization $w(z) = w_0 + w_a z/(1+z)$—over a static cosmological constant ($\Lambda$CDM). However, testing this with the Cosmic Microwave Background alone is notoriously difficult because of the "geometric degeneracy": different combinations of $w_0$, $w_a$, $H_0$, and $\Omega_m$ can yield the exact same angular scale of the sound horizon ($\theta_s$) and first acoustic peak position (quantified by the CMB shift parameter $\mathcal{R}$). In this paper, the authors perform a full Bayesian MCMC forecast using mock CMB temperature, polarization, and lensing power spectra for the upcoming Simons Observatory (SO) and CMB-S4 experiments to test whether CMB data alone can distinguish the DESI-preferred $w_0w_a$CDM cosmology from a $\Lambda$CDM "shadow" model with an identical shift parameter $\mathcal{R}$. Overturning their own previous Fisher matrix forecasts, they find that non-Gaussian degeneracy directions prevent even CMB-S4 from separating dynamical dark energy from $\Lambda$CDM on its own, proving that low-redshift distance probes (like BAO and Type Ia supernovae) remain indispensable for pinning down late-time dark energy evolution.

Main conclusions
  • Next-generation CMB experiments (SO and CMB-S4) alone cannot distinguish a DESI-preferred dynamical dark energy universe ($w_0 = -0.476, w_a = -1.511$) from its $\Lambda$CDM "shadow" model, as the 2D posteriors in the $(w_0, w_a)$ plane remain wide and heavily overlap due to the CMB geometric degeneracy.
  • Full Bayesian MCMC forecasts yield significantly weaker dark energy constraints from the CMB alone than earlier Fisher matrix estimates predicted, demonstrating that Gaussian approximations underestimate the non-Gaussian curved degeneracy between $w_0, w_a, \Omega_m$, and $H_0$.
  • Combining future CMB mocks with low-redshift distance probes (BAO and Pantheon+ Type Ia supernovae) breaks the geometric degeneracy and tightens the present-day dark energy equation of state uncertainty to $\sigma(w_0) \simeq 0.05\text{–}0.06$ for both SO and CMB-S4.
  • The statistical preference for evolving dark energy remains sensitive to the choice of low-redshift BAO dataset: pairing future CMB data with DESI BAO recovers the dynamical dark energy fiducial, whereas pairing with SDSS BAO shifts the posterior back toward $\Lambda$CDM ($w_0 = -1, w_a = 0$).

Breaking the background and linear degeneracies: Using nonlinear clustering to discriminate between dark energy models

RelevantYunhao Gao, Baojiu Li, Jie Wang, Carlton M. Baugh
arXiv:2611.26460 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: Directly relevant to simulating and modelling non-linear structure formation in beyond-$\Lambda$CDM / dynamical dark energy models, showing which beyond-linear degrees of freedom must be kept in simulations and emulators for surveys like Euclid.

In cosmological analyses, dynamical dark energy (DE) is typically parameterized by its background equation of state $w(a)$ and a purely time-dependent effective sound speed squared $\bar{c}_s^2(a)$, which completely fixes its background and linear-perturbation evolution. However, distinct scalar-field theories—such as different $k$-essence models (scalar fields with non-canonical kinetic terms in their Lagrangian $K(\phi, X)$)—can share identical $w(a)$ and $\bar{c}_s^2(a)$ while differing in their higher-order nonlinear interactions. The authors introduce an exact, non-perturbative fluid formulation that splits the $k$-essence Lagrangian into kinetic and potential fluid components, and run 1D spherical-collapse simulations of $10^{14}\,M_\odot$ cluster-sized halos for two different $k$-essence Lagrangians tuned to the exact same $w_0 w_a$ background and linear sound speed ($\bar{c}_s^2 = 10^{-6}$). They demonstrate that nonlinear structure formation breaks the background and linear degeneracy—producing显著 differences in halo density profiles—and prove that only the local sound speed's dependence on DE density perturbations needs to be tracked to capture this effect in future 3D cosmological simulations.

Main conclusions
  • Two $k$-essence models sharing the exact same background expansion ($w_0=-0.8, w_a=-0.1$) and linear effective sound speed ($\bar{c}_s^2 = 10^{-6}$) produce central dark-energy densities that differ by up to $50\%$ (and kinetic densities differing by $80\%$) inside a $10^{14}\,M_\odot$ dark matter halo at $z=0$.
  • Through gravity, this nonlinear dark-energy clustering alters the total matter + dark energy density profile by a few percent out to $r \simeq 2\,\text{Mpc}$, providing a potential observational signature on small scales.
  • The nonlinear difference between models is driven almost entirely by how the local sound speed $c_s^2(\phi, X)$ responds to large local dark-energy density perturbations ($\delta\rho_{\rm DE}/\bar{\rho}_{\rm DE} \sim \mathcal{O}(1)$), which enhances or suppresses pressure support in halo centers.
  • Fixing all other theory-specific terms in the fluid equations (the scalar field $\phi$, Lagrangian $K$, and continuity source term $S$) to their background values yields density profiles matching the full simulation to within $\lesssim 10^{-4}$ for dark energy and $\lesssim 10^{-5}$ for total density, greatly simplifying future 3D N-body implementations.

Splitting the action: A fully nonlinear fluid method for clustering dark energy

RelevantYunhao Gao, Baojiu Li, Jie Wang, Carlton M. Baugh
arXiv:2611.26461 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO
Why: This paper develops a new simulation methodology for nonlinear structure formation in beyond-$\Lambda$CDM (clustering dark energy / $k$-essence) models, directly aligning with your focus on non-linear modelling and simulations of dark energy and modified gravity.

In standard $\Lambda$CDM or simple quintessence models, dark energy has a sound speed equal to the speed of light ($c_s = c$), meaning pressure waves quickly iron out any small-scale dark energy ripples; however, in more general dynamical theories like $k$-essence (scalar fields with non-canonical kinetic terms), $c_s$ can be very small, allowing dark energy to cluster alongside dark matter on nonlinear halo scales. Existing 3D simulations of clustering dark energy rely on linearized equations of state (like $\delta P = \bar{c}_s^2 \delta\rho$) or perturbative field expansions that break down once dark energy density contrasts become large ($\delta_{\mathrm{DE}} \sim \mathcal{O}(1)$). To solve this, the authors introduce a clever "action-splitting" method that separates the scalar-field action into a pure kinetic fluid component and a potential component (which acts like a source/heat reservoir), ensuring the simulated kinetic density obeys an exact equation of state $P_k = \rho_k c^2$ across all scales without any perturbative approximations. They test this fully nonlinear fluid framework in 1D spherical-collapse simulations of two $k$-essence models coupled to dark matter, laying the groundwork for future 3D cosmological simulations using particle-based hydrodynamics like SPH.

Main conclusions
  • By splitting the dark energy action into a kinetic part (with $P_k = \rho_k c^2$) and a potential part (with $P_V = -\rho_V c^2$), the authors derive a closed, exact set of relativistic fluid equations (evolving $\rho_k$, fluid 4-velocity $u^a$, and $\phi$) that require no perturbative expansions in small quantities.
  • In 1D spherical-collapse simulations of two representative $k$-essence models alongside dark matter, the method remains stable and accurately tracks dark energy density contrasts into the deeply nonlinear regime up to $\delta_{\mathrm{DE}} \sim \mathcal{O}(1\text{–}10)$ during halo collapse.

Inhomogeneous Cutoff Regularization of Loops in Cosmological Correlators

arXiv:2611.26495 · PDF · HTML · hep-th, astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-ph

When calculating quantum loop corrections to primordial fluctuations during inflation, physicists must introduce a high-energy (ultraviolet, or UV) momentum cutoff to keep the integrals finite. Because long-wavelength perturbations stretch or squeeze the local spatial geometry once they cross outside the causal horizon, a standard uniform comoving cutoff ($a\Lambda_{\rm UV}$) accidentally regulates different causal patches of the universe at different local physical energy scales, breaking general relativistic coordinate invariance. To solve this, the authors apply the "separate universe" picture—treating each horizon-sized patch as a locally homogeneous universe rescaled by the long-wavelength scalar ($\zeta_L$) and tensor ($\gamma_L$) fluctuations—and define an inhomogeneous comoving momentum cutoff that fixes the same physical UV scale for every local observer. Expanding this perturbation-dependent cutoff boundary generates new diagrammatic counterterms that link short-wavelength quantum loops to long-wavelength background modes. The authors demonstrate that these extra boundary terms exactly cancel spurious UV divergences in the one-loop scalar and tensor power spectra and squeezed bispectrum, confirming within cutoff regularization that primordial perturbations freeze out and remain conserved on superhorizon scales.

Main conclusions
  • In an expanding universe with long-wavelength perturbations, keeping the physical ultraviolet cutoff scale $\Lambda_{\rm UV}$ identical across different Hubble patches requires a locally rescaled comoving cutoff: $\Lambda \to a(t)\Lambda_{\rm UV}e^{\zeta_L}[\hat{k}_i\hat{k}_j(e^{-\gamma_L})^{ij}]^{-1/2}$.

Abstract

We argue that a cutoff corresponding to a fixed physical scale must be defined with respect to the local background geometry. In the presence of superhorizon perturbations, this is naturally implemented within the separate universe picture, where different local patches are regulated at the same physical scale. UV Bounds on Inflationary Parity Violation

Relatedcross-listAlexander Cassem, Mark P. Hertzberg
arXiv:2611.26594 · PDF · HTML · hep-th, astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-ph

In an isotropic and homogeneous universe, spatial parity violation (an asymmetry between left- and right-handed spatial configurations) cannot appear in the 2-point power spectrum or 3-point bispectrum of density fluctuations, making the 4-point correlation function (the trispectrum) the lowest-order statistic where it can be detected in galaxy surveys. A popular way to generate such a primordial signal during inflation is through dynamical Chern-Simons (dCS) gravity, an effective field theory (EFT) modification to General Relativity where the rolling inflaton couples to a parity-odd curvature term ($\phi \,{}^*RR$), making graviton exchange chiral (favoring one graviton helicity over the other). The authors investigate whether the dCS coupling can actually be made large enough to produce an observable parity-odd trispectrum without breaking the underlying high-energy (ultraviolet, or UV) physics—testing concrete UV completions involving chiral fermions as well as heterotic and Type IIB string theory—while also satisfying existing observational bounds on the parity-even tensor-scalar-scalar (TSS) bispectrum. They show that keeping the EFT mathematically consistent and within observational bispectrum limits forces the dCS coupling to be very small, meaning that any future detection of large-scale parity violation would be exceedingly difficult to explain with standard dCS inflation unless the graviton-inflaton-inflaton interaction vertex is dramatically enhanced.

Main conclusions
  • Current observational bounds on the parity-even tensor-scalar-scalar (TSS) bispectrum already restrict the parity-odd scalar trispectrum amplitude $\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{odd}} = \Pi_{\mathrm{c}} r F$ (where $r$ is the tensor-to-scalar ratio, $F$ is a vertex enhancement factor, and $|\Pi_{\mathrm{c}}| \le 1$ is the fractional chirality of the graviton propagator), leaving little room for a detectable signal when $r$ is near current observational limits.
  • Requiring a consistent high-energy (UV) completion severely tightens the bound on the graviton chirality parameter from the naive $|\Pi_{\mathrm{c}}| \le 1$ to $|\Pi_{\mathrm{c}}| \ll 5.9 \times 10^{-3}$ in chiral fermion completions, with similarly stringent limits in heterotic and Type IIB string compactifications once perturbativity ($N_7 g_s \lesssim 1$ for $N_7$ D7-branes) and EFT validity are enforced.
  • Because both $\Pi_{\mathrm{c}}$ and the product $r F$ are tightly bounded, generating a parity-odd trispectrum detectable by upcoming large-scale structure surveys requires an extreme, non-standard enhancement of the TSS vertex $F$ combined with a very small tensor-to-scalar ratio $r$.
