Late-time cosmic acceleration from matter inhomogeneities: constraints from DESI DR2 and SNe Ia

Highly relevantMariano Cadoni, Andrea Cozzumbo, Riccardo Murgia, Matteo Pili, Mirko Pitzalis, Andrea P. Sanna, Matteo Schulz, Ilaria Usai
arXiv:2611.31374 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
Why: This paper is directly on your topic of exact GR inhomogeneous cosmologies and acceleration without $\Lambda$, as it develops an exact non-perturbative GR framework linking nonlinear structure to cosmic acceleration and constrains it with DESI DR2 BAO and Pantheon+ SNe Ia.

To explain late-time cosmic acceleration without invoking a cosmological constant $\Lambda$ or modified gravity, the authors test the Anisotropic Fluid Inhomogeneous Cosmology (AFIC) framework, an exact spherically symmetric General Relativity (GR) solution that models nonlinear structure formation non-perturbatively. In this model, spacetime reduces to standard homogeneous FLRW at early times and on very large scales, while late-time inhomogeneities on scales $\lesssim 200\text{ Mpc}$ are encoded in a single input function, the Misner-Sharp (MS) mass $M(r,z)$ (a covariant measure of the mass enclosed within a sphere of areal radius $R$), whose large-scale asymptotic limit generates an effective dark-energy-like acceleration. Using model-independent Gaussian Process reconstructions of the angular diameter distance $D_A(z)$ from DESI DR2 Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) data and Pantheon+ Type Ia Supernovae (SNe Ia), the authors locate the maximum areal radius $R_{\max} \equiv D_A(z_{\max})$ on our past light cone, which directly yields the total MS mass and cosmic energy density without assuming a cosmological model. They find energy densities consistent with flat $\Lambda$CDM, showing that AFIC can match current expansion data, though breaking the degeneracy between emergent inhomogeneous acceleration and a true cosmological constant will require future multi-probe observations such as CMB lensing and the late Integrated Sachs-Wolfe (ISW) effect.

Main conclusions
  • From DESI DR2 BAO data (calibrated with Planck $r_d = 147.09\text{ Mpc}$), the Gaussian Process reconstruction locates the apparent horizon peak at $z_{\max} = 1.613 \pm 0.012$ and $R_{\max} = 1773.4 \pm 6.1\text{ Mpc}$, yielding an enclosed MS mass $M(R_{\max}, z_{\max}) = (1.853 \pm 0.006) \times 10^{22}\,M_{\odot}$ and total energy density $\rho = (53.68 \pm 0.37) \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg m}^{-3}$.
  • From Pantheon+ SNe Ia data (anchored at $h = 0.7$), the reconstruction gives $z_{\max} = 1.58 \pm 0.11$ and $R_{\max} = 1713 \pm 38\text{ Mpc}$, corresponding to $M(R_{\max}, z_{\max}) = (1.790 \pm 0.040) \times 10^{22}\,M_{\odot}$ and $\rho = (57.5 \pm 2.6) \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg m}^{-3}$, both fully compatible with the flat $\Lambda$CDM prediction ($\rho_{\text{FLRW}} \approx 55.28 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg m}^{-3}$).
  • Because standard collisionless cold dark matter cannot source the large anisotropic stress required by AFIC to drive acceleration, confirming this mechanism would shift the problem from needing exotic dark energy to selecting viable non-CDM dark matter scenarios.
  • Background expansion measurements alone cannot distinguish the emergent inhomogeneous term $\lambda g(z)$ from a cosmological constant; directly reconstructing $M(r,z)$ via late ISW cross-correlations, CMB lensing, and redshift-space distortions will be required to falsify the model.

Abstract

We present the first observational constraints on the Misner-Sharp (MS) mass of the universe, within a newly proposed exact General Relativity solution providing a non-perturbative description of nonlinear cosmic structure formation. At early times, when statistical homogeneity and isotropy are satisfied, our model reduces to usual FLRW cosmology, accommodating Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) data. At late times, the departure from homogeneity and isotropy on scales $\lesssim 200$ Mpc – codified in the scale-dependent redshift distribution of the MS mass – naturally induces cosmic acceleration, through an extensive term generated by the MS mass asymptotic limit on large scales. We outline broader implications and future observational prospects, highlighting how multi-probe strategies – particularly CMB lensing and the late Integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect – can ultimately test this solution to the dark energy puzzle.

This paper addresses the theoretical challenge of describing a massive field strongly mixed with the curvature perturbation during inflation, a regime where standard single-field effective field theories (EFTs) typically fail. Abstract

We study a massive field strongly mixed with the curvature perturbation during inflation, in the regime where the latter is described at low energies by a single-field effective field theory (EFT) with a quadratic dispersion relation. What this EFT captures, and what lies beyond it, are encoded in a connection problem, which relates the solutions that define the vacuum at early times to those that describe observables at late times. We solve it with a general method that does not rely on an exact solution. An expansion in the inverse mixing, keeping both fields, yields the EFT as its leading order, with corrections under systematic control. It selects the vacuum of the EFT, fixes a phase that the EFT cannot determine on its own, and describes the heavy mode at all times. Particle production lies beyond all orders of this asymptotic expansion. We show that it arises from a Stokes phenomenon at the complex branch points of the dispersion relation and that, in a rotating basis of the two fields, it reduces to the familiar production of particles with half the heavy mass. A reflection symmetry of the equations of motion, combined with the canonical commutation relations, then determines all exponentially small effects exactly, and with them the solution of the connection problem, which combines particle production with a conversion between light and heavy quanta. Together with the expansion, these exact results give a compact analytical formula for the cosmological collider signal, whose exponential suppression, half of the usual Boltzmann one, directly reflects the halved mass of the produced particles. The expansion only requires a separation of scales and the adiabaticity of the heavy mode, while the reflection symmetry holds for many systems in de Sitter space. We therefore expect this approach to bring physical understanding and simple formulas well beyond the present case.

A unified stochastic random walk theory for Kolmogorov spectrum, halo mass functions, and halo density profiles

Why: The paper provides a fundamental analytical stochastic derivation for halo mass functions and Einasto halo density profiles, which are key non-linear LSS components relevant to non-linear modeling and emulators.

Cosmic structure formation produces universal statistical distributions—such as the Press-Schechter halo mass function (HMF) describing halo abundances and the Einasto profile describing dark matter halo density—that remarkably parallel the universal energy spectra seen in fluid turbulence. In this paper, the author proposes a unified theoretical framework using continuous-time random walks and Fokker-Planck diffusion equations in a generalized 'scale space' (such as wavenumber $k$, halo mass $m_h$, or radius $r$) governed by scale-dependent waiting times. Applied to cosmology, hierarchical halo growth in mass space yields a generalized HMF transitioning from primordial-spectrum dependence at low masses to a gravity-dominated scaling near the characteristic mass $m_h^*$, while radial particle migration reproduces the Einasto density profile with an outer slope of $\rho(r) \propto r^{-4/3}$. This provides a novel, physically motivated stochastic foundation for universal halo distributions that bypasses standard assumptions of spherical collapse or empirical halo fitting.

Main conclusions
  • Constructed a unified Fokker-Planck transport framework in scale space with scale-dependent waiting times $\tau(x) \propto x^{-\gamma}$ to describe turbulence, halo mass functions, and halo density profiles under a common mathematical formalism.
  • Recovered Kolmogorov's $-5/3$ turbulence spectrum with an exponential cutoff $\exp(-k^{4/3})$ and derived the Kolmogorov constant $C_K$ using a self-consistency condition $\gamma=2/3$ matching local eddy turnaround times.
  • Derived a generalized 'double-\lambda$' halo mass function that reduces to the Press-Schechter form in linear theory while capturing a shift from primordial initial conditions at low masses to a universal gravity-dominated regime (\lambda=2/3) near $m_h^*$.
  • Predicted dark matter halo density profiles of the Einasto form, showing an inner profile imprinted by primordial fluctuations and a universal gravity-dominated outer slope of $\rho(r) \propto r^{-4/3}$ near the scale radius $r_s$.

Abstract

We develop a unified stochastic framework for nonequilibrium systems and apply it to turbulence and cosmic structure formation. Spectra and distributions evolve by transport in "scale space", described by random walks and Fokker-Planck equations with scale-dependent waiting times. We reformulate the turbulent cascade as a random walk in wavenumber space with waiting time $\tau_{gk}\propto k^{-\gamma}$. With $\gamma=2/3$ fixed by self-consistency, we recover the Kolmogorov $-5/3$ law and obtain the full energy spectrum, including the inertial-dissipation crossover, exponential cutoff and spectrum constant, without applying dimensional analysis to the spectrum itself. In bottom-up hierarchical structure formation, low-mass haloes collapse early and retain primordial imprints, while prolonged assembly near the characteristic mass $m_h^*$ erases this memory and establishes a universal spectrum $P(k)\propto k^{-1}$. We model this as a random walk in halo mass space with $\tau_g\propto m_h^{-\lambda}$. This yields a stretched Gaussian halo mass function that reproduces the Press-Schechter form, with small-scale scaling $f_M\propto m_h^{-\lambda}$ and an exponential cutoff at $m_h>m_h^*$, both set by $\lambda$. It also produces a double-$\lambda$ mass function: $\lambda$ reflects the primordial spectrum at low masses and approaches $2/3$ near $m_h^*$, where gravity dominates. For individual haloes, a spatial random walk with $\tau_{gr}\propto r^{-\gamma}$ generates a double-$\gamma$ density profile that reproduces the Einasto profile, with small-scale scaling $\rho_r\propto r^{-2\gamma}$ and an exponential cutoff at $r>r_s$, both set by $\gamma$. Here $\gamma$ is fixed by the initial spectrum in the inner core and approaches $2/3$ ($\rho_r\propto r^{-4/3}$) in the gravity-dominated outskirts. This framework thus describes turbulent cascades and cosmic structure formation on a common footing.

Why: The paper directly addresses the dynamical dark energy ($w_0w_a$) interpretation of DESI DR2 BAO and CMB data using neural-network emulators, which aligns with your medium-relevance focus on evolving dark energy and emulator-based multi-probe likelihoods.

Recent analyses combining DESI baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) data with cosmic microwave background (CMB) measurements under $\Lambda$CDM have shown hints of modified recombination history, which alters the sound horizon and last-scattering epoch $a_\star$ to reconcile inferred distances. This paper investigates whether this preference for modified recombination persists when allowing for late-time dynamical dark energy (DDE) using the CPL ($w_0 w_a$) parameterisation. The authors perform joint fits of a 7-parameter free-electron fraction reconstruction (ModRec) and DDE to DESI DR2 BAO, $5\times 2$pt galaxy clustering and lensing, three CMB likelihoods (Planck 2018, Planck PR4, P-ACT), and DES-Dovekie SNe Ia using neural-network emulators for fast inference. They demonstrate that when DDE is allowed, the apparent preference for modified recombination vanishes, while dark energy parameter constraints remain robust.

Abstract

Several recent analyses report a hint of modified recombination when the latest cosmic microwave background (CMB) and baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) data are jointly analysed. We examine whether this preference persists when allowing for departures in the late-time expansion history of the Universe. In-in formalism & $\mathcal{T}_{C}^{\star}$-product: no contribution from total time derivatives

Yohei Ema, Muzi Hong, Ryusuke Jinno, Kyohei Mukaida
arXiv:2611.31272 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, hep-ph, hep-th

In the in-in (Schwinger-Keldysh) formalism used to calculate inflationary correlation functions, integrating interaction Lagrangians by parts introduces total time derivatives. Recent studies claimed that when these derivatives involve field velocities, they leave non-vanishing boundary contributions at the evaluation time $\tau_\star$ (the end of inflation). The authors disprove this claim using a path integral construction and Dirac canonical quantization with second-class constraints. They show that the path integral naturally prescribes a contour-ordered $\mathcal{T}_C^\star$-product where time derivatives act outside the time-ordering step functions, which generates contact terms at the contour turning point $\tau_\star$. When these contact terms are properly accounted for, the apparent boundary contributions from the forward and backward branches completely cancel out, restoring the exact physical equivalence of actions differing by total derivatives.

Main conclusions
  • Total time derivatives in interaction Lagrangians do not contribute boundary terms at the observation time $\tau_\star$ in in-in perturbation theory.
  • The evaluation point $\tau_\star$ is merely an intermediate turning point on the closed time path contour $C$, meaning boundary terms from the forward ($C_+$) and backward ($C_-$) branches identically cancel.
  • Deriving operator ordering from the path integral yields the $\mathcal{T}_C^\star$-product, which introduces contact terms whenever time derivatives act on contour step functions.
  • Previous claims of non-zero boundary contributions in inflationary 3-point and 4-point correlators arose from using a naive time ordering that omitted these necessary contact terms.

In the in-in formalism, total time derivatives are sometimes argued to contribute nontrivially to inflationary correlators at the evaluation point, i.e., at the end of inflation. RelatedMoritz S. Fischer
arXiv:2611.31364 · PDF · HTML · astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.GA, hep-ph

In standard $\Lambda$CDM, dark matter is modeled as collisionless, but self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) models allow particles to scatter non-gravitationally, which can alter small-scale halo profiles and induce gravothermal core collapse. State-of-the-art $N$-body implementations simulate these collisions using kernel smoothing and Monte Carlo interaction probabilities, but resolving the scattering mean free path in dense halo cores traditionally requires computationally expensive increases in particle numbers. The author presents a novel numerical scheme where the interaction probability is fixed to unity within a dynamically calculated interaction radius that depends on the particle relative velocity and timestep size, bypassing standard kernel functions and random-number drawing. Implemented in OpenGadget3, this method handles both large-angle and frequent small-angle scattering while mitigating unphysical successive scatterings between the same particle pair.

Main conclusions
  • Replaces kernel-based probabilistic scattering with a deterministic interaction distance threshold $r_{ij} \propto (\sigma v \Delta t)^{1/3}$, ensuring that only the closest particle pairs interact.
  • Significantly reduces computational overhead, with SIDM scattering routines accounting for only a few percent of the total simulation runtime, well below the gravitational solver cost.
  • Improves spatial resolution at fixed particle count, allowing simulations to properly resolve the scattering mean free path during deep gravothermal collapse phases.
  • Incorporates an adaptive fallback mechanism that enlarges search radii and lowers interaction probabilities whenever time steps become short enough to risk unphysical successive collisions.
